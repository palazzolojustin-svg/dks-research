# D02: MARGIN STRUCTURE (wave 1, thesis #2 discovery)
Agent D02 | 2026-10-07 | domain: falling costs / under-modelled gross-margin levers in core DKS for FY27 (freight, diesel, shrink, Fort Worth DC, occupancy, labor, interchange, healthcare, AI pricing)

**Bottom line:** Cost structure is NOT a viable thesis #2. I tested the hypothesis raised by F1, F3 and F5 that "FY26's temporary costs reverse in FY27". It fails for 1H FY27 and is small for the full year. Diesel futures are steeply backwardated, but they still put DICK'S Q1 FY27 diesel up 19% y/y. Ocean freight (3.5x the pre-war level), cotton (+25%) and 2027 employer healthcare (+8.2%, the biggest rise since 2003) are all still rising. My fuel model puts the FY26 fuel headwind at about 26bps of DICK'S sales. On the futures curve it reverses by only about +4bps in FY27 (+11bps on the EIA path), and that reversal falls in 2H. The interchange settlement is real money, but it lands in FY28. Retail wage disinflation is the only clean FY27 positive, and healthcare roughly offsets it. Consensus DSG GM of 36.31% FY27E (+7bps) looks fair on cost inputs. The swing factor is promotions, not costs.

Variance key used throughout: 1bp of DICK'S FY27E segment margin = $1.52M pre-tax = **$0.0124/share** (× (1−27.46%) ÷ 89.09M).

## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **D02-1 + D02-4 as a counter-thesis smoking gun.** "The costs that management called temporary are not temporary yet." The futures market prices DICK'S Q1 FY27 diesel +19% y/y, and transpacific freight is 3.5x pre-war and rising into the inventory that sells in 4Q26-1H27. A PM would remember it because it directly tests the "FY26 cost stack laps in FY27" idea that three folder agents (F1, F3, F5) scored as a thesis #2 candidate at +$0.31-0.49/share. The hard data points the other way for 1H FY27.
2. **D02-5 (retail wage disinflation, BLS):** the only clean, hard, positive FY27 cost datapoint: +$0.21/share if passed through. Healthcare roughly halves it (D02-6).
3. None of these is a novel *long* smoking gun. Thesis #2 should come from another domain (D01/D03/D04/D06).

## Evidence against / caveats
- Everything above is AGAINST or small. Net FY27 cost-input variance vs consensus GM, my INFERENCE: fuel +4 (futures) to +11 (EIA) bps; DC lap +5-8bps; tariffs 0 to +10bps; ocean freight on vertical imports 0 to −15bps; cotton/poly −bps (unsized). SG&A: wages +17bps (if passed through); healthcare −11bps; interchange 0 in FY27. **Net ≈ −5 to +20bps ≈ −$0.06 to +$0.25/share**, centred around +$0.05-0.10 with low confidence. That is not a pitchable variance.
- Q3 FY26 (reported ~11/24-25): diesel averaged $5.87 in Q3-to-date against $5.65 in the guide week (2026-08-24); post-guide weeks average ~$6.16. My model puts the extra headwind at only ~$2M (~6bps of Q3 sales), so it is immaterial on its own; consensus 3Q26E DSG GM of 35.42% already sits −62bps y/y.
- The fuel-exposure assumptions (1.5% trucking, 27% fuel share, 1.0% parcel) are unverified; DKS does not disclose e-com penetration or freight spend (C01A:94). Doubling the exposure doubles every fuel number above but does not change the sign or the timing.
- Shrink: the DKS 10-K says shrink "generally stabilized in 2025" (C01A:304, BURIED-ish but low value). Industry peers (Target, Ulta, Dollar General) reported shrink improvement in FY25; I found no 2026 DKS-specific data. Not a FY27 lever of size.
- AI pricing: DKS runs an Oracle AI pricing model handling ~11M SKU-price combinations a week; markdowns on golf apparel re-run in 72 hours (NRF Jan-2025, modernretail.co). That is qualitative only, and margin in 2026 was set by vendor price-matching anyway.
- Vendor tariff refunds: Nike expects $986M of IEEPA refunds (supplychaindive 2026-07-08) with no mention of sharing with wholesale partners. No evidence of a refund pass-through to DKS beyond the known Deckers precedent.
