## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **F4-1, consensus implies about 0% underlying comp.** This is the cleanest "variance vs consensus" exhibit for thesis #1, because it uses consensus's own store path. The same arithmetic that reproduces the last 6 quarters (underlying about 3.0%, matching UBS) shows consensus 3Q26E-2Q27E leaves −0.4% to +0.4% for the legacy fleet. That is below Wells Fargo's bear 1.2%. A PM remembers "the Street is more bearish than the bear." It needs H-agent evidence on HoS year-1/2 sales (to pin the +$20M) and F1's relocation share. Pair it with the F4-2 caveat so it is not attacked.
2. **F4-5, landlords are writing bigger checks.** 1H26 construction allowances of $129M are +83% y/y, against a consensus 2H that halves. It is hard data showing third parties underwriting HoS economics. It is stronger if H04 finds REIT disclosures of the Dick's TI packages.
3. (Against) **F4-2 + F4-3.** The returns are gross of cannibalization, and HoS openings have slipped vs every Aug-25 model. The pitch should concede that unit counts carry no upside and argue sales per store and the base comp instead.

## Evidence against / caveats
- F4-2: the slide economics are explicitly gross of cannibalization on other stores, and returns blend remodel, lease and purchase deal structures.
- F4-3: HoS openings trailed the JPM and Barclays Aug-25 paths by 3-6 stores. FY26 HoS was cut from 53-60 (broker models) and ~55 (mgmt) to 49. Consensus FY27 already equals the company's 67-68. There is no unit-count upside.
- F4-1 is model-dependent. With HoS +$15M and FH +$0, implied underlying rises to +0.3 to +1.1%, which is closer to, but mostly still below, WF's 1.2%. My back-test 4Q25 underlying of only 0.74% shows that legacy-store comps can drop near zero in a tough quarter. That is what consensus assumes for 3Q26/4Q26, given the promotional footwear market (digest: DSG segment OM 3Q26E 7.17% vs 8.92% PY).
- Primer data point: a regular DICK'S store does ~$15-16M, while FH year-1 is ~$14M. The FH sales uplift may be small or zero, and FH's case rests on capex returns (40% cash-on-cash), not comp.
- F4-5: some of the 2H decline in consensus allowances is rational, because consensus 2H26 has HoS +8 vs +13 in 2H25. Allowances lag openings, so quarterly data is lumpy.
- Bloomberg sq-ft consensus (3Q26E 46,185K, +1.1% y/y; 4Q26E +1.8%; 2Q27E +1.4%) implies modest footage growth. At JPM's "70% of footage growth flows into comp" rule, that is only about 0.8-1.3 pts of relocation comp. This contradicts the 1.8-2.2 pts from my store-count model (F4-1), so consensus may itself be internally inconsistent between its sq-ft and store-count rows (few contributors on these rows).
