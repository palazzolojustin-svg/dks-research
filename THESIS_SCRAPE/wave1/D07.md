## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **D07-1 + D07-2 together.** The company put out a public "transformative" store-labor redesign (2026-04-14). It booked severance and training charges in Q2 FY26. It never sized the savings. It is happening on the exact line (personnel, +44bp) that explains ~80% of the 1H FY26 core margin decline. A PM will remember "core margin isn't broken, it's a labor-model transition that's already been restructured, and the Street hasn't credited a dollar of savings." It is not yet hard proof of savings. The proof arrives with the Q3 FY26 10-Q segment table (DICK'S personnel % y/y: the pitch would ideally show deleverage narrowing from +44bp toward ≤+15bp).
2. **D07-4** is a secondary support: it shows that part of the 2026 "other expense" deleverage was a one-time World Cup marketing push.

## Evidence against / caveats
- Part of the personnel step-up is structural. HoS needs ~168 staff vs ~65 for a FH (WF unit model, F6-3), and Q2 FY26 alone opened 5 HoS and 8 FH. More HoS means a higher labor ratio until the stores ramp.
- Management frames Built to Win as service investment: more Specialists and more leads, which may mean higher pay rates. The release says nothing about cost. DKS calls itself an "employer of choice", and in 2024 it deliberately added payroll coverage.
- Healthcare: "higher teammate healthcare costs" were cited in Q2 FY26 and are probably inside personnel. They may persist into FY27.
- Incentive comp: after the August cut, the 2H FY26 accrual is likely low and rebuilds in FY27 (a headwind; not quantifiable from filings).
- SBC rose $57.3M (FY23) → $71.0M → $123.7M (FY25, incl FL awards). Grants made at ~$200+ now vest at ~$137, which risks tax shortfalls (FY23/FY24 had −4.9/−1.8pt ETR benefits from excess SBC). That is a mild FY27 ETR headwind.
- The OBBBA cash-tax deferral flatters FCF (D07-7). There is a new class action (D07-8).
- Consensus may already include some personnel leverage (FY27 DSG margin +29bp), so the net variance is smaller than the gross +$0.52.
