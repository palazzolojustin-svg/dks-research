## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **D03-1, the CPI-vs-consensus-ticket gap.** A one-table exhibit: category inflation went from −5% to +5% in 18 months, yet the Street's Q3/Q4 ticket (+1.0-1.25%) sits below every DKS ticket print in 7 quarters, all of them made during deflation. It is novel (no CPI anywhere in the corpus), reproducible from government data, and translates directly into comp bps. The weak spot is the Q2 FY26 spread compression (ticket +3.6% vs basket ≈ +4.3%) and management's stated price investment. A PM will ask why ticket slowed in Q2 when inflation rose. Answer to prepare: mix (trading cards, WC jerseys) plus promo matching. The Sept CPI print (~Oct 14) and Q3 results will show whether that holds.
2. **D03-2, the historic El Niño with the 2015 DKS analog (a risk, not support).** A hard, dated NOAA probability (75% chance of the strongest event since 1950) plus DKS's own 2015 guide cut. A PM would remember it. It cuts against a Q4 beat, but the 2023 analog limits the damage, and it gives FY27 an easy Q4 compare.
3. **D03-3, the realized +$49.8B refund season.** It quantifies the Q1 FY26 tailwind that the FY27 lap is built on. It cuts against FY27 1H, though consensus 2.41% already discounts most of it.

## Evidence against / caveats
- DKS did not pass category inflation through in Q2 FY26 (ticket +3.6% vs basket ≈ +4.3%); Q2 call: "pricing investments we're making today as an investment in the future"; GM guided to "decline slightly". Hobart (Mar-26): AUR strength "It's really not due to inflation". So D03-1 is a ticket floor argument, not a pass-through argument.
- BSM weekly DKS spend in Sept-26 was −3.0% avg (count −4.45%, ticket +1.17% w/e 27-Sep) (digest l.463). Card data point to weak transactions, which would offset a ticket beat. D06 owns this.
- El Niño (D03-2), golf (D03-6) and the refund lap (D03-3) are all headwinds to a near-term core beat.
- GLP-1: WF names DKS a relative loser; mgmt says no measurable correlation.
- Participation tailwinds are slow-moving (+1.8%/yr) and already in the run-rate; the known bear (long-term decline) is rebutted, but this does not create a step change versus consensus.
- Google Trends 2026 data has a collection artifact; do not use Mar-Jun 2026 values.
