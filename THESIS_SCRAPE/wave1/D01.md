# D01 (wave 1): COMPETITOR CAPACITY EXIT / SHARE CAPTURE
Agent D01. Run 2026-10-07. Domain: sporting-goods / athletic capacity leaving the market in 2025-2027 inside DKS trade areas, and whether consensus under-models DKS's recapture.
Variance convention: $/share = Δrevenue × incremental margin × (1 − 27.46%) ÷ 89.09M sh; comp bps = Δrevenue ÷ FY27E DKS segment revenue $15,203M.

**Bottom line (INFERENCE):** competitor capacity really is leaving, but slowly, and it is a long-tail event, not a single large one. Three pieces of evidence show it: (1) new BLS QCEW data show US sporting-goods stores with 10-49 employees fell by 390 establishments (−6.5%) and lost 8,300 jobs (−6.7%) between March 2023 and March 2026, while small and large stores were flat; (2) JD will close ~175 Hibbett doors over FY27-29 and convert or close all 145 remaining standalone Finish Line stores by FY28; (3) Big 5 continues to trickle closures. My estimate of what DKS recaptures is only ~+30-50bps of core comp a year ($0.11-0.16/sh, about 1% of FY27E EPS). Capacity additions offset most of that: Academy opens 20-25 stores a year, SCHEELS adds megastores, and Target is re-investing in sports and fandom. Against KNOWN_BRIEF ("share gains"), this is a supporting mechanism and does not stand on its own. **Do not use as thesis #2.** Score 2.4/5 average (see ranking).

## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **The QCEW "missing middle" (D01-1):** −390 mid-size sporting-goods establishments (−6.5%) and −6.7% employment from Mar-2023 to Mar-2026, with large stores flat. It is fresh, government-sourced and quarterly, and it can be updated (2Q26 data due ~Dec-2026). It best shows structural consolidation toward large-format stores, which also helps the thesis #1 narrative (HoS/FH capture the exiting mid-size demand). It is not a smoking gun on size, though: ~$0.1/sh. The county control (D01-8) also shows the exit is not much worse in DKS counties than the general urban-retail decline.
2. **The Sports Authority 2016 analog (D01-4):** consensus at −2.2% vs +2.8% printed, guidance raised by ~300bps. Memorable as a pattern ("the Street models the liquidation headwind, never the recapture"), but no current exit of that scale exists.
3. **JD's dual retreat in the same window (D01-2/3):** 175 Hibbett closures, plus Finish Line standalone going to zero by FY28, plus NA comparable −3.5% against DKS core +4.9-6.0%. Good for a slide on the competitive map, but already directionally known as "share gains".

## Evidence against / caveats
- Net capacity is probably flat to up. Academy (+20-25/yr), SCHEELS (+1-2 megastores a year in DKS metros), JD-banner big boxes, Shoe Palace, off-price and Target's sports and fandom reinvestment offset the exits (D01-6).
- QCEW exit is decelerating: establishments were −2.1%/−1.8% y/y in 3Q/4Q25 and −0.2% in 1Q26.
- Expert claims don't survive testing. Kohl's is not closing stores and Nike is gaining there (against expert E4's "share from Kohl's"). Academy is not "divesting macro markets" (no Academy closures; FY25 10-K: 0 closed). It is simply growing faster in satellite towns, which is consistent with E14's second half.
- Hibbett and Big 5 closures are mostly low-volume, remote or Western stores, where DKS density is lower than in its Northeast and Midwest core.
- Consensus already embeds share gains: core comp of 1.6-2.4% vs a sporting-goods average of ~0%. Incremental recapture is small relative to the HoS/FH contribution (150-250bps).
- County control (D01-8): sporting-goods establishments fell 2.99pt more in DKS counties than in non-DKS counties, but all retail shows a 2.21pt gap too. The DKS-specific excess is only ~0.8pt over 3 years.
- QCEW establishments ≠ stores ≠ sales. The 10-49 class includes some DKS-owned Golf Galaxy/GGG doors and is affected by seasonal hiring timing (March snapshot).
- Two source conflicts with the Wells primer (C06_R5): Big 5 deal value $33.6M (not ~$112M equity); and Eddie Bauer stores are in liquidation (not "open during restructuring").
