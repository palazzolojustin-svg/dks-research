DICK'S Sporting Goods (DKS) · long pitch · research memo · v2

DKS Long Thesis Memo

Updated 7 Oct 2026 after four research waves across the full research folder and public web data (company data through 5 Oct 2026). This version focuses on thesis 1. DKS fiscal 2026 ends late January 2027.

Bottom line

Thesis 1: consensus is too low on the DICK'S business for the next two quarters. The consensus comps of 1.69% (Q3) and 1.59% (Q4) only work if DICK'S older stores stop growing. They have grown in every one of the last ten quarters, and new public data show House of Sport stores selling at or above plan. We estimate about +1.5 points of comp in 2H FY26, about +$0.16 a share (range −$0.10 to +$0.43), with Q4 alone worth up to about +$0.19. The single most memorable fact: Bloomberg consensus implies a 4.74% two-year comp for Q4, the weakest in the series, against 9.7–10.8% for six straight quarters.

What we learned since v1. The 2H26 call held up. The unresolved question is how much each House of Sport adds over the store it replaces. Government tax data say less than the company's $20M (a median near $6M across six stores). Government jobs data and mall-loan filings say about $12–24M. That split mainly changes the fiscal 2027 view, which is close to neutral. It does not change the 2H26 view, because that rests on total comp, not on how comp splits between formats.

Thesis 2, the "Built to Win" store-labor reset, is real and unpriced but does not beat consensus on its own. It is summarized briefly below.

Thesis 1 · Consensus has the older stores stalling just as House of Sport scales

The claim. DICK'S comp has two engines. The first is the new formats: House of Sport (100,000+ sq ft, with climbing walls, turf fields and batting cages) and Field House (about 50,000 sq ft). The second is the other ~700 older stores. Consensus for the second half of fiscal 2026 assumes the second engine stops.

The arithmetic. Take the 1.69% and 1.59% comps consensus expects for Q3 and Q4 and remove the format contribution on consensus's own store-opening path. What is left implies the older stores comp at about 0%. On the same model, those stores averaged +3.5% over the last eight quarters and never fell below +1.3%. Every way we ran it, consensus needs the older stores to slow 3.1–3.7 points from their own trailing pace.

Why the format debate does not break this. If House of Sport adds less than we assume (the tax-data view), the format piece shrinks. Then consensus implies a slightly higher older-store comp, about +0.7%. But the older stores' trailing pace rises by the same amount, so the required slowdown barely changes. The 2H26 variance rests on total comp. Our evidence for total 2H comp is about 3.1%, from trailing trends and third-quarter foot-traffic and card data, against 1.63% in consensus. That does not depend on the split (our estimate).

Fiscal 2027 is close to neutral. Fewer of the 2027 openings are relocations (about 44–54%, against 81% in 2025), new stores take some sales from nearby older stores, and this year's World Cup and Foot Locker clearance sales must be lapped. With those, consensus already assumes the older stores recover to about +1.7%. If the per-store House of Sport gain is at the low end of the evidence, fiscal 2027 turns slightly negative.

Smoking gun · thesis 1

Consensus is pricing the weakest holiday quarter in the series

Bloomberg's consensus file carries a "two-year stacked" comp row for the DICK'S business. It drops to 4.74% for Q4 FY26 and stays near 4.9% in late fiscal 2027. Actuals have run 9.7–10.8% for six straight quarters, and management framed the year at about 7.5% in March. This row was in the research folder but had never been surfaced.

If Q4 simply printed management's ~7.5% two-year framing (about a 4.4% comp against 1.59%), that is about +$114M of revenue and +$0.19 a share for Q4 alone (our estimate). It is also a simple test: the 4Q26 consensus row fails if Q4 comp prints above ~3%.

Why the older stores have not stalled

  • Mall-loan filings, 11 store-years. Older, non-relocated stores ran on average only 0.6 points below reported comp in FY22–FY24 (median 1.3 points). Wells Fargo's bear case subtracts 2.4–2.7 points. The sample is small (±1.45 points).
  • Consensus needs ticket to collapse. To get to 1.69% in Q3, average ticket would have to fall from +3.6% to about +1%. Outside COVID, that kind of drop has happened only once since FY12.
  • Q3 foot traffic. Using the exact data behind Placer.ai's published charts, the 1.69% Q3 comp needs older-store traffic to fall to about −6% in September and October, worse than August. The historical link between visits and comp points to a Q3 comp of about +2.7% to +3.3%.
  • The bear's traffic data misses the best stores. Placer tracks House of Sport as a separate chain, so the widely quoted "DICK'S traffic is down" series leaves them out. With them added back, DICK'S-family visits were positive for four straight quarters.

How much does a House of Sport really add?

This is the key open question, and the evidence splits by method. The company and UBS assume about $35M of sales against roughly $15M for the store it replaces, a gain of about $20M per relocation. Mall-loan filings confirm the $15M base: the three replaced stores with filed sales average $15.0M. The evidence on the gain:

Our read. Tax data understate DICK'S own gain. They miss online sales credited to the store and cross-county shoppers. Minnesota exempts most clothing from sales tax (outside knowledge). And a gain taken from a local competitor nets to zero in a county total. Jobs data and landlord filings overstate the typical store, because they lean toward big, high-traffic malls. Five House of Sport stores replaced two DICK'S doors at once, which lowers the net gain. Our best estimate is about $12–16M for a typical relocation, $20–25M for the A-mall tail, and about $30M+ for a net-new store. That is the base-case $16M in the variance table above. The 2H26 view holds even at the low end; fiscal 2027 does not (our estimate).

The hard evidence for the format

Every item below is a public document or government dataset, and none was in the research folder or the existing primers before this work.

Two House of Sport stores with filed sales: Tampa and Ridgedale

The International Plaza (Tampa) loan filing says the 130,000 sq ft store "generated $12.3 million in gross sales in its first three months", about $40–44M a year after adjusting for seasonality. The Ridgedale Center (Minnetonka) loan filing gives the store's 2024 sales as $31,197,485 ($271/sq ft) in its third year. That excludes most online sales, so on a comparable basis it sits about 6–11% above the company's $35M target. Rent at both is 5.5–6% of sales, versus 6.5–16% at older stores in the same filings.

SEC EDGAR: BBCMS 2025-5C38 term sheet (31 Oct 2025); Ridgedale CMBS 424H (5 Aug 2025, n5210_x6-424h.htm)

Government jobs data: every opening adds about 110 jobs, and they stay

We ran an event study on Bureau of Labor Statistics county employment for sporting-goods stores across 34 House of Sport openings (2021–26), against 373 other DICK'S counties. It shows about +121 net jobs in the opening quarter and +117 a year later. A placebo test using the same dates on similar counties shows −10 to −24. 71% of real openings beat the placebo's 95th percentile, against 5% by chance.

BLS QCEW county files (NAICS 451110), 2019Q1–2026Q1

DICK'S own leasing deck: mall traffic +14% after an opening

DICK'S filed its June 2025 real-estate deck, built on Advan cellphone foot-traffic data, with the Town of Braintree, MA. It shows malls gaining 14% more visits in the year after a House of Sport opens, then +2% and 0%. Comparable malls went −6%, +1% and −2%. House of Sport drew "2X more total athlete visits and +20% greater dwell time than DICK'S" in 2024. No copy turned up in other towns' records.

Town of Braintree DocumentCenter View/16382 and View/16388 (South Shore Plaza)

DICK'S projections in city records: Corpus Christi and Novi

In Corpus Christi, TX (Sep 2026) the company projected a relocation would take the store from about $20M to $40M of revenue and from 626,000 to over 2 million visits a year. In Novi, MI, its own floor plan shows the House of Sport with only 12.7% more selling space than the store it replaces (59,889 vs 53,127 sq ft), but 84% more footwear space. So the gain comes from the format, not from size.

Corpus Christi Legistar 26-1417 (passed 8 and 15 Sep 2026); Novi MI zoning packet (DKS staffing letter, Apr 2025)

Government tax data: the first House of Sport doubled its county

New York taxable sporting-goods sales in Ontario County (Victor) went from about $34M a year to $67–70M and held for five years, first of 50 upstate counties. Later stores were weaker, as the table above shows.

Cities and landlords are funding the build-out

Joliet, IL will reimburse up to $37M of a $38.0M House of Sport, and DICK'S takes title. Landlord construction allowances rose from $76M (FY24) to $162M (FY25) and $129M in 1H FY26, now about 21% of DICK'S capex. Store leases signed but not yet started rose from $352M to $756M at the end of FY25. Macerich reports Freehold, NJ drew "over 800,000 customers" in about nine months and calls it the "strongest opening out of 35." Advan reports the 2024 class of stores is outperforming the 2023 class.

What cuts against thesis 1

  • Tax data show smaller gains. Outside the first store, tax data show much smaller per-store gains (median about $6M across six). If that is right, the format adds about 80bp of comp, not 150–170bp, and fiscal 2027 turns negative.
  • Build costs have risen. Net capex per House of Sport rose from about $11.5M (for ~35% first-year cash return, March 2024) to "slightly over $20M" (~25%, Nov 2025), on the same $35M sales target.
  • Mall halo not seen in one filing. The Ridgedale loan filing shows the mall's other small shops flat on sales per sq ft (2021 $545, 2024 $547), while the company deck claims +17.9% across its four sample malls.
  • Visits overstated. Advan puts House of Sport visits at about 1.33x a regular DICK'S, not "2x" or "3x". Mature stores' Google review velocity has faded, from 1.72x to 1.54x a legacy store.
  • No store-count upside. The named pipeline matches consensus (49 at FYE26, 68–69 at FYE27), and several flagship mall sites have slipped to 2028–29.
  • Near-term risks. Traffic panels are soft into Q3, footwear is promotional, and management guided Q3 cost deleverage. At the segment margin rather than 20% flow-through, the 2H benefit falls to about +$0.09.

Thesis 2 · The "Built to Win" store-labor reset (supporting point)

Store personnel cost explains about 44 of the 54bp DICK'S margin decline in 1H FY26. In the summer of 2026 DICK'S quietly rebuilt its store labor model. It announced "Built to Win" in April, took a $15.3M charge, and switched over in late June and July. It cut the hourly lead role, demoted assistant managers into a new Team Captain layer (employees report a median −$11,000 a year) and centralized store hour budgets. No analyst has asked about it in 65 cost questions.

The savings are real but modest: about $30M gross (+21bp, +$0.26 a share) on our base case. Peer programs and DICK'S own 2023 restructuring suggest $18–55M. With consensus revenue growing only 3% against 3%+ wage inflation and heavier House of Sport staffing, most of the saving goes to meeting consensus margin. Its real value is as leverage on thesis 1: with the new model, each extra point of comp adds about +$0.36 a share. The test is the personnel line in the Q3 10-Q in early December.
