Agent B11, wave 3, 2026-10-07. Method as in H07: official taxable/gross sales by locality and industry around each House of Sport (HoS) opening, against matched controls (regional sum, median growth of large peer localities, and pooled peers). New York is left to B5.
Novelty: Virginia, Minnesota-with-controls and Iowa tax series appear nowhere in the digest, primers, KNOWN_BRIEF, the memo or earlier wave files. I grepped Albemarle, Wegmans, 5th Street Station, Westridge, Greenbrier and virginia.gov on 2026-10-07. B5 (wave 2) fetched raw Minnetonka files but called the series "not comparable" and never ran a control test, so the control-city result below is NEW. ## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **Pooled panel B11-4.** This is official tax-return data from 4 states. Only the 2021 flagship (Victor) clears the +$20M bull assumption. The typical relocation or conversion adds ~$6-9M, and the 2-into-1 conversions (Chesapeake, Charlottesville) add ~$3-7M. It is a *negative* smoking gun for the HoS-variance leg, and the PM should hear it from the analyst first. It reframes thesis #1 to: HoS economics are real (Live Oak net-new ~$30M; Ridgedale $31M), but consensus's low core comp is less inconsistent than the memo claims.
2. **Chesapeake closure-quarter dip (B11-2).** A clean, dated, ~-$3M hole in the exact quarter the DSG + F&S closed proves the data captures DKS store sales. This makes the small post-opening increments credible rather than noise.
3. **Minnetonka with controls (B11-3).** Combined with B9's $31.2M, it gives a rare fully triangulated relocation: HoS store sales ~$31M, net city gain ~$12M, implied prior store ~$19M.

## Evidence against / caveats
- All measures are **net market** changes. Sales an HoS takes from in-county specialty competitors (same NAICS) net to zero, so the DKS increment is understated. Hobby, book and music stores are in 451/459. Gross sales in Minnesota can include non-retail receipts.
- Charlottesville is only 3 quarters in, and its base already included the Public Lands consolidation (Jan-2025) plus a likely partial-closure quarter (2025Q3 excess about -$0.7M). The year-2 read (2026Q3-2027Q2) could be larger, since the company says HoS comps are positive in years 2-4.
- Virginia 2025Q4 brought a large statewide NAICS reclassification (452 General Merchandise down, 000 "unknown" up). State 451 shows no break (2025Q4 -3% y/y, in line with trend), but it is a risk.
- Minnesota is annual, 2021 is the pandemic-boom base, and 6 of 15 control cities have no 2024 file yet.
- Small n. All four new-state cases are relocations or conversions, not net-new mall anchors. Net-new stores (Live Oak; and from 2024, Ross Park, Prudential, Jersey City) should add closer to the full ~$30-35M. The FY26-27 opening mix matters: a higher net-new share lifts the average.
- FOR the thesis: none of the cases shows *negative* net category sales after opening. There is no sign of the HoS hollowing out its own trade area.
