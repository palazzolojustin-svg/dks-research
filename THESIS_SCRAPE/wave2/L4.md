## Top findings (ranked)

[L4-1] **Lowe's 2017 is the closest analog, and its pattern mirrors DKS 1H FY26. Lowe's operating salaries deleveraged on rate and hours, Lowe's installed a "new store staffing model" / "store leadership model", and operating salaries then levered 20-40bp a quarter (27bp for FY17) at a 2-4.5% comp, starting in the first quarter. A second wave delivered 45bp in FY19.**

The setup in 2016 (10-Q 2016-09-02):
- "30 basis points of deleverage in operating salaries primarily due to increases in both hourly rate and hours" (Q2 FY16), then a further 11bp in Q3.
- This is the same shape as DKS 1H FY26 (+44bp).

The first wave in 2017:
- Q1 FY17 (10-Q 2017-06-06): "we realized the benefits of the new store staffing model, which was designed to provide better leadership and accountability, allowing us to grow sales with more efficient staffing" → **40bp leverage in operating salaries on a +1.9% comp**.
- Q2: 20bp "as we modified our store leadership model" (comp +4.5%).
- Q3: 27bp "as we streamlined our store leadership model" (9M: 35bp).
- FY17: **27bp** (comp +4.0%; 10-K 2018-04-02).

Partial give-back in 2018:
- Q1 FY18: **32bp deleverage** at a +0.6% comp. Management had flagged "investments in customer-facing hours".
- FY18 overall still levered 15bp, with Q4 FY18 at 52bp "from expense management".

The second wave in 2019:
- Payroll hours were moved from "60% … tasking and only 40% … selling in 2018" to ">50% … selling" (8-K 2020-02-26 EX-99.2).
- The Project Specialists Interiors role was eliminated.
- Retail operating salaries levered 80bp in Q1 FY19 and 50bp in Q2.
- **FY19 salaries levered 45bp** (comp +2.6%; 10-K 2020-03-23).

[L4-3] **Barnes & Noble 2018 is the upper bound. A "new labor model" for its stores cut ~1,800 roles for a $10.7M charge. Store payroll fell 80-150bp of store sales within 1-3 quarters, even on negative comps.**
- 8-K 2018-02-13: "implemented a new labor model for its stores that has resulted in the elimination of certain store positions"; "annual cost savings of approximately $40 million" (≈1.1% of ~$3.6B sales).
- Before the change, store payroll deleveraged every quarter by +25 to +110bp of store sales ("as sales deleverage and wage increases offset productivity gains").
- After implementation in Q4 FY18, store payroll as % of store sales:
  - **−150bp in Q2 FY19** ("on the implementation of the new store labor model during the fourth quarter of fiscal 2018");
  - −135bp for 26 weeks, −100bp for 39 weeks;
  - **−80bp for FY19** ("on a new labor model"), plus −40bp in benefits on the lower payroll.
- Per-role math (arithmetic): $10.7M ÷ 1,800 ≈ **$5.9K severance per role**; $40M ÷ 1,800 ≈ **$22K annual savings per role**.

[L4-4] **AGAINST: the 2022-2026 peer cycle is store-labor REINVESTMENT, not cuts. DKS's design (3,200 "leadership opportunities", Team Captains paid +36% over the old Lead floor) looks more like the reinvestment archetype than the cost-out one.**
- **Home Depot FY2022:** a "new store leadership structure" that "created new management positions in our stores … increasing the number of managers on the floor at any given time" (10-K 2023-03-15). SG&A then rose 17.4% → 18.0% in FY24, "primarily … higher payroll costs", and 18.0% → 18.6% in FY25, "higher payroll and related costs" (10-Ks 2025-03-21, 2026-03-18).
- **Dollar General FY2023:** after its "Fast Track" labor-productivity era, DG reversed course with "approximately $150 million in retail labor, primarily through labor hours" to fix store standards and "higher than targeted" store-manager turnover. That is ≈ +40bp of sales (INFERENCE: $150M ÷ ~$38.7B).
- **Walmart Apr-2026:** eliminated the "store lead" position and converted holders to "emerging store manager" (base $85-120K; $119-276K with bonus) or "coach" (base $65-100K). The restructuring "will not result in layoffs" (Yahoo Finance memo 2026-04-28; HR Grapevine 2026-04-30).
- **Target Feb-2026:** cut ~100 store-district roles and ~400 supply-chain roles "to put significantly more payroll in our stores — primarily in additional labor and hours" (CNBC 2026-02-09, internal memo).
- **Sportsman's Warehouse 1H FY25:** SG&A flat at 35.4% of sales because of "reinvestment into customer facing … areas … including store payroll" (8-K 2025-09-04 EX-99).
- Taken together with ASO's Q2 FY26 store-labor test (WA5-6, DUP), DKS's peers are spending more on store service in 2026. That raises the odds DKS reinvests part of any Built to Win savings.

[L4-5] **Hidden costs and give-backs of cost-out redesigns: Big Lots's 2019 removal of assistant leaders "slightly backfired" through shrink (~30bp of GM). Bed Bath's $16.9M store-management realignment produced no visible payroll savings.**
- **Big Lots:**
  - In Q2 2019 it introduced "a new, simpler management structure" in stores. CEO Bruce Thorn (Q4 2019 call, 8-K 2020-03 EX-99.2): "in certain volume stores, … remove one of the Assistant Leaders, and transform those hours there into more customer-facing hours".
  - Q4 2019 SG&A levered 30bp, "primarily from lower store payroll and corporate headquarters expense", on a −0.9% comp.
  - But shrink "contributed approximately 30 basis points to the gross margin rate erosion, and we believe stemmed from a transition in the store labor model" (CFO Ramsden). The analyst's question framed it as having "slightly backfired".
  - Store labor was "a big component" of the $100M three-year program ($40M in 2019).
- **Bed Bath & Beyond:** the "acceleration of the realignment of our store management structure" cost $16.9M in Q2 FY17 (plus $3.9M in Q1 FY19). Payroll still ranked first among SG&A deleverage drivers in FY17 on falling comps.
- **AEO:** the "efficiencies in our store labor model" only partly offset wage rates. Store compensation still rose +$24M in FY23 and +$16M over 39 weeks of FY24.
- The netting: across the cost-out cases, the net P&L benefit looks like ~50-75% of gross once shrink, reinvested hours and wage drift are counted (INFERENCE).

## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **L4-1, the Lowe's 2017 mirror.** "Lowe's operating salaries deleveraged 30bp on 'increases in both hourly rate and hours' (2016). It installed a new store staffing/leadership model and levered 40bp the very next quarter on a 1.9% comp, 27bp for the year." It is hard, dated, from SEC filings, and it is the same setup as DKS's +44bp 1H FY26. It also gives a PM a concrete check: DKS Q3 FY26 personnel should flip within one quarter if Built to Win is a cost-out.
2. **L4-2, the charge-multiple table.** It turns the unsized $21M charge into a defensible $19-78M range (central ~$55M) and shows the honest result: the central case is roughly what consensus already embeds. The upside needs the B&N or Lowe's-wave-2 tail, or the comp beat (thesis #1).

## Evidence against / caveats
- **Archetype risk (L4-4).** DKS's own release stresses ">3,200 new leadership opportunities". Team Captains carry higher pay (H06-3), and HoS is excluded. That is closer to Home Depot FY22 and Walmart 2026 (service-up) than to Lowe's 2017 or B&N (cost-out). Unlike Bed Bath, B&N, Big Lots and DKS 2023, however, the severance-backed charge does show heads came out.
- **Hidden costs (L4-5).** Shrink (Big Lots ~30bp GM), reinvested customer-facing hours (Lowe's Q1 FY18 −32bp) and later reversal (DG FY23 +$150M) are all documented.
- **Transferability.**
  - B&N and Petco cut on falling sales (distressed). Their bps include sales deleverage swings.
  - Lowe's disclosed no charge and its 2017 program mixed leadership changes with hours scheduling.
  - The Gap and L Brands multiples include HQ roles, which have higher savings per $ of severance than store roles.
  - DKS's 2023 multiple (~1.1x) is the company's own record and sits at the low end.
- **Scaling.** The 0.85 factor for HoS/GG exclusion is my estimate (HoS ≈ $1.4-1.7B and Golf Galaxy ≈ $1B+ of DSG revenue; not disclosed). The bps-of-sales transfer assumes DKS's personnel intensity (~14%) is at least Lowe's/B&N's, which is not disclosed for those peers.
- **Best Buy** (L5-7) shows the program can need several waves (2019, 2021, 2024, 2026). I did not re-report it.
- **No peer data is available for 2026 cost-out store-labor programs** apart from Petco (late FY24-FY25). The 2026 peer flow (Walmart, Target, SPWH, ASO) is all reinvestment.
