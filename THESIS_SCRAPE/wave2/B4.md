# B4 (wave 2, finished in wave 3): FIELD HOUSE EVIDENCE
Agent B4, started 2026-10-07 AM, cut off by a usage limit, resumed and finished 2026-10-07 PM (wave 3). Thesis #1 (HoS/FH comp floor). Focus: the Field House (FH) store list, relocations and the size of the store replaced, municipal/press evidence, actual sales evidence for FH stores, and the comp lift per FH relocation.

**Bottom line:** we now have the first actual store-level sales evidence for Field House relocations. It comes from New York State taxable sales in counties where DKS is the only sizeable sporting-goods store. **An FH relocation adds roughly +$0-5M a year of in-store sales (median about +$2-3M). An HoS adds +$25-28M (Victor, known).** FH relocations are mostly same-size boxes (50-53K sq ft replacing ~50K), so the lift comes from format, not space. This validates B7's base assumption of +$2M per FH relocation (range $0-3M): FH is a minor comp contributor (~10-20bp a year), and the comp floor is an HoS story. Separately, a 2024 CFO quote shows HoS net capex rose from ~$11.5M to "slightly over $20M" on an unchanged $35M sales target, cutting year-1 cash-on-cash from ~35% to ~25%. Returns compressed on cost, not sales. FH economics ($14M, ~20% EBITDA) did not change.

## Top findings

[B4-1] **First actual-sales test of Field House relocations: in three single-DKS upstate NY counties, an FH/next-gen relocation added about +$1-5M a year of sporting-goods taxable sales. Only one case was durable. HoS Victor added +$25-28M on the same data.**
- Method: NY taxable sales and purchases for NAICS 4511/4591 (sporting goods, hobby, musical instrument stores) by county and quarter. I compare the 4 post-opening quarters with the same quarters a year earlier, against NY State and against the median upstate county (Ontario excluded for its $240.9M Sep-Nov 2025 misreport, H07-7). DKS is the only big-box sporting-goods retailer in these counties, and all of them tax clothing and footwear locally (NY Pub 718-C, 2025: Tompkins 4%, Jefferson 4%, Ulster 4%, Ontario 3.5%, Cayuga 4%). Their taxable sales therefore capture most in-store DKS sales, including apparel and footwear.
- **Tompkins (Ithaca #156, Shops at Ithaca Mall → #1535 South Meadow Square, ~Dec-2023; plan reported by Ithaca Voice 2022-10-19):**
  - Year 1 (Dec-23 to Nov-24): $24.43M → $28.93M, **+18.4%** vs State +0.4% and median upstate county −2.0%. Excess **+$3.8-5.0M**.
  - Year 2 (Dec-24 to Nov-25) vs the pre-year: +20.4% vs +1.1% to +6.6%. Excess **+$3.4-4.7M**. The lift held.
  - QCEW corroboration (sporting-goods stores, H07 file): Tompkins employment went from ~122-142 (2023 Q1-Q3) to 164-177 (2024 Q3 to 2025 Q1). Quarterly payroll went from ~$1.46-1.62M to $1.94-2.48M (+30%). Labour rose in line with sales, so this was not a productivity gain.
- **Jefferson (Watertown #158, Salmon Run Mall → #1545 Towne Center, 50K sq ft next to Target, opened 2024-04-17; WWNY 2022-10-03 and 2024-04-17):**
  - Year 1: +7.2% vs State +0.6% and median −1.0%. Excess **+$0.9-2.7M**.
  - Year 2: −1.4% vs +3.1% to +8.9%. Excess **−$1.5M to −3.4M**. No durable lift.
- **Ulster (Kingston #154, Hudson Valley Mall → #1622 Hudson Valley Plaza, 53K sq ft ex-Gander Mountain, moved 2026-03-11; Daily Freeman 2026-03-04):**
  - First quarter only (Mar-May 2026, preliminary): $9.37M → $10.89M, +16.2% vs State +6.2% and median +8.1%. Excess +$0.8-0.9M, about **+$3M annualised**. It includes the grand-opening effect.
- **Monroe (Rochester Marketplace Mall #427 → Henrietta #1630, 50K, opened 2025-05-08):** not readable. Monroe exempts clothing and footwear under $110 from local tax (Pub 718-C: Monroe 0%), and its base is ~$140M/yr. Year 1 was −0.7% vs State +5.8%.

## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **B4-1, "Field House relocations add ~$2-3M a year; House of Sport adds ~$25M, on the same government data."** It is the first actual-sales measurement of FH in the folder. It turns the FH part of the comp floor from an assumption into evidence: small and positive, and durable in only 1 of 2 mature cases. Its value is defensive and clarifying. It tells the PM that the comp-floor debate is about HoS openings and legacy comp, and that bulls cannot lean on FH scaling "across the majority of our DICK'S stores".
2. **B4-2, the HoS capex creep (~$11.5M → $20M+, 35% → 25% cash-on-cash) at an unchanged $35M sales target.** It is a memorable, dated management quote that no one in the corpus has surfaced. It cuts against the returns narrative and helps explain the 75-100 → 67-68 target cut.

## Evidence against / caveats
- n is small (3 readable FH counties). Watertown's year-1 lift reversed in year 2. County category totals include hobby and music stores and any other sporting-goods sellers. Ithaca's +18-20% could partly reflect non-DKS changes, though QCEW employment moved in step.
- Taxable sales are in-store only. E-commerce and BOPIS attribution is not captured, which would add perhaps 15-20% (INFERENCE).
- NY Mar-May 2026 data is preliminary. Kingston has one quarter.
- The NY sample is small-market upstate. FH relocations in larger metros (Henrietta) are not measurable with this data.
- The capex comparison is HoS net capex as quoted in trade press 2024 vs the company deck 2025. Definitions may differ (e.g., landlord allowances, owned vs leased). "Slightly over $20M" is KNOWN; only the 2024 baseline is new.
- Store-number pairing from Wayback is a proxy: capture dates lag openings, and some new numbers are renumbered conversions (ex-Field & Stream).
- FH labour intensity (Schererville +55 hires + temps) and HoS pay premiums (H06-7) are a cost the format math must carry.
