## P-TRACK VERDICT (P3, official-price lens)
**Category inflation is real for 2H26, but official data do NOT support "ticket will run well above consensus because of CPI/tariff pass-through".** DKS ticket has had no measurable relationship with category CPI for ten years (R² 0.00). It has run at about +4% since FY24 whether category prices fell 2% or rose 4%, which points to mix (HoS, premium footwear) rather than price. When CPI jumped in 1H26, DKS ticket did not speed up: the ticket-minus-CPI spread fell from +5.8pt (Q1 FY25) to −0.7pt (Q2 FY26). The CPI tailwind also fades on its own: on a flat-price path, the basket falls from about +3.8% (3Q26) to +0.5% (2Q27) as 2026 increases lap, and the NY Fed estimates the tariff level effect is shrinking (≈3% in Feb-26 → ≈2% in Aug-26 after the SCOTUS IEEPA ruling). Ticket upside therefore rests on mix persistence, which is a comp/format argument (thesis #1 territory), not a new pricing mechanism. Bloomberg's ticket/transaction rows (+1.25/+1.50 for 3Q26E) also do not add up to its comp row (1.69%; P4 flags this too), so "+1% ticket" is a weak anchor for variance. **Recommendation: do not use price/ticket as a standalone thesis #2.** At most, use it as a 2H26 support point: ticket gap scenarios are worth $0.22 (no CPI link) / $0.46 (half pass-through) / $0.78 (ticket persists at 3.6%) of EPS over 3Q26-2Q27 (25% flow-through). Only the middle case is CPI-driven, and the upper case is the known "low comp bar" point.

## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **[P3-1/P3-2] Ten years of data show DKS ticket ignores category CPI (R² 0.00), and the spread fell from +5.8pt to −0.7pt as inflation arrived.** This is the decisive test of the price/ticket thesis, and it comes out against a CPI-driven ticket beat. It reframes ticket as mix, which supports thesis #1 (HoS/premium mix) rather than standing alone.
2. **[P3-5] The shoe-retailer PPI margin index is down 42% y/y to a 10-year low.** It is a hard official datapoint that footwear retail is absorbing, not passing through, cost. It matters for FL-segment margin and is a risk a PM would remember.
3. **[P3-6] NY Fed (2026-10-06): the tariff level effect fell from about 3% to about 2% after SCOTUS.** The price tailwind is temporary.

## Evidence against / caveats
- Against the ticket thesis: P3-1, P3-2, P3-5, and the fade in P3-3/P3-6. A higher MXP (P3-4) means cost pressure, and DKS has chosen to "invest" in price, so more pass-through would cost gross margin rather than add pure upside.
- For: category CPI of +3.7-4.6% in 2H26 is about 3pt above the +1.0-1.25% ticket row; DKS ticket has been ≥ +3.6% for 8 straight quarters regardless of CPI (mix). If mix persists, the ticket row is too low (scenario C), but that overlaps with the known comp/HoS points.
- Data caveats: Oct-2025 CPI/MXP was never published (government shutdown) and is interpolated in the quarterly averages. Q4 ticket values are derived from FY minus 39-week figures. FY16-FY23 ticket is consolidated DKS (incl. GG). DSG mix weights are FY24. The Sep-26 CPI was not yet out on 2026-10-07. PPI retail-margin indexes are noisy month to month.
