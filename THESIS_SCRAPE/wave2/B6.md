## Smoking-gun candidates
1. **B6-1 + B6-2 together (a negative smoking gun):** DKS has run AI scheduling (Legion) since ~2023, while personnel % rose. The vendor's own best-case economics are worth <=17bp. A PM who asks "isn't this just AI scheduling?" gets a clean answer: no. The tool is already in the base, so any FY27 saving is structural (fewer ASM seats, cheaper Captain layer, centrally budgeted hours: L2/L3/L5), not software. This protects the pitch from an easy rebuttal.
2. **B6-4:** central "Intera" hours budgets plus the ASM job description losing "payroll budget" show that hours control moved up to DM/HQ under Built to Win. It is the mechanism by which hours can be cut.
3. **B6-3** (supplier RFID mandate) is novel but an enabler, not a number.

## Evidence against / caveats
- Legion went live in 2023 and personnel % still rose 94bp in FY23 and stayed ~14% in FY24-FY25. Either DKS reinvested the hours (management said it added payroll coverage in 2024) or the tool did not save much. Both cut against "technology will deliver FY27 leverage".
- Vendor numbers are commissioned (Forrester TEI) or self-published case studies: treat them as an upper bound. Dollar General's $10M in 9 months is ~$76/employee/yr, which is tiny.
- RFID accuracy complaints (phantom inventory, display scans missing on-floor shoes) suggest the tech stack is not yet efficient, so de-layered stores may need hours added back (see the Captain posts: "seeming impossible to get all of this crap done", "barely finishing SFS on time").
- The 2023 rollout date rests on one employee post plus a 2024 vendor blog. The rollout may have been phased 2023-24.
