# J12: $/job calibration (HoS and large-box analogs)
## Methodology
- Goal: pin sales per job between $70K and $215K using stores where both headcount and sales are public. Searched (WebSearch + curl fetch, ~20 calls) for HoS hiring counts in press releases/local news/city filings, and Scheels/Bass Pro city-incentive documents. Sales taken from items already in the folder (CMBS panel B8, H04, H07 tax data, B5 QCEW) and paired with NEW headcount sources. Municipal sales-tax-by-vendor data does not exist publicly for DKS (taxpayer-confidential), so no vendor-level tax point was found.
- Ratio = sales $M / jobs, shown as a range (low sales / high jobs to high sales / low jobs). All implied $/job are INFERENCE. Data: data/J12_calibration.csv; script: scripts/J12_calib.py; fetch helper scripts/J12_fetch.py; raw in raw/J12/.
- Key framing (INFERENCE): there are two different ratios. (a) GROSS = whole-store sales / whole-store headcount (a productivity ratio). (b) MARGINAL = sales increment / net county job increment (what $70K and $215K were each trying to be). (b) is not a constant: for a relocation, increment = S_HoS - S_legacy and net jobs = J_HoS - J_legacy, so a HoS that is about as productive per head as the legacy store it replaces gives a low marginal ratio by construction.

## Pinning $/job (answer)
- Use $155-210K per head for gross HoS sales (not $70K, not $215K as a marginal rule). $215K is about right as a gross, per-head productivity for HoS (corporate $234K, WF $208K, Novi model $211K), and $70K is a marginal ratio unique to Johnson City where the increment was small.
- Applying $215K to NET QCEW jobs (+110) is only valid when legacy productivity is similar and net/gross offsets are ignored; the 34-event average implies ~$22M, but dispersion across stores is large (Johnson City $69K, Live Oak $90-126K, Victor $150-220K, Minnetonka $207K, Tampa $325K+).
