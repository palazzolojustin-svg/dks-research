## B. Passages that cut AGAINST or qualify the HoS-outperformance read

- **No HoS-specific sell-through or traffic data in any call.** Positive HoS statements are qualitative impressions (presentation, experience, exclusives). The only HoS-labelled number is the UGG-type 238% sell-in (item 16), off a small base. Items 8-9 and 19 attribute growth to "doors added" (store count), which is volume from new space, not productivity per door.
- **Item 8 (Planning Manager, 2026-07-14, line ~123):** "Right now, we have been seeing them performing in line with it. Again, the only thing that really has the adjustments is the increase in store footprint." The same expert's sell-in for May-July was only "about 3%-5% increase overall" (line ~104, 00:05:31), with "a downward shift when it comes to lifestyle product and you would call it obscure type things, training, tennis, soccer", and the Aug-Oct 10-15% is "half of that is due to increased number of doors" (item 9). Exceeds expectations only on "certain products" while "meet expectations on the core, regular, and everyday product" (line ~112).
- **Same expert, competition (line ~209):** "The biggest elephant in the room when it comes to competition is the renewed relationship that DICK'S Sporting Goods has with Nike... half of their sales, to 30% of DICK'S Sporting Goods sales in footwear, are all Nike" and "that's going to be a bit of a worry for other brands". Also: "the only time DICK'S Sporting Goods will lose market share... is with run specialties. These smaller doors highlight the knowledge of run." Also the expert says DKS is "reducing some of the Foot Locker" doors (item 8); the ratio of HoS to Foot Locker closures is not quantified.
- **Item 6 (Merchandising Director):** the "outperform" claim is only "slightly" and is tied to the raised guidance, not to store-level data. The same expert says (00:17:39) lifestyle "is a tougher space for them to crack into just because they've already set that consumer expectation that they're the House of Sport" (verbatim: "They've entered into the lifestyle space. While they've seen pretty tremendous growth for it, that's a tougher space for them to crack into just because they've already set that consumer expectation that they're the House of Sport. Now, how do they pivot?") - file `DKS_Expert-Call_2026-07-13_Merchandising-Director_Global-Athletic-Apparel-Brand.md`, line ~320. Here "House of Sport" is a brand-perception phrase, and the point is a limit on DKS in lifestyle.
- **Item 4 (Under Armour):** "SCHEELS is the only other retailer that has a similar experience, similar space" - a peer is named as comparable at the store-experience level.
- **Item 2 (premium apparel Director of Sales):** says DKS and other retailers are "not really apples to apples", and builds the HoS view from public results rather than brand data. Item 3 (same expert): "Q1 results were good... House of Sport is getting a lot of attention" - attention, not figures.
- **Item 13-15 (second interview):** DKS accounts for under 5% of the brand's revenue (AlphaSense summary), so this is a small account's anecdote. The expert also states the experience is "real consistent across the board with all their stores", i.e. HoS stores are only a bit better than other DKS doors.
- **2026-07-09 VP Wholesale Planning file, line ~404 (AlphaSense summary, not verbatim):** "Retail traffic is up, and the retailer is outperforming other accounts due to merchandising, especially in their House of Sport stores and shop-in-shop concepts." This summary actually belongs to the embedded casual/golf/swim apparel interview (items 13-15), so do not count it as a separate source. The same summary notes "slight overstock in swim inventory (~5%)".
- **Item 16 caveat:** the 238% sell-in is for a "newer business" brand, and sell-through is only "in line with sell-in" (not above).
