## One-page executive summary

**What DKS is.** The largest US sporting-goods retailer:
- FY2025 DICK'S Business sales were $14.11B at an 11.12% segment margin, with comps of +4.5% (FY25), +6.0% (Q1 FY26) and +4.9% (Q2 FY26).
- In Sept-2025 it bought Foot Locker (FL) for $2.51B. FL is a ~2,212-store global sneaker chain on a ~$7.4–7.5B FY26 sales guide that is now losing money: segment profit −$31.9M in Q2 FY26, with a FY26 guide of −$80M to −$40M.
- Stack-family Class B shares (23.57M at 10 votes each) control ~78% of the votes (derived).

Sources: 10-K FY2025, Q2 FY26 10-Q via `_primer_notes/S02…`, `S04…`, `reconciliation.md`.

**What happened.**
- On 2026-08-25 DKS cut FY26 non-GAAP EPS guidance from $13.50–14.50 to **$11.00–12.00**. Operating income came down $260M at the midpoint: FL −$190M, core −$70M. The tax rate rose to ~29%.
- The stock fell **−30.7%** on 38.85M shares, after a −25% fall in the preceding two months. ASO fell only −5.8% that day.
- Since then:
  - **7 insiders bought $5.59M** (VWAP $130.59), including the CFO's $999,983, but not the CEO or the Chairman.
  - DKS issued **$1B of 6.2%/6.9% 2036/2056 notes** (the "2026 debt"; no 2026 maturity exists), naming "repurchases of shares" among the uses of proceeds.
  - Short interest rose to 13.7% of float.

Sources: `_primer_notes/S05b…`, `S07a…`, `S13…`.

**What the Street believes.** "Core fine, FL broken, footwear cycle clears in 2027."
- 11 Buys, 5 Holds, 1 Underperform (BMO, $110); mean PT ~$158.
- Consensus: FY26 EPS $11.49 and FY27 **$13.36 (+16%)** on revenue of only +2.3%, i.e. a margin-recovery call.
- The stock trades at **11.9x FY26E / 10.3x FY27E** with a 3.6% dividend yield. It traded at 17–18x forward at the Jan-2025 and Jan-2026 peaks.

Sources: `_primer_notes/S12b…`, `S13…`.

## 1. How DKS makes money

### 1.1 The machine in one paragraph
DKS is the largest US sporting-goods retailer (~$14.1B FY2025 DICK'S Business sales; ~9% share of a ~$140B US "core" TAM by its own definition, `02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/slides_text/DKS_Investor-Presentation_2026-03_slide-text.md`) that bought a struggling global sneaker chain (Foot Locker, ~$7.4–7.5B FY2026E sales guide) in September 2025. The core business sells national brands (Nike ~25% of core purchases; total company 31% in FY2025 including ~5 months of FL — `01_DKS_SEC_FILINGS/10-K/DKS_10-K_FY2025_filed-2026-03-27.md`) plus ~13% owned "vertical brands" (DSG, CALIA, VRST, Maxfli…) through ~890 large-format stores that also fulfil most online orders. Profit comes from (i) merchandise margin earned on full-price selling of allocated/"high-heat" national-brand product and higher-margin vertical brands, (ii) leverage of a largely fixed occupancy and labor base, and (iii) small but very high-margin "alternative profit pools" (GameChanger SaaS, DICK'S Media Network). The Foot Locker segment is a mall/urban sneaker specialist with ~2,212 owned stores in 20+ countries that earns low-20s/high-20s gross margin and currently loses money.

**Claimed unit economics (management, Nov-2025 investor deck — `02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/slides_text/DKS_Q3-FY2025_Investor-Presentation-Dec-2025_slide-text.md`; repeated in `00_DKS_SYNTHESIS/DKS_Earnings_Calls_Results_Guidance_and_Key_Quotes.md` §5.8):**

Caveats in the deck's own footnote: sales are **gross of cannibalization** and returns blend remodel, lease and purchased deal structures (`_primer_notes/S07b_Letters_AnnualReports_Slides.md` (f)). Wells Fargo's independent build (`03_DKS_STREET_RESEARCH/2026-08-17_WellsFargo_DKS_Sporting_Goods_Retail_Primer_The_X-s_and_O-s_of_the_Ind.md`, Exh. 28) gets HoS 4-wall ROIC 27.2% / payback 3.7 years at 47.5% merch margin and 124% of legacy-store productivity, with ROIC falling to 9.9% if sales are $25M instead of $35M. The full returns slide was **not repeated after Nov-2025**, and the **"75 to 100 House of Sport by end-2027" target disappeared** from the FY2025 10-K and FY2026 10-Qs; announced openings reach only ~67 by end-2027 (~35 at FYE25 + ~14 in FY2026 + ~18 under construction for 2027; derived from `_primer_notes/S02_DKS_10K_FY2024_FY2025.md` (f) and `_primer_notes/S04…` (d)18). Stack called 67–68 vs 75 "a pretty meaningless difference" (JPM Forum 2026-04-08, `_primer_notes/S09_Conferences_DealCall_AGM.md`).

**Important mechanic: most new formats are relocations/conversions that stay in the comp base.** In FY2024 only 1 of 7 HoS and 4 of 15 Field House "openings" were net-new (`_primer_notes/S05a_DKS_8K_2022-11_to_2025-03.md` (f)); ~75% of FY2026 openings are relocations/remodels (FY2025 10-K). Because a relocated store's sales count in comps from the original open date (FY2022 definition change), a box that does +100% more volume after becoming a HoS adds directly to the comp. Wells estimates relocations add **+200–275bp** a year to the DICK'S comp and GameChanger +20–40bp, leaving "underlying" core comps of only **+1.0–1.5%** FY2026–28E (2.8% FY24, 1.9% FY25 by its disaggregation; Wells primer Exh. 17/39). UBS puts HoS at ~150bp (~30%) of the comp (`_primer_notes/S12a_Street_2026-05_to_2026-06.md`). This is one of the central debates (Section 9).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Pro forma with a full year of FL the company is ~49% footwear and ~76% softlines (Wells primer Exh. 83). Footwear is "the engine that pulls the train" (Hobart, Q2 FY25 call 2025-08-28). DKS does not disclose margin by category, but the filings and calls establish: vertical brands carry **"700 to 900 basis points of higher margin compared to the national brands"** (raised from "600 to 800" in Nov-2024; Gupta, Q1 FY25 call 2025-05-28); trading cards/licensed carry lower margin ("slightly lower margin", Gupta, Q3 FY25 call 2025-11-25); hardgoods (golf, fitness) are lower-margin than apparel; GameChanger/DMN carry ~80% gross margin (UBS estimate, `_primer_notes/S11_Street_2025-08_to_2026-03.md`). The 2016–18 exit from hunting (~$1B of sales at ~1,700bp lower margin; Stack, Goldman 2025-09-04) is the template for mix-led margin gains.

### 1.4 Vendor economics: Nike, allocation, vertical brands, allowances
- **Nike concentration** (10-Ks): DKS ~23% (FY2022), ~24% (FY2023), ~25% (FY2024), **~31% of consolidated purchases (FY2025, incl. ~5 months of FL)**. FL alone: 75% (2020) → 68% (2021) → 65% (2022) → ~65% (2023) → ~59% (2024) (FL 10-Ks; `_primer_notes/S14a…`, `S14b…`). Wells estimates ~37% of pro forma merchandise sales (Wells primer Exh. 85). No long-term contracts; all purchases on short-term POs (all 10-Ks).
- **Allocation is the economic moat.** DKS's 2017 vendor segmentation (strategic / transactional / other) and its return to the top of Nike's wholesale list (Nike contributed >6pts of DSG growth in 2022; Wells primer Exh. 20) explain why DKS out-comped Academy (ASO) by double digits: "brand access" is the primary reason ASO under-comps DKS (Wells primer, expert-call section). Premium footwear decks are in ~90% of stores (Gupta, Q3 FY24 call).
- **Vertical brands** ~13% of DICK'S Business sales (~$1.8B FY2025; FY2025 10-K risk factor line 679) — DKS's #2 "vendor"; the 2024 goal was $2B (Hobart, MS conference 2024-12-03). Vertical brand share actually slipped from ~14% to ~13% in FY2023 (`_primer_notes/S01_DKS_10K_FY2022_FY2023.md` (f)).
- **Allowances and terms.** Vendor allowances (markdown support, co-op advertising) are part of merch margin; the FY2025 10-K added "markdown allowances" to vendor incentives "not guaranteed" (`_primer_notes/S02…` (f)12). Expert calls (June–July 2026) describe stable trade terms, DKS requiring a specific inbound margin, and **DKS self-funding markdowns on MAP brands with no margin agreements (UGG)** (`_primer_notes/S10_Expert_Calls.md`).

### 1.5 Omnichannel, ScoreCard, GameChanger, DICK'S Media Network
- **Omni:** ~70% of FY2025 sales from omni athletes; 90%+ of sales store-enabled; 80%+ of online orders fulfilled from stores (Mar-2026 deck). DKS stopped disclosing e-commerce % after 2021 (no 10-K/10-Q since; `00_DKS_SYNTHESIS/DKS_Financial_Statements_from_10K_10Q.md` §7c); Wells estimates ~15–16%. E-commerce described as profit-neutral after in-housing in 2017.
- **ScoreCard loyalty:** ~30M members, >75% of DICK'S Business sales; ~8M Gold members >50% of sales (FY2025 10-K). **ScoreCard+** paid tier at $99/yr launched July 1, 2026 (Q2 FY26 call).
- **GameChanger** (youth-sports scoring/streaming SaaS): "nearly $150 million in revenue during fiscal 2025" and ~10M unique active users (FY2025 10-K); ~40% CAGR (Hobart, Q4 FY25 call). Included in comps since FY2024.
- **DICK'S Media Network (DMN):** retail media launched 4Q FY2024; ~20B impressions FY2025 (Mar-2026 deck); "has now reached profitability" (UBS 2026-08-26). Not quantified; Wells estimates <1% of sales. On the Q2 FY26 call management attributed part of the +79bp DICK'S GM gain to DMN and GameChanger; JPM's split: IEEPA refunds ~50bp, DMN ~25bp, GameChanger ~35bp (`_primer_notes/S12b…` (e)10), implying core merchandise margin ex these items was roughly −30bp (derived).

### 1.6 Cost structure — a CFO's view
FY2025 segment P&L (FY2025 10-K Note 17; $000; ratios derived):

| | DICK'S Business | % sales | Foot Locker (Sep 8–Jan 31) | % sales |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 14,108,943 | 100% | 3,106,177 | 100% |
| Cost of merchandise & services (incl. supply chain for FL) | 7,100,929 | 50.3% | 1,873,614 | 60.3% |
| Occupancy | 1,197,019 | 8.5% | 414,557 | 13.3% |
| Personnel | 1,972,850 | 14.0% | 497,616 | 16.0% |
| Other segment expenses | 2,269,702 | 16.1% | 372,610 | 12.0% |
| **Segment profit (loss)** | **1,568,443** | **11.1%** | **(52,220)** | **(1.7)%** |

Read-across: (i) DKS's reported gross margin includes occupancy and distribution in COGS, so GM moves with sales leverage on rent; (ii) FL's occupancy is ~13% of sales vs ~8.5% at DICK'S — a mall-rent-heavy model where comp declines deleverage quickly; (iii) FL's merchandise cost ratio is ~10pts worse — the "allocation + markdown" gap Wells attributes mostly to Nike.

**Leases** (FY2025 10-K Note 8): operating lease liabilities $5.84B (from $3.00B pre-FL); total lease cost $1.165B FY2025; weighted remaining term 7.71 years; leases signed not commenced $756M. "Approximately three-quarters of our DICK'S Sporting Goods stores will be up for lease renewal at our option over the next five years." FL leases have 5–10-year initial terms (vs 10–15 for DICK'S), which makes FL closures cheaper and faster than they look.

**Capex** rose from $364M (FY2022) → $587M → $803M → $1,137M gross (FY2025) → **~$1.6B gross / ~$1.4B net guided for FY2026** (~$1.0B DICK'S, ~$0.4B FL) (press releases; Q2 FY26 call). D&A $488.6M FY2025 vs capex $1,137M — capex is running ~2.3x D&A (derived), so HoS depreciation will be a margin headwind for years (UBS: "incremental D&A from HoS offsets GM gains", 2026-03-13).

**Working capital:** inventory turns ~2.8x; DICK'S inventory +6% y/y at Q2 FY2026 "in line with our total sales growth" (Gupta, 2026-08-25); FL inventory $2.0B at Aug-1-2026 (up from $1.7B at Q1). Supply-chain finance liabilities small ($25.8M). Landlord construction allowances doubled to $161.7M in FY2025 (`_primer_notes/S02…` (f)17).

**Free cash flow** (CFO − gross capex; derived from 10-K/10-Q): FY2022 $557.8M; FY2023 $939.9M; FY2024 $509.3M; FY2025 $400.2M; **1H FY2026 $48.8M; LTM to Aug-1-2026 $239.4M** (424B2 2026-09-23 table, `_primer_notes/S07a_DKS_Debt_Documents.md`). Dividends alone run ~$450M/yr ($1.25/qtr × ~89M shares).

### 1.7 What actually drives gross margin and EBT margin quarter to quarter
Management's own bridge: DICK'S non-GAAP operating margin rose from 5.22% (FY2019) to 11.12% (FY2025): +699bp gross margin (+328bp merchandise margin, +343bp fixed-cost/occupancy leverage, +28bp supply chain) less −109bp SG&A and pre-opening (Mar-2026 slides; `00_DKS_SYNTHESIS/DKS_Earnings_Calls_Results_Guidance_and_Key_Quotes.md` §5.16). Wells' version: GM ~700bp above 2019 (~360bp merch margin, ~310bp occupancy, ~30bp other), OM ~600bp higher. Sales per sq ft rose from ~$208 to ~$317 (Wells Exh. 21).

The quarter-to-quarter swing factors, in order of importance:
1. **Merchandise margin** — promotional intensity, clearance, full-price selling of allocated product, vertical-brand mix, freight, shrink, and (in FY2025–26) tariffs and IEEPA refunds. Merch-margin expansion decelerated through FY2025 (+84bp Q3 FY24 → +18bp Q2 FY25 → +5bp Q3 FY25) and turned negative in Q1 FY2026 (~−29bp derived); Q2 FY26's +137bp was "due primarily to IEEPA tariff refunds attributable to tariff costs incurred in the current year" (Q2 FY26 10-Q; `_primer_notes/S04…` C3).
2. **Occupancy leverage** — mostly a function of comp; also new-DC (Fort Worth, April 2026) and HoS rent.
3. **SG&A** — store labor, marketing (World Cup spend in Q2 FY26), technology, incentive comp, healthcare. SG&A leveraged in only **one of seven** DICK'S quarters from Q3 FY24 to Q2 FY26 (`_primer_notes/S08_Earnings_Calls.md` (f)). Management's stated leverage point: "low single digits comp" (Gupta, 2025-08-28).
4. **Pre-opening** — lumpy with HoS openings (~$90M FY2026 guide; peaks in Q3).
5. **Below the line** — interest income on cash (falling as cash falls), deferred-comp marks (neutral to EBT), and the **tax rate** (25.5% → 27% → 29% FY2026 guide as FL Europe losses get no tax benefit).

**The trend that matters:** DICK'S segment margin fell year on year in three of the four post-close quarters: −55bp Q3 FY25 (8.92% vs 9.47%), +88bp Q4 FY25 (10.97% vs 10.09%, the one quarter SG&A levered), −66bp Q1 FY26 (10.69% vs 11.35%) and −42bp Q2 FY26 (12.60% vs 13.02%). Q2 FY26 was roughly −96bp ex the ~54bp current-year IEEPA refund (derived; `_primer_notes/S04…` (d)12; segment figures from `_primer_notes/reconciliation.md` A4). These declines came despite comps of +5.7%, +6.0% and +4.9%. The FY2026 DICK'S segment margin guide (10.6–10.9%) is 20–50bp below FY2025's 11.12%.

## 2. Industry and value chain

### 2.1 Market size, structure and share
- **Size:** "$150–250B US market (depending on definition)" growing "+LSD" (Wells primer, 2026-08-10). DKS uses ~$140B US core TAM (footwear, apparel, hardlines) and ~$300B global; ASO uses $245B including outdoor/hunting/work. DKS claims ~9% US share (2025), up ~50bp in 2025 (Mar-2026 deck).
- **Euromonitor share of US sporting goods & athletic apparel/footwear retail (B&M basis)** (Wells primer Exh. 82):

- **Amazon:** ~13% of traditional sporting goods sales, flat for most of a decade; category e-commerce penetration ~40% (E-Marketer via Wells Exh. 89). Barriers: bulky items, try-on, premium brands limiting distribution.
- **Consolidation:** 27 events since 2003 (Sports Authority liquidation 2016; Bass Pro/Cabela's ~$5B; JD/Finish Line ~$558M; JD/Hibbett ~$1.1B at ~6x EBITDA; DKS/FL $2.5B EV at ~0.3x sales and ~6–7x depressed EBITDA — "the defining event of the cycle") (Wells Exh. 80).

### 2.2 Demand drivers
Participation is at record highs: 23% of Americans do sports/exercise daily (ATUS, all-time high); youth (6–17) participation 58% in 2024 vs 49% in 2021; average spend on a child's primary sport $1,016 (+46% vs 2019); record 181M outdoor participants; record 77M gym members (Wells primer pp.42–50). The category is **price-elastic (−1.35, same as children's clothing)** and historically deflationary (prices fell in 83% of months since 2000); 2026 YTD prices +3.5% on tariff pass-through with units +6.0% (Wells Exh. 94–98). Census sporting-goods retail sales shrank 1–4% a year in 2022–25 then grew +7% YTD 2026 (Wells Exh. 99).

### 2.3 Athletic footwear economics and the Nike cycle
- **Brand strategy swing.** Nike's 2017–2023 "Consumer Direct Acceleration" cut wholesale doors and shifted high-heat product to Nike Direct; Foot Locker's "high heat" allocation fell from ~40% to ~20% (Wells channel checks), cutting FL's Nike high-heat sales from ~$1.6B (FY21) to ~$0.7B (FY23) (Wells Exh. 8). Under CEO Elliott Hill (from Oct-2024) Nike pivoted back to wholesale: NA wholesale revenue +11%, +24%, +11%, +9.8% (derived), +9% in Nike Q1 FY26–Q1 FY27; FY26 NA wholesale $11,958M (+14%) vs NA Direct $8,553M (−6%) (Nike 10-K/10-Qs, `_primer_notes/S17_NKE_SEC_Filings.md` Table 1). Nike's top-3 US customers rose to ~29% of US sales (from ~25%) (FY26 10-K).
- **Sell-in vs sell-through.** Needham calls Nike's Nov-2025 quarter "the largest period of domestic wholesale sell-in" in its history (~$1.5B more wholesale product ≈ ~$3B at retail) (`_primer_notes/S16a…`, `S19b…`). By summer 2026 lifestyle franchises (AF1, Dunk, Jordan 1, Samba) were clogging the channel; >30% of Nike's assortment was on discount across 14 wholesale partners (25% a year earlier) (`_primer_notes/S16b…`). Nike guided FY27 revenue down high-single digits, ~400bp of extra 2Q pressure from lapping "higher North America sell-in", and Sportswear/Jordan/China resets extending "beyond fiscal 2027"; Bloomberg consensus has NA wholesale −10.4% c-n in Sep–Nov 2026 (≈ DKS Q3) and −4.8% in Dec–Feb (`_primer_notes/S16b…`, `S17…`).
- **Performance vs lifestyle.** Running (Nike Vomero/Pegasus, HOKA, On, ASICS, New Balance), "brown shoe" (UGG, Birkenstock) and women's basketball are strong; legacy lifestyle silhouettes are weak (expert calls, `_primer_notes/S10…`; Stack, Q2 FY26 call). DKS is relatively advantaged because its footwear skews performance and it is less dependent on launches than FL ("Foot Locker is more dependent on launch and retro product", Stack 2026-08-25).

### 2.4 Mall vs off-mall; competitors; who is gaining
- **DICK'S:** ~30% of stores in malls (Hobart, Q1 FY25 call); HoS has opened A-mall access (Palm Beach Gardens, Cerritos, Tysons). **Foot Locker:** ~74% mall-based in NA at FY2022 (`_primer_notes/S14a…`); off-mall share of NA gross sq ft 34% → 39% → 42% (FY22–24); ~35% of FL mall stores in C/D malls (Wells); >75% of Champs within a mile of a Foot Locker.
- **Academy (ASO):** comps negative every year since FY2022; Q1 FY26 +2.9% (all ticket; transactions −1.5%), **Q2 FY26 −0.4% (transactions −5.3%)** in the same weeks DKS comped +4.9% (`_primer_notes/S20a_ASO_Academy.md`). ASO's footwear sales fell 1.0% in Q2; ex a one-time IEEPA refund its GM fell ~70bp, partly from re-investing refund money in price. ASO added 55 Jordan shop concepts in calendar Q2 2026 (UBS via `_primer_notes/S16a…`) and HOKA entered 15 Academy doors (`_primer_notes/S18b…`) — brand access is slowly broadening down-market.
- **JD Sports (Finish Line/Hibbett):** NA like-for-likes ~−7% in mid-2026; JPM models −6%/−5% for its Q3/Q4 (`_primer_notes/S20b…`, `S19b…`). Weak specialty-sneaker peer = negative read for FL, neutral-to-positive for DICK'S share.
- **Brand DTC:** On wants ~75% of key-partner doors (DKS, FL, JD named) by 2029 (Investor Day 2026-09-22) while DTC grows faster; DECK says sporting goods/athletic specialty is HOKA's main US whitespace ("several key wholesale partners remain underpenetrated, particularly in athletic specialty") (`_primer_notes/S18b…`, `S19b…`). Nike reinvesting in wholesale but UBS reports investor worry that Nike is moving into lower-end doors (Famous Footwear Jordan styles +63%; possible Decathlon) — a dilution of DKS/FL's preferred-partner status.
- **Others:** Walmart/Target/Amazon and off-price at the value end; SCHEELS as the only comparable experiential competitor; run specialty for pinnacle running product (expert calls).

**Who is gaining:** DKS core (comp +4.9% vs ASO −0.4% and JD NA ~−7% in mid-2026); brand DTC (On DTC +34%, HOKA DTC +17%); adidas (World Cup, shelf space from Nike per DB channel checks). **Who is losing:** Foot Locker, Academy (traffic), JD NA, department stores, Nike lifestyle.

## 5. The Foot Locker acquisition in full

### 5.1 Why DKS bought it (management's stated logic)
Stated logic, from the 2025-05-15 deal call and the 424B3 (`_primer_notes/S06a…`, `S06b…`, `S09…`):
- (i) Enter the global athletic-specialty / sneaker-culture market (~$300B global TAM), reaching an urban/mall, younger, more diverse customer DKS under-serves.
- (ii) International expansion, since DKS was US-only. Stack cited DKS's "interest in global expansion" as early as January 2024.
- (iii) Bring "Retail 101" operational expertise and brand relationships to FL.
- (iv) $100–125M medium-term cost synergies from procurement and direct sourcing.
- (v) Run FL as a standalone business unit, "maintain the Foot Locker brand", and keep the two banners separate.

Stack: "decisions … for a lifetime" (Q1 FY25 call, 2025-05-28).

**The unstated logic, visible in the filings.** Nike's return to wholesale makes FL, still its largest sneaker-specialty partner, more valuable to Nike. ### 5.7 Exit and option economics (derived; my framework)

What is FL worth in pieces? There are no disclosed segment assets by banner, so all of this is indicative:
- **FL North America (Foot Locker, Kids, Champs, WSS):** ~67–70% of FL sales; international was ~30–33% of FL FY2024 revenue (Wells primer; BI 07/23/26, in `_primer_notes/S12b…`). Owns the Nike and Jordan relationship and the "Fast Break" upside. This is the core of any keep case.
- **FL EMEA (most of the ~30% international; APAC/atmos the rest):** EMEA comps were −10.2% / −11.4% in FL Q1/Q2 FY2025 (`_primer_notes/S15b…`); loss-making, with a valuation-allowed tax position. A licensing exit (the "licensed stores in Europe, Middle East, Asia" model already exists, per the Q3 FY25 10-Q) would remove losses and ~4pts of tax-rate drag. Tax drag ≈ 4pts × ~$1.45B pre-tax ≈ $58M ≈ $0.65/share (derived). Cash exit costs are unknown; European store-closure and labor costs are typically high.
- **WSS (Hispanic-family value banner, ~99 stores left) and atmos (Japan streetwear):** small. They are candidates for sale or closure under the "menu".
- **Capex lever:** FL capex of ~$0.4B (FY26) is discretionary. Cutting it to maintenance (~$0.15–0.2B) frees ~$0.2B a year (derived).

**Bottom line.** DKS paid ~$2.5B and has booked $516M of charges. At today's price the market assigns FL roughly −$1B (derived). For that to be right, FL must keep burning cash indefinitely or impair the core. FL's 2018–21 history, the Nike wholesale pivot, and short FL lease terms (5–10 years) argue that a credible "rightsize and simplify" plan makes −$1B too pessimistic. Execution and timing are the risk (Section 9, Debate 3).

### 6.6 FY2026 and FY2027 set-up: what has to be true for consensus

### Debate 1: Foot Locker endgame. Fix it, shrink it, or exit it? (Largest impact: ±$15–25/share)

**What the market implies.** At $137.34, a 12x multiple on core-only earnings (~$12.6/share, derived) implies FL is worth about −$1.1B. At 7x core EBITDA it is ≈ −$1.2B; at 6.5x, ≈ −$0.1B (all derived).

**Bull case** (Wells, UBS scenarios 3–4, management's "menu"):
- FL's collapse was mostly Nike allocation, which cost ~490bp of the 880bp margin decline (Wells). Nike is now pivoting back to wholesale (NA wholesale +14% in FY26) and needs FL.
- Fast Break stores comp "+DD with improving merch margin" (Wells). FL margins can rebuild to 3–4% by FY28 and 7–8% in Wells' bull case.
- The losers are severable:
  - EMEA: 559 stores, comps −10% in 2025, plus ~4pts of consolidated tax drag.
  - WSS: already cut from 151 to 99 stores.
  - Champs: 364 stores, more than 75% within a mile of a Foot Locker.
  - FL capex of ~$0.4B.
- FL leases are short (5–10 years). Stack answered "simply yes" to rightsizing or monetization (2026-08-25) and spoke of "creating a menu … no preconceived ideas" (2026-09-14).

**Bear case** (BMO $110, Barclays "TRAP", Wells' "is proving to be an albatross"):
- FL is a structurally declining mall sneaker chain whose biggest supplier is building DTC and broadening distribution. UBS notes Famous Footwear Jordan styles +63% and a possible Decathlon move.
- Exiting Europe is slow and expensive.
- FL soaks up management time, capital (~$0.4B of FY26 capex), inventory ($2.0B, up from $1.7B in Q1) and the tax rate.
- Losses are getting worse, not better: FL segment Q2 FY26 was −$31.9M.

**Evidence today:**
- FL pro forma comps −4.7 / −3.4 / +0.6 / −3.6% across the four post-close quarters.

### 10.4 Variant insights

**Variant 1 (bearish vs the Street's framing): "The core is not the safe half."**
- The Street attributes the core margin cut to promotions and models FY27 DICK'S margin recovering to ~10.8%.
- I think the core is structurally de-levering at ~10.5% or below, for four reasons:
  - (a) Roughly half the reported comp is bought with relocation capex. The underlying comp is +1.0–1.5% (Wells), and ticket dominates.
  - (b) SG&A levered in only 1 of 7 quarters, even at 4–6% comps.
  - (c) D&A will keep growing faster than sales: ~$1.0B of core net capex vs ~$490M of total D&A.
  - (d) FY26 was flattered by tariff refunds, the World Cup and 1H settlement income (the last excluded from non-GAAP, but a cash cushion).
- Consequence: FY27 core OI is ~$1.60B, not $1.65B, and FY27 EPS is ~$12.4–12.7 before buybacks, not $13.36.

*Work to make it bulletproof:*
- (i) Build a store-level comp decomposition. List every HoS and FH relocation from the 8-Ks and the store list. Use Placer visits by location (old box vs new) to estimate the relocation lift per store. Back out the like-for-like box comp.
- (ii) Rebuild the SG&A bridge by line from 10-K Note 17 (personnel 14.0%, other 16.1%, occupancy 8.5%) and the per-store opex of new formats.
- (iii) Build a D&A schedule from capex vintages and useful lives.
- (iv) Run 3–4 expert calls with ex-DKS FP&A or store-operations staff on HoS year-2+ productivity and labor models.
- (v) Size the IEEPA, World Cup and Knicks lap with management IR.

*What would disconfirm it:* DICK'S SG&A leverage in Q3 or Q4 at a ≤2% comp; a FY27 guide with core margin expansion ≥30bp ex-refunds; cohort data showing HoS years 2–4 comping +5% or more.

**Variant 2 (bullish vs the market): "FL is a call option priced as a liability."**
