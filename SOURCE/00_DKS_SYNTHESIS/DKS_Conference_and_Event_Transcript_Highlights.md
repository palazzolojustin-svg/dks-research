## 2. Foot Locker Acquisition Announcement Call — 2025-05-15

### 2a. Deal terms (Navdeep Gupta, prepared remarks)

- Price: "DICK'S will acquire Foot Locker for $24 per share."
- Consideration election: "Foot Locker shareholders will have the option to receive either $24 in cash or 0.1168 shares of DICK'S common stock. The election is not subject to a minimum or maximum amount of cash or stock consideration."
- Valuation: "This transaction implies an equity value of approximately $2.4 billion and an enterprise value of $2.5 billion."
- Financing (Q&A, to Adrienne Yih): "as we guided today, our intention is to use a combination of cash, long term debt, as well as equity as we look to finance this transaction." Balance sheet: "We finished last year with $1.7 billion of cash on the balance sheet." Credit: "even if these were to be fully financed using the debt, it won't impact unfavorably our credit ratings today and our intention is to continue to maintain a strong investment grade credit rating on a go forward basis as well."
- Foot Locker bonds: "we are working with our advisors to see if there is an opportunity to exchange the obligations and if need be we'll be able to take those notes out as well."
- Approvals / timing: "the deal has been unanimously approved by both DICK'S and Foot Locker's board of directors, and while we believe it is expected to close in the second half of 2025, it is subject to customary closing conditions, including the Regulatory and Foot Locker shareholder approval."
- Regulatory (Hobart, to Mike Baker): "we are not expecting any regulatory concerns. Obviously, we're going to go through the process and see where we net out, but we're not expecting that to be too cumbersome."

### 2b. Synergy and accretion targets (Gupta)

- "We expect to deliver between $100 million to $125 million in cost synergies in the medium-term, primarily from procurement and direct sourcing efficiencies. We believe these synergies are well-supported and achievable."
- "We also expect the deal to be accretive to EPS in the first full fiscal year post-close excluding one-time transaction costs and costs to achieve these synergies."
- Synergy composition (to Paul Lejuez): "we feel there is opportunity both on the gross margin line and getting the scale of the two companies, bringing them together, as well as there are opportunities on the SG&A line when you think about the procurement opportunities that we see across the company."
- Procurement scope (to Mike Baker): "we are talking about both because you have opportunity both on the procurement vendor, the direct sourcing vendors as well as the non-merge vendors."
- Hobart on what is and is not in the synergy number: "because of that, the opportunities that Navdeep is talking about are really things like procurement and purchasing and things like that, those are the synergies that we are counting on at this point."
- Comp / EPS assumptions withheld (Stack, to Michael Lasser): "We have a set of assumptions that we're not ready to make public. But as we go through this transition between now and close, when we close, we'll provide what we think this – what the comp is going to be, what we think the EPS is going to be, a better view of what the accretion is going to be."

### 2c. Preliminary Q1 2025 (released same morning; Gupta)

- "Our first quarter comps grew 4.5% and we delivered a non-GAAP EPS of $3.37 ahead of last year." Full Q1 results scheduled for May 28, 2025.

### 2d. Foot Locker footprint and profile as described (Ed Stack, prepared remarks)

- "With Foot Locker, we have the opportunity to expand our business internationally, which bring our total store count to more than 3,200 in key regions, and open the door to an approximately $300 billion global sports retail market."
- US TAM: "in the US alone, the total addressable market across footwear, apparel and hardlines is approximately $140 billion."
- Banners: "Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS and atmos."
- "With approximately 2,400 stores across 26 countries at the end of 2024, including key markets in North America, Europe, Asia, Australia and New Zealand, and a licensed store presence in Europe, the Middle East and Asia."
- "completing over 400 store refreshes last year, and growing digital sales to 18% of total revenue."
- "In 2024, Foot Locker generated $8 billion in revenue."
- Footwear share of combined sales (Gupta, to Adrienne Yih): "the footwear business as a combined company would be close to about 50% of the total sales of the company."

### 2e. Strategic rationale as stated

Five benefits (Hobart, slide 6): "First, the combination is focused on creating a global platform within the growing sports retail industry. Second, it'll allow us to serve a broader set of consumers across differentiated concepts. Third, it will allow us to strengthen relationships with brand partners through global reach. Fourth, we will invest in future growth through an industry leading omni-channel experience. And fifth, we will unlock operational efficiencies to create shareholder value. Overall. This is a growth oriented transaction grounded in operational discipline."

Other rationale statements:
- Stack: "The convergence of sport and culture has never been stronger and we're seeing tremendous momentum and opportunity across our industry."
- Stack on precedent: "we have successfully acquired and integrated major brands before like Galyan's, which was similarly sized acquisition relative to the scale of our business when we acquired them."
- Stack on consumer / real estate: "Foot Locker has a consumer – it serves a consumer in neighborhood that we in many cases are never going to get. They're in places that we're not going to be able to find 50,000 square feet or 60,000 square feet or [ph] address (00:27:49) that we could – that would give us the return."
- Hobart on complementary real estate: "Here in the US, Foot Locker has a real estate portfolio that is highly complementary to ours, with stores in different markets that serve different needs and demographics from DICK'S."
- Hobart on consumer types: "Foot Locker enables us to reach more sneakerheads and lifestyle focused shoppers, a great addition to the performance focused consumers who are key customers for us."
- Hobart on TAM: "the TAM that we're operating in is growing from $140 billion to $300 billion. That's because of the global reach."
- Stack on footwear: "we've always talked about footwear as the engine that pulls this train."
- Stack on sports calendar: "with the World Cup coming in 2026, Olympics in 2028, the World Cup is going to be the biggest sporting event this country has ever seen."

### 2f. Nike relationship commentary (Ed Stack)

- Timing: "Foot Locker was really, a big part of Foot Locker under pressure was what the relationship they had with Nike. Nike moved into DTC and away from wholesale partners a while back, hurt Foot Locker pretty significantly, Nike's changed their position really leaning back into wholesale. I think Elliott and his team are doing a great job and we're pretty excited about what's going on with Nike."
- "Foot Locker is going to be a beneficiary of that move back to from a wholesale standpoint. And so, we think the timing of this is perfect."
- "We've talked with Nike, Foot Locker is a very important part of Nike's long term strategy and we're happy to have Foot Locker and us together, take advantage of all those things that are going to come down the pipe with Nike and some of the other key brands that we do business with."
- On Nike mix at FL (to Robby Ohmes): "I think what's going to happen is that the Nike business will probably go up. We expect the Nike business will go up at a higher margin rate because they will be able to sell more product at full price, as they weren't getting as much product that could be sold at full price as they did in the past. And that's a big precept of this."
- Brand feedback: "From talking with other – some of the brands on this and we have talked to a couple of them, they're pretty enthusiastic about this."
- Distribution risk (to Simeon Gutman): "everything that we've talked to them about, everything that we've seen is that they're not looking to change their distribution strategy and broaden it significantly where access to some of this high heat product would go to other places that it doesn't have today... The brands have indicated they're going to continue to lean into specialty and make sure that their product is positioned in a premium way."

### 2g. Integration plan and governance

- Hobart: "It's really important to know that we plan to run these two businesses separately... Similarly, the Foot Locker team will be focused exclusively on running the Foot Locker business, and we will have a team here, a small team here led by Ed that will help with that integration and just sharing some of that knowledge and expertise and executional excellence that Ed mentioned."
- Banner question (Hobart): "For now, because we are leading the two companies, we're going to keep them separate. The consumer may or may not know that DICK'S and Foot Locker are one."
- Store closures (Hobart, to Robby Ohmes): "They've indicated there are some that are potentially being closed, but that's something that we're going to get involved with in the next several months and have more of an opinion. It's not our expectation that we'll be closing a significant number of Foot Locker stores. We are here to reinvigorate the Foot Locker brand."
- DICK'S growth unchanged (Hobart): "it's really critical to know that nothing is changing, with our growth rate, our expectations for House of Sport, for our Field House concepts."
- Board precept (Stack): "the board was very adamant about with us, with their direction, is that when we do this, it cannot impact what we're doing from DICK'S, it can impact what we're doing from a House of Sport, Field House, what we're doing from GameChanger, investing from a technology standpoint, and the flywheel that we've got going with DICK's and strong DICK's is."
- International (Hobart, to Chris Horvers): "DICK'S is staying on its current growth trajectory and current strategies, which don't include, at this time, international expansion." Stack: "So we have no plans to take DICK'S internationally."
- Apparel opportunity at FL (Stack, to Michael Lasser): "They've talked that their apparel business is deteriorating. They haven't invested in that in bringing apparel back for this consumer. This kid dresses from toe to head."

1. **Brian Nagel (Oppenheimer)** — Why now; brand engagement. Stack: "This is something we've looked at for and have thought about it for a long time." 3. **Adrienne Yih (Barclays)** — Pre-deal Nike conversations; TAM share assumptions; combined footwear share; leverage/bonds. Stack: "yes, we talked with several brands about the opportunity for us to do this... They gave us a surrogate impression of what was going on with Foot Locker." Also: "part of our job is to be able to see around the corners and see what's coming next." Gupta: footwear "close to about 50% of the total sales." Leverage: $1.7B cash; mix of cash, LT debt, equity; investment grade maintained.

4. **Simeon Gutman (Morgan Stanley)** — Why buy a shrinking comp business; five-year distribution protection. Stack: "Foot Locker has been shrinking, but still – I hope everybody can remember what happened with Nike's change in their distribution strategy... Foot Locker bore the brunt of that change." Also: "almost everybody knows we're pretty conservative. We don't get over skis." On brand distribution: "We think this makes us much bigger, much more important partners to those brands." Hobart: "this obviously widens the addressable market for us significantly."

5. **Robby Ohmes (BofA)** — Nike % of FL sales post-merger; HoS growth; store rightsizing. Stack: Nike business "will probably go up... at a higher margin rate." Hobart: no change to DKS growth; "It's not our expectation that we'll be closing a significant number of Foot Locker stores."

6. **Kate McShane (Goldman)** — Tariff risk vs. timing. Stack: "tariffs are something we think about all the time... There was some good news that came out of Geneva last week, that is positive. But we've been able to manage through tariffs in the past. We'll manage through these tariffs again."

7. **Michael Lasser (UBS)** — Why different from failed horizontal retail mergers; FL comp assumption. Stack: "We think the big difference here is that footwear is part of our core competence. We know this business extremely well... we've got visibility for Foot Locker performing meaningfully better." "We don't have a lot of big egos here in trying to do something just to do it. We're very conservative. And if we didn't see this clear line of sight to this or we thought that this was going to impact what we're able to do with DICK's, we wouldn't be doing it." Comp assumption: not disclosed until close.

8. **Chris Horvers (JPMorgan)** — International opportunity for DKS; customer crossover. Hobart: no international for DKS now; "who knows about the future." Crossover: "our market share in this $140 billion market that we've been competing in thus far is still around 80% [sic — transcript; elsewhere management cites ~8.5%–9%]. So there's an enormous market share... with our 90% of our stores having premium full service footwear decks... So I don't see these two things as being cannibalistic."

9. **Mike Baker (D.A. Davidson)** — Golf Galaxy analogy; FTC; procurement definition. Stack: "it strengthened our partnership with those golf brands, which has really helped us not only in Golf Galaxy but DICK's going forward. And this is pretty similar to that... I think it's a very good analogy." Hobart: no regulatory concerns expected. Gupta: both merch and non-merch procurement.

Closing (Stack): "We thank everybody for joining us today. We're very excited about this acquisition."
