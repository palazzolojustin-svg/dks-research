Full chronology:

- **March 2023:** FL announces the "Lace-Up Plan" (technology, loyalty, digital, store refreshes, re-imagined store concepts, focus on core banners), with "aspirational long-term sales goals and EBIT margin targets."
- **Jan 2, 2024:** Ed Stack (DKS Executive Chairman) messages Dillon requesting a meeting, citing industry trends and strategic merit of a combination.
- **Jan 4, 2024 (Chicago area):** Dillon and Frank Bracken (FL) meet Ed Stack, Lauren Hobart (DKS CEO) and Michael Stack (DKS VP Strategy/Corp Dev; since May 2025 SVP Footwear & Golf Galaxy). Stack outlines DKS's interest: scale, global expansion, and that DKS "envisioned operating the Foot Locker banner independently from DICK'S Sporting Goods' other banners." Open to all-cash, all-stock, or mix.
- **Jan 11, 2024:** FL board (Evercore and Skadden present) decides FL "could create more value ... by continuing to focus on the execution of the Lace-Up Plan" and declines to pursue discussions. Dillon informs Stack Jan 12.
- **Feb 26, 2024:** Stack messages that the DKS board concluded a further conversation "was warranted." **Feb 29:** Stack says a written letter will come in two weeks; DKS has been doing public diligence.
- **Mar 6, 2024:** FL reports Q4 2023 and guides FY2024 with a **two-year delay** in Lace-Up targets; FL stock falls from $34.31 (Mar 5) to $24.24 (Mar 6).
- **Mar 12, 2024:** DKS letter assumes FL is not interested given the market reaction; reiterates openness and that price "would need to reflect the risk and work required to execute the Lace-Up Plan."
- **Apr 11, 2024 (Paris, vendor meetings):** Stack tells Dillon DKS "was exploring other potential strategic options"; requests meeting with Dona Young (FL Chair).
- **Apr 23, 2024:** JD Sports announces agreement to acquire Hibbett; Stack messages Dillon shortly after asking to speak. Apr 25: agree to meet May 1.
- **May 1, 2024 (Chicago):** Young and Dillon meet Stack and Hobart. DKS open to all-cash or an election of cash vs. cash/stock mix. FL says its stock price "did not appropriately reflect the value of successfully executing the Lace-Up Plan"; not the right time.
- **May 10, 2024:** Stack says DKS board met that day; DKS is engaging a financial advisor, legal advisor and consultant; written proposal to follow after DKS's May 29 and FL's May 30 earnings calls.
- **May 20, 2024 FL board:** Reviews "the complexities and risks to achieving the Lace-Up Plan, the differences between Wall Street analyst estimates and the targets in the Lace-Up Plan, and recent investor feedback ... regarding the timing and achievability." Discusses structural risks: "relies heavily on a single product category, with merchandise sourced from a limited number of brand partners." Reviews sensitivities.
- **Jun 7, 2024:** DKS letter: **$31–$34 per share** (20–31% premium to $25.88 on Jun 6, 2024), cash or cash/stock mix; subject to diligence with price possibly adjusted; wants to speak with "a brand partner that DICK'S Sporting Goods considered to be important to the success of Foot Locker"; proposes exclusivity; response requested by Jun 21. Same day FL signs Evercore engagement letter.
- **Jun 10, 2024 FL board:** Reviews management's most recent forecasts "and two illustrative sensitivity cases." Approves a two-phased diligence process (limited info first; more only if DKS raises price). Authorizes NDA.
- **Jun 14–Jul 25, 2024 NDA negotiation:** FL proposed 24-month standstill; DKS countered with exclusivity + 3-month standstill (Jun 17); Skadden countered 18 months, no exclusivity (Jun 18); DKS refused >3 months (Jun 19) and said it was "very serious"; FL floated 12 months (Jun 20); Jun 21 meeting cancelled. Jul 12: DKS drops exclusivity but holds on standstill and says it was "prepared to make its proposal to acquire Foot Locker public" if FL would not engage. Jul 15: Stack offers 4 months max. Jul 18: FL board agrees to 4-month standstill. **Jul 25, 2024: NDA signed, 4-month standstill, no "don't ask, don't waive."**
- **Jul 26, 2024 (Palm Beach, FL):** Management meeting with Goldman Sachs and Evercore; FL presents Lace-Up Plan, financial model (aggregated) through FY2028, technology, real estate, new formats.
- **Aug 28, 2024:** FL Q2 2024 results; stock falls from $32.81 to $29.45.
- **Sep 5, 2024:** DKS letter at **$37.00 per share**, cash or mix; response by Sep 16.
- **Sep 15, 2024 FL board:** Skadden reviews fiduciary duties. Evercore reviews preliminary valuation and pro forma capital structure. Board reviews alternatives: Lace-Up execution, JVs, divestitures, acquisitions, operational transformation, capital structure/return changes, capital markets transactions, minority investments (including private investors), licensing partnerships, and other strategic or financial buyers. Board prefers all-cash and wants DKS to pay more because DKS "would retain the benefits of potential synergies in an all-cash transaction." Board believes FL "was undervalued by the market ... because the market was not taking into account the Lace-Up Plan." **Instructs Dillon to counter at $41.00.**
- **Sep 16, 2024:** Dillon conveys $41.00. Stack: DKS "may not be willing to pay $41.00." **Sep 18:** DKS stays at $37.00 pending diligence on Lace-Up progress.
- **Sep 23, 2024 FL board:** Dillon updates on "the recently announced CEO succession at an important Foot Locker brand partner" and its impact on FL results. Board refuses further diligence "until DICK'S Sporting Goods put its best foot forward on price." Evercore delivers same message to Goldman Sachs (Sep 25).

- **Sep 29, 2024 FL board:** Business update: CEO succession and "performance issues at an important Foot Locker brand partner"; CFO Baughn reports "weaker than expected operating performance in September, particularly in Foot Locker's international business." Evercore updates valuation comparing the $37 and $40 proposals. Board discusses other strategic bidders and **decides not to reach out** "in light of the risk of a public disclosure of such outreach and the low likelihood that other strategic acquirers would have strategic interest in, or the financial capacity required to complete, an acquisition of Foot Locker." Authorizes diligence at $40.00.
- **Oct–early Nov 2024:** "Extensive due diligence" by DKS, advisors and consultants, including two all-day management meetings in New York and an online data room, "including a focus on its execution of the Lace-Up Plan."
- **Oct 31, 2024:** WLRK sends initial draft merger agreement. **Nov 1 FL board:** Q3 likely at low end of guidance; "continued weakness at an important Foot Locker brand partner." Skadden flags draft issues: no antitrust commitment, no reverse termination fee, expense reimbursement on no-vote. **Nov 6:** Skadden revised draft (lower termination fee, enhanced antitrust efforts, RTF added, expense reimbursement removed).

Nov 18/26: FL confidential information destroyed.
- **Dec 4, 2024:** FL Q3 2024 results; FY2024 sales and EPS guidance lowered; stock falls from $24.17 to $22.02.
- **Feb 18, 2025 FL board:** Reviews Lace-Up progress; discusses inflation, consumer sentiment, "lack of innovation in athletic apparel and footwear categories and lagging international consumer confidence."
- **Mar 5, 2025:** FL Q4 2024 results and 2025 outlook: "ongoing pressure on margin recovery and another year of significant investment." **"The outlook for 2025 was meaningfully lower than financial information shared with DICK'S Sporting Goods during its due diligence review in October and November of 2024."**
- **Mar 13-14, 2025:** DKS letter at **$24.00 per share**. Letter says FL's falling stock price and analyst reports "reflected a lack of confidence in Foot Locker's ability to achieve the Lace-Up Plan on a stand-alone basis," consistent with DKS's November conclusions; DKS "had the expertise, brand partner relationships and credibility to restore Foot Locker to its rightful position"; $24.00 "reflected the significant work, effort and investment that would be required"; only limited confirmatory diligence needed; response by Mar 20; "time was of the essence."

Stack: DKS "did not intend to lower its offer price of $24.00," open to cash/stock/mix, and **DKS "had acquired Foot Locker shares through open market purchases, and ... currently owned approximately 4.5% of Foot Locker's common stock."** Feedback requested by Apr 8.

- **Apr 19-24, 2025:** Advisor calls on election mechanics/timing, regulatory efforts, fee sizes. Apr 21: Goldman reaffirms $25.50, 80% cap, election at shareholder meeting, RTF, and expense reimbursement on no-vote. Apr 24: DKS revised proposal — election "in the days immediately prior to closing."
- **Apr 25, 2025:** Dillon note to FL board: agreed $25.50, fixed ratio, 80% stock cap, election before closing, **RTF of 4% of transaction value**, **termination fee of 2.75%**, **1% expense reimbursement on no-vote.** Apr 26: WLRK draft merger agreement. Apr 29: FL data room opened. Apr 30: Skadden revised draft; FL posts preliminary Q1 2025 financial information.
- **May 1, 2025:** Stack: DKS management "very concerned by Foot Locker's lower than anticipated financial performance in the first quarter which indicated that Foot Locker's business was declining more rapidly than DICK'S Sporting Goods had expected."

Company X partnership deemed "vague and uncertain" and worth less than DKS deal.

DKS stated several times it was prepared to make its proposal public.

## 3. Management projections (most important section)

*Section: "The Merger—Certain Financial Projections Utilized by the Foot Locker Board of Directors and Foot Locker's Financial Advisor—Foot Locker Management Financial Projections"*

**What exists in the document:** Two five-year tables for Foot Locker standalone, each containing only three line items: Revenue, Adjusted EBITDA, and Unlevered Free Cash Flow. There is **no** disclosure of comps, gross margin, EBIT, net income, EPS, capex, D&A, working capital, tax rate, or levered FCF (**not in document**). There are **no DKS standalone projections, no combined-company projections, and no quantified synergy estimate** anywhere in the proxy (**not in document**). The EPS inputs used by Evercore in its present-value-of-future-share-price analysis are referenced but not disclosed.

### 3a. Foot Locker Management Financial Projections ("management case" / "Forecasts")

Developed at the FL board's direction (Mar 23, 2025 instruction; presented Mar 25, 2025) as "the most likely base case in the current environment through fiscal year 2028." This case was (i) used by the FL board, (ii) approved for Evercore's use in its fairness opinion (where it is called the "Forecasts"), and (iii) provided in part to DKS — **"a subset of the Foot Locker management financial projections to DICK'S Sporting Goods (excluding 2029 estimates and unlevered free cash flow estimates)."** The board noted on Mar 25, 2025 that Wall Street estimates were **lower** than this case.

| ($ in millions) | FY2025E | FY2026E | FY2027E | FY2028E | FY2029E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $8,000 | $8,246 | $8,523 | $8,828 | $8,986 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $453 | $591 | $702 | $776 | $830 |
| Unlevered Free Cash Flow | $135 | $151 | $285 | $288 | $339 |

Derived (not in document; arithmetic on the table above): revenue growth 3.1% / 3.4% / 3.6% / 1.8%; Adj. EBITDA margin 5.7% / 7.2% / 8.2% / 8.8% / 9.2%; UFCF conversion of EBITDA 30% / 26% / 41% / 37% / 41%. For reference, FL's FY2024 (ended Feb 1, 2025) reported net sales were $7,988 million and income from operations $103 million (pro forma historical column).

Stated assumptions for this case (from the Background): EBIT margin assumptions, brand-partner sales "including sales of products from an important Foot Locker brand partner," and "a re-allocation of capital investment from Foot Locker's international businesses to Foot Locker's more profitable domestic banners."

### 3b. Additional Scenario (downside case considered by the board)

Developed by FL management at the board's direction after (per the proxy) "the announcement of tariffs and associated market turmoil and potential consumer impacts, a slower recovery by an important Foot Locker brand partner, and weaker than anticipated first quarter results." Stated assumptions: **"revenue growth of approximately 1% per year for fiscal years 2026-2028 and limited the degree of EBIT margin expansion."** Considered by the board when approving the merger; provided to Evercore "for its reference" and used by Evercore only in the informational "Other Factors" analyses (not in the opinion itself).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $7,951 | $8,023 | $8,103 | $8,184 | $8,225 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $432 | $534 | $619 | $685 | $731 |
| Unlevered Free Cash Flow | $59 | $138 | $228 | $272 | $309 |

## 5. Reasons for the merger and negative factors cited

*Section: "The Merger—Foot Locker's Reasons for the Merger; Recommendation of the Foot Locker Board of Directors"*

**DKS board's reasons: Not in document.** The proxy contains no "DICK'S Sporting Goods' Reasons for the Merger" section (DKS stockholders do not vote). DKS's stated rationale appears only indirectly through the Background (Jan 4, 2024: scale, global expansion, operating FL banner independently; Mar 14, 2025 letter: "expertise, brand partner relationships and credibility to restore Foot Locker to its rightful position in the industry") and the risk factors ("DICK'S Sporting Goods expects to operate Foot Locker as a standalone business unit within its portfolio and maintain the Foot Locker brands and expects to grow the business by applying operational expertise"; "DICK'S Sporting Goods expects significant cost synergies").

### FL board: positive factors (May 14, 2025, unanimous)

1. **Merger Consideration** relative to pre-approval market prices.
2. **Premium:** 80.3% to $13.31 (May 13, 2025); 98.6% to 1-month VWAP $12.08; 62.6% to 3-month VWAP $14.76; 32.2% to 6-month VWAP $18.16; 6.4x TTM EV/EBITDA.
3. **Shareholder election** without proration.
4. **Downside protection** of the cash election (certainty of value, no cap or proration).
5. **Fixed exchange ratio** lets stock electors benefit from any DKS appreciation between announcement and closing.
6. **Participation in upside** including "the potential to realize synergies."
7. **De-risking Foot Locker's strategic plan.** Board "remains supportive of" the Lace-Up Plan but cited: macro/tariff/recession risk and "softer consumer traffic trends globally"; **"Foot Locker purchased 85% of its merchandise in 2024 from its five top suppliers, with approximately 59% purchased from its largest supplier. Each of its banners is highly dependent on Foot Locker's largest supplier"**; ongoing store-refresh capex; omnichannel technology investment; promotional pricing pressure on merchandise margins; non-U.S. businesses that "have underperformed Foot Locker's U.S. businesses and will require significant investments, particularly in Europe."
8. **De-risking the business model** ("relies heavily on a single product category, with merchandise sourced from a limited number of brand partners and sales highly dependent on consumer enthusiasm for new and innovative product offerings"); DKS "has a broader portfolio of product offerings, less brand partner concentration and a wider customer base."
9. **Uncertainty of future stock price** if independent, including risk of missing guidance and Lace-Up targets.
10. **Review of strategic alternatives** — "no other alternatives were reasonably likely to create greater value."
11. **Negotiation outcomes:** termination fee size, regulatory risk allocation, RTF, no fee on no-vote, election timing immediately before closing with no cap/proration. Board believed this "was the best transaction that could be obtained ... at the time."
12. **Expected cost synergies** (benefit to stock electors; no amount given, "no assurance that any particular amount").
13. **Evercore opinion** (noting it did not address the stock election).
14. **Likelihood of consummation** (no financing condition; DKS regulatory efforts).
15. **Merger agreement terms:** fiduciary out; $59.5 million fee viewed as not likely to deter competing bids; $95.5 million RTF; no fee on no-vote; specific performance.
16. **Timing:** soliciting other parties "would jeopardize or, at a minimum, delay the merger"; FL retains ability to consider unsolicited proposals until the vote.
17. **DKS business and reputation** (reverse diligence).
18. **DKS financing strength** (committed debt financing).

### FL board: negative factors

- Cash electors forgo long-term Lace-Up upside and combined-company appreciation.
- Stock electors face valuation variance; value at closing could be below $24.00.
- Risk of non-completion (regulatory, shareholder vote) and consequences for stock price and business.
- $59.5 million termination fee may deter competing offers.
- Business disruption, management distraction, employee attrition, transaction costs.
- Interim operating restrictions requiring DKS consent.
- Announcement uncertainty for "employees, vendors, suppliers, landlords" and litigation risk.
- Need for HSR and non-U.S. antitrust clearances, which "may be delayed or not granted."
- Risks in the "Risk Factors" section.
- Insider interests (acknowledged).

Board conclusion: negatives "were significantly outweighed" by benefits; the merger "would maximize the immediate value of Foot Locker shareholders' shares and minimize the risks and uncertainty affecting the future prospects of Foot Locker, including the potential execution risks associated with the Lace-Up Plan."

## 8. Merger-related risk factors (condensed)

*Section: "Risk Factors—Risks Relating to the Merger", "—Risks Relating to DICK'S Sporting Goods Following Completion of the Merger"*

**Nike:** The word "Nike" does not appear anywhere in the proxy. The brand-partner relationship is referenced exclusively as "an important Foot Locker brand partner" / "its largest supplier." The only quantification is in the Reasons section: "Foot Locker purchased 85% of its merchandise in 2024 from its five top suppliers, with approximately 59% purchased from its largest supplier. Each of its banners is highly dependent on Foot Locker's largest supplier." There is no merger-specific risk factor about brand-partner consent, contract change-of-control, or allocation; the generic risk factor states "Completion of the merger may trigger change in control provisions in certain agreements to which Foot Locker is a party" and that counterparties "may require a fee for such waivers or seek to renegotiate the agreements on terms less favorable."

Risks relating to the merger:
1. Fixed exchange ratio — stock electors "cannot be sure of the value of the consideration they will receive." Cash fixed at $24.00.
2. Closing conditions (2/3 vote, HSR, foreign antitrust) may not be satisfied; outside date May 15, 2026, extendable to Aug 15, 2026 and Nov 15, 2026; $95.5 million RTF.
3. Regulators "may impose conditions that could have an adverse effect" or prevent completion.
4. Insider interests differ from shareholders'.
5. No-shop and $59.5 million fee "may discourage other companies from trying to acquire Foot Locker."
6. Business uncertainties and interim operating restrictions.
7. Disruption of business relationships: "customers, suppliers and other persons ... may delay or defer certain business decisions or might decide to terminate, change or renegotiate their relationships," including "an adverse effect on DICK'S Sporting Goods' ability to realize the expected synergies."
8. Loss of key personnel.
9. Direct and indirect costs: "significant non-recurring costs"; "this net benefit may not be achieved in the near term, or at all."
10. Failure to complete could hurt FL's stock price and business.
11. Termination risk and fees for both parties.
12. Pro forma information is preliminary; purchase price allocation may differ materially.
13. Change-in-control provisions in FL contracts.
14. Litigation (demand letters received from July 1, 2025).

Risks relating to DKS following completion:
15. **Dilution:** issuance of up to ~16.5% of DKS shares "may result in fluctuations in the market price of DICK'S Sporting Goods common stock, including a DICK'S Sporting Goods common stock price decrease"; post-close selling by former FL holders could depress the stock.
16. **Financing:** DKS "cannot guarantee that it will be able to obtain such financing on favorable terms"; merger not conditioned on financing.
17. **Synergies / integration:** "DICK'S Sporting Goods may be unable to successfully operate the Foot Locker business or realize the cost synergies or other anticipated benefits of the merger." "Although DICK'S Sporting Goods expects significant cost synergies and other benefits to result from the merger, there can be no assurance that DICK'S Sporting Goods will actually realize any of them, or realize them within the anticipated timeframe. DICK'S Sporting Goods expects to operate Foot Locker as a standalone business unit within its portfolio and maintain the Foot Locker brands and expects to grow the business by applying operational expertise." Listed challenges include "operating multiple banners, a differentiated store concept and successfully addressing the challenges facing Foot Locker's business" and "coordinating operations in countries in which DICK'S Sporting Goods has not previously operated."
18. Managing expanded operations; "no assurances that the combined company ... will realize the expected operating efficiencies, cost savings or other benefits."
19. Former FL holders will have less influence; **Stack family Class B stock controls a majority of combined voting power** and "would control the outcome of a vote on any corporate transaction."
20. Different stockholder rights under DKS's Delaware governing documents.
21. Combined-company stock may be affected by different factors.
22. **Leverage:** "DICK'S Sporting Goods' aggregate indebtedness as of May 3, 2025, was approximately $7.38 billion (including approximately $3.08 billion of operating leases). DICK'S Sporting Goods' pro forma indebtedness as of May 3, 2025 ... would be as much as approximately $13.03 billion (including approximately $5.47 billion of operating leases) assuming all Foot Locker shareholders receive the cash consideration." Consequences listed include interest-rate exposure on "variable rate term facilities," competitive disadvantage, constrained future borrowing, and cash flow diverted to debt service. "While DICK'S Sporting Goods is focused on maintaining investment grade ratings from these agencies, it may be unable to do so."

## 9. What the proxy tells you that the earnings calls do not

Facts only, with location in the document.

1. **Foot Locker's own five-year standalone plan.** Revenue $8,000 → $8,986 million and Adjusted EBITDA $453 → $830 million (FY2025E–FY2029E), UFCF $135 → $339 million (management case); and a downside "additional scenario" of $7,951 → $8,225 million revenue, $432 → $731 million Adjusted EBITDA, $59 → $309 million UFCF. DKS has not disclosed any FL projections in its own filings. (*Foot Locker Management Financial Projections*)
2. **DKS received only a subset of the plan** — FY2025E–FY2028E revenue and Adjusted EBITDA, "excluding 2029 estimates and unlevered free cash flow estimates." (*Certain Financial Projections*)
3. **The Street was below FL management's case**, and the FL board itself judged by May 8, 2025 that there was "the increasing likelihood that Foot Locker would significantly underperform the management case for the year." (*Background*, Mar 25 and May 8, 2025 meetings)
4. **DKS's price history:** $31–34 (Jun 2024) → $37 (Sep 5, 2024) → $40 "best and final" (Sep 27, 2024) → walked away (Nov 15, 2024) because it "had not been able to validate Foot Locker management's financial case" → $24 (Mar 14, 2025) → $25.50 "best and final" (Apr 16, 2025) → $22 (May 6, 2025) → $24 agreed (May 11, 2025). DKS held at $24 through the April tariff shock. (*Background*)
5. **DKS's diligence conclusion in Nov 2024:** FL "required a much more significant turnaround effort than it had anticipated prior to diligence"; and in Mar 2025 "significant investment would be required by DICK'S Sporting Goods to meaningfully improve the state of the Foot Locker business." (*Background*, Nov 15, 2024 and Mar 16, 2025)
6. **DKS bought ~4.5% of FL in the open market before signing** — 4,269,998 shares (4.47%) as of the record date; 4.3 million shares bought in the 13 weeks ended May 3, 2025 at a carrying value of $55.6 million (~$12.93/share derived); one-time pro forma gain of $46.9 million (cash) / $32.5 million (stock). (*Summary—Record Date; Pro Forma Note 3A1, 4K1*)
7. **DKS's original intent and operating plan:** from the first Jan 4, 2024 meeting, DKS "envisioned operating the Foot Locker banner independently"; DKS cited "DICK'S Sporting Goods' interest in global expansion"; DKS asked in June 2024 to "speak with a brand partner that DICK'S Sporting Goods considered to be important to the success of Foot Locker." (*Background*)
8. **DKS threatened to go public with its offer** at least three times (Jul 12, 2024; Mar 21, 2025; implied Apr 1, 2025), and refused a standstill longer than 4 months. (*Background*)
9. **Regulatory divestiture cap:** DKS must accept remedies only for FL assets with ≤ **$100 million** of FY2024 revenue; DKS controls antitrust strategy; HSR filed June 23, 2025; foreign filings required. RTF $95.5 million. (*The Merger Agreement—Regulatory Approvals and Efforts to Close*)
10. **Financing specifics:** $2.4 billion bridge (reduced to $2.15 billion), new $2.0 billion revolver (June 6, 2025, matures June 6, 2030), pro forma assumption of **$1.7 billion senior notes at 6.4%** = ~$110 million annual interest; FL 4.000% Notes due 2029 exchange offer and covenant strip (consents received June 20, 2025). (*Financing of the Merger; Pro Forma Introduction; Note 4L*)
11. **Pro forma leverage:** total indebtedness including leases rises from ~$7.38 billion to as much as ~$13.03 billion (all-cash case). (*Risk Factors*)
12. **Pro forma FY2024 diluted EPS of $12.61 (all cash) or $11.91 (all stock) vs. DKS standalone $14.05**, with no synergies and including one-time costs; Q1 FY2025 pro forma is a loss of $(1.52)/$(1.12) because of FL's $276 million impairment quarter. (*Pro Forma Statements of Operations; Note 5*)
13. **Purchase accounting:** goodwill of only $289 million (cash) / $118 million (stock) on ~$2.3 billion / ~$2.0 billion consideration; $105 million PP&E step-up; $72 million favorable-lease intangible; FL trademarks carried at $210 million (cash case). (*Note 3A, 3B, 3C, 3K*)
14. **One-time costs quantified:** DKS transaction costs $56.4 million; FL seller costs $55.3 million; executive severance/acceleration $22.6 million; retention $7.5 million; bond exchange fee $2.0 million. (*Notes 3D–3I*)
15. **FL supplier concentration:** 85% of 2024 purchases from top five suppliers, ~59% from the largest. (*Foot Locker's Reasons for the Merger*)
16. **FL board's internal view of its own business:** repeated references to "the structural risks inherent in Foot Locker's business model," international "significantly below the management case," "continued pressure through fiscal year 2026 on an important Foot Locker brand partner," "lack of innovation in athletic apparel and footwear categories." (*Background*, Feb 18, May 8, 2025)
17. **Only one other party ("Company X") ever surfaced**, and it declined to acquire FL, offering instead a minority investment with board rights and a global partnership; FL never ran a market check. (*Background*, May 7–10, 2025)
18. **Evercore's valuation ranges:** all trading-comp, LBO and premiums-paid ranges fell below $24.00; the management-case DCF ($20.95–$30.90) and precedent transactions ($17.55–$40.85) bracketed it; analyst targets topped out at $24.00. Evercore's fee ~$40 million, almost all contingent on closing. (*Opinion of Foot Locker's Financial Advisor*)
19. **Election timing:** elections close 5 business days before the estimated closing date, so FL holders choose with near-final knowledge of DKS's price; no cap or proration means the actual cash/stock mix (and therefore DKS's actual debt and share issuance) is determined only at closing. Stock consideration was worth $25.07 vs. $24.00 cash on July 10, 2025. (*Election Procedures; Market Price Information*)
20. **DKS dividend covenant:** regular quarterly dividends and increases are expressly unrestricted during the pendency; special dividends are prohibited. (*Conduct of Business of DICK'S Sporting Goods Pending the Closing*)
21. **Expected close:** "second half of 2025"; outside date May 15, 2026 with two 3-month antitrust extensions. (*Expected Timing of the Merger*)
22. **Not in document:** quantified cost synergies, DKS or combined-company projections, a DKS-side (Goldman Sachs) fairness opinion, a DKS board "reasons" section, comparative per-share data, any mention of Nike by name, FL comps/gross margin/EPS/capex projections, or any identification of the foreign antitrust jurisdictions.
