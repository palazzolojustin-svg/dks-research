### 2.2 Joint press release (Exhibit 99.2) — exact wording
Source: `DKS_425_2025-05-15_120246.md` lines 453–610.

- Headline: "DICK'S Sporting Goods to Acquire Foot Locker to Create a Global Leader in the Sports Retail Industry".
- "This transaction implies an equity value of approximately $2.4 billion and an enterprise value of approximately $2.5 billion."
- Foot Locker: "approximately 2,400 retail stores across 20 countries in North America, Europe, Asia, Australia and New Zealand, and a licensed store presence in Europe, the Middle East and Asia. In 2024, Foot Locker achieved net worldwide sales of $8 billion. DICK'S expects to operate Foot Locker as a standalone business unit within its portfolio and maintain the Foot Locker brands."
- Consideration: "(i) $24.00 in cash or (ii) 0.1168 shares of DICK'S common stock for each share of Foot Locker common stock. The election is not subject to a minimum or maximum amount of cash or stock consideration."
- "Based on the closing price of Foot Locker common stock on 5/14/2025, the $24.00 per-share consideration represents a premium of approximately 66% to Foot Locker's 60-trading day volume weighted average price. The total consideration represents an acquisition multiple of approximately 6.1x fiscal 2024 adjusted EBITDA."
- "DICK'S intends to finance the acquisition through a combination of cash-on-hand and new debt."
- Synergies/accretion: "accretive to EPS in the first full fiscal year post-close (excludes transaction and other one-time costs to achieve synergies) and to deliver between $100 to $125 million in cost synergies in the medium-term achieved through procurement and direct sourcing efficiencies."
- Timing: "expected to close in the second half of 2025," subject to FL shareholder approval and regulatory approvals.
- Advisors: Goldman Sachs (financial advisor to DKS, "provided fully committed bridge financing"); Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz (DKS legal); Evercore (FL financial); Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom LLP (FL legal).
- Strategic rationale (five bullets, headings verbatim): "Create a global platform within the growing sports retail industry."; "Serve a broader set of consumers across differentiated concepts."; "Strengthen relationships with brand partners through global reach."; "Invest in future growth through an industry-leading omnichannel experience."; "Unlock operational efficiencies that create shareholder value."
- Quotes: Ed Stack ("We have long admired the cultural significance and brand equity that Foot Locker and its dedicated Stripers have built…"); Lauren Hobart ("…we'll create a new global platform…"); Mary Dillon, CEO of Foot Locker ("…offers the choice of significant and immediate cash value or the opportunity to invest in the combined company…").
- DKS store count in boilerplate: "more than 850 DICK'S Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, Public Lands and Going Going Gone! stores".

### 2.4 Merger Agreement 8-K (Item 1.01) — material terms
Source: `DKS_425_2025-05-15_120955.md` lines 94–178.
- Parties: DKS; RJS Sub LLC (NY LLC, wholly owned); Foot Locker, Inc. (NY corporation). Merger Sub merges into FL; FL survives as wholly owned subsidiary.
- Election period: "not less than twenty business days ending on the date that is five business days prior to DICK'S good faith estimate of the Closing date."
- Equity awards: employee RSUs and PSUs convert into DKS time-based RSUs based on the stock consideration (performance goals deemed achieved per award agreement); non-employee director RSUs/deferred units vest and cash out at the cash consideration; options cashed out for spread over $24.00 (out-of-the-money options cancelled for no consideration).
- Closing conditions: adoption by holders of two-thirds of outstanding FL common stock; HSR expiration and clearance under "the antitrust laws of certain other jurisdictions"; no Restraint; S-4 effectiveness; reps/warranties and covenants; no continuing FL Material Adverse Effect.
- Financing: commitment letter dated May 15, 2025 with Goldman Sachs Bank USA for "up to $2.4 billion of senior bridge term loans" to fund cash consideration, repay certain FL indebtedness and pay fees/expenses.
- Termination: outside date May 15, 2026 "subject to two automatic extensions of three months each if on such date all of the closing conditions except those relating to regulatory approvals have been satisfied or waived"; 30-day cure for breaches.
- Fees: FL pays DKS $59,500,000 on change of recommendation / Superior Proposal; DKS pays FL $95,500,000 "if the Merger Agreement is terminated in connection with a failure to obtain the requisite antitrust approvals."

### 2.5 Investor call transcript — prepared remarks and Q&A (every number and material statement)
Source: `DKS_425_2025-05-15_120906.md`. Participants: Nate Gilch, Ed Stack (Executive Chairman & Chief Merchandising Officer), Lauren Hobart, Navdeep Gupta; analysts Brian Nagel (Oppenheimer), Paul Lejuez (Citi), Adrienne Yih (Barclays), Simeon Gutman (Morgan Stanley), Robert Ohmes (BofA), Kate McShane (Goldman), Michael Lasser (UBS), Christopher Horvers (JPMorgan), Michael Baker (D.A. Davidson).

Prepared remarks:
- Stack: Galyan's precedent — "a similarly sized acquisition relative to the scale of our business when we acquired them."
- TAM: US ~$140B; global ~$300B; combined >3,200 stores.
- FL: ~2,400 stores, 26 countries at end of 2024; >400 refreshes in 2024; digital 18% of revenue; 2024 revenue $8B.
- Gupta: Q1 comps +4.5%, non-GAAP EPS $3.37; terms as above; "committed to maintaining our investment grade ratings."

Q&A (quoted where wording matters):
- Timing / Nike (Nagel): Stack — "a big part of Foot Locker under pressure was what the relationship they had with Nike. Nike moved into DTC and away from wholesale partners a while back, hurt Foot Locker pretty significantly, Nike's changed their position really leaning back into wholesale. I think Elliott and his team are doing a great job … Foot Locker is going to be a beneficiary of that move back … we have talked to a couple of [brands], they're pretty enthusiastic about this."
- Synergy timing/composition (Lejuez): Gupta — "$100 million to $125 million of synergy over the medium term. And we'll provide more color as we are closer to the close … opportunity both on the gross margin line … As well there are opportunities on the SG&A line when you think about the procurement opportunities." Hobart — "we plan to run these two businesses separately … we will have a team here, a small team here led by Ed that will help with that integration … the synergies that we are counting on at this point" are "procurement and purchasing and things like that." On banners: "our plan right now is to run them as separate entities … The consumer may or may not know that DICK'S and Foot Locker are one."
- Brand conversations / purchase concentration / footwear mix (Yih): Stack — "we talked with several brands about the opportunity … They gave us a surrogate impression of what was going on with Foot Locker." Also: "with the World Cup coming in 2026, Olympics in 2028, the World Cup is going to be the biggest sporting event this country has ever seen." Gupta — "the footwear business as a combined company would be close to about 50% of the total sales of the company."
- Leverage / FL bonds (Yih): Gupta — "We finished last year with $1.7 billion of cash on the balance sheet. So, as we look at this transaction, the immediate impact on even if these were to be fully financed using the debt, it won't impact unfavorably our credit ratings today … our intention is to use a combination of cash, long term debt, as well as equity as we look to finance this transaction. In terms of the planning to take out the Foot Locker bonds that are outstanding, we are working with our advisors to see if there is an opportunity to exchange the obligations and if need be we'll be able to take those notes out as well." (Analyst premise of "close to 3 times leverage" was not confirmed or denied.)
- Why buy a shrinking FL (Gutman): Stack — "Foot Locker has a consumer — it serves a consumer in neighborhood that we in many cases are never going to get. They're in places that we're not going to be able to find 50,000 square feet or 60,000 square feet"; "footwear as the engine that pulls this train"; "We've talked with Nike, Foot Locker is a very important part of Nike's long term strategy"; board direction that the deal "cannot impact what we're doing from DICK'S … House of Sport, Field House … GameChanger." Hobart — "the TAM that we're operating in is growing from $140 billion to $300 billion."
- Distribution risk (Gutman): Stack — brands "are not looking to change their distribution strategy and broaden it significantly … they really want to be in a specialty type environment."
- Nike share within FL (Ohmes): Stack — "I think Foot Locker will get more high heat product … the Nike business will probably go up. We expect that Nike business will grow up at a higher margin rate because they will be able to sell more product at full price."
- Store closures (Ohmes): Hobart — "that team has obviously done an assessment of all of their stores. They've indicated there are some that are potentially being closed … It's not our expectation that we'll be closing a significant number of Foot Locker stores."
- Tariffs (McShane): Stack — "There was some good news that came out of Geneva last week … we've been able to manage through tariffs in the past."
- Why different from failed retail mergers (Lasser): Stack — "footwear is part of our core competence … we've got visibility for Foot Locker performing meaningfully better"; FL apparel "deteriorating … They haven't invested in that."
- Underwriting comp assumption (Lasser): Stack — "we're not going to provide any information right now … when we close we'll provide what we think this — what the camp is going to be, what we think the EPS is going to be, a better view of what the accretion is going to be."
- International for DKS (Horvers): Hobart — DKS strategies "don't include, at this time, international expansion"; Stack — "we have no plans to take DICK'S internationally."
- Customer crossover (Horvers): Hobart — "our market share in this $140 billion market that we've been competing in thus far is still around 80%" [as transcribed; likely "8%"] … "with our 90% of our stores having premium full service footwear decks … I don't see these two things as being cannibalistic."
- Golf Galaxy analogy (Baker): Stack — "it strengthened our partnership with those golf brands … I think it's a very good analogy."
- FTC (Baker): Hobart — "we are not expecting any regulatory concerns."
- Procurement definition (Baker): Gupta — "both … the procurement vendor, the direct sourcing vendors as well as the non-merge vendors."
