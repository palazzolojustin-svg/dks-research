## 1. Business description essentials (FY2025 10-K, Item 1, lines 285-626)

### 1.1 Company overview (verbatim, line 289)
> "DICK'S Sporting Goods, Inc. ... is a leading global sports retailer offering an extensive assortment of authentic, high-quality sports equipment, apparel, footwear and accessories. Our banners include DICK'S Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, Public Lands and Going Going Gone! stores in addition to the experiential retail concepts DICK'S House of Sport and Golf Galaxy Performance Center which are all located across the United States. Additionally, as owner and operator of Foot Locker, which includes Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS and atmos banners, we serve the global sneaker community across North America, Europe, Asia and Australia, plus a licensed store presence in Europe, the Middle East and Asia. We also own and operate GameChanger, a youth sports mobile platform for live streaming, scheduling, communications and scorekeeping."

- Founded 1948 (Binghamton, NY) by Richard "Dick" Stack; Edward W. Stack became President/CEO in 1984; renamed DICK'S Sporting Goods, Inc. in April 1999 (line 291).
- Two reportable segments from FY2025: **DICK'S** (DICK'S Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, Going Going Gone!, Public Lands, GameChanger) and **Foot Locker** (Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS, atmos) (line 293). Employees = "teammates"; customers = "athletes" (DICK'S) / "sneakerheads" (Foot Locker); Foot Locker store employees = "stripers" / "blue shirts" (Champs).
- HQ: 345 Court Street, Coraopolis, PA 15108.
- Change vs FY2024 10-K: FY2024 described a single-segment U.S. "omni-channel sporting goods retailer"; FY2025 adds "global", the Foot Locker banners, and two segments.

### 1.2 Store concepts — definitions (FY2025 10-K lines 325-329, 413-427)
- **DICK'S Sporting Goods store**: "unites several sports specialty stores under one roof and typically contains the following specialty shops: Team Sports, Athletic Apparel, Outdoor, Golf, Fitness and Footwear" ("store-within-a-store") (line 417).
- **DICK'S House of Sport**: "provide highly experiential destinations for our athletes. These innovative formats are built around experience, service, community and product" (line 327). Experiential elements include HitTrax batting cages, Trackman golf simulators, premium full-service footwear decks, House of Cleats.
- **DICK'S Field House**: "we are incorporating House of Sport learnings into our most typical 50,000 square foot DICK'S store, which we are calling our DICK'S Field House concept" (line 327).
- **Golf Galaxy Performance Center**: redesigned Golf Galaxy launched 2021, "equipped with Trackman golf technology and include an elevated staffing and service model" (line 327).
- **Going Going Gone!**: clearance/value concept — "improve our clearance optimization through the consolidation of clearance inventory for omni-channel selling opportunities to better serve our value athletes" (line 417).
- **Public Lands**: outdoor banner (3 stores at FYE; one closure planned for conversion to a Field House in early FY2026, line 1392).
- **Foot Locker Business**: "a portfolio of specialty athletic footwear and apparel banners, including Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS and atmos" plus licensing agreements with third-party operators in the Middle East, Europe and Asia generating royalty income (line 425).
- **Fast Break initiative** (new in FY2025): eleven-store Foot Locker pilot "which includes enhanced in-store presentation and a more focused assortment"; expanded by ten stores (Los Angeles) in early 2026; goal "to have approximately 250 locations ahead of the back-to-school season" in 2026 (lines 399, 1247, 1605).

### 2.5 Acquisition-related charges and synergies (FY2025 10-K lines 399, 1247, 1557, 3416)
- MD&A (line 1315): "Net income also includes $307.3 million, net of tax, or $3.61 per diluted share, of acquisition-related costs". Foot Locker pro forma comparable sales for FY2025 "decreased 3.3%", including an International decrease of 8.1% (line 1235).
- FY2025 acquisition-related charges $390.0 million: $217.9 million write-down and liquidation of inventory; $164.2 million merger and integration costs (legal/regulatory fees, professional services, employee retention and severance, including $41.7 million accelerated equity expense for terminated executives); $7.9 million bridge financing costs. Also a $13.4 million asset impairment charge in corporate and other.
- "We expect these actions and other merger and integration costs to result in pre-tax charges of $500 million to $750 million, of which $390 million was incurred in fiscal 2025." "We expect approximately $150 million of acquisition-related costs in fiscal 2026."
- "we expect the acquisition to deliver between $100 million to $125 million in cost synergies in the medium-term, to be primarily achieved through procurement and direct sourcing efficiencies."
- Subsequent event (Note 19 / Item 9B): March 23, 2026 Foot Locker position eliminations and relocation to New York HQ, "approximately $40 - $55 million of charges primarily in the first quarter of 2026".

## 11. Cybersecurity (Item 1C, lines 1011-1041) and the August 2024 incident
- 8-K filed August 28, 2024 (Item 8.01): "On August 21, 2024, the Company discovered unauthorized third-party access to its information systems, including portions of its systems containing certain confidential information." Company activated its response plan, engaged external experts, notified federal law enforcement; "The Company has no knowledge that this incident has disrupted business operations" and "believes that this incident is not material."
- FY2024 10-K and FY2025 10-K (Item 1C) do not reference the August 2024 incident by date; both state: "risks from cybersecurity threats, including the results of any previous cybersecurity incidents, have not materially affected the Company, its business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. While we have no knowledge of any material data security breaches to date..." The FY2024 10-K notes the CISO departed in October 2024 (CTO served as interim CISO) and a new CISO "with over 25 years of experience ... joined the Company in January 2025" (FY2024 10-K line 829).
- FY2025 Item 1C (new content): focus areas were Foot Locker integration and AI; "the Company temporarily operated under a dual-CISO structure" (DKS CISO and Foot Locker CISO) through March 2026; Foot Locker systems "continued to operate under their pre-acquisition cybersecurity framework, which may differ from the Company's established standards." Governance: Audit Committee oversight; CISO reports to the CTO, who reports to the CEO; quarterly updates to the Audit Committee; NIST Cybersecurity Framework; IR Plan with materiality review.

## 12. Risk factors — condensed inventory (FY2025 10-K Item 1A, lines 627-995) with change flags

Flags: **NEW** = heading did not exist in FY2024 10-K; **EXPANDED** = heading retained but materially rewritten/broadened; **UNCHANGED** = substantively the same. The FY2025 10-K rewrote nearly every heading (terminology changed from "athletes/teammates" to "customers/employees" and added "global" scope), so "EXPANDED" is used where the substance broadened, not merely the wording.

### Risks Related to Our Industry and Macroeconomic Conditions
1. Macroeconomic conditions may adversely affect consumer discretionary spending... — **EXPANDED**: adds geopolitical paragraph: "Ongoing conflicts and tensions —such as those in the Middle East (particularly the recent U.S. military operations in Iran), Ukraine and Venezuela—... may create economic instability, disrupt supply chains, increase fuel and transportation costs, or elevate cybersecurity risks."
2. Intense competition in the sporting goods and retail industries could limit our growth and reduce our profitability. — UNCHANGED in substance.
3. Fluctuations in product costs and availability could adversely affect our business... — **EXPANDED (tariffs)**: "Significant increases in those costs—whether due to trade tensions, shifting tariff policies in key sourcing countries like China, Mexico, and Canada (including recently enacted tariffs and potential additional shifts in tariff policies in the future), currency fluctuations, material shortages, supply chain disruptions, or other factors—could increase manufacturing and other costs". FY2024 heading had first added "tariffs" ("including tariffs recently enacted or that may be enacted in the future by the federal government or by other countries in response to U.S. tariffs"); FY2023/FY2022 headings did not mention tariffs.
4. Our reliance on products manufactured outside the United States and our significant international operations expose us to numerous global economic, political, regulatory, and supply-chain risks... — **EXPANDED (international operations new)**: "A substantial portion of our merchandise, including most of our vertical brand merchandise, is manufactured abroad ... changes in import duties, quotas, tariffs, and other trade restrictions; the potential introduction or expansion of taxes on imported goods; the loss of favorable trade status; trade remedy actions; geopolitical tensions". Adds foreign indirect taxes, VAT, customs valuation and currency exposure of international retail operations.

### Risks Related to Our Operations and Reputation
5. If we are unable to anticipate or respond effectively to changes in consumer demand, preferences, fashion trends or shopping patterns... — EXPANDED (adds "fashion trends", reflecting Foot Locker).
6. Our vertical brand offerings and specialty concept stores expose us to potential increased costs... — UNCHANGED; discloses vertical brands "approximately 13% of our overall sales within the DICK'S Business."
7. Harm to our reputation could adversely impact our ability to attract and retain customers and employees. — UNCHANGED.
8. Our strategic plans and initiatives may initially result in a negative impact on our financial results... — EXPANDED: adds Fast Break pilot, AI/machine learning, DICK'S Media Network, GameChanger.
9. An inability to execute our real estate strategy could adversely affect our financial results. — **EXPANDED (mall exposure new)**: "Because a meaningful portion of our stores, particularly within our Foot Locker Business, are located in shopping centers or malls, our performance is influenced by the volume of mall traffic and the sustained success and relevance of those locations." Unauthorized access to, or disclosure of, sensitive or confidential information could result in substantial costs, operational disruption, legal exposure, and reputational harm, and evolving privacy and cybersecurity regulations may increase our compliance obligations... — EXPANDED: adds "cyber threats continue to evolve rapidly accelerated by emerging technologies such as advanced artificial intelligence and machine learning. 12. Problems with our information systems could disrupt our operations... — UNCHANGED.
13. We may be unable to attract, train, engage and retain key employees and to adequately respond to employee organizing efforts. — EXPANDED: "whether any unionization or other organizing efforts could occur with our employees within one or more of our domestic or international markets."
14. The loss of one or more of our key executives... — EXPANDED: "including recent hiring and appointments of key executives within our Foot Locker Business."
15. The seasonality of certain categories of our operations, along with the current geographic concentrations of our stores... — UNCHANGED (DICK'S peak Q2/Q4).
16. We cannot provide any guaranty of future dividend payments or that we will continue to repurchase our common stock... — UNCHANGED.

If we do not successfully manage our inventory levels and protect against inventory shrink... — EXPANDED: "our inventory shrink generally stabilized in 2025, we have experienced elevated levels of inventory shrink in the past, which could return or worsen." 18. Our future growth may depend on the Foot Locker Business's ability to expand its market share in international markets, including through licensed or franchise arrangements... — **NEW**: "Our Foot Locker Business has retail operations in 20 countries across North America, Europe, Asia, and Australia, plus a licensed store presence in Europe, the Middle East, and Asia."
