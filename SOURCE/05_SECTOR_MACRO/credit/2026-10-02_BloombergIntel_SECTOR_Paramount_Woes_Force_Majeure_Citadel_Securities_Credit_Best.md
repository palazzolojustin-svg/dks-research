4. ATI’s Junk Climb Could Reach High-BB Ledge with Below 2x in View
Contributing Analysts Matthew Geudtner (Credit)
ATI's rising earnings and cash flow through 2027 may support upgrades on its almost $1.6 billion of
junk bonds climbing higher into the BB tier over the next year. The multiyear effort to reduce debt,
transfer pension liabilities to Apollo-owned Athene and improve operations should keep
strengthening financial risk, with margins, leverage and cash flow coverage screening in line with or
better than BI's high-grade medians. Upgrades from Ba2/BB would leave ATI, with leverage potentially
well below 2x, on the doorstep of investment grade. Liquidity remains strong, refinancing risk is
subdued and technicals are favorable after the redemption of the 5.875% notes due in December
2027. (10/02/26)

BASF Bonds Look Tight to Peers, M&A Shifts Risk to Widening
Contributing Analysts Timothy Riminton (European Basic Materials)
BASF's euro curve screens very tight to single-A given the potential for an Evonik acquisition putting
ratings at risk. We think bonds would widen if BASF makes further moves to go ahead with the
purchase. The smaller dollar curve is wider, yet we see little room for tightening. With more
divestment proceeds and high cash balances, we think BASF will continue to proactively manage its
maturities, provided it doesn't need that cash for M&A. (10/01/26)
Communications
Paramount Junk-Note Sale Size to Rival SoftBank $10 Billion Deal
Contributing Analysts Stephen Flynn (Credit) & Sharon Chen (BI) (Credit)
Paramount Skydance expects to sell about $12.4 billion equivalent of junk-rated second lien notes to
help fund its pending $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, with the amount of dollar-
denominated notes issued likely to rival SoftBank Group's $10 billion sale of BB+ rated bonds on Sept.
23. Paramount second lien bonds could be among the largest groups in the high yield market, as
almost $13 billion of Warner bonds received offers to exchange into new Paramount second-lien
notes. (09/28/26)
T-Mobile US, Netflix Lead $134 Billion Bonds With Ratings Upside
Contributing Analysts Stephen Flynn (Credit)
About $134 billion of communications bonds have upgrade potential, led by T-Mobile US, Netflix and
EchoStar. Fitch has maintained a Positive Outlook on T-Mobile since May 2026, citing balanced capital
allocation and strong operating momentum as key credit drivers. Notes with upgrade potential may
be overshadowed by about $320 billion of sector bonds at risk of downgrades. M&A, asset sales and
related leverage changes have emerged as key ratings drivers across communications, with several
issuers already seeing outlook or watch actions tied to major transactions. (09/28/26)
Comcast, AT&T Amid $320 Billion Sector Bonds With Ratings Risk
Contributing Analysts Stephen Flynn (Credit)
Dollar-denominated communications bonds amounting to $320 billion are at risk of lower ratings,
including obligations of AT&T, Comcast, Optimum's CSC Holdings, Paramount Skydance, Rogers
Communications, and Warner Bros. Discovery. When compared with $134 billion of debt with a positive
ratings bias, rating actions could be a headwind to communications returns. Comcast bonds (A3/A-)
will likely fall out of the single A ratings category next year, while Rogers bonds (Baa3/BBB-) are on
the cusp of high grade, yet the company is focused on improving its profile. (09/30/26)

6. Sunoco M&A, Earnings a Path to High Grade Spread: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Amalia Hakobyan (Credit)
THESIS: Sunoco's acquisition spree has transformed its credit profile, more than tripling its earnings
base since 2023 while adding geographic diversification and a larger contribution from fee-based
infrastructure. Strong cash generation provides capacity for further deals and refinancing, though
continued acquisitions could leave little time for leverage to settle into its 4x target before the next
deal. Senior unsecured bond (Ba1/BB+/BB+) valuations continue to reflect Sunoco's high yield status
despite its increased scale and broader business profile, leaving room for spread compression toward
investment grade levels. (09/28/26)
7. Cheniere Energy Sets Sail for 73 Million Tonnes of LNG Per Year
Contributing Analysts Spencer Cutter (Credit)
With expansion projects coming on line, Cheniere Energy expects to produce about 53.5 million
tonnes of liquefied natural gas in 2026, a year-over-year increase of over 6 million. Additional growth
is expected to be driven by the construction of Trains 8 and 9 and a debottlenecking project at
Corpus Christi, which are about half complete and may boost production to about 63 million tonnes
per annum (MTPA) and consolidated Ebitda to $7.7 billion. Beyond that, a Sabine Pass expansion
project that's expected to reach its final investment decision in early 2027 and another Corpus Christi
project could boost production to 73 MTPA.
The company boosted its share-buyback program to $10 billion through 2030 and spent $1.1 billion
on repurchases in 1H. Even as debt climbed by $2 billion since 2025, leverage edged lower to 3.1x
and export cargoes and volume grew by 19-22% year-over-year. (09/25/26)

Pemex Bonds Need More Government Support to Extend 20% Rally
Contributing Analysts Spencer Cutter (Credit)
Pemex faces headwinds from Mexico's soft economic outlook, potential increased competition from
Venezuelan heavy-crude exports and, despite a $20 billion cut in financial debt over the past year,
debt and debt-like obligations that remain north of $200 billion. After generating a 26% return
between June 2025 and August 2026, Pemex bonds have lost 6% and may need a second wave of
government support to regain momentum. (10/01/26)
VoltaGrid Bonds Trade Wide of Junk Peers Before AI Power Ramp-Up
Contributing Analysts Amalia Hakobyan (Credit)
VoltaGrid (B1/B+/BB-) provides modular gas-fired power solutions, with an existing business largely
serving oil & gas customers and a rapidly expanding behind-the-meter data-center platform.
Significant investment in contracted data-center projects is driving a sharp increase in capital
spending and debt, including $2 billion of senior secured second-lien notes (B3/B+/BB-) and a $3
billion asset-based loan facility issued to help fund the build-out. The 2030 notes (B3/B+/BB-) yield
about 7.4%, above behind-the-meter peers and the broader high-yield energy index, reflecting
execution risk around the build-out. Any credit improvement will likely depend on Texas-based
project Frontier beginning to ramp by year-end 2026, followed by greater customer diversification as
the data-center business scales. (09/28/26)
8. Western Midstream Mergers Could Boost Credit-Rating Prospects
Contributing Analysts Spencer Cutter (Credit)
Western Midstream boosted its 2026 Ebitda guidance in 2Q, to $2.85 billion from $2.7 billion, given
strong 1H performance and the Brazos Delaware acquisition, which closed in June. Though capital
spending is expected to come in at the high end of the $850 million-$1 billion budget, the company
also boosted its distributable cash flow and free cash flow guidance by $200 million each. Growth
projects or acquisitions that push Ebitda toward $4 billion while maintaining a leverage ratio below
3.5x could lead to an upgrade on Western's current Baa3/BBB-/BBB- credit ratings and help narrow
the 10-25 bp gap of the company's bonds relative to peers. News reports have indicated that Western
could be a potential buyer of Kinetik Holdings.

9. Citadel Securities BBB- Bonds Reflect Lower Risk: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Arnold Kakuda (Credit) & Nicole Castelblanco (Credit)
THESIS: Market maker Citadel Securities' bonds (Baa3/BBB-) reflect its lower-risk model, as its 10-year
bonds trade more than 100 bps tighter than those of split-rated rival Jane Street, which has taken on
more hedge-fund-type risk, leading to higher revenue but a $15 billion loss in July. Citadel Securities'
Baa3 bonds have a positive outlook from Moody's, but its 2036 notes already trade more than 20 bps
tighter than one-notch-higher-rated Jefferies. (10/02/26)
10. Lincoln National Corrals Risks in Private Credit: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts David Havens (Credit) & Nicole Castelblanco (Credit)
THESIS: Lincoln National has restored its balance sheet strength after large reserve charges in 2022,
though its share price, which has been stuck in neutral for the past year, indicates a lack of visible
earnings growth. Even with 20% of its investments in private credit, asset quality is strong with 91% of
private holdings investment grade. Bond spreads are slightly elevated, leading to possible excess
returns if market condition remain stable. (09/30/26)
11. Jane Street Largesse After Big Earnings Hit Soothes Bondholders
Contributing Analysts Arnold Kakuda (Credit)
Jane Street's generosity with bondholders during its August debt refinancing following its $15 billion
loss in July may explain why its new bonds have rallied over 90 bps relative to rival Citadel Securities,
and could ease concerns about potential hiccups in the future. The large earnings hit could have
widened spreads on old bonds by over 100 bps, leading to losses of more than 3% for notes with
duration over 3 years. Instead, make-whole calls on old bonds averaged a 3.5 point premium, for a 6
point swing. Crossover candidate Jane Street's new bonds (rated Ba1/BB/BBB-) priced about 200 bps
wider than slightly higher-rated Citadel Securities (median BBB-), but now trade 95-110 bps wider
than its rival. Jane Street's old bonds used to trade just 50 bps wide to Citadel before news of the
August refinancing. (09/29/26)

Healthcare
Danaher's $18 Billion Debt Capacity Can Keep M&A Engine Running
Contributing Analysts Jean-Yves Coupin (Credit) & Keerthana Kothapalli
In a fragmented industry, M&A remains central to Danaher's strategy for driving growth and realizing
scale benefits. Following its $9.8 billion acquisition of Masimo in June, we expect Danaher to use its
ample debt capacity to pursue further M&A, as evidenced by the pending StatLab acquisition. For its
A2/A- ratings, the risk from higher leverage is mitigated by strong cash generation and a successful
integration track record. (09/30/26)
12. Solventum Bond Risks Amid Trian-Driven Deals: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Jean-Yves Coupin (Credit)
THESIS: Solventum's (Baa3/BBB) sale of its Purification and Filtration business to Thermo Fisher has
dramatically improved its balance sheet strength as leverage declined to a 2x low. With activist
investor Trian pushing for further portfolio adjustment, bondholders are facing heightened
uncertainty despite management's conservative financial targets. Reduced scale and diversification
could offset the improvement in financial flexibility and fuel further underperformance of its bonds
relative to BBB-rated healthcare peers Baxter, GE Healthcare, Illumina and HCA. (09/28/26)
Teva, Viatris Bonds Take the Lead as IG Bond Curve Flattens
Contributing Analysts Jean-Yves Coupin (Credit)
Healthcare investment grade (IG) bonds at the long end of the curve recouped some of their summer
losses in September as the curve flattened. Teva's new bond issuance was warmly received, helping to
reprice the low-BBB pharma curve, with Viatris a main beneficiary of this trend. (10/01/26)
AI Will Boost Cost Efficiencies in Healthcare, Improve Outcomes
Contributing Analysts Jean-Yves Coupin (Credit)
The healthcare industry is particularly well suited for rapid adoption of AI. It's driving operating
efficiencies, while the use of predictive health-intelligence tools could improve outcomes. They will
contribute to counterbalancing the problem of healthcare cost inflation. Health insurers are
particularly suited for an acceleration in AI adoption, given the extent of customer interactions, large
proprietary datasets and clinical expertise. (10/02/26)

Broadcom Deepens Circular Financing With Anthropic: Credit React
Contributing Analysts Suchi Trivedi
RECENT EVENT REACTION: Broadcom's potential $42 billion financing for Anthropic appears credit-
positive overall, as AI semiconductor growth could outweigh added counterparty and balance-sheet
risk. Reuters reported the facility may fund about one-third of Anthropic's $125.2 billion five-year TPU
lease commitment, extending the circular-financing model also used by Nvidia to support its AI
ecosystem. The added exposure warrants monitoring, though Broadcom's rapid AI-revenue growth
makes the roughly 15-bp widening in long-dated spreads since early August appear excessive.
Positive outlooks on its A3/A-/A- ratings leave room for further upgrades, though larger AI-financing
commitments could slow that path. (10/01/26)
AI Spending Could Turn Isolated Tech Risk Into a Ratings Wave
Technology credit downgrade risk is concentrated today, with $270 billion of bonds exposed and
three issuers -- Oracle, IBM and Salesforce -- accounting for 80% of the total, yet that balance could
shift quickly if AI monetization disappoints. A more conservative stance from raters could put one or
two additional hyperscalers on watch as capital spending, leases and off-balance-sheet financing push
leverage toward downgrade thresholds. That could add hundreds of billions of dollars of debt to the
downgrade-risk pool, turning what now looks like company-specific pressure into a broader sector
concern. (09/28/26)
Nvidia Can Fund AI Ecosystem, Still Buy Back Big: Credit React
Contributing Analysts Suchi Trivedi
RECENT EVENT REACTION: Nvidia's $150 billion stock-repurchase increase underscores exceptional
financial flexibility, with strong free cash flow and liquidity supporting record shareholder returns
without compromising its AA ratings profile. That flexibility is increasingly valuable as Anthropic and
other AI developers sign ever-larger neocloud contracts, and contracted compute demand across
the ecosystem reaches well into the tens of billions of dollars, while Nvidia uses take-or-pay
guarantees, strategic investments and other credit support to help finance capacity. The $235 billion
remaining buyback through fiscal 2028 is sizable, yet Nvidia's cash generation and operating scale
leave substantial room to return capital while continuing to fund customers, partners and the
broader AI build-out. (09/28/26)
