TransDigm’s $3 Billion Maneuver Helping Fan Technical Tailwind
Contributing Analysts Matthew Geudtner (Credit)
TransDigm tendering for the 6.75% secured bonds due in 2028 with proceeds from the $3 billion
pricing of 6.75% secured paper maturing in 2035 extends favorable technicals for the supplier's
capital structure. The maneuver sustains a history of managing upcoming spires well ahead of
maturity, keeping refinancing risk subdued with interest coverage still tracking near the high-end of
target. (09/22/26)
Bombardier’s $1.9 Billion War Chest Padded with Eye on Cadence
Contributing Analysts Matthew Geudtner (Credit)
Bombardier bolstered an already strong $1.9 billion liquidity position at midyear by increasing
capacity under its secured revolving line of credit to $750 million after quarter-end and extending
the maturity to 2031. With the cash-flow pace rising in 4Q vs. 3Q, generation in 2H may eclipse $650
million and keep liquidity well above a $1-$1.5 billion goal to start 2027. (09/22/26)
Chemicals
BASF 3Q Consensus Faces Risk of Miss on Rhine and Power Costs
Contributing Analysts Timothy Riminton (European Basic Materials)
BASF's high exposure to Europe, with its largest assets on the Rhine, leave it looking exposed to cost
pressures from the low river levels and high gas prices in 3Q. We anticipate a substantial miss to
consensus Ebitda for this quarter. Yet with some signs of markets tightening emerging again, a
better-than-expected 4Q could still leave overall 2H Ebitda in line with current consensus and near
the upper end of guidance. (09/24/26)
Communications
Paramount's New Debt Comparables Likely to Be Charter, SpaceX
Contributing Analysts Stephen Flynn (Credit)
Paramount Skydance is expected to sell about $40 billion of first lien bonds and loans to fund its
pending $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. The bonds will likely have at least two
investment grade ratings, comparable to Charter Communications secured notes (Ba1/BBB-/BBB-)
and SpaceX bonds (Baa1/BBB/BBB+), which trade among the widest in high grade communications.
The Warner purchase may close by Sept. 30, given the recent settlement of an antitrust lawsuit
brought by 12 state attorneys general. (09/24/26)

4. Large Casinos Can Weather Near Term; Smaller Pressured by Debt
Contributing Analysts Jody Lurie (Credit) & Keerthana Kothapalli
Large US casino operators, including MGM, Boyd and Sands, remain well positioned to absorb softer
economic conditions, supported by liquidity and cash generation, though leverage and free-cash-
flow conversion vary widely. Las Vegas and regional demand remains mixed in 2026, showcasing the
importance of geographic and product diversity, particularly in the face of a growing predictions
market. Differences in credit quality persist, with highly levered smaller operators facing greater
refinancing and liquidity risk.

Scrapped Diller Purchase of MGM a Credit Positive: Credit React
Contributing Analysts Jody Lurie (Credit)
RECENT EVENT REACTION: MGM Resorts' bond spreads (unsecured B1/BB-) could narrow in relief after
Barry Diller's People Inc. walked away from its proposed takeover. People still owns a 27% stake in
MGM, leaving capital-allocation questions open, particularly as Fertitta's purchase of Caesars
(unsecured B3/B-) progresses. A longer-term credit risk may come from competing cash demands.
MGM's Osaka joint venture, BetMGM expansion and other capex weigh on cash as MGM continues to
spend on share buybacks. As of 2Q, $1.4 billion remained under its buyback authorization. MGM
withdrew its New York casino license bid last year, citing changing terms. We saw that as a sign of
cautious cash use given the macro backdrop and company strategy. (09/23/26)
Royal Caribbean Sandals JV a Digestible Structure: Credit React
Contributing Analysts Jody Lurie (Credit)
RECENT EVENT REACTION: Royal Caribbean's (Baa2/BBB) bonds could hold steady vs. Carnival's
(Ba1/BBB-), as Royal's 50% purchase of Sandals Resorts could strain the balance sheet less than initial
reports suggest. With $3 billion in new debt, we calculate net leverage rising just 0.2-0.3x to about 3x,
using 2027 MODL Ebitda plus Royal's portion of Sandals' $600 million in Ebitda, and factoring in
previously announced ship financing. Royal forecasts net leverage within its target range and no
ratings impact, implying either confidence in rater treatment or prior discussions with the raters.
Still, net leverage near a downgrade threshold would remain well above management's mid-2x year-
end 2025 goal stated a year ago. (09/23/26)
5. Nike Spread Can Tighten as Margin Fix Takes Hold: Credit Outlook

8. Despite Skeptics, the Athene-Apollo Combo Works: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts David Havens (Credit) & Nicole Castelblanco (Credit)
THESIS: Athene and its parent Apollo Global Management have been at the vanguard of alternative-

13. Entegris Cuts Debt, Rides Semiconductor Upturn: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Ilona Ioffe (Credit)
THESIS: An improving semiconductor backdrop, along with Entegris' focus on ramping capacity and
reducing debt, is strengthening company fundamentals. The credit, largely a pure-play on advanced
materials for semiconductors, is benefiting from recovering wafer utilization, customer investment
and secular AI-driven demand. Entegris' 2030s (Ba2/BB-/BB) yield about 6%, 40 bps inside Qnity's
B1/BB/BB+ 2033s and 20 bps inside on a maturity-adjusted basis. We expect Entegris to remain mostly
range-bound this year, given its deleveraging focus vs. Qnity's greater M&A flexibility and longer
duration. With the 2030s becoming callable and trading below the call price, current levels retain
some call-related upside, while a later takeout would provide additional coupon carry. (09/22/26)

14. Big Tech Bond Boom: Another Record in 2027 as AI Splurge Endures
Contributing Analysts Suchi Trivedi
Big tech looks poised to break another record for high grade issuance in 2027, defying concerns that
this year's pace isn't sustainable as surging AI investment overwhelms the sector's formidable cash
generation. Issuance across currencies has already topped $400 billion in 2026, surpassing 2025's
record, and could reach $500 billion next year as refinancing needs, shareholder returns and still-
rising AI capital spending keep borrowing elevated. This debt surge is unlikely to trigger broad ratings
pressure as Ebitda growth keeps leverage under control for most large issuers and funding markets
remain receptive despite vocal skepticism. (09/23/26)
Meta Muse, AI Build-Out Reinforce Long-Term Growth Potential
Meta's AI investment could sustain high-teens revenue growth through 2030, but the rising capital
burden may push free cash flow into negative territory, keeping buybacks subdued even as Ebitda
margins hold near 50-55% and the balance sheet retains substantial capacity. The new Muse agent and
broader AI adoption offer another long-term monetization path, while $130-145 billion of 2026 capex,
$84 billion of debt and expanding data-center commitments make capital efficiency the key test of
Meta's next phase. (09/24/26)
How AI Capex, Debt Drove Amazon Commitments Above $650 Billion
Contributing Analysts Suchi Trivedi
As capital spending surges above $200 billion this year, Amazon.com's free cash flow has turned
sharply negative, while debt has risen significantly, a trend likely to extend in 2027. Even with total
commitments and obligations (including leases and unconditional purchase obligations) now above
$650 billion, Amazon's A1/AA/AA- credit profile remains stable, as rising AI revenue should drive Ebitda
higher and leverage lower as investments are monetized. (09/19/26)
Utilities
Xcel Energy Credit Navigates Capex, Cash Flow and Wildfire Risk
Contributing Analysts Barbara Chapman (Credit)
Xcel Energy's (Baa1/BBB/BBB+) credit profile is being tested by a $60 billion capital plan, weak cash-
flow metrics and lingering wildfire risk, though substantial equity funding, relatively low parent debt
and strong market access provide support. Funds from operations-to-total debt is expected to
recover as rate relief and load growth bolster cash flow, but further capital-spending increases could
pressure funding needs. With recent wildfire liabilities largely resolved and spreads tightening,
execution of the investment plan and restoration of credit metrics are increasingly important.
(09/25/26)

Hughes Network Mediation Push Points to Narrow Examiner Role
Contributing Analysts Negisa Balluku (Litigation)
EchoStar's Hughes Network Systems Chapter 11 will likely remain focused on mediation and liquidity,
with the bankruptcy court likely to favor a limited role for the examiner even if the current standstill
-- set to expire on Oct. 2 -- fails. A broader examiner probe could duplicate existing investigative
work, increase costs and strain liquidity, while a targeted mandate would provide scrutiny of potential
estate claims without unnecessarily disrupting restructuring negotiations. (09/23/26)
Getty Images' Stumbles Put Capital Structure In Focus (Correct)
Contributing Analysts Philip Brendel (Credit)
Getty Images has suffered a series of losses on strategic gambles or material actions, from refusing to
allow holders to exercise warrants to its recent failed attempt to acquire Shutterstock. The losses
may eclipse $300 million, of which $111 million has been paid to warrant holders. With liquidity shy of
$100 million, Getty has engaged Guggenheim,skipped a Sept. 1 coupon payment and it's now
exploring financing alternatives. With Getty Images facing significant decisions ahead, we're initiating
our distressed coverage. (Corrects Shutterstock name.)
