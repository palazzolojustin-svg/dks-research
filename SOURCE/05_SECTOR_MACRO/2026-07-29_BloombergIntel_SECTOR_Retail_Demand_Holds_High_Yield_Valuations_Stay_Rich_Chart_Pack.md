1. Retail Demand Holds; High Yield Valuations Stay Rich: Chart Pack
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- High yield retail bonds may widen as tariff
and labor risks challenge cash-flow recovery, while high grade retail
credit has begun to reprice. Despite high grade retailer bonds widening
6 bps and high yield tightening 2 bps on average in the past month; the
Bloomberg US corporate bond retail index OAS is 17 bps tighter than
the broader high grade index and is 57 bps through the broader high
yield index. Resilient consumer demand supports credit, yet rich high
yield valuations leave little cushion. Expectations for Ebit recovery are
led by Gildan Activewear and Tapestry in high grade and Capri, Victoria's Secret and Under Armour in
high yield. Laggards include Bath & Body Works and Kohl's in high yield and Nike and Tractor Supply in
high grade.

2. Heavyweights Overstate Narrow Inventory Problem
The sector's 6% dollar-weighted inventory excess over company histories overstates the breadth of
the risk. The median retailer is 1.2% below its three-year average, year-over-year inventory growth is
only 0.2% and inventory-to-trailing-sales is 0.5 point lower. Walmart accounts for 42% of the net dollars
above historical levels, while Walmart, Home Depot, Dick's, Costco and TJX Companies represent about
98%. Yet Walmart's inventory-to-sales ratio rose just 0.2 point and turnover remains 9.1x. Costco and
TJX have lower inventory intensity than a year ago, while acquisitions explain much of the build-up at
Dick's and part of Home Depot's.
This concentration argues against a broad retail inventory penalty. Company selection should matter
more as large balances become a credit concern only where sales weaken, markdowns rise or
working-capital conversion deteriorates. (08/07/26)
