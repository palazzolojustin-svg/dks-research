China's Weak 1H Retail Sales Put Full-Year Forecasts at Risk
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- China's 1.3% retail-sales growth in 1H makes meeting our 1.7% full-year
estimate and consensus' lowered 2.1% projection increasingly difficult, as it requires a sharp 2H
acceleration. Slack June credit data reinforces 2H's downside risk if support from the PBOC fails to
land. Beneath the surface, there are emerging signs of opportunistic gold buying, while a persistent
fall in online services spending could hit Meituan and Trip.com. (07/28/26)
1. Subsidy Lift Belies Fragile Consumption
China's 1H retail-sales contraction of nearly 7% in subsidized consumer-goods categories, led by a 11%
slump in 2Q vs. a 2% drop in 1Q, reinforces our view that any trend upticks amid broader consumption
weakness will reflect policy support rather than a durable recovery. Excluding autos, sales of these
goods fell 8% in June vs. a 6% decline in the first five months of 2026, highlighting subsidy fatigue and
weak underlying demand. The collapse in sales of autos, appliances, furniture and construction-
related items in 1H shows big-ticket spending remains hostage to subsidies and housing sentiment.
Cultural, office appliances and communications equipment were the outliers, posting a stronger June
on a year-on-year basis vs. declines in prior months. But this was led by the government's release of
about 62.5 billion yuan of spending incentives from a third tranche of ultra-long special bonds, which
restored subsidy availability after earlier allocations ran thin, rather than to any pickup in underlying
demand. Subsidy rates and caps were left unchanged from January-May and from 2025 levels based
on our checks. Hence, the rebound was funding-driven rather than demand-led, underscoring our
thesis that the subsidy lift belies fragile consumption, with policy cash flow -- not consumer
conviction -- dictating the timing of category-level swings.
A 3% drop in June jewelry sales, vs. a 15% decline in April-May as bullion prices dropped below 32,000
yuan per troy ounce, signals gold-linked demand is edging from defensive to opportunistic, with
consumers showing greater willingness to add exposure on pullbacks in precious-metal prices.

2. China's Luxury Demand Should Recover
Demand for luxury goods in mainland China should improve through 2026 even if gold-jewelry
demand fades. Richemont reported double-digit sales growth in Greater China led by Hong Kong and
Macau in April-June, showing stronger momentum vs. the 3% for full fiscal 2026 ended March. This was
likely driven by robust sales of jewelry as the company highlighted strong demand for the category in
the Asia-Pacific region. LVMH cited strong 1Q demand for branded leather goods, led by new Dior
collections. Its jewelry lines, including Tiffany and Bulgari, gained traction sequentially from weak
2025 bases. Kering's jewelry houses, including Boucheron, also performed well. Prada said 1Q
spending was strong, with both Prada and Miu Miu growing.
Moncler reported robust double-digit growth from Chinese customers at home and abroad in 1Q.

3. Seizing More Specialized Sportswear Trends
China's 2026 retail sales growth for clothing, shoes and related products will likely trail that of non-
subsidized consumer goods as Chinese shoppers become more selective in their spending. However,
labels with stronger product innovation and differentiated performance-driven offerings are poised
to secure stronger sales momentum. To achieve this, Nike, Adidas, Anta, Puma and Li Ning will need to
pivot to more sports-led innovation and performance franchises to capture Chinese consumers'
gravitation toward specialized athletic gear. This shift is critical to defending local market shares
against fast-growing labels such as Arc'teryx and Salomon under Amer Sports in China.
Lululemon and Anta's Maia Active are well-positioned to capture Chinese consumers' growing appetite
for specialist yoga and training gear in 2026. (07/28/26)

4. China’s Growth Goal Is Just in Reach With ‘Drip-Feed’ Stimulus
Contributing Analysts Chang Shu (Economics) & Eric Zhu (Economist) (Economics)
China's year-end economic strategy is relying on targeted support, not firepower, to jump-start a
recovery. The economy is starting to pick up from a mid-year lull, led by strong tech-related exports
and calibrated policy interventions. With the government accelerating investment and rolling out
targeted steps following pledges for stronger countercyclical actions, growth should gain momentum.
But the modest size and scope of the stimulus mean that the economy is likely to see stabilization,
not a sprint into the year-end.
We project GDP growth at 4.4% in 3Q and 4.5% in 4Q, after a 4.7% expansion in the first half. On that
trajectory, full-year growth will reach 4.5%, just clearing the low end of the official 4.5%-5% target
range. The conservative policy approach suggests Beijing will aim for a similar pace of growth in 2027.

5. Risk of Sub-2% Retail Sales Growth in 2026
China's 2026 retail sales will rise just 1.7% from a year earlier, missing consensus' latest downsized
forecast of a 2.1% gain under current stimulus programs, based on Bloomberg Intelligence's China
retail-sales scenario model. June's data support our expectation, showing 1H retail sales grew just
1.3%, raising the risk of full-year growth toward the low end of a sub-2% outcome.

Our forecasts have yet to factor in a potential 10-basis-point interest-rate cut and 25-bp cut to

Beijing's efforts to support the economy. Historical easing cycles suggest such moves alone are

7. Gold Volatility Dims Jewelry Demand; Niche Demand Intact
Jewelry, especially gold items, could outperform other non-subsidized discretionary categories only
if bullion prices remain stable. The sequentially narrower 3% drop in June jewelry retail sales hints
that bullion price swings from March-May only temporarily tempered demand. Consumers still see
gold jewelry as a store of value, but volatility is fueling more selective, timing-conscious buying
rather than a structural loss of confidence.
Within garments, footwear and accessories, demand for branded luxury should rise in 2026 after two
years of contraction. Pricier sportswear labels such as Arc’teryx, Salomon and Lululemon will likely
also benefit from rising demand for specialized athletic gear while sharper innovation and stronger
online engagement will be key to lifting cosmetics sales as China’s beauty market matures. (07/15/26)

8. Optimizing Selling Channels Can Fuel Beauty Growth
Contributing Analysts BI China Consumer Team
China's June retail sales in cosmetics expanded 12.6% year-on-year, the fastest growth in the past 20
months, vs. a 1% uptick in overall retail sales, signaling resilient beauty demand among mainland
shoppers so far in 2026. This brought 1H growth in the category up to 6.3%, making it likely that
cosmetics sales will rise by our forecast of 2.7% this year, after jumping more than 5% last year.
As China's cosmetics market matures, brands will need sharper innovation and more effective
engagement in the channels that matter most, especially online, to drive repeat purchases and
defend market share. The recovery of Hainan duty-free sales this year is likely to support global
premium brands' sales. (07/19/26)

9. Sustaining Online Spending Remains Critical
China's digital-commerce operators are unlikely to materially scale back incentives in 2026 if
consumer sentiment stays lackluster. Over the last 12 months, major platforms including Alibaba,
Meituan and JD.com have been intensifying a structural push to lock in habitual usage through on-
demand convenience and expedited delivery. Sustaining this behavior will likely require continued
discounts, and these companies are set to add more perks as they push agentic applications and
tools designed to cement new consumption habits.
China's online retail sales of physical goods expanded 3.6% year-on-year in June, but spending on
online services fell 1.5% -- the category's first decline on record -- after platforms pulled back
promotional subsidies from a high base. This suggests service-category volume growth will likely
decelerate through 2H as discount intensity normalizes. Unlike physical goods, where purchases can
be deferred or trade-in incentives banked for later use, on-demand services (food delivery, travel,
