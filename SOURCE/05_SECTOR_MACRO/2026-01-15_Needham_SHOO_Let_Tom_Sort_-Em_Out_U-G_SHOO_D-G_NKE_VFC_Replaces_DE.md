OUR CALL
In this report, we look ahead to 2026 for the Apparel/Footwear sector, while updating
thoughts on some of our covered names. After a tough 2025 for the group (down -15% on
average vs. SPX +16%), there's reason for hope in 2026: a resilient consumer, hopefully no
more tariffs (and a potential refund of already-paid tariffs), and various margin tailwinds
(pricing, FX, etc.) Amid this backdrop, we are adding Buy-rated VFC to our Conviction List,
as we believe the TNF and Timberland brands had strong holiday performances, the long-
struggling Vans brand is showing some green shoots, and we expect steady improvement in
margins and the balance sheet over the next 24-36 months. Additionally, we are upgrading
SHOO to Buy from Hold, as we believe they are benefiting from favorable fashion trends,
there's a major sales/margin/EPS recovery opportunity in 2026/2027, and they should get
meaningful M&A accretion from the Kurt Gieger acquisition over the next few years. On
the flip side, we are downgrading shares of NKE to Hold from Buy, as the turnaround is
progressing slower than we expected, we're concerned about the recent level of sell-in to the
North America wholesale channel, China appears highly problematic, and Street numbers
for the next 12-24 months look too high to us. Additionally, we are removing DECK from
the Conviction List, as we believe the company has entered a slower-growth stage of life at
both the UGG and Hoka brands, and margins may drift downward in the coming years to
sustainable long-term levels. But we remain at Buy on the stock (though with a lower level
of conviction than before) due to the quality of the two brands and the cash-rich balance
sheet, as well as compelling valuation vs. peers.
Reasons for Both Optimism And Skepticism On The Consumer For 2026. Making a
forecast on year-long consumer demand in January is obviously very challenging, but we do
see a few puts and takes worth highlighting. On the positive side, the consumer has been
resilient in the face of macro uncertainty, particularly during key shopping events (back-
to-school, Holiday, etc.) There's also an expectation that President Trump's "Big Beautiful
Bill" will result in bigger tax refunds, which could positively impact Q1 results. Furthermore,
unemployment remains fairly low, real wages are growing, interest rates may continue to fall,
and the stock market has been hitting all-time highs (providing a favorable "wealth effect"
to higher-income consumers). However, there are key sources of uncertainty: consumer
balance sheets aren't necessarily as strong as one might think (household debt at all-time
highs, savings rate below pre-COVID norms), the threat of inflation looms, and geopolitical
events remain a source of "noise".
Margins Will Likely Be A Big Story For 2026. Following a tough 2025 when most
companies in our coverage saw their margins get dinged by tariffs (and sometimes by
fundamental factors as well), 2026 will likely be a year when companies try to get back
at least some of what they lost last year. Mitigation strategies should be a tailwind for
most of the space, and there's optionality for the group if the Supreme Court rules that
tariffs must be refunded to companies (this recovery opportunity is key to our upgrade
of SHOO). Furthermore, there's a lesser-discussed margin tailwind for 2026 as well: the
weaker U.S. dollar. Due to hedging programs that many companies employ, the FX impact
on gross margin oftentimes gets pushed out by 12-24 months. So, while most global softlines
companies saw top-line tailwinds from FX in 2025, they should now see a gross margin
benefit in 2026 as their hedges roll off. All the companies in our coverage have sizable
international presences (20%-60% of revenues), so this should be a tailwind across the group.

VFC: ADD TO CONVICTION LIST, RAISE PT TO $25
After years of challenged fundamentals, we believe that brighter days are ahead for VFC. Our checks for Holiday suggest strong
demand for The North Face and Timberland (the company's largest and 3rd largest brands, respectively), which is important
given that these "cold weather" brands skew heavily to calendar Q4. Furthermore, if sell-throughs were strong during the peak
season in Fall/Winter'25, they are likely to be rewarded with strong wholesale order books for Fall/Winter'26. Meanwhile, Vans
still has work to do, but we have seen some "green shoots" lately that might mean that the worst is behind them. We also view the
recently-completed Dickies sale as "addition by subtraction", as it barely impacts the P&L but reduces debt leverage by about
half a turn. And on that note, we think the debt balance will continue to move lower, which should improve investor sentiment
on this name (as the balance sheet was one of the core tenets of the bear thesis in recent years). Taking all this into account, we
are raising our 12-month price target to $25, which is based on a low-20's P/E on calendar 2027 EPS of ~$1.17 (which likely still
reflects some "under-earning" on the part of the company).
VFC's P&L has one of the biggest skews to calendar Q4 in our coverage, due to the focus on cold-weather apparel and footwear at 2 of its
3 largest brands, The North Face (TNF) and Timberland. Specifically, TNF and Timberland each generate ~35% of annual revenues and
~55% of annual EBIT in calendar Q4 (the company's fiscal Q3). Even when factoring in the brands that have less of a seasonal skew, VFC
is still highly leveraged to this quarter, as it accounts for ~30% of consolidated revenues and last year it was almost 85% of annual EPS.
Thus, the current quarter is truly a "make or break" quarter for VFC in terms of annual EPS generation.
Fortunately for these brands, this quarter has been meaningfully colder YoY, in each month of the quarter. Using weather data from New
York City as a proxy, we can see how cold it's been. In October, the average temperature was ~4 degrees Fahrenheit lower YoY, while
November was down ~5 degrees YoY and December was ~4 degrees colder on average. Admittedly, the weather was far less favorable in
the Western part of the country, but we believe that the cold conditions in the Northeast and Midwest have caused weather to be a net
positive. We believe that colder temperatures are favorable for cold-weather categories such as boots, outerwear, fleece, etc., which are
the core categories for TNF and Timberland.

DOWNGRADE TO HOLD, PRICE TARGET WITHDRAWN
Although we continue to believe that CEO Elliott Hill is doing the right things for the brand long-term (re-focus on sport, re-focus
on product innovation, re-engagement with key wholesale partners, etc.), we believe that the turnaround is taking longer than
expected, and visibility into the turnaround remains low. One potential strategic misstep, however, could be the aggressive sell-
in to the North American wholesale channel (+24% last quarter and +11% the quarter prior). We are concerned that this could
cause near-term challenges, as we don't believe that "brand heat" is at levels that support that level of sell in (and at the very
least, it results in difficult compares later this calendar year). Additionally, challenges in China and the Converse brand appear
very intense and the timeline to get those businesses back on track is uncertain at best. And, while we believe that NKE has a path
to recovering the earnings they've lost the last few years, we believe that consensus estimates are too aggressive on the pace of
recovery, presenting risk to numbers over the NTM. Furthermore, share are up almost 15% since the post-EPS trough (vs. SPX
+1%), largely due to a couple of high-profile insider purchases (CEO Elliott Hill purchased $1 million worth of shares on 12/29
and board member Tim Cook purchased almost $3 million worth of shares a week earlier), which has pushed the stock up close
to our prior $68 price target, resulting in less than 10% potential upside vs. our prior target. Thus, taking all this into account,
we are stepping to the sidelines pending greater clarity around the turnaround.
While the company has expressed optimism around the state of the turnaround in North America (due to the solid +9% organic growth
in the November quarter), the channel-level dynamics paint a different picture, in our view. As recently as 1Q25 (Aug'24), the wholesale
and DTC channels were moving in lock-step (both down LDD), but over the past 5 quarters there has been a major divergence in sales
by channel. The DTC channel has remained deeply negative (down 10%-15% in 4 of the past 5 quarters), while wholesale has accelerated
dramatically and was up a surprising +24% in fiscal 2Q26 (Nov'25).
Admittedly, some of this channel-level disparity is by design: DTC was over-extended due to the prior regime's overly-aggressive push into
the channel, as well as the oversaturation of Classic styles like Jordan retros and Dunks (which sell in both channels, but became highly
skewed to DTC in recent years). Likewise, the Wholesale channel has become a bigger focus of the company upon Mr. Hill's return, as the
channel was unwisely neglected during the prior regime.
However, we are somewhat concerned about the magnitude of the delta between the two channels (34 percentage points spread in fiscal
2Q26). First off, selling that much product into the wholesale channel ($3.55 billion in a single quarter, the biggest North America wholesale
revenue quarter in the company's history) could lead to issues down the road if the product is slow to sell, such as reduced orders in future
seasons and additional discounting. Management did note that a significant portion of the wholesale growth last quarter was clearance/
liquidation-driven, but that's not healthy for the brand either. And at the very least, it makes for difficult compares in fiscal 2027, which
could result in a slower revenue recovery than the market expects.

SHOO: UPGRADE TO BUY, $50 PRICE TARGET
Although SHOO shares have bounced significantly since the post-Liberation Day trough, we believe the stock will continue
climbing in 2026. We believe the company is a key beneficiary of changing fashion trends (less focus on athleisure, more focus
on "dress up"), with a major recovery opportunity in sales, margins, and EPS over the next few years. Additionally. last year's
acquisition of the Kurt Geiger brand may yield strong results, as we believe the business is under-earning (both in terms of
revenue and margin). We're raising our Q4/FY25 EPS forecasts to the high end of guidance (with potential for further upside
if the quarter was better than we thought), now calling for EPS of $0.46/$1.68 respectively (vs. $0.43/$1.65 prior). We're also
raising our FY26/27 EPS forecasts to $2.30/$2.67 (from $2.00/$2.16 prior), given greater optimism around the company's margin
recapture opportunity.
We believe that fashion trends are shifting, which should be beneficial to SHOO in 2026 and potentially beyond. In 2025 we saw a key shift
in trend by sub-category, with a meaningful slowdown in athletic/athleisure trends (negative comps at LULU, Athleta, NKE; slower growth
at Hoka, etc.) and an acceleration in non-athletic brands (RL, LEVI, Coach handbags, etc.). Notably, heading into the holiday season each
of the past 2 years, we asked consumers about their purchase intent for the holidays, and compared the results YoY. Among women we
surveyed, some of the biggest increases in purchase intent came from SHOO's core categories: boots experienced a nearly-800bps increase
in purchase intent, while dress shoes were up almost 250bps. Fitting the "dress up" theme, "clothes to wear to work" were also very strong
(up more than 600bps YoY). We believe this bodes well for SHOO's performance during Q4, which could lead to momentum as we turn
the calendar to 2026.

one of the biggest success stories coming out of the pandemic, as the trend towards cozy/comfortable footwear brought many first-time
shoppers to the brand, who stuck with the brand for subsequent purchases due to their newfound appreciation for the product quality
(particularly the comfort level). However, after adding about $1 billion of incremental revenue relative to pre-COVID levels (on a pre-
pandemic base of just $1.5 billion), demand for UGG seems to have slowed of late. While wholesale sell-in has remained strong (partially
due to retailers being "starved" of product in past years and suffering from insufficient inventory levels), the DTC business has slowed

RISKS TO TARGET
DECK
Potential downside risks include:
■
Hoka operates in a highly competitive athletic footwear market.
■
Hoka could run out of new channels of distribution, slowing potential growth.
■
UGG growth could stall if new product introductions fail to resonate with consumers.
■
Margins could come under pressure if growth slows and more discounting/markdowns are necessary.
NKE
Risks to the NKE story include:
■
It could longer than expected for new CEO Elliott Hill to effect change at the company, particularly given long lead times for new
product development.
■
The company has a large exposure to China (15% of revenues), which is a market that has become far more challenging in recent
quarters.
■
The ability to wean consumers off of discounts and get them to buy more at full-price could prove more challenging than expected,
which could either keep a lid on margins or negatively affect revenues.
RL
Potential risks to the stock include:
■
After 8 years of raising prices, the company could hit a "wall" on pricing, stifling revenue growth going forward.
■
The macroeconomic environment in Europe is getting more challenging, and RL has one of the highest exposures to the region in our
coverage (~30% of revenues).
■
The company has experienced a significant amount of multiple expansion recently (now 20x NTM consensus EPS vs. 5- and 10-year
averages of 20x), so if fundamentals erode there could be significant multiple compression.
SHOO
Downside risks to SHOO include:
■
The company could face integration issues from its recent Kurt Geiger acquisition.
■
SHOO is the most fashion-sensitive company in our coverage, presenting risk if its design team fails to keep up with the latest trends.
■
Wholesale customers have been very cautious lately, which could present headwinds from a sell-in perspective, as wholesale
customers manage their inventories tightly.
VFC

might struggle to get back to sustainable growth.

The North Face has been resilient of late. If trends in North America and EMEA remain volatile while tough macro conditions in Asia
slow growth there, then VFC's largest brand could see deteriorating fundamentals.
■
The company's FY28 plan includes a significant amount of EBIT improvement independent of revenue growth ($500-$600 million),
which could fail to occur due to unexpected headwinds (tariffs, cost inflation, etc.)
