On Can Top Hoka in Apparel Market Share as Retail Strategy Grows
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- On could surpass Hoka in global apparel market share over the next
decade, securing 0.3% of a market that Euromonitor projects could reach $304 billion by 2030. On's
current 0.1% share compares with Hoka's 0.2%, based on our calculations, but it can expand as
investments in product innovation, premium retail and brand-building speed adoption beyond its
core footwear franchise. On is aided by expanding head-to-toe assortments, an elevated flagship-
store experience and significant opportunities in the US and China. Our analysis suggests apparel
sales could grow at a roughly 36% CAGR through 2030, increasing more than fourfold to nearly CHF800
million. (08/06/26)
1. On's Apparel Strategy Could Unlock Its Next Growth Leg
The global sports-apparel market represents On's largest long-term growth opportunity as it expands
beyond its footwear roots into a broader premium sportswear brand. The category is projected to
increase to nearly $304 billion by 2030 from roughly $247 billion today, according to Euromonitor,
supported by rising athletic participation, increased health and wellness spending and consumers
wearing performance apparel in more aspects of daily life. Unlike athletic footwear, where market
leadership is more concentrated, the top 10 sports-apparel brands account for just 32% of global
sales, leaving meaningful room for emerging premium names, like On, to gain share via product and
design innovation and differentiated positioning.
Though footwear remains On's largest business, apparel is increasingly becoming a strategic growth
pillar that expands the company's addressable market. Unlike the Deckers-owned Hoka brand where
the apparel strategy remains more closely tied to the footwear franchise, On is investing to establish
the segment as a stand-alone growth engine. These initiatives quadruple sales to almost CHF800
million.
On plans to broaden its apparel assortment beyond running into training, tennis and lifestyle, and can
further expand the number of consumer occasions it addresses while increasing opportunities for
cross-selling and repeat purchases. A growing focus on women's apparel, technical innovation and
premium design should further strengthen the assortment, while lifestyle campaigns featuring
Zendaya and FKA Twigs, together with expanding co-created collections and fashion collaborations,
continue to elevate the brand's cultural relevance. (08/06/26)

The US represents On's largest apparel-growth opportunity, accounting for roughly 45% of the global
sports apparel market, yet the company still holds only about 0.1% market share, according to
Euromonitor, suggesting meaningful room for expansion. Though Hoka maintains a larger presence
with a 0.2% share, we believe On is better positioned to narrow the gap and surpass Hoka as it expands
its premium flagship network and strengthens brand awareness.
Owned retail remains central to increasing apparel penetration, giving On greater control over
merchandising, storytelling and the customer experience than wholesale channels alone. Despite the
size of the opportunity, both On and Hoka remain underpenetrated from a retail perspective,
operating just 14 and 17 US stores, respectively.
Unlike Hoka, where stores remain primarily footwear-centric, On merchandises apparel as a core part
of the shopping experience, with elevated visual displays and complete head-to-toe assortments that
more closely resemble premium athletic retailers such as Lululemon and Alo Yoga. This differentiated
retail model should support higher apparel attachment rates while broadening the brand's appeal
beyond core runners.
We believe On has a credible path to increasing its US sports-apparel market share from roughly 0.1%
today to about 0.3% by 2030, supported by continued flagship expansion and higher apparel
attachment rates in owned retail. That would represent more than CHF200 million of incremental
apparel sales over the next five years. (08/06/26)

3. On's Apparel Pricing Rivals Premium Activewear Leaders
On's apparel pricing is positioned at the premium end of the athletic market. Across core women's
apparel categories, pricing generally aligns with or exceeds premium clothing leaders such as
Lululemon and Alo Yoga, while remaining meaningfully above Hoka. The premium is most pronounced
in technical categories -- including leggings, outerwear and performance bottoms -- where product
innovation and fabric technology appear to support higher price points (08/06/26)

4. China Premium Retail Strategy Aids Apparel Adoption
China represents one of On's most compelling long-term apparel opportunities, accounting for just
over one-fifth of the global sports apparel market, and could grow at a roughly 5.8% CAGR through
2030, based on Euromonitor forecasts. Unlike the US, where the opportunity is driven primarily by low
market share, China benefits from On's rapidly expanding premium retail footprint and growing brand
awareness among higher-end consumers.
On now operates 38 owned stores in Greater China, its largest owned retail market, and the region
continues to account for a significant share of new flagship openings. Larger-format stores in cities
including Chengdu, Shenzhen and Shanghai showcase the brand's complete head-to-toe offering in
upscale shopping destinations, helping establish On as a broader sportswear brand rather than simply
a performance-footwear company.
The strategy is already delivering results, with several flagship locations generating more than 20% of
sales from apparel, well above the company average. As On expands its flagship network, we expect
China to contribute a disproportionate share of apparel growth through 2030, supported by higher
penetration, a growing premium consumer base and continued retail expansion. (08/06/26)
