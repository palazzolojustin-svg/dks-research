Analyst Day Unlikely To Change Investor Sentiment,
But L-T Bull Thesis Remains
OUR MAIN TAKEAWAY
Although we remain long-term bullish here, we don't view the Analyst Day on 9/22 as a positive
catalyst for shares. The external environment has become far more challenging, which is
impacting athletic industry fundamentals and hurting investor sentiment to an even greater
degree. So, while ONON will likely give a 3-year growth algo, investors will likely be skeptical of
overly-bullish targets and overly-conservative targets could stoke fears about slowing growth.
We believe the most likely scenario is a 3-year revenue target CAGR in the low-teens, with
50bps of annual margin. That said, we believe ONON has done the smart thing by reining in
wholesale sell-in and avoiding the discounting that has become pervasive in the athletic space,
protecting the brand's long-term health. But shares could languish near-term before regaining
momentum.
Operating Environment Is Tough, But The Brand's Long-Term Health Has Been Protected. In Q2,
ONON lowered constant-FX revenue guidance for the first time ever, to low-20's growth (prior: at least
23%). Amid an increasingly promotional environment for athletic footwear, sell-through trends have
slowed as consumers have taken advantage of reduced prices at other brands. However, we believe
that ONON's decision to maintain pricing integrity will benefit the brand in the long run, and we're
encouraged by the continued strength in the DTC channel and strong social media engagement. We're
also extremely encouraged by gross margin, which was up almost 400bps YoY despite incremental
tariffs (a nearly-200bps headwind) and the highly promotional environment for athletic footwear.
Tough To Envision The Financial Algo Allaying Investor Fears. Unfortunately, the timing of
the event isn't ideal, as it comes on the heels of ONON's first-ever top-line guidance cut and
cautious commentary from other athletic-industry players. Thus, management may choose to be more
conservative with their targets than they would have been 6-12 months ago (stoking fears about
slowing growth). Plus, given the external environment, investors would likely remain skeptical of an
overly bullish guide. Thus, we think ONON is caught between a rock and a hard place as we head into
this event.
Analyst Days Haven't Usually Been The Catalyst That Softlines Execs Hope For. While Analyst
Days have been helpful to provide investors and analysts with details around companies' strategies
and financial aims, they haven't usually been near-term catalysts for the stocks. Among the stocks in
our coverage, there have been 25 Analyst Days in the past 10 years, and the average 1-day, 1-week and
1-month returns have all lagged the SPX.
Slight Model Tweak, No Change To Annual EPS. We've lowered our Q3 EPS by 0.02, and raised Q4 by
0.03. We believe that the wholesale pressure will be more Q3-skewed, whereas we previously assumed
both quarters would be impacted equally.

FACING SOME NEAR-TERM CHALLENGES, BUT DOING THE RIGHT THINGS FOR THE
LONG-TERM
On the Q2 earnings print, ONON lowered constant-FX revenue guidance for the first time in its 5-year history as a public company, with a
very slight reduction to low-20's growth (vs. "at least 23% growth" prior). The only other "cut" was in 2Q24, where they reiterated constant-
FX growth but lowered reported revenues due to FX, so the fact that they reduced constant-FX guidance was jarring to investors. As a
result, ONON shares have been under heavy pressure (down more than 30% since the print vs. SPX -2%) and short interest now exceeds
10% of the float (whereas it was below 5% of the float 5 months ago).
Figure 1 - ONON's Short Interest Has More Than Doubled Since April
Source: FactSet and Needham research
The guidance cut was attributed to slower growth in the U.S. wholesale channel. Amid an increasingly promotional environment for
athletic footwear, sell-through trends for some of ONON's "everyday" styles have slowed, as consumers have taken advantage of reduced
prices at other brands. This aligns with commentary from mall-based wholesale accounts, who have become increasingly cautious lately.
For example, DKS reported a disappointing comp decline of almost 4% at their Foot Locker segment (vs. a slight increase in Q1) and they
lowered FY Foot Locker comp guidance to a range of flat-to-down-2% vs. an original range of up-1%-to-3%. Additionally, they lowered FY
EBIT margin guidance by ~100bps (including a 50bps reduction at the core Dick's banner and larger cut at Foot Locker), attributed to the
highly promotional environment, which is expected to cause gross margin to decline at both segment. Furthermore, JD Sports reported
Q2 like-for-like sales in North America declined almost 7%, which was a significant deterioration vs. Q1's less-than-1% decline.
We believe this promotional pressure is largely a function of Nike having too much inventory in the channel. In late-CY25, NKE made the
unwise decision to ship a lot of product into the North American wholesale channel, which appears to be backfiring. NKE has had four
consecutive quarters of double-digit sell-in growth in North America (+14% YoY over the LTM), and the November 2025 quarter was the
largest period of domestic wholesale sell-in during the company's long history. Essentially, over the LTM, NKE has shipped in $1.5 billion
more wholesale product YoY, and when grossing that up to retail sales, it's about $3 billion of incremental product at retailers.

Simply put, NKE does not have the brand heat necessary to support this elevated level of sell-in, and as a result retailers have needed to
aggressively discount the Nike brand in order to clear their excess inventory. We also believe that some other large brands have slowed
(Adidas, New Balance), necessitating discounts to move product for back-to-school. This seems to have "crowded out" demand for smaller
brands such as On.
Figure 3 - Sneaker Retailers Such Have Foot Locker Have Been Very Promotional
Source: Footlocker.com, captured August 2026
However, we do believe that underlying indicators of demand for On remain intact. Furthermore, we believe that ONON's decision to
maintain pricing integrity will benefit the brand in the long run. First, in terms of demand, we are encouraged by the continued strength in
the DTC channel. While the rate of growth at wholesale got cut nearly in half (from greater-than-25% growth in Q1 to less than 13% growth
in Q2), DTC actually accelerated from 29% growth in Q1 to 34% in Q2. And while North America wholesale is the biggest pressure point at
the moment, management spoke to accelerating DTC demand in North America. Thus, as brand awareness and brand loyalty continue
to rise, more customers are going directly to ONON's stores and website. And though management did not explicitly guide DTC sales for
2H, they did emphasize that the guidance cut was driven entirely by wholesale, and they expect DTC to remain strong through year-end.
We believe that the DTC channel is more reflective of "brand heat" than the wholesale channel, so we would be more concerned if the
performance by channel were reversed (i.e. wholesale vastly outpacing DTC). We also think management's decision to restrict sell-in to
the North America wholesale channel is long-term positive, as it's difficult to wean consumers off of discounts (as brands such as Nike
and Under Armour are learning). So, they are sacrificing some sales volume in the near-term in order to maintain pricing integrity for
the long-term.

Another reason why we're encouraged by ONON's current brand heat is the continued growth of their social media following. ONON
currently has 3.7 million Instagram followers, which is a 28% increase vs. September 2025 (2.9 million followers). No other brand we cover
is anywhere close to that growth rate - the only other brand in our coverage that's seen a +DD increase in Instagram followers is DECK's
UGG brand, at +14% (half the rate of growth of ONON). We believe this is reflective of rising brand awareness and a customer count
that continues to grow strongly. This potentially supports continued DTC strength and a re-inflection in the wholesale channel when the
market is cleaner (perhaps after the Holidays).

ANALYST DAYS HAVEN'T USUALLY BEEN THE CATALYST THAT SOFTLINES EXECS HOPE
FOR
While Analyst Days have been helpful to provide investors and analysts with details around companies' strategies and financial aims, they
haven't usually been near-term catalysts for the stocks. Among the stocks in our coverage, there have been 25 Analyst Days in the past 10
years, and 15 times the stock has been down on the day of the event (-1% on average vs. SPX flat). It gets even worse as the distance from
the event lengthens, as the average stock declined 3% after a week and almost 7% a month after the event (lagging the average returns
of the SPX by 260bps and 500bps respectively). It's not until we reach 90 days post-Analyst Day that we actually see any signs of relative
outperformance, (up 3% on average vs. SPX +2%).

VALUATION (PRICE TARGET: $37.00)

POTENTIAL UPSIDE DRIVERS
Potential upside drivers include:
■
The company could gain market share and wholesale shelf space faster than we expect, given the momentum at the brand.
■
The company has major growth opportunities in Asia and apparel, potentially presenting long runways for growth.
■
If ONON can continue generating strong full-price sell-throughs, margins could exceed our expectations given the possible leverage
on sales growth.
RISKS TO TARGET
ONON
Key risks to our bullish thesis on this stock include:
■
The company operates in a highly-competitive athletic footwear market, where trends can change very rapidly.
■
If the company fails to generate compelling product innovations, consumer interest could wane and growth could slow.
■
The company's margins are among the best in the industry due to their ability to sell predominantly at full price. If the promotional
retail environment starts to affect them, they may need to discount more, driving potential margin degradation.
