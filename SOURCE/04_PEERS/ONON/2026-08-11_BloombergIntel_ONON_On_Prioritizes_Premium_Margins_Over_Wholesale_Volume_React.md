1. On's Long-Term Potential Intact, Demand Robust: Equity Outlook
THESIS: On Holding is still in the early stages of penetrating most of its athletic-shoe and apparel
markets and product categories, leaving ample room to build awareness and share as it expands into
sports beyond running. Growth is fueled by innovation in running, a wider apparel reach and direct-
to-consumer mix. New-store openings and accelerating international demand, particularly in Asia-
Pacific, reinforce its global runway. A higher DTC sales mix, robust new-product pipeline and greater
economies of scale are key to driving market-share gains and maximizing margin. (08/11/26)

2. On’s Wholesale Reset Puts US Demand at Fore: Earnings Outlook
Post-2Q Earnings Outlook: On's sales growth may slow in 3Q before improving in 4Q as the company
deliberately reins in wholesale sell-in, particularly in the Americas, to protect full-price positioning
and clear the channel ahead of a major running-product refresh. In 2Q, FX-neutral sales rose 21.6%,
buoyed by 34.3% DTC growth, while wholesale slowed to 12.7% amid heavy promotions across brands
and softer sell-through in some everyday-running franchises in the Americas. With new products
coming and a broader refresh of all everyday-running franchises over the next 14 months, we'll be
watching US wholesale trends closely for evidence that the softness is transitory and underlying
demand remains intact.
Gross margin could be a key offset to slower near-term sales growth after expanding 390 bps to 65.4%
in 2Q, driven by higher DTC mix, full-price execution and operating efficiencies despite higher US
tariffs. (08/11/26)

Key Topics
2026 Targets In Reach
On's Target of Doubling Revenue by 2026 Appears Within Reach
On looks set to reach its target of 3.55 billion Swiss francs in sales by the end of 2026, even amid
foreign-exchange headwinds. We see ample runway for low-20% annual growth, given On's 2.9% share
of the global sports footwear market and even smaller share of sportswear. Gains will come from
direct-to-consumer momentum, disciplined retail expansion, persistent wholesale demand,
underpenetrated markets like China and product innovation. (03/31/26)
3. Sharper Direct-to-Consumer Focus, Brand Awareness Key to Gains
On could reach its midterm sales goal of 3.55 billion Swiss francs by 2026, our scenario analysis
suggests, as strong brand momentum, rising direct-to-consumer penetration and broad global
demand keep supporting outsize growth. Along with DTC, China and apparel remain key drivers, while
increasing productivity in retail accelerates awareness and conversion. Gains across channels and
regions could support annual sales growth in the low-20% range, though that pace could moderate as
scale increases. Strong DTC momentum -- with penetration reaching over 40% -- and disciplined
wholesale expansion suggest a more balanced and resilient growth model.
Continued investment in innovation, retail expansion and infrastructure should support long-term
growth, while reinforcing premium positioning and full-price sales. (03/31/26)

5. China Represents Largest Growth Potential
Geographically, China is On's biggest opportunity, similar to many athleisure peers given the country's
growing middle class, rising interest in sports and greater spending power among its shoppers.
Expanding its footprint in Mainland China through new store openings and larger formats, coupled
with scaling up its omnichannel presence with major digital platforms to reach a broader base of
consumers, should help it capture share. Since On opened its first store in 2019, sales in China have
been growing at a triple-digit pace, inching closer to its aspiration of 10% of sales beyond 2026.
Asia Pacific represents 17% of sales, suggesting ample runway to drive gains. Still, the Americas will
remain its largest region, and though EMEA is its most mature, On is still underpenetrated in key
markets such as France, Spain and Italy. (05/13/26)

6. Direct-to-Consumer Sales to Outpace Wholesale Growth
On's plan to at least triple its store footprint by 2026, coupled with an increased focus on expanding
its e-commerce presence, could see direct-to-consumer sales grow at a 31% compound annual rate
over three years -- key to doubling sales. Stores will play a bigger role in driving brand awareness,
especially in markets with a limited number of premium and specialty partners. Still, even as On's
store count triples to 90, online will be the majority of DTC sales.
Brand momentum for footwear and apparel continues to convert into high sales growth across all
channels, allowing the company greater control of wholesale growth while shifting demand to DTC. As
a result, wholesale gains appear poised to moderate -- but may remain solid -- with our scenario
analysis suggesting a 22% CAGR. (03/17/26)

DTC Key Growth Driver
Store Expansion, Digital Strength to Fuel On's DTC Acceleration
Increasing direct-to-consumer gains will be key to doubling On's sales by year-end 2026, aided by
accelerated store openings. Meantime, greater brand awareness and store proximity will boost
customer loyalty, creating a "digital halo effect." Controlling wholesale growth should maintain On's
premium brand equity and convert momentum into DTC demand. (03/13/26)

7. Retail Stores Act as Brand Amplifier
On's retail stores will play a larger role in its direct-to-consumer strategy as it accelerates the pace of
openings to fuel greater brand awareness and reach while driving cross-shopping with its other
channels. Management has noted that after opening a new store, e-commerce traffic can be 3x
higher, and wholesale partners in the market also see an uplift. Accelerating store openings to 20-25
a year could help push retail store sales penetration to 10% in the long term and will be driven largely
by new retail formats as On shifts beyond flagship locations.
E-commerce may continue to outpace On's overall growth as the company increases investments in
its digital platforms to improve customer experience. Expanding distribution capacity to meet rising
demand from greater brand momentum is also key to omnichannel expansion. (03/10/26)
On's Owned Store Count, Retail Format Strategy
8. Wholesale Growth to Be More Controlled
Wholesale will remain key to expanding On's reach and brand awareness, while growth could ease as
the brand focuses on increasing efficiency with existing partners through a more controlled
approach. Having secured most major strategic accounts in core markets, growth should increasingly
come from adding shelf space and market share within key partners such as Dick's, JD and Foot
Locker. With products in just 40-50% of key accounts' locations, On has ample runway to continue its
mid-single-digit door expansion annually.
As brand awareness rises, same-store gains and improved assortments stand to drive the majority of
wholesale growth in the mid-to-high teens, supporting both expansion and premium positioning.
(03/13/26)

Sports, Product Expansion
On's Shift to Sportswear From Footwear Brand Broadens Reach
On's pivot to a sportswear from a footwear name is helping to create more complete looks through
newer apparel offerings, while expansion beyond running should widen its consumer reach. Entry into
tennis, training and lifestyle opens a larger addressable market, though running will remain a key
revenue driver as On continues to gain share in the category. (03/17/26)
9. On Makes Strides in Competitive Running
Even as On expands into new sports, running will remain a core growth driver, given the category
dominates the athletic-footwear market and continues to become more mainstream globally,
presenting an opportunity to gain share. Top sneaker brands including Nike, Adidas and Puma have
been in a race to create the fastest shoe for leading marathon runners while gaining broader appeal
in the running community, but have struggled to gain loyalty among everyday runners. However, On
has successfully become a top three running brand in most of its main markets and with key retail
partners over the past five years.
On appears poised to capture more share, aided by major franchise refreshes this year. In the US
sports footwear market, On's share has risen to 6.3% from just 1.2% in 2020. (03/13/26)

10. On Looks to Tennis for Versatile Styles
On's partnership with retired tennis champion Roger Federer has helped the brand gain credibility in
the sport, though it's still in the early stages of building meaningful scale. Rather than assembling a
broad athlete roster, On has focused on a small number of high-impact partnerships with rising stars,
such as Iga Świątek and Ben Shelton, using major tournaments to build brand heat as both wear its
head-to-toe on-court collection. Alongside On's multiyear partnership with actress Zendaya, these
endorsements should keep raising visibility and drive engagement with younger consumers.
Tennis is also reinforcing On’s positioning as an all-day brand, with athlete-endorsed court styles
increasingly translating into lifestyle demand -- particularly for the Roger franchise. (03/17/26)
