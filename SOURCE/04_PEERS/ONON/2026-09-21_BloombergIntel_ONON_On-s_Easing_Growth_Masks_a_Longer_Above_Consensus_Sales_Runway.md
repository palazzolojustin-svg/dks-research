On’s Easing Growth Masks a Longer, Above-Consensus Sales Runway
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- On can sustain roughly 20% annual sales gains over the next three years,
ahead of consensus for midteens gains, as its expanding businesses offset maturation in the core.
Apparel, owned retail and international markets are underpenetrated, while the move into soccer
with Kylian Mbappé opens another sizable opportunity. Strong margins and cash generation give On
capacity to invest behind these initiatives, suggesting recent growth moderation may understate the
brand's longer-term runway. (09/21/26)
1. On Can Sustain 20% Sales Growth, Ahead of Street
On appears poised to extend roughly 20% annual sales gains over its next long-term planning period,
in line with the low end of its prior 20-25% ambition and ahead of consensus for midteens increases
through 2030. Though growth is moderating as On scales up, we believe the slowdown reflects a
substantially larger revenue base rather than a lack of runway, with multiple drivers still early in their
development. On's sales CAGR from 2023 through our 2026 calculation is roughly 25%, broadly
matching the 26% target set at its last investor day despite recent softness in US wholesale.
Sustaining 20% becomes a higher bar as the business grows, making the upper end of On's prior 20-
25% ambition less likely, we believe. Still, further expansion in apparel, owned retail, international
markets and newer sports, including soccer, can support gains near the lower end. A 20% target would
imply meaningful upside to current longer-term sales estimates while remaining consistent with the
framework On laid out in 2023. (09/21/26)

2. Soccer Adds a New Engine to On's Existing Playbook
On's move into soccer stands to become a meaningful new driver as the brand expands beyond
running into a roughly $95 billion global soccer-sportswear market, based on IMARC estimates. Even
modest penetration matters: In a scenario where On captures 0.5% of the market, annual sales would
approach $500 million, illustrating the scale of the opportunity as footwear, apparel and lifestyle
products begin rolling out in 2027.

3. Apparel Could Quadruple as On Builds Beyond Footwear
On's apparel sales can grow about 36% annually through 2030 to nearly CHF800 million, our analysis
suggests, as the brand builds apparel into a stand-alone growth engine beyond footwear. Expansion
into training, tennis, lifestyle and soccer, along with women's product, technical innovation and
fashion partnerships, should broaden the occasions and consumers On can address. The global
sports-apparel market could reach $304 billion by 2030, according to Euromonitor, yet On holds only
about a 0.1% share.
The US offers the largest whitespace, accounting for roughly 45% of global sports-apparel sales, while
On’s share is around 0.1%. Our analysis suggests share can rise toward 0.3% by 2030 as premium store
expansion and head-to-toe assortments increase apparel attachment and cross-selling. (09/21/26)

4. Retail, International Expansion Can Extend On's Runway
On's expanding retail footprint and still-low penetration across major international markets can
support durable growth as its core US business matures. Stores have become an increasingly
important sales channel, with the company on track to more than double its footprint since 2023 to
about 86 locations. Still, that's well below Nike's roughly 990 and Adidas' 886 full-price sites, leaving
room for the channel to reach 10% of On's sales over time.
