Margin Beat Amidst Macro Pressure 
Solid Margin Beat Supported by Premium Positioning 
On Holding AG delivered a strong Q1’26, with record net sales of CHF 832 
million (2% above estimates) and accelerated profitability, underscoring the 
company’s premium positioning and disciplined execution. Revenue grew 
14.5% y/y (26.4% constant currency), marking the first quarter above CHF 
800 million. Mgmt. highlighted during the conference call that innovation 
remains appealing to customers. The Cloudmonster 3, powered by 
LightSpray technology, is selling several hundred pairs daily through direct-
to-consumer (DTC) channels and is outperforming its previous version across 
all regions. Younger consumers in the U.S., especially females, are 
increasingly joining the brand, with the 18–24 demographic experiencing the 
largest-ever rise in DTC penetration—a trend that is continuing into Q2 2026. 
Back to earnings, adj. EPS of CHF 0.37 exceeded by ~37% while adj. 
EBITDA reached CHF 174 million (+45.4% y/y), ~22% above estimates, with 
gross margin expanding 450 bps y/y to 64.2%, comfortably beating estimates 
at 61.7%. Notably, management now frames ~64.5% as a new baseline, 
despite tariff headwinds. Additionally, FY gross margin guidance was raised 
by 150bps (~170 bps above vs. 2025), driven by premium positioning, 
minimum discounting, as well as supply chain efficiency. 
Mixed Channel Results 
The Wholesale channel grew revenue by 13% y/y to CHF 510 million, 2% 
ahead, while(DTC) grew above the growth rate of Wholesale at 16.4% y/y but 
came in below estimates.   
 
Investment Opinion: Buy

Region-wise, Asia-Pacific was the standout, with revenue of CHF 174.0 million rising 44.4% y/y (61.4% constant currency), now 
accounting for over 20% of total sales. Growth was broad-based across countries, with particularly strong momentum in Greater China and 
South Korea, reflecting the early penetration phase, allowing for outsized growth vs. more mature markets. In comparison, EMEA delivered 
CHF 207 million (+22.8% y/y), supported by broad-based demand, while the Americas generated CHF 451 million (+3.1% reported, 17.1% 
constant currency), reflecting FX headwinds but continued underlying strength.  
Apparels Business Accelerates 
On’s Apparel business continues to scale rapidly and is evolving into a meaningful second growth pillar beyond footwear. Revenue 
reached CHF 55 million, growing 45% y/y (57.5% constant currency), broadly in line with expectations and significantly outpacing the 
company average. Apparel growth was particularly strong in DTC, contributing over 10% of DTC sales for the first time. In our view, 
apparel is helping ONON penetrate younger and more lifestyle-oriented consumers, with increased share among the 18–24 age cohort. 
Valuation  
We anticipate a solid revision to ONON’s 2026 and 2027 earnings estimates, which could drive a rebound in shares trading at a price-to-
sales ratio of 3.1x, significantly below its 5-year average. at 7.8x, still higher than 1.01x price-to-sales for the MSCI ACWI ex-US Consumer 
Discretionary index. Given the firm’s higher growth trajectory and higher margin profile, we expect ONON shares to trade at a premium to 
slow-growing peers in the footwear/athleisure Industry. In our view, the unique business model, which caters to premium and performance 
categories of buyers, sets it apart from the competition. 
Investment Thesis  
In our view, On Holding AG presents a compelling investment opportunity as a premium global sportswear brand demonstrating robust 
growth, innovation, and strategic execution. We believe the brand awareness has risen materially but remains far from saturated, creating 
a long runway for new customer acquisition while existing customers deepen engagement through higher basket sizes, repeat purchases, 
and multi-category adoption. Importantly, this demand has been captured largely at full price, underscoring pricing power and validating 
management’s emphasis on disciplined distribution rather than volume maximization. Margin trajectory is another pillar of the thesis. In our 
view, ONON has demonstrated that premium positioning translates into structurally higher gross margins, not only through price mix but 
also via operational efficiencies that scale with growth. The guided EBITDA range for 2026 suggests that incremental growth is accretive, 
not dilutive, and that reinvestment is increasingly funded by internal cash generation rather than margin sacrifice.  
Beyond footwear, we believe category expansion represents a meaningful and underappreciated growth lever. Apparel and accessories 
are scaling faster than the core business and offer attractive margins due to their high penetration in direct-to-consumer channels. 
Furthermore, we believe innovation remains a catalyst rather than a cost center. Breakthroughs such as LightSpray manufacturing and 
ongoing advancements in materials and design differentiate ONON in a crowded athletic market and extend its technological moat.  
Taken together, our investment thesis reflects a company that is scaling demand without compromising discipline, expanding margins 
while investing for growth, and broadening its opportunity set across categories, channels, and geographies in a way that generates value 
over time.

The Ameriprise Quantitative Risk Profile (QRP) assigns a risk 
level for an individual stock that ranges from Low to High. The 
QRP is based on four factors: Beta, Correlation, Standard 
Deviation, and Maximum Drawdown, as measured over the past 
52 weeks. Two factors, Beta and Correlation, are relative to the 
S&P 500 Index, while Standard Deviation and Maximum 
Drawdown are stock-specific. The four factors are equally 
weighted with their ranking displayed on the color-coded graphic.  
Stocks with a higher combined score reflect a higher risk profile. 
Therefore, investors should expect greater volatility relative to the 
S&P 500 Index, higher share price volatility, and a greater 
potential maximum drawdown. For a detailed definition of the four

The Analyst Risk Profile (ARP) is based on the analyst’s knowledge of the company and its sector, including competitive dynamics and 
end markets, capital structure, management team, capital allocation decisions, future revenue and earnings expectations, and other 
factors that are reflected in a color-coded risk profile that ranges from Low to High. The analysts’ assessment of these and other factors 
may produce an Analyst Risk Profile that may differ from the Quantitative Risk Profile.  
In addition to the general risks of owning equities, On Holdings faces risks related to supply chain disruption, geopolitical risks, and 
currency risks (reports in the Swiss franc, but net sales are recognized in US dollar in the US). As most of the products are sourced from 
emerging markets, a possible rise in tariffs in the US could lower consumer spending on shoes and apparel. Additional risk factors include 
the rapidly changing footwear industry, failure to innovate and expand to other markets, unfavorable changes in labor laws in Vietnam, 
potential margin compression in case of a weaker retail environment, excess inventory due to incorrect demand forecast, etc.  
Company Profile 
On Holdings AG, founded in 2010 and based in Zurich, Switzerland, is a premium global sportswear brand known for its focus on 
innovation, design, and sustainability. The company operates worldwide, with a significant presence in the US, EMEA (Europe, the Middle 
East, and East Africa), Australia, and China. Its product range includes shoes, apparel, and accessories tailored for specific athletic 
activities, as well as casual and leisure use. On Holdings has developed several proprietary footwear technologies, including CloudTec, 
CloudTec Phase, Helion superfoam, and Speedboard. While the company designs shoes in Switzerland, most manufacturing takes place 
in Vietnam (90%) and Indonesia (10%). The products are sold through wholesale partnerships with selected retailers, own retail stores, 
and direct-to-consumer (DTC) through digital channels. In 2021, On Holdings AG became a publicly traded company on the New York 
Stock Exchange (NYSE).

Quantitative Risk Profile (QRP) Factor Definitions:  
Beta: Beta measures a security or portfolio's volatility relative to an index, in this case, S&P 500 plus non-overlapping Recommended list 
stocks. If a security has a beta coefficient greater than one, it is considered more volatile. If a security has a beta coefficient of less than 
one, its price can be expected to rise and fall more slowly. 
Correlation: Correlation is generally interpreted as the percentage of a stock's price movement that can be explained by movements in a 
benchmark, in this case, S&P 500 plus non-overlapping Recommended list stocks. 
Standard Deviation (of price): Standard Deviation is a statistic that measures the dispersion of a set of data relative to its mean. When 
the data points are further away from the mean, there is a greater deviation from the mean and a higher standard deviation. 
Maximum Drawdown: Maximum drawdown is the maximum observed loss from a peak to a trough of a portfolio in a given time period.
