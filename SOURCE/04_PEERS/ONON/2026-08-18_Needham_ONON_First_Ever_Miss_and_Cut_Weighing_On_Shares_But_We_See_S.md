First-Ever Miss-and-Cut Weighing On Shares, But
We See Silver Linings
OUR MAIN TAKEAWAY
ONON is getting hit hard (down 19% as of this writing, in a flat market) after missing consensus
Q2 revenue and cutting FY guidance. However, we think that underlying trends remain strong,
as the top-line weakness is driven by wholesale sell-in (DTC remains strong) and gross margin
trends remain stellar. While lowering numbers is less than ideal, we think there are silver
linings here, and we'd buy the pullback. We're tweaking our FY26/27 EPS forecasts slightly
(now 1.24/1.50 from 1.22/1.52), and our PT moves down to $37 (from $45). For Q2 itself,
revenue growth of 22% was below Street +25% (all references to revenue growth in this report
are constant-FX), the FY outlook was lowered to low-20s revenue growth (vs. at least 23%
previously), though the adjusted EBITDA margin guidance of 19.5%-20.0% was reiterated.
TOM'S TAKEAWAYS
Tom’s Takeaway #1: Wholesale Caution Clouds Continued Strength in DTC. DTC was up 34% in
Q2, an acceleration over the prior 2 quarters, with management noting that momentum in the channel
remains "highly encouraging". However, the wholesale business was only up 13% in Q2, a significant
slowdown from 25% growth in Q1. While the company had previously alluded to slower wholesale
growth in the 2H on the last call (Q1 print 5/12), citing the need for a clean marketplace ahead of
several new product launches in early 2027 (new additions to CloudSurfer and CloudFlow franchises,
along with additional scaling of LightSpray), it appears that they begun to reduce sell-in earlier than
initially expected (hence the Q2 slowdown) and will be doing so at a larger magnitude than they
had expected (with Q3 more impacted than Q4). Management noted that the marketplace has been
promotional, particularly in the Americas region, leading to some softer sell-outs in some everyday
running franchises, Thus, while the underlying DTC strength remains encouraging, the deliberate
slowdown in wholesale is driving the lowered top line outlook, which understandably has investors
frustrated.
Tom’s Takeaway #2: EMEA, Asia Business Remains Solid. Management noted that outside of the
Americas region, they continue to see strong sell-out rates in the wholesale channel. EMEA was up
21% in Q2 (vs. 26% in Q1) and appears to be outpacing peers in the region. Asia was also solid at 55%
growth in Q2 (vs. 61% in Q1), led by strength in China and Japan.
Tom’s Takeaway #3: Margin Outlook Remains Strong. Gross margin expanded 390bps YoY, well
above consensus +300bps, driven by strong full-price selling, higher DTC mix, and lower freight. This
offset the headwind from tariffs (which was not quantified). Thus, underlying margin trends remain
very strong. For FY26, the company now expects gross margin of at least 65% (up from previous
expectation of 64.5%-65.0%), representing growth of over 220bps YoY (with potential for additional
upside as tariff refunds are contemplated in guidance).

At a Glance
OUR INVESTMENT THESIS
ONON has been one of the strongest fundamental names in our coverage, with industry-leading top-line growth and healthy margin
expansion. We believe the company has a continued runway for strong growth, as they grow brand awareness and gain shelf space with
the biggest and best sneaker retailers in the world.
BULL CASE ASSUMPTIONS
2H26 revenues could come in better than
expected, given rising brand awareness
and strong DTC trends. If so, FY26
revenues could grow low-to-mid 20's
constant-FX. If revenues were to grow
~20% next year as well, we think the stock
could exceed $50.
BASE CASE ASSUMPTIONS
ONON has been one of the strongest
growth stories in our coverage since going
public in 2021, and we expect growth
to remain strong for years to come. We
expect revenues to grow 20-25% constant-
FX in FY26 followed by 15%-20% growth
in FY27. Additionally, we expect ~50bps of
adjusted EBITDA margin expansion each
year. Our 12-month price target of $37 is
based on 20x FY27E EPS of 1.50 CHF, or ~
$1.85 when converting the EPS forecast to
USD (note that the share price is in USD,
but the P&L is in CHF).
BEAR CASE ASSUMPTIONS
The biggest potential bear case would
stem from the brand "hitting a wall" in
their growth trajectory. If this were to
occur, then the downside potential in the
shares would be profound, given how lofty
the valuation multiples are. If growth were
to slow to sub-20% in FY26 and FY27 could
slow to LDD. Margins could be protected,
as the company could slow the pace of
investment, but they probably wouldn't
see any margin expansion beyond 2026.
If this were the case, then FY26 adjusted
EBITDA would be ~100 million CHF below
our forecast. In this scenario, we think the
stock could fall to the mid-$20's.
COMPANY DESCRIPTION
ONON is a global athletic footwear brand, headquartered in Switzerland but generating a majority of revenues in North America.

VALUATION (PRICE TARGET: $37.00)
Our 12-month price target of $37 is based on 20x FY27E EPS of 1.50 CHF, or ~$1.85 when converting the EPS forecast to USD (note that
the share price is in USD, but the P&L is in CHF).
POTENTIAL UPSIDE DRIVERS
Potential upside drivers include:
■
The company could gain market share and wholesale shelf space faster than we expect, given the momentum at the brand.
■
The company has major growth opportunities in Asia and apparel, potentially presenting long runways for growth.
■
If ONON can continue generating strong full-price sell-throughs, margins could exceed our expectations given the possible leverage
on sales growth.
RISKS TO TARGET
ONON
Key risks to our bullish thesis on this stock include:
■
The shares garner the highest valuation multiples in our coverage (P/E, EV/EBITDA), presenting significant risk of multiple
contraction if investor sentiment wanes.
■
The company operates in a highly-competitive athletic footwear market, where trends can change very rapidly.
■
If the company fails to generate compelling product innovations, consumer interest could wane and growth could slow.
■
The company's margins are among the best in the industry due to their ability to sell predominantly at full price. If the promotional
retail environment starts to affect them, they may need to discount more, driving potential margin degradation.
