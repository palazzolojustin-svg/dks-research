On Has More Room to Run as Growing Scale Benefits Lift Profit
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- On's high-teens sales increases should extend through 2029 as low
market share, rising brand awareness and geographic whitespace support gains off a larger base.
Faster growth in EMEA and Asia-Pacific should broaden its regional mix, while running, sneaker and
apparel drive most of the more than CHF2 billion in planned incremental sales. Earnings should
compound faster as scale and automation lift adjusted Ebitda margin to at least 22%, while stronger
cash generation makes the model increasingly self-funding and supports continued investment
alongside On's first $1 billion share-buyback authorization. (10/01/26)
1. Core Categories Anchor On's 2029 Growth Plan
On's target to increase sales at a high-teens compound annual rate through 2029 appears achievable
as it relies mainly on established businesses, reducing the need for newer sports to drive near-term
gains. Running, sneaker/lifestyle and apparel are expected to generate more than 75% of the
absolute sales increase through 2029, with apparel targeted to triple and running remaining On's
largest area of R&D investment. Training, tennis, outdoor and kids provide additional growth, while
football and golf contribute only modestly to the three-year plan. Limited soccer drops start in 2027
before standard cleats and performance all-day products launch in 2028, leaving newer sports as
potential upside and brand-awareness drivers rather than a requirement for On to reach at least
CHF5.6 billion in 2029 sales.
Channel growth should remain balanced, with direct-to-consumer targeted to reach 50% of sales by
2029 from roughly 45% in 2026, led by store expansion and stronger e-commerce, while wholesale
remains a major growth engine through deeper penetration and more shelf space with existing
partners. (10/01/26)

On is on track to exceed 10% of sales while expanding its premium retail footprint. In EMEA, high-teens

3. Brand Awareness, Low Share Leave Room to Outgrow Market
On's further expansion beyond running, with its entry into soccer and golf, coupled with an increased
focus on ramping up its newer apparel offering, significantly increases its addressable market and
provides ample opportunity to drive share gains and boost sales. Global brand awareness has climbed
from about 12% in 2023 to more than 30%, though it remains well below larger, more established
lifestyle sportswear peers, leaving room for management's more than 50% target. Broader exposure
to sneaker, apparel and new sports should extend On's reach beyond core runners, while athletes,
cultural partnerships and an expanding distribution footprint support awareness and customer
acquisition.
On has just a 1.3% share in global sportswear (footwear and apparel), as apparel remains in the early
stages. In footwear, though, On is gaining traction, rising to the No. 7 sports-footwear retailer in 2025
and widening its gap with Deckers' Hoka brand, which holds a 1.4% share. (10/01/26)

Distribution offers the clearest source of operating leverage. As a percentage of sales, distribution
costs are on track to be 10% in 2026, down from 13.4% in 2023, with management targeting at least

execution, rather than further pricing or mix gains, the key drivers of On's path to at least 22%.

5. Cash Generation Opens Door to Shareholder Returns
Rising free cash flow should allow On to increase capital returns while continuing to fund growth,
adding another support for earnings gains. Cash has more than doubled to about CHF1.2 billion from
CHF500 million in 2023, while the company has maintained a net-cash position despite rapid
expansion. Organic investment remains the priority, including roughly 100 additional stores through
2029 along with technology and infrastructure spending.
Strong Ebitda-to-free-cash-flow conversion is also generating cash beyond On’s reinvestment needs.
That supports its first share-repurchase authorization of up to $1 billion through 2029 while
maintaining net cash, suggesting the growth model is becoming increasingly self-funding as scale
improves. (10/01/26)
