On's Long-Term Potential Intact, Demand Robust: Equity Outlook
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- THESIS: On Holding is still in the early stages of penetrating most of its
athletic-shoe and apparel markets and product categories, leaving ample room to build awareness
and share as it expands into sports beyond running. Growth is fueled by innovation in running, a wider
apparel reach and direct-to-consumer mix. New-store openings and accelerating international
demand, particularly in Asia-Pacific, reinforce its global runway. A higher DTC sales mix, robust new-
product pipeline and greater economies of scale are key to driving market-share gains and
maximizing margin. (08/11/26)

Earnings
On’s Wholesale Reset Puts US Demand at Fore: Earnings Outlook
Post-2Q Earnings Outlook: On's sales growth may slow in 3Q before improving in 4Q as the company
deliberately reins in wholesale sell-in, particularly in the Americas, to protect full-price positioning
and clear the channel ahead of a major running-product refresh. In 2Q, FX-neutral sales rose 21.6%,
buoyed by 34.3% DTC growth, while wholesale slowed to 12.7% amid heavy promotions across brands
and softer sell-through in some everyday-running franchises in the Americas. With new products
coming and a broader refresh of all everyday-running franchises over the next 14 months, we'll be
watching US wholesale trends closely for evidence that the softness is transitory and underlying
demand remains intact.

Key Topics
2029 Goals Make Sense
On Has More Room to Run as Growing Scale Benefits Lift Profit
On's high-teens sales increases should extend through 2029 as low market share, rising brand
awareness and geographic whitespace support gains off a larger base. Faster growth in EMEA and
Asia-Pacific should broaden its regional mix, while running, sneaker and apparel drive most of the
more than CHF2 billion in planned incremental sales. Earnings should compound faster as scale and
automation lift adjusted Ebitda margin to at least 22%, while stronger cash generation makes the
model increasingly self-funding and supports continued investment alongside On's first $1 billion
share-buyback authorization. (10/01/26)
Core Categories Anchor On's 2029 Growth Plan
On's target to increase sales at a high-teens compound annual rate through 2029 appears achievable
as it relies mainly on established businesses, reducing the need for newer sports to drive near-term
gains. Running, sneaker/lifestyle and apparel are expected to generate more than 75% of the absolute
sales increase through 2029, with apparel targeted to triple and running remaining On's largest area
of R&D investment. Training, tennis, outdoor and kids provide additional growth, while football and golf
contribute only modestly to the three-year plan. Limited soccer drops start in 2027 before standard
cleats and performance all-day products launch in 2028, leaving newer sports as potential upside and
brand-awareness drivers rather than a requirement for On to reach at least CHF5.6 billion in 2029
sales.
Channel growth should remain balanced, with direct-to-consumer targeted to reach 50% of sales by
2029 from roughly 45% in 2026, led by store expansion and stronger e-commerce, while wholesale
remains a major growth engine through deeper penetration and more shelf space with existing
partners. (10/01/26)

On Sales Growth
International Growth Can Diversify On's Regional Mix
On's geographic mix should become increasingly diversified through 2029 as faster expansion in EMEA
and Asia-Pacific reduces reliance on the Americas and supports management's high-teens
companywide sales target. Asia-Pacific should remain the fastest-growing region, led by China, where
On is on track to exceed 10% of sales while expanding its premium retail footprint. In EMEA, high-teens
to low-20s annual growth over the next three years should come mainly from closing penetration gaps
within existing markets rather than entering new ones. On's share in southern and eastern Europe,
Benelux and Scandinavia is only about half that in DACH and the UK, leaving room to deepen
distribution and awareness.
Cloudtilt’s rapid growth and On’s emergence as a Gen Z lifestyle brand in Europe should also broaden
demand beyond runners and support further sneaker and apparel penetration. The Americas should
remain a solid contributor as On deepens penetration across channels despite its larger base.

Operating Leverage on Path to Lift Ebitda Margin Above 22%
On is positioned to expand its adjusted Ebitda margin by more than 200 bps to reach management's
target of at least 22% by 2029 as selling, general and adminstrative expense leverage and automation
drive the next leg of profitability. Gross margin is expected to remain at 65% or better, supported by
premium pricing and full-price selling, with management planning to reinvest underlying efficiencies
into product innovation and quality rather than rely on further gross-margin expansion.
Distribution offers the clearest source of operating leverage. As a percentage of sales, distribution
costs are on track to be 10% in 2026, down from 13.4% in 2023, with management targeting at least
another 100 bps of improvement as Atlanta automation ramps up and two legacy hubs consolidate
into one automated facility. Marketing should also become more efficient as brand investment is
spread across a larger revenue base, while G&A can benefit from capabilities already built and greater
use of automation. Selling expenses will rise with store expansion, though retail profitability remains
above the company average.
That should allow adjusted Ebitda to grow more than 20% annually through 2029, faster than sales,
without requiring additional gross-margin upside. Maintaining a 65%-plus gross margin while
extracting greater productivity from distribution, marketing and overhead makes scale and execution,
rather than further pricing or mix gains, the key drivers of On's path to at least 22%. (10/01/26)

Cash Generation Opens Door to Shareholder Returns
Rising free cash flow should allow On to increase capital returns while continuing to fund growth,
adding another support for earnings gains. Cash has more than doubled to about CHF1.2 billion from
CHF500 million in 2023, while the company has maintained a net-cash position despite rapid
expansion. Organic investment remains the priority, including roughly 100 additional stores through
2029 along with technology and infrastructure spending.
Strong Ebitda-to-free-cash-flow conversion is also generating cash beyond On’s reinvestment needs.
That supports its first share-repurchase authorization of up to $1 billion through 2029 while
maintaining net cash, suggesting the growth model is becoming increasingly self-funding as scale
improves. (10/01/26)

On's Apparel Strategy Could Unlock Its Next Growth Leg
The global sports-apparel market represents On's largest long-term growth opportunity as it expands
beyond its footwear roots into a broader premium sportswear brand. The category is projected to
increase to nearly $304 billion by 2030 from roughly $247 billion today, according to Euromonitor,
supported by rising athletic participation, increased health and wellness spending and consumers
wearing performance apparel in more aspects of daily life. Unlike athletic footwear, where market
leadership is more concentrated, the top 10 sports-apparel brands account for just 32% of global
sales, leaving meaningful room for emerging premium names, like On, to gain share via product and
design innovation and differentiated positioning.
Though footwear remains On's largest business, apparel is increasingly becoming a strategic growth
pillar that expands the company's addressable market. Unlike the Deckers-owned Hoka brand where

On Apparel Sales Growth Scenario
On Could Triple US Apparel Share by 2030
The US represents On's largest apparel-growth opportunity, accounting for roughly 45% of the global
sports apparel market, yet the company still holds only about 0.1% market share, according to
Euromonitor, suggesting meaningful room for expansion. Though Hoka maintains a larger presence
with a 0.2% share, we believe On is better positioned to narrow the gap and surpass Hoka as it
expands its premium flagship network and strengthens brand awareness.
Owned retail remains central to increasing apparel penetration, giving On greater control over
merchandising, storytelling and the customer experience than wholesale channels alone. Despite the
size of the opportunity, both On and Hoka remain underpenetrated from a retail perspective, operating
just 14 and 17 US stores, respectively.
Unlike Hoka, where stores remain primarily footwear-centric, On merchandises apparel as a core part
of the shopping experience, with elevated visual displays and complete head-to-toe assortments that
more closely resemble premium athletic retailers such as Lululemon and Alo Yoga. This differentiated
retail model should support higher apparel attachment rates while broadening the brand's appeal
beyond core runners.
We believe On has a credible path to increasing its US sports-apparel market share from roughly 0.1%
today to about 0.3% by 2030, supported by continued flagship expansion and higher apparel
attachment rates in owned retail. That would represent more than CHF200 million of incremental
apparel sales over the next five years. (08/06/26)

China Premium Retail Strategy Aids Apparel Adoption
China represents one of On's most compelling long-term apparel opportunities, accounting for just
over one-fifth of the global sports apparel market, and could grow at a roughly 5.8% CAGR through
2030, based on Euromonitor forecasts. Unlike the US, where the opportunity is driven primarily by low
market share, China benefits from On's rapidly expanding premium retail footprint and growing brand
awareness among higher-end consumers.
On now operates 38 owned stores in Greater China, its largest owned retail market, and the region
continues to account for a significant share of new flagship openings. Larger-format stores in cities
including Chengdu, Shenzhen and Shanghai showcase the brand's complete head-to-toe offering in
upscale shopping destinations, helping establish On as a broader sportswear brand rather than simply
a performance-footwear company.
