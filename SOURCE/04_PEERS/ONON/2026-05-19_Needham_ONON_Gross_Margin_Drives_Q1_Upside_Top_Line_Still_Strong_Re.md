Gross Margin Drives Q1 Upside, Top-Line Still
Strong, Reiterate Buy
OUR CALL
We remain bullish following a solid Q1. Although ONON is no longer beating sales to the
extent they once did, they're seeing impressive upside on margins, while the overall level of
sales growth remains solid and broad-based. We're raising our FY26/27 estimates (FY26/27
EPS now 1.22/1.52 from 1.13/1.42), but we're lowering our price target, as we think the macro
environment warrants a more conservative target multiple. Our PY moves to $45 (low-mid 20's
P/E on FY27, translated to USD) from $52, which utilized a high-20's multiple. For the quarter,
revenue growth of 26% constant-FX was modestly above consensus +25%, and adjusted EBITDA
of 174 million CHF was well above the Street's 145 million CHF. FY revenue guidance was
reiterated at 23%+ growth constant-FX, though they raised adjusted EBITDA margin guidance
by 100bps to 19.5%-20.0%.
TOM'S TAKEAWAYS
Tom’s Takeaway #1: Asia Continues To Become A Bigger Contributor to Growth. Asia remains
the fastest-growing region, with 61% growth in Q1 (all references to revenue growth in this report are
constant-FX), outpacing solid growth from EMEA (+26%) and the Americas (+18%). As a result, Asia
accounted for over 50% of the growth in CHF, the highest percentage we've seen. Strength in the region
was pronounced in Korea (triple-digit growth) and China (growth above the region's average). That
said, on the larger sales base, the growth rate in Asia did slow from +85% in Q4.
Tom’s Takeaway #2: Strong Full-Price Sell-Through Rates. Gross margin expanded 430bps YoY,
well above consensus +150bps, despite the headwind from U.S. tariffs. Additionally, management
raised FY gross margin guidance by 150bps, to at least 64.5%, up at least 170bps vs. 2025 (100bps tariff
headwind offset by FX). This is due to the company's focus on premium, full-price selling, plus supply
chain efficiency.
Tom’s Takeaway #3: Encouraged By Developments With LightSpray. The highly-innovative
LightSpray product is getting closer to meaningful sales volume, as the new factory in Korea opened
in February, increasing production capacity by 30x. ONON is currently selling "several hundred" pairs
per day in the DTC channel, at elevated price points ($280-$330).
Tom's Takeaway #4: Apparel Continues To Gain Traction. While footwear growth was solid at
+24%, apparel was even stronger at +58%. With increasing awareness and acceptance of On as a "toe-
to-head" brand, apparel accounted for over 10% of DTC sales for the first time, and it's approaching
7% of sales consolidated.

At a Glance
OUR INVESTMENT THESIS
ONON has been one of the strongest fundamental names in our coverage, with industry-leading top-line growth and healthy margin
expansion. We believe the company has a continued runway for strong growth, as they grow brand awareness and gain shelf space with
the biggest and best sneaker retailers in the world.
BULL CASE ASSUMPTIONS
4Q25 could come in better than expected,
due to growing brand awareness, strong
product innovation, DTC expansion, and
new wholesale distribution. If revenues
were to grow 30%+ again, and FY26 were
to grow mid-to-high 20's constant-FX,
then FY26 revenues could potentially be
~500bps above our current forecast, with
upside to margins as well (FY27 adjusted
EBITDA could be 20% or higher). In this
scenario, we think the stock could exceed
$60.
BASE CASE ASSUMPTIONS
ONON has been one of the strongest
growth stories in our coverage since going
public in 2021, and we expect growth
to remain strong for years to come. We
expect revenues to grow 20-25% constant-
FX in FY26 followed by 15%-20% growth
in FY27. Additionally, we expect ~50bps of
adjusted EBITDA margin expansion each
year. Our 12-month price target of $45
is based on a low-to-mid 20's P/E on
FY27E EPS of 1.52 CHF, or ~$1.95 when
converting the EPS forecast to USD (note
that the share price is in USD, but the P&L
is in CHF).
BEAR CASE ASSUMPTIONS
The biggest potential bear case would
stem from the brand "hitting a wall"
in their growth trajectory. If this were
to occur, then the downside potential
in the shares would be profound, given
how lofty the valuation multiples are.
If growth were to slow to the low-
to-mid 20's in 4Q25 and sub-20% in
FY26, then FY26 revenues could end up
being 5% below the FY26 target, if not
worse. Margins could be protected, as
the company could slow the pace of
investment, but they probably wouldn't
see any margin expansion beyond 2024.
If this were the case, then FY26 adjusted
EBITDA would be ~100 million CHF below
our forecast. In this scenario, we think the
stock could fall to the mid-$20's.
COMPANY DESCRIPTION
ONON is a global athletic footwear brand, headquartered in Switzerland but generating a majority of revenues in North America.

VALUATION (PRICE TARGET: $45.00)
Our 12-month price target of $45 is based on a low to mid 20's P/E on FY27E EPS of 1.52 CHF, or ~$1.95 when converting the EPS forecast
to USD (note that the share price is in USD, but the P&L is in CHF).
POTENTIAL UPSIDE DRIVERS
Potential upside drivers include:
■
The company could gain market share and wholesale shelf space faster than we expect, given the momentum at the brand.
■
The company has major growth opportunities in Asia and apparel, potentially presenting long runways for growth.
■
If ONON can continue generating strong full-price sell-throughs, margins could exceed our expectations given the possible leverage
on sales growth.
RISKS TO TARGET
ONON
Key risks to our bullish thesis on this stock include:
■
The shares garner the highest valuation multiples in our coverage (P/E, EV/EBITDA), presenting significant risk of multiple
contraction if investor sentiment wanes.
■
The company operates in a highly-competitive athletic footwear market, where trends can change very rapidly.
■
If the company fails to generate compelling product innovations, consumer interest could wane and growth could slow.
■
The company's margins are among the best in the industry due to their ability to sell predominantly at full price. If the promotional
retail environment starts to affect them, they may need to discount more, driving potential margin degradation.
