Global Growth Continues to Drive Scale Economies. ONON delivered outsized margin
expansion in 1Q26 (adjusted EBITDA 21.0% vs. consensus 17.8%) driven by strong gross margin
and +26.4% constant currency sales growth. Sales guidance for FY26 continues to call for +23%
constant currency growth (unchanged) with ongoing strong brand momentum in DTC, albeit
with some wholesale moderation as the company is aiming to enter FY27 with a clean retailer
channel to support new launches in 1H27. Currency headwinds have eased slightly but remain
aggressive versus others in our coverage due to ONON's Swiss Franc exposure, with sales
guidance implying approximately 650bps of currency headwinds impacting FY26 net sales
growth (prior expectation was ~900bps of headwinds). While we acknowledge that: 1) growth is
expected to moderate in wholesale in 2H26 and 2) upwards sales guidance revisions have been
more limited versus the prior year period, we reiterate our Overweight rating as we believe
these are more than offset by: 1) ONON brand is still in the acceleration phase of mass adoption
with U.S. brand awareness just crossing the 30% mark in 1Q26; 2) continued door growth into
the key sporting goods retailers: Dick's Sporting Goods, Foot Locker (DKS), and JD Sports (JD.L -
covered by Richard Taylor); and 3) mix-accretive DTC growth supported by elevated new store
openings in 2026. On 5/12/26, ONON shares closed -0.6% versus the SPX -0.2%. 
Tariff Exposure Update. 1Q26 GM expanded +430bps y/y despite higher U.S. tariffs, with
management commenting that this level of GM is the company's new baseline for the year,
reflecting continued strength in ASP and operational execution. GM guidance for FY26 was
raised +150bps to 64.5%, with supply chain initiatives, strong full-price selling, and favorable FX
more than offsetting the 150bps expected tariff headwind. Important to note, the company's
guidance assumes 20% incremental tariff rates from Vietnam and excludes any potential
refunds, suggesting further GM upside should tariff rates remain at 10%, or go to 15%.

Quarter in Review. Adj. EBITDAR of CHF174mn beat consensus of CHF143mn, driven
predominantly by higher GM, but also helped by a sales beat and SG&A. Sales of CHF832mn
beat vs. cons. CHF826mn, and GM of 64.2% beat vs. cons. of 61.4%, driven by ASP strength and
operational discipline, partially offset by higher U.S. tariffs. OpEx rate of 47.6% of sales came in
better than expected (vs. cons. 47.7%). By region, APAC led with +61.4% constant currency
("CC") growth (cons. +62.3%), followed by EMEA with +25.6% CC growth (cons. +20.4%), and the
Americas with +17.1% CC growth (cons. +18.5%). The company updated FY26 guidance, seeing
CC sales growth of +23% y/y (unchanged from prior and vs. cons. +24.6%), GM of at least 64.5%
(from prior 63.0% and vs. cons. 63.1%), and adj. EBITDAR margin in the range of 19.5% to 20.0%
(from prior 18.5% to 19.0% and vs. cons. of 19.0%). 
Inventory. Based on our proprietary inventory analysis, ONON has posted ten consecutive
quarters of positive sales-to-inventory growth. In FY1Q26 the Inventory Management Spread
("IM Spread") was 1360 basis points and worsened from 1841 basis points in the prior quarter.
ONON's Gross Margin Return on Inventory ("GMROI") has improved for eleven consecutive
quarters, and its Operating Margin Return on Inventory ("OMROI") has improved for fifteen
consecutive quarters.
Raising estimates; lowering PT to $46 (from $57). Our FY26, FY27, and FY28 adj. EPS
estimates are CHF1.39, CHF1.63, and CHF1.97 (from CHF1.26, CHF1.52, and CHF1.83),
respectively. Our new PT of $46 (from $57) is based on an NTM P/E multiple of 22x (from 30x)
applied to our FY27 adj. EPS estimate of CHF1.63 (from CHF1.52), or USD$2.08, converted into
USD at a rate of 0.7834 USD/CHF. We raise our EPS estimates as we raise our sales and GM
assumptions, partially offset by greater SG&A deleverage. We lower our multiple 8x turns to 22x
to be more in line with the Street NTM multiple of 18.8x and to account for the recent sector de-
rating, although we note we are still at a premium given ongoing brand heat to fuel DTC growth
and continued room for expansion into key wholesale doors.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: ONON in "HUNT" Mode
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.

Tariff Exposure
Tariff Exposure Update. 1Q26 GM expanded +430bps y/y despite higher U.S. tariffs, with
management commenting that this level of GM is the company's new baseline for the year,
reflecting continued strength in ASP and operational execution. GM guidance for FY26 was
raised +150bps to 64.5%, with supply chain initiatives, strong full-price selling, and favorable FX
more than offsetting the 150bps expected tariff headwind. Important to note, the company's
guidance assumes 20% incremental tariff rates from Vietnam and excludes any potential
refunds, suggesting further GM upside should tariff rates remain at 10%, or go to 15%.
Prior Tariff Commentary. 4Q25 GM expanded +180bps y/y despite higher U.S. tariffs, driven by
continued strong full-price selling, sustainable operating efficiencies, and favorable FX. The
company expects these drivers to continue to support GM expansion in FY26, along with an
ongoing shift towards DTC sales, despite tariff impacts. It is also important to note that the
company's GM guidance assumes the pre-Supreme Court ruling rates, suggesting potential
upside to guidance if the 15% or 10% incremental tariffs remain.
