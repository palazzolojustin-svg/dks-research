Choosing brand health over promotional wholesale growth. ONON posted a rare sales miss
as wholesale sell-out softened due to deliberate management of sell-in by ONON management
in certain everyday running franchises, particularly in the "highly promotional" Americas region.
DTC trends accelerated to +34% constant currency (from +29% in 1Q26), supporting favorable
mix shift with GM of 65.4% ahead of consensus of 64.0%. However, wholesale concerns with a
lack of renewed innovation not until 2027, reduced sales guidance, and potential further sell-in
reduction actions through year-end outweighed the positives. With the sales deceleration, we
note ONON's sales-to-inventory growth spread turned negative and view this is a yellow flag
and a metric to watch going forward. Sales guidance for FY26 now calls for low-20% constant
currency growth, below its prior guidance of at least 23%, reflecting the wholesale actions due

Tariff exposure update. As of 6/30/26, ONON's total IEEPA tariffs paid were CHF55.6mn, of
which CHF52.7mn was submitted through the U.S. Customs refund processing. The company
has not recognized any of these potential refund amounts and will recognize refunds when
realization of these amounts is deemed virtually certain. As of 6/30/26, the majority of the
potential refund relates to inventory already sold. As of 8/11/26, ONON has received
approximately CHF27.9mn in IEEPA tariff refunds. This amount will be recognized in 3Q26. In
2Q26, GM expanded +385bps y/y to 65.4%, driven by strong DTC momentum, operating
efficiencies, and favorable freight/FX, which more than offset higher U.S. tariffs. 2Q26 results
and updated FY26 GM guidance of at least 65% (raised from prior guidance of at least 64.5%)
exclude the potential benefit from any tariff refunds, which management anticipates are likely
to come throughout 2H26 and expects to begin recognizing in 3Q26. Likewise, the company's
reiterated adj. EBITDA margin guidance of 19.5% to 20.0% does not incorporate any benefit
from future tariff refunds.
Quarter in review. Adj. EBITDAR of CHF168mn missed consensus of CHF173mn, due to a sales
miss and higher SG&A rate. Sales of CHF850mn missed vs. cons. CHF881mn; this was split by a
DTC beat of CHF388mn vs. cons. of CHF378mn and a wholesale miss of CHF462mn vs. cons. of
CHF507mn. The OpEx rate of 49.9% of sales came in worse than expected (vs. cons. 48.7%),
driven by an increase in store-related personnel costs/depreciation and increased marketing
investments, partially offset by lower delivery and warehousing costs. GM of 65.4% beat vs.
cons. of 64.0%, driven by higher DTC mix, operating efficiencies, and favorable freight/FX,
partially offset by higher U.S. tariffs. By region, APAC led with +54.7% constant currency ("CC")
growth (cons. +58.3%), followed by EMEA with +20.5% CC growth (cons. +18.6%), and the
Americas with +13.0% CC growth (cons. +17.0%). The company updated FY26 guidance, seeing
CC sales growth in the low-20% range (from prior at least +23% and vs. cons. +24.2%), GM of at
least 65.0% (from prior at least 64.5% and vs. cons. 64.4%), and adj. EBITDAR margin in the
range of 19.5% to 20.0% (unchanged from prior and vs. cons. of 19.9%).
Inventory a potential yellow flag. Based on our proprietary inventory analysis, ONON has
posted one quarter of negative sales-to-inventory growth. In FY2Q26 the Inventory Management
Spread ("IM Spread") was (220) basis points and worsened from 1360 basis points in the prior
quarter. ONON's Gross Margin Return on Inventory ("GMROI") has worsened for one quarter,
and its Operating Margin Return on Inventory ("OMROI") has worsened for one quarter.
Estimates lowered slightly, reducing multiple; PT to $42 (from $46). Our FY26, FY27, and

estimates slightly as we raise our gross margin, more than offset by incremental SG&A
deleverage to account for the DTC mix shift. We lower our multiple 5x turns to 17x as: 1) we roll
forward our PT, which is now based on our FY28 adj. EPS estimate vs. our prior FY27 estimate; 2)
to account for increased risk related to the wholesale slowdown; and 3) to be more in line with
the Street NTM multiple of ~16x.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: ONON in "HUNT" Mode
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.

Tariff exposure update. As of 6/30/26, ONON's total IEEPA tariffs paid were CHF55.6mn, of
which CHF52.7mn was submitted through the U.S. Customs refund processing. The company
has not recognized any of these potential refund amounts and will recognize refunds when
realization of these amounts is deemed virtually certain. As of 6/30/26, the majority of the
potential refund relates to inventory already sold. As of 8/11/26, ONON has received
approximately CHF27.9mn in IEEPA tariff refunds. This amount will be recognized in 3Q26. In
2Q26, GM expanded +385bps y/y to 65.4%, driven by strong DTC momentum, operating
efficiencies, and favorable freight/FX which more than offset higher U.S. tariffs. 2Q26 results and
updated FY26 GM guidance of at least 65% (raised from prior guidance of at least 64.5%)
exclude the potential benefit from any tariff refunds, which management anticipates are likely
to come throughout 2H26 and expects to begin recognizing in 3Q26. Likewise, the company's
reiterated adj. EBITDA margin guidance of 19.5% to 20.0% does not incorporate any benefit
from future tariff refunds.
Prior tariff commentary. 1Q26 GM expanded +430bps y/y despite higher U.S. tariffs, with
management commenting that this level of GM is the company's new baseline for the year,
reflecting continued strength in ASP and operational execution. GM guidance for FY26 was
raised +150bps to 64.5%, with supply chain initiatives, strong full-price selling, and favorable FX
more than offsetting the 150bps expected tariff headwind. Important to note, the company's
guidance assumes 20% incremental tariff rates from Vietnam and excludes any potential
refunds, suggesting further GM upside should tariff rates remain at 10%, or go to 15%.
