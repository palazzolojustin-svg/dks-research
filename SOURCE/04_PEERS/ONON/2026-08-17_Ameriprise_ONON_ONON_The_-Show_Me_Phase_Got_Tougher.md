The ‘Show Me’ Phase Got Tougher 
What Caused the Downside? 
Note that ONON reports in Swiss francs or CHF. ONON cut FY revenue 
guidance from at least +23% y/y cc to low-20%, implying CHF3.47 billion-
CHF3.56 billion of revenue vs. FactSet consensus estimates at CHF3.56 
billion. At the midpoint, it was a 1.3% miss vs. consensus, while the low end 
is 2.5% below estimates. In our view, the rare guidance downshift was 
primarily a quality-of-growth decision, not a demand collapse. ONON is 
preparing a major refresh of everyday running franchises, including 
Cloudsurfer 3, Cloudsurfer Max 2, and Cloudflow, with new foams, new 
technologies, and Swiss-engineered precision fit. Mgmt. noted the goal is to 
create a clean runway so these innovations can “land well” in late 2026 and 
2027.  
The core issue was weaker sell-through in some everyday running 
franchises, especially in the Americas wholesale, within a highly promotional 
marketplace. Rather than force additional inventory into wholesale channels, 
ONON intentionally held back sell-in to protect full-price integrity and keep 
inventories clean. Management explicitly said the revised top-line guidance 
was “very much linked to the wholesale actions,” while DTC remained strong 
and above expectations. This explains why GM guidance rose from at least 
64.5% to at least 65.0%, while adj. EBITDA margin stayed 19.5%-20.0%. 
Operational efficiencies also helped. Q2 GM reached 65.4%, up 390 bps y/y, 
beating estimates by 2.2%, despite higher U.S. import tariffs and before any 
tariff refund benefit. In short, we believe this was a deliberate ‘reset the 
channel before the innovation wave’ move, not a broad demand reset.  
 
Investment Opinion: Buy

Mixed Q2 Print  
Q2 revenue of CHF850 million missed estimates of CHF878 million by 3.2%, despite strong DTC momentum. The shortfall was 
concentrated in wholesale, which was CHF462 million vs. estimates at CHF507 million, a 9% miss. DTC partly offset this at CHF388 
million (up 26.0% y/y reported and 34.3% constant currency) vs. estimates at CHF378 million. DTC reached 45.7% of sales, a Q2 record, 
and management said e-commerce growth exceeded expectations across all regions. This mix shift was the primary reason gross margin 
guidance increased 
Adj. EBITDA was CHF168 million, missing estimates by 2%, but adj. EBITDA margin of 19.8% beat estimates by 30 basis points. This 
margin performance reflected a stronger DTC mix, full-price discipline, and operating efficiencies, even as the company absorbed higher 
U.S. import tariffs and excluded any tariff refund benefit. 
Segment Analysis 
Q2 geographic performance reinforced the thesis that the issue was concentrated rather than global. Americas revenue was CHF452 
million, up 4.5% y/y reported and 13.0% constant currency, but missed estimates by 4.6%, reflecting the wholesale pressure and FX drag. 
EMEA was stronger at CHF228 million, up 15.4% y/y reported and 20.5% constant currency, beating estimates. APAC remained the 
fastest-growing region, with revenue of CHF171 million, up 43% y/y on a reported basis and 55% in constant currency, though it missed 
estimates by 4.7%. Mgmt. highlighted EMEA’s clean marketplace as an example of prior channel pruning now paying off, while APAC 
continued to benefit from strong momentum across Japan, South Korea, and Greater China. In our view, the key readthrough is that 
ONON still has strong international growth engines, but America's wholesale is being reset for healthier long-term execution.  
Segment-wise, Shoes revenue increased 11% y/y (18.9% constant currency) to CHF782 million, but missed estimates by 4.6%. Apparel 
revenue came in at CHF54 million, up 48% y/y reported and 56% constant currency, beating estimates by 3.8%. Apparel remains a key 
optionality lever, with management highlighting tennis, training, and SenseTec as areas expanding the brand beyond footwear. Lastly, 
Accessories revenue was CHF15 million, up 88% y/y reported and 102% constant currency, well above estimates. Given the small base 
and unusually large beat, this is directionally positive but less important to the investment debate than DTC, wholesale, footwear, and 
apparel. 
Valuation  
From a valuation perspective, we believe ONON should continue to command a premium multiple only if investors view this quarter as a 
disciplined brand-management reset rather than a demand reset. The bull case is supported by strong DTC growth, expanding gross 
margin, high brand awareness, younger consumer adoption, apparel growth, APAC momentum, and a credible technology-driven product 
pipeline into 2027. The bear case is that premium valuation leaves little room for execution missteps, and the stock may struggle if 
wholesale does not reaccelerate after the channel reset or if the upcoming running launches fail to restore confidence in everyday running 
growth. For comparison, after the post-earnings sell-off, shares trade at a price-to-sales of 2.7x, a meaningful discount to the five-year 
average. price-to-sales of 7.2x.   
Investment Thesis  
In our view, On Holding AG presents a compelling investment opportunity as a premium global sportswear brand demonstrating robust 
growth, innovation, and strategic execution. We believe the brand awareness has risen materially but remains far from saturated, creating 
a long runway for new customer acquisition while existing customers deepen engagement through higher basket sizes, repeat purchases, 
and multi-category adoption. Importantly, this demand has been captured largely at full price, underscoring pricing power and validating 
management’s emphasis on disciplined distribution rather than volume maximization. Margin trajectory is another pillar of the thesis. In our 
view, ONON has demonstrated that premium positioning translates into structurally higher gross margins, not only through price mix but 
also via operational efficiencies that scale with growth. The guided EBITDA range for 2026 suggests that incremental growth is accretive, 
not dilutive, and that reinvestment is increasingly funded by internal cash generation rather than margin sacrifice.  
Beyond footwear, we believe category expansion represents a meaningful and underappreciated growth lever. Apparel and accessories 
are scaling faster than the core business and offer attractive margins due to their high penetration in direct-to-consumer channels. 
Furthermore, we believe innovation remains a catalyst rather than a cost center. Breakthroughs such as LightSpray manufacturing and 
ongoing advancements in materials and design differentiate ONON in a crowded athletic market and extend its technological moat.

In addition to the general risks of owning equities, On Holdings faces risks related to supply chain disruption, geopolitical risks, and 
currency risks (reports in the Swiss franc, but net sales are recognized in US dollar in the US). As most of the products are sourced from 
emerging markets, a possible rise in tariffs in the US could lower consumer spending on shoes and apparel. Additional risk factors include 
the rapidly changing footwear industry, failure to innovate and expand to other markets, unfavorable changes in labor laws in Vietnam, 
potential margin compression in case of a weaker retail environment, excess inventory due to incorrect demand forecast, etc.  
Company Profile 
On Holdings AG, founded in 2010 and based in Zurich, Switzerland, is a premium global sportswear brand known for its focus on 
innovation, design, and sustainability. The company operates worldwide, with a significant presence in the US, EMEA (Europe, the Middle 
East, and East Africa), Australia, and China. Its product range includes shoes, apparel, and accessories tailored for specific athletic 
activities, as well as casual and leisure use. On Holdings has developed several proprietary footwear technologies, including CloudTec, 
CloudTec Phase, Helion superfoam, and Speedboard. While the company designs shoes in Switzerland, most manufacturing takes place 
in Vietnam (90%) and Indonesia (10%). The products are sold through wholesale partnerships with selected retailers, own retail stores, 
and direct-to-consumer (DTC) through digital channels. In 2021, On Holdings AG became a publicly traded company on the New York 
Stock Exchange (NYSE).
