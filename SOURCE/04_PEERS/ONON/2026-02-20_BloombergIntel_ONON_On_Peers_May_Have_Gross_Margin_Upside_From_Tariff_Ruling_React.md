On, Peers May Have Gross Margin Upside From Tariff Ruling: React
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- RECENT EVENT
REACTION: Footwear retailers, including
Deckers, On and Steve Madden, could see
incremental gross-margin tailwinds
following the Supreme Court's decision to
strike down President Donald Trump's
reciprocal tariffs, though potential duty refunds might take years to materialize. The benefit will likely
phase in as higher-cost inventory clears, suggesting a more visible impact in 2H rather than an
immediate step-change. (02/20/26)
1. On: Equity Outlook
THESIS: On Holding's raised 2025 guidance and updated three-year targets highlight sustained brand
momentum and structural growth potential beyond its core running. Currency-neutral sales are
poised to rise 34% this year vs. at least 31% prior, with management forecasting a 30% compound
annual growth rate through 2026 vs. 26% before. Gains are fueled by innovation across running,
expanding apparel reach and direct-to-consumer mix. New store openings and accelerating
international demand, particularly in Asia-Pacific, reinforce global runway. On's premium pricing
power, full-price discipline and scale efficiencies support continued long-term margin expansion.
(11/12/25)

2. On Momentum Builds on Premium, Global Strength: Earnings Outlook
Post-3Q Earnings Outlook: On's improved 3Q revenue, up 24.9%, and raised full-year forecast show
consumer demand for the brand is strong, fueled by a robust innovation pipeline and international
momentum. Guidance for sales to climb 34% vs. at least 31% suggests the brand's core running
franchises, expanding direct-to-consumer (DTC) presence and apparel traction can keep fueling high-
quality full-price sales growth, supported by premium positioning. DTC investment, including
omnichannel capabilities and loyalty, helped propel the channel 27.6% higher.
In 3Q, gross margin rose 510 bps to 65.7% and might reach 62.5% for the year, based on guidance, as
scale efficiencies, lower logistics costs and sustained pricing power help offset additional tariff

Key Topics
2026 Targets In Reach
On's Target of Doubling Revenue by 2026 Appears Within Reach
On, with just a 2.3% share of the global sports-footwear market and even less in sportswear, could
increase sales at least 25% over each of the next two years and achieve its 2023 goal of doubling the
top line by 2026. Gains will likely be driven by continued online strength, new store openings,
persistent wholesale demand, expansion in underpenetrated markets like China and product
innovation. (06/17/25)
3. Sharper DTC Focus, Brand Awareness Key to Driving Gains
On could comfortably reach and exceed its midterm goal of doubling sales to 3.55 billion Swiss francs
by 2026, our scenario analysis suggests, as it remains in the early stages of a global expansion with
ample opportunity to expand share, driven by broad-based gains across regions and channels. China,
direct-to-consumer and apparel may see the greatest growth as the brand accelerates the pace of
opening stores to 20-25 a year, with half of those planned earmarked for China. Stores will also play a
key role in tripling apparel sales as the channel boasts higher penetration than online or wholesale.
On could continue to drive mid- to high-20% annual sales gains, even as growth moderates as it
increases scale through continued investments in technology and infrastructure. (06/16/25)

5. China Represents Largest Growth Potential
Geographically, China is On's biggest opportunity, similar to many athleisure peers given the country's
growing middle class, rising interest in sports and greater spending power among its shoppers.
Expanding its footprint in Mainland China through new store openings and larger formats, coupled
with scaling up its omnichannel presence with major digital platforms to reach a broader base of
consumers, should help it capture share. Since On opened its first store in 2019, sales in China have
been growing at a triple-digit pace, inching closer to its aspiration of 10% of sales beyond 2026.
Asia represents just 11% of sales, suggesting ample runway to drive gains. Still, the Americas will
remain its largest region, and though EMEA is its most mature, On is still underpenetrated in key
markets such as France, Spain and Italy. (06/16/25)

DTC Key Growth Driver
Store Expansion, Digital Strength to Fuel On's DTC Acceleration
An increased focus on driving direct-to-consumer gains will be key to doubling On's sales by 2026 and
will come with accelerated store openings, as well as a digital halo effect, amid greater brand
awareness and store proximity. More controlled wholesale growth should help maintain On's premium
brand equity while converting momentum into DTC demand. (06/18/25)
7. Retail Stores Act as Brand Amplifier
On's retail stores will play a larger role in its direct-to-consumer strategy as it accelerates the pace of
openings to fuel greater brand awareness and reach while driving cross-shopping with its other
channels. Management has noted that after opening a new store, e-commerce traffic can be 3x
higher, and wholesale partners in the market also see an uplift. Accelerating store openings to 20-25
a year could help push retail store sales penetration to 10% in the long term and will be driven largely
by new retail formats as On shifts beyond flagship locations.
E-commerce may continue to outpace On's overall growth as the company increases investments in
its digital platforms to improve customer experience. Expanding distribution capacity to meet rising
demand from greater brand momentum is also key to omnichannel expansion. (06/18/25)

On's Owned Store Count, Retail Format Strategy
8. Wholesale Growth to Be More Controlled
Wholesale will remain a key driver in expanding On's reach and brand awareness, but growth may
moderate as the brand takes a more controlled approach and focuses on increasing efficiency with
existing partners. New partners will begin to drive less incremental growth compared with previous
years, given On has established relationships with most key strategic retailers in its main markets and
can be more selective about entering new ones.
With just 20% penetration among some of its key larger wholesale accounts -- Dick’s, Foot Locker and
JD -- On has significant room to expand and build a presence with its newest partners. This year, the
brand plans to expand its wholesale door count about 5% to 11,300. As a result, strong 20-24%
wholesale growth over the next two years will largely be driven by same-store sales gains. (06/16/25)

9. On Makes Strides in Competitive Running
Even as On expands into new sports, running will remain a core growth driver, given the category
dominates the athletic-footwear market and continues to become more mainstream globally,
presenting an opportunity to gain share. Top sneaker brands including Nike, Adidas and Puma have
been in a race to create the fastest shoe for leading marathon runners while gaining broader appeal
in the running community, but have struggled to gain loyalty among everyday runners. However, On
has successfully become a top three running brand in most of its main markets and with key retail
partners over the past five years.
On appears poised to capture more share, aided by two new speed-performance technologies
launching this year. In the US sports footwear market, On's share has risen to 5.2% from just 1.2% in
2020. (06/18/25)
Brands Share in the US Sports Footwear Market
10. On Looks to Tennis for Versatile Styles
On's partnership with Roger Federer has helped it quickly gain credibility in tennis, but the company
is still building its brand in the sport. The expansion of its professional tennis roster in 2024, with the
addition of Iga Swiatek and Ben Shelton, along a multiyear partnership with Zendaya as a new brand
partner can aid sales and drive brand heat, particularly with a younger generation. Both players
debuted custom editions of the Roger Pro and On's newly developed on-court head-to-toe collection.
Tennis will also play a key role in how On comes to life as a brand that can be worn all day, unlocking
athlete-endorsed and aspirational court styles that can be adopted off-court. It has already begun to
do this with The Roger franchise, which includes a pro tennis shoe and a sneaker inspired by the
sport. (06/18/25)

11. Product Expansion Into Apparel Can Extend Reach
On's foray into apparel in 2019 provides the company an opportunity to tap into the global $238.7
billion sports apparel market, which is forecast to grow to over $310 billion by 2029, based on
euromonitor data. The move has made On a true head-to-toe sportswear brand, with apparel sales
reaching over $100 million CHF in 2024 as efforts to scale up its apparel business, aided by elevated
collections, brand-building campaigns and through its physical stores. In 2025, On plans to continue
to push apparel-first categories such as training, which could help drive category sales over 55% this
year, based on our analysis.
The expansion of its own physical doors is also helping to grow On's apparel business given stores
have a higher penetration of clothing sales and allow consumers to test and feel the products in
person. (06/18/25)
