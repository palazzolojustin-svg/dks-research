Compelling LT Growth Runway; Reiterate Buy
We expect P/E expansion to drive ONON toward our $85 PT:
We think On's continued focus on innovation, performance, athletes, sports, and direct-
to-consumer selling, while maintaining a premium brand position will lead to industry-
leading sales growth and earnings beats. ONON’s +26% y/y ex-FX sales growth and 
exceptionally strong margin results in 1Q reinforce our conviction in this view. We 
forecast On delivering 18%, 20%, and 29% 5-yr. sales, adjusted EBITDA, EPS CAGRs, 
respectively, and expect strong growth to continue from there. Our view is this type of 
growth outlook justifies a 33x FY2 P/E vs. just ~16x today. We think ONON’s growth and 
margin expansion will positively surprise the market and boost the stock’s P/E.
3 key takeaways from ONON's 1Q26 report: 
1. On’s brand is gaining heat with younger consumers. Mgmt. highlighted, in 1Q, the 
share of 18-24 year olds significantly grew within the company’s DTC customer 
base. We believe On’s expanding reach with this younger demographic is an 
underappreciated LT growth driver.
2. ONON’s FY26 sales growth outlook looks beatable, in our view. ONON maintained 
its full year ex-FX sales growth guidance at +23% despite delivering +26% topline 
growth in 1Q. We view this as a sign of management’s caution given the uncertain 
macro environment, rather than an indication of weaker demand expected in 
2H26. We believe the company’s brand building investments and healthy product 
pipeline should continue to support topline momentum. 
3. ONON’s 1Q gross margin expanded ~430 bps y/y to 64.2%. This result was 
significantly better than expected, despite tariff headwinds, due to robust full-
price selling and improved supply chain efficiency. Plus, ONON raised its FY26 gross 
margin guidance 150 bps to 64.5%. We note this outlook assumes a 20% 
incremental tariff rate on Vietnam, even though the company currently is seeing a 
10% rate, and does not factor for any potential refunds. We see both factors as 
opportunities for upside to FY26 gross margin. 
We raise our FY26 EPSe by ~11%; our FY27-FY28 EPSe stay unchanged: 
ONON’s ~10c EPS beat vs. UBSe drives the majority of our FY26 EPS increase. Plus, we 
now forecast higher FY26 gross margin due to ONON benefiting more than expected 
from its premium-focused strategy. This is partially offset by higher expected SG&A 
expenses tied to marketing investments. Together, these factors fuel a 15c increase in 
our FY26 EPSe. 
Valuation: Our PT is $85 and rating is Buy:
Our $85 PT is based on ~33x our CHF 2.10 FY28 EPSe (FX adj. CHF/USD 1.25). Our 
multiples analysis indicates a $85 PT puts ONON's valuation in-line with peers in terms of 
P/E and P/Sales (Fig. 5). Our DCF analysis also supports a $85/share valuation (Fig. 7).

Q: How much will the athletic wear industry grow?
We expect the global athletic wear industry to grow at a +M-HSD% annual rate for a multi-year 
period.  In the ten years before the pandemic, the industry averaged 7% growth.  We believe COVID-
19 has amplified the trends toward health & wellness and casual dress, two key industry sales drivers. 
Given this and the fact that sportswear is still only 25% of total apparel and footwear industry sales, 
we believe the industry can maintain strong growth rates into the out years.
Q: What kind of market share can ONON achieve?
We believe ONON can achieve at least 2% market share over the long term.  ON has already shown it 
can address a large market.  We believe it is a rare brand that has the characteristics to become a 
global, multi-sport, multi-category athletic wear brand, similar to Nike, Adidas, and Puma.  If ONON 
equals Puma's FY21 sales over the long term, we estimate it could achieve 2% market share.
Q: What is ONON's long-term EBIT margin potential?
We forecast ONON delivering a mid-to-high teens EBIT margin over time. ONON has two important 
characteristics that we believe give it  a high margin potential: 1) it is a premium, high-priced brand 
that plans to compete on innovation, rather than price, and 2) it will be a direct-to-consumer, 
eCommerce-led business.  We also think its expansion to apparel will be accretive to margins over 
time. We note premium athletic wear brands with these qualities, such as  Lululemon, have similar 
margin outlooks.
We rate ONON Buy.  We believe the market sees On as mainly a running shoe brand and doesn’t fully 
appreciate the brand's potential to address a larger market. We think On's growth will lead to  beat & 
raise reports, which could surprise the market and drive the stock's P/E higher. We forecast On 
delivering a 18%, 20%, and 29% 5-yr. sales, adjusted EBITDA, and EPS CAGR, respectively and 
expect strong growth to continue from there. This type of growth would likely make On one of the 
world's fastest-growing athletic wear brands and worthy of a premium multiple, in our view.
UBS Evidence Lab's Global Athletic Wear survey indicates global sportswear demand remains strong. 
It also shows global awareness of the On brand remains low, suggesting much room for growth as 
consumers discover the brand.  At the same time, the survey shows consumers who have heard of On 
have an increasingly favorable view of it.
Our reverse DCF analysis indicates the market is pricing ONON's sales to  rise in the mid-teens range, 
below our ~18% 5-yr. forecast. The analysis suggests EBIT margins would remain in the low-teens%, 
below our high-teens% FY30E estimate.

ONON 1Q26 Earnings Review 
ONON delivered solid 1Q26 sales and EBITDA margin beats. ONON's 1Q26 net 
sales rose +14.5% y/y (+26.4% ex-FX) to CHF 831.9M. ONON's revenue growth rate 
was ~150 bps above the Street's forecast. By channel, wholesale and DTC channels 
increased +25.1% and +28.7%, respectively, on a y/y ex-FX basis. By region, EMEA sales 
increased +25.6% y/y, Americas +17.1% and APAC +61.4%, on an ex-FX basis. Gross 
margin expanded ~430 bps y/y to 64.2%, ~280 bps above the 61.4% consensus 
forecast. Total OpEx dollars rose +15.1% y/y and OpEx as a % of sales was 47.6%, 
reflecting ~25 bps of y/y deleverage. This drove 1Q adjusted EBITDA of CHF 174.3M, 
which was ~20% higher than the Street's forecast and also reflected an 21.0% adjusted 
EBITDA margin, ~325 bps above consensus. Together, ONON delivered 1Q EPS of CHF 
0.37, vs. consensus' CHF 0.27 estimate.  Lastly, inventory rose 2% y/y (Fig. 1).

By channel, we forecast DTC sales growing +19.5% y/y (+25.5% ex-FX) and wholesale

FX), Americas increasing +13.6% (+18% ex-FX), and APAC rising +40.0% (+47% ex-
FX). Based on current spot rates, we see a ~600 bps FX headwind to 2Q sales growth. 
For FY26, we forecast net sales rising +18.6% y/y (+24.3% ex-FX) to CHF 3.58B. We 
note ONON’s FY26 guidance reflects +23% y/y sales growth ex-FX, which translates to 
reported sales of CHF 3.51B. Here are key details behind our forecasts:

We model 2Q26 DTC sales increasing +19.5% y/y (+25.5% ex-FX) to CHF 
368M. On’s brand awareness in the US eclipsed the 30% mark for the first time in 
1Q. The company’s marketing investments as a percentage of sales also grew 
~200 bps y/y to 13.1% in the quarter. We expect these marketing investments will 
further increase awareness of the On brand, which should result in higher 
customer traffic to the company’s direct-to-consumer channels. Plus, we believe 
ONON will continue to open new stores at a healthy clip over the course of the 
year. At the same time, we flag 2Q26 DTC sales will comp against +54% growth 
from the prior year. 

We forecast 2Q26 wholesale sales up +17.4% y/y (+23.5% ex-FX) to CHF 
508M. During the 1Q call, ONON mentioned its products are available at 
approximately 50% of the doors operated by its key wholesale partners, including 
Dick’s Sporting Goods, Foot Locker, and JD Sports. We expect ONON to continue 
to gradually expand its presence at additional retail doors as well as capture more 
shelf space within its existing wholesale partner locations. Additionally, we 
anticipate the company will increase its footprint at run specialty retailers through 
launches of new performance running shoes.
Gross Margin: We model 2Q26 gross margin expanding 250 bps y/y to 64.0%. The 
company’s gross margin reached 64.2% 1Q26, up ~430 bps y/y. ONON noted stronger

bps of tariff headwinds in 1Q. Importantly, the company considers the stronger full price 
selling and cost efficiencies as sustainable benefits to gross margin through FY26 and 
beyond. We also expect ongoing DTC share expansion and favorable FX to contribute to 
gross margin expansion. Finally, the company flagged the current product its selling has 
been tariffed at a 10% rate, which is lower than the 20% rate its FY26 outlook 
contemplates. This factor could be another driver of gross margin upside starting in 
2Q26. For FY26, we forecast gross margin expanding ~170 bps y/y to 64.5%.

UPSIDE ($105): We assume a ~33% 5-yr. upside case EPS CAGR (FY25-30E). In our 
upside case, we assume ONON is able to fully mitigate the impacts of US tariffs. ONON 
proves it can be much more than a running footwear brand. It delivers meaningful 
growth in sports such as tennis and in categories such as apparel. Plus, the brand remains 
'cool' and the company is able to leverage this position to significantly increase its lifestyle 
offering without sacrificing any of its credibility or authenticity as a performance sports 
brand. Stronger sales growth leads to better fixed costs leverage, resulting in better EBIT 
margins. ONON's P/E remains elevated at ~35x and we apply this multiple to our CHF 
2.40 FY28E upside EPS, which implies a $105 upside valuation (FX 1.25 CHF/USD).
BASE ($85): We forecast a ~29% 5-yr. base case EPS CAGR (FY25-30E). In our base case, 
we contemplate some pressure from US tariffs over FY25-FY26. Yet, ONON benefits from 
strong industry demand tailwinds and also continues to take big market share in the 
running footwear category. ONON delivers strong growth not only in Europe, but also in 
North America and China. We forecast ~18% annual sales growth through FY30. As the 
company grows, its channel mix should shift to its own website and stores, driving gross 
margin expansion. Plus, we also anticipate the company is able to leverage fixed costs. 
Both factors combine to help ONON achieve a ~20.7% EBITDA margin by FY30E. We 
think ONON's EPS growth would justify a ~33x P/E and we apply this multiple to our CHF 
2.10 FY28E EPS to derive our $85 price target (FX 1.25 CHF/USD).
DOWNSIDE ($19): We forecast a ~16% 5-yr. EPS CAGR (FY25-30E). In our downside 
case, we assume ONON is unable to effectively mitigate the impacts of US tariffs.  In this 
scenario, global sportswear demand slows as inflation proves not transitory. Plus, 
ONON's innovation gain limited traction with consumers, particularly in the performance 
running category. This, along with global macro challenges and an increasingly 
competitive environment  stymies industry growth and ONON's market share gains. 
Supply chain issues limit ONON's sales growth and rising costs pressure margins more 
than expected. We think ONON will have weaker long-term prospects and be  able to only 
garner a ~11x P/E. We apply this multiple to our CHF 1.40 FY28E EPS to derive our $19 
downside valuation (FX 1.25 CHF/USD).

Company Description
On Holding AG develops and distributes sportswear products worldwide through 
independent retailers and global distributors. It also has its own online presence and its own 
stores. The company was founded on Sept 28, 2012 and is headquartered in Zurich, 
Switzerland. For fiscal years 2022, 2021, and 2020, it generated net sales of CHF1,222.1m, 
CHF 724.6m, and CHF 425.3m, respectively, representing 68.7% and 70.4% YoY growth. As 
of March 21, 2023, it has 19 stores worldwide and is present in more than 60 countries across 
the globe.
Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Our price target for On is based on P/E multiple analysis, supported by a DCF valuation. On’s 
products are primarily manufactured by third party partners. Any disruption in On’s global 
supply chain would be a risk for the shares. Similarly, On’s products are primarily sold through 
third party retailers that have significant influence over end-market sales of the company’s 
products. Further, the athletic apparel and footwear industry is subject to volatility due to 
fashion cycles. Any deterioration in On’s ability to maintain a rapid pace of bringing 
compelling new product innovation to market would also be a risk for the stock. Other risks 
include slowing growth in China, global competition, and low visibility related to global 
consumer spending levels.
