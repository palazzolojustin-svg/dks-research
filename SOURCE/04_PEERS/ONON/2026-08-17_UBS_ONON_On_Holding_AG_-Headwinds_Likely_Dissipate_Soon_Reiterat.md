Headwinds Likely Dissipate Soon; Reiterate Buy 
We expect P/E expansion to drive ONON toward our $73 PT vs. $31 today:
On's continued focus on innovation, performance, athletes, sports, direct-to consumer 
selling, and commitment to maintaining a premium, full-price brand image will lead to 
industry leading sales growth and upside earnings surprises. We continue to see On as 
one of the world's fastest-growing athletic wear brands and worthy of a higher multiple 
than the market is giving the stock. The stock pulled back on a disappointing 2Q26 
report. However, we see this as a buying opportunity. The issues affecting the 2Q26 
report are mostly due to short-term industry dynamics. We believe ONON's upcoming 
new product launches likely catalyze a 4Q26 sales growth rate inflection. This and a 
bullish September analyst day likely remind the market On is an exceptional growth 
stock, boost sentiment and the stock's ~17x FY2 P/E.
2 reasons why we remain bullish on ONON: 
1. ONON is executing its full-price strategy. The company cited softer-than-expected 
sell-through in certain mature running franchises, particularly in the Americas 
wholesale channel, amid an elevated promotional environment (UAA cited a 
similar dynamic in its recent earnings call). ONON is intentionally limiting sell-in 
rather than discounting to drive volumes, which will likely weigh on its near-term 
wholesale sales growth. Yet, we believe this type of action will help ONON 
maintain its industry-leading gross margins and support solid demand for its 
upcoming innovation in FY27. 
2. Underlying demand remains robust. ONON delivered 34% ex-FX DTC sales growth 
in 2Q26, despite lapping 54% gains from 2Q25, with management attributing the 
strength in part to robust younger consumer acquisition. Meanwhile, the Cloudtilt 
franchise rose 190% y/y in the quarter, highlighting the brand’s ongoing traction 
in lifestyle. Plus, ONON highlighted retail partner feedback for its upcoming 
launches, including the Cloudsurfer 3 with its new Super Foam tech, has been 
highly positive. We believe these indicators support the view On’s brand 
momentum remains strong. Find more reasons inside and here: link.
Our FY26-FY28 EPSe remain unchanged: 
We lower our FY26 net sales growth forecast by ~190 bps due to a more cautious 2H 
wholesale channel outlook. However, a higher FY26 gross margin forecast reflecting a 
favorable DTC mix, full-price selling, and operating efficiencies offsets this. Collectively, 
our FY26 EPS estimate stays unchanged at CHF 1.45. We continue to model an 18% 
sales and 29% EPS 5-yr. CAGRs. See more detail inside.
Valuation: We lower our PT 12% to $73:
Our $73 PT is based on ~29x our CHF 2.10 FY28 EPSe (FX adj. CHF/USD 1.22). We lower 
the multiple we use to value ONON by ~4 turns to more appropriately reflect the more 
challenging macro environment. This drives the entirety of our PT revision.

Q: How much will the athletic wear industry grow?
We expect the global athletic wear industry to grow at a +M-HSD% annual rate for a multi-year 
period. In the ten years before the pandemic, the industry averaged 7% growth. We believe COVID-
19 has amplified the trends toward health & wellness and casual dress, two key industry sales drivers. 
Given this and the fact that sportswear is still only 25% of total apparel and footwear industry sales, 
we believe the industry can maintain strong growth rates into the out years.
Q: What kind of market share can ONON achieve?
We believe ONON can achieve at least 2% market share over the long term. ON has already shown it 
can address a large market. We believe it is a rare brand that has the characteristics to become a 
global, multi-sport, multi-category athletic wear brand, similar to Nike, Adidas, and Puma. If ONON 
equals Puma's FY21 sales over the long term, we estimate it could achieve 2% market share.
Q: What is ONON's long-term EBIT margin potential?
We forecast ONON delivering a mid-to-high teens EBIT margin over time. ONON has two important 
characteristics that we believe give it a high margin potential: 1) it is a premium, high-priced brand 
that plans to compete on innovation, rather than price, and 2) it will be a direct-to-consumer, 
eCommerce-led business. We also think its expansion to apparel will be accretive to margins over 
time. We note premium athletic wear brands with these qualities, such as Lululemon, have similar 
margin outlooks.
We rate ONON Buy. We believe the market sees On as mainly a running shoe brand and doesn’t fully 
appreciate the brand's potential to address a larger market. We think On's growth will lead to beat & 
raise reports, which could surprise the market and drive the stock's P/E higher. We forecast On 
delivering an 18%, 20%, and 29% 5-yr. sales, adjusted EBITDA, and EPS CAGR, respectively and 
expect strong growth to continue from there. This type of growth would likely make On one of the 
world's fastest-growing athletic wear brands and worthy of a premium multiple, in our view.
UBS Evidence Lab's Global Athletic Wear survey indicates global sportswear demand remains strong. 
It also shows global awareness of the On brand remains low, suggesting much room for growth as 
consumers discover the brand. At the same time, the survey shows consumers who have heard of On 
have an increasingly favorable view of it.
Our reverse DCF analysis indicates the market is pricing ONON's sales to rise in the mid-teens range, 
below our ~18% 5-yr. forecast. The analysis suggests EBIT margins would remain in the mid-teens%,

ONON 2Q26 Earnings Review 
ONON delivered 2Q26 EPS of CHF 0.35, 1c above the Street. Total 2Q26 net sales 
increased +13.5% y/y (+21.6% ex-FX) to CHF 850.3M. ONON's revenue growth rate 
was ~400 bps below the Street's forecast. By channel, DTC revenue increased +26.0% 
y/y (+34.3% ex-FX), while wholesale grew +4.8% (+12.7% ex-FX). By region, APAC 
revenue rose +43.1% y/y (+54.7% ex-FX), EMEA increased +15.4% (+20.5% ex-FX), 
and the Americas rose +4.5% (+13.0% ex-FX). Gross margin expanded ~390 bps y/y to 
65.4%,125 bps above the Street's 64.1% consensus forecast, reflecting a higher DTC 
mix, operational efficiencies, and full-price discipline. SG&A expenses increased +18.8% 
y/y to CHF 424.1M, resulting in a 49.9% SG&A rate and implying ~220 bps of 
deleverage. This drove 2Q adjusted EBITDA of CHF 168.1M, which was 3% below the 
Street's forecast and also reflected a 19.8% adjusted EBITDA margin,15 bps above 
consensus. Together, ONON delivered 2Q EPS of CHF 0.35 vs. consensus' CHF 0.34 
estimate.  Lastly, inventory rose 31% y/y. See more detail in Figure 1 below.

Modeling Detail
Sales: We model 3Q26 net sales increasing 19.3% y/y (+18.8% ex-FX) to CHF 947M. 
Based on current spot rates, we see a ~40 bps FX tailwind to 3Q26 sales growth. For 
FY26, we forecast net sales rising 17.6% y/y (+22.1% ex-FX) to CHF 3.54B. We note 
ONON’s updated FY26 guidance calls for low-20% constant-currency net sales growth, 
which implies reported sales of CHF 3.47B to CHF 3.56B at current spot rates. Here are 
key details behind our forecasts:

We model 3Q26 DTC sales increasing 30.6% y/y (+30.0% ex-FX) to CHF 
411M. 2Q26 DTC sales rose 34.3% y/y ex-FX. Management highlighted strength 
across both e-commerce and owned retail, with e-commerce exceeding 
expectations in all regions and stores delivering continued growth in new doors 
and comp sales. The company also noted that consumers under 34 now represent 
more than one-third of ONON’s customer base, while newer visitors are showing 
increased engagement, which should support continued DTC traffic and 
conversion. We expect DTC momentum to remain strong in 3Q26, supported by 
higher brand awareness, younger customer acquisition, full-price selling, and 
continued productivity from premium retail stores. This should also support solid 
back-to-school selling in the US. 

Americas DTC growth likely remains strong. Based on management 
commentary, we believe 2Q26 Americas ex-FX DTC sales growth likely 
meaningfully outpaced overall Americas ex-FX sales growth of +13% ex-FX. 
Assuming Americas channel mix of DTC/wholesale is roughly in-line with the 
overall company at 40%/60%, we estimate 2Q Americas DTC sales potentially 
rose in the low-to-mid 20% range y/y (ex-FX), depending on the assumed drag 
on wholesale growth. This also then implies 2Q Americas wholesale sales rose 
mid-to-high single digits% y/y ex-FX. 

We forecast 3Q26 wholesale sales increasing 11.9% y/y (+11.5% ex-FX) to 
CHF 537M. Wholesale sales grew 12.7% on a constant-currency basis in 2Q26, 
reflecting a ~12 percentage point deceleration q/q, as management deliberately 
managed sell-in to protect channel inventory health and full-price integrity. The 
company cited softer sell-out in some everyday running franchises in a highly 
promotional wholesale environment, particularly in the Americas, and indicated 
that additional wholesale actions would occur in 3Q. As a result, we expect 
wholesale growth to remain pressured in 3Q26. That said, ONON described the 
wholesale issue as mostly isolated in the Americas and transitory. Furthermore, the 
Cloudsurfer 3 will begin rolling out to run specialty partners in October, giving 
these partners exclusive access to the product for three months, before a broader 
launch takes place in January. Management has indicated the initial response from 
retail partners for the Cloudsurfer 3, which features the company’s new super 
foam technology, has been strong, supporting a potential acceleration in 
wholesale sales growth in 4Q relative to 3Q. 
Gross Margin: We model 3Q26 gross margin contracting ~50 bps y/y to 64.9%. The 
company’s gross margin reached 65.4% in 2Q26, up 390 bps y/y, even while fully 
absorbing higher U.S. import tariffs. ONON noted the 2Q26 gross margin expansion was 
driven by stronger full-price execution, a higher DTC mix, operational efficiencies, 
favorable freight mix, and some positive FX effects. We believe the core drivers of gross 
margin improvement should remain visible through the balance of the year. We also

Three important points
1. ONON continues to invest in marketing. Despite the change in the sales 
outlook, ONON isn't changing its strategy. We believe the company will invest 
~13.5% of sales in marketing this year. This is one of the highest ratios in the 
athletic wear industry and we think will help sustain strong growth. We note 
ONON's global brand awareness is just at 30%. We expect it to reach 60% 
over time and this should be an important sales driver. Strong marketing 
investment should help ONON capitalize on this opportunity. Taking a step 
back, ONON is acting the way long-term investors should hope a company 
would respond to a macro slowdown. The wrong answer is to pull short-term 
levers to keep up appearances with Wall St. ONON is doing the opposite. The 
stock may not get rewarded in the short-term, but we think the stock will 
respond over the long-term as ONON becomes the next great global athletic 
wear brand.
2. ONON is proving its product diversification strategy is working. This is 
important because a key bear thesis tenent is On is a one-shoe brand. Given 
the company mentioned its "everyday running" styles are selling though 
slower than expected, one might have expected a bigger sales growth 
guidance change since these styles are likely considered On's "core." 
However, ONON is still guiding to over 20% ex-FX sales growth for the year 
despite the slowdown in core styles. This indicates to us the strength of the 
breadth of On's product assortment.
3. We see big share gain opportunities ahead in the US wholesale channel as 
Nike loses share. Some investors have asked why is Nike causing a problem for 
On and the rest of the industry now when it has been struggling for years? It is 
important to remember Elliott Hill took over as Nike CEO in October 2024. By 
mid-2025, he was pushing wholesale channel partners to give Nike a 2nd 
chance and take more inventory. Given long lead times, that product landed 
at retail this year. Our guess is it is not selling as well as expected and key retail 
channel partners are having to use deep discounts to clear it. Brands often 
match promotions in order to maintain market share. However, On knows 
this is a race to the bottom and has smartly decided to not participate. What 
we think happens is retailers will clear excess Nike goods. Once this happens, 
they will restock with new product. However, they won't go back to Nike 
because the sell-through isn't good and instead shift shelf space to brands 
such as on who have momentum with consumers and pricing power.

UPSIDE ($92): We assume a ~32% 5-yr. upside case EPS CAGR (FY25-30E). In our upside 
case, we assume ONON is able to fully mitigate the impacts of US tariffs. ONON proves it 
can be much more than a running footwear brand. It delivers meaningful growth in 
sports such as tennis and in categories such as apparel. Plus, the brand remains 'cool' and 
the company is able to leverage this position to significantly increase its lifestyle offering 
without sacrificing any of its credibility or authenticity as a performance sports brand. 
Stronger sales growth leads to better fixed cost leverage, resulting in better EBIT margins. 
ONON's P/E remains elevated at ~32x and we apply this multiple to our CHF 2.35 FY28E 
upside EPS, which implies a $92 upside valuation (FX 1.22 CHF/USD).
BASE ($73): We forecast a ~29% 5-yr. base case EPS CAGR (FY25-30E). In our base case, 
we contemplate modest pressure from US tariffs over FY25-FY26. Yet, ONON benefits 
from strong industry demand tailwinds and also continues to take big market share in the 
running footwear category. ONON delivers strong growth not only in Europe, but also in 
North America and China. We forecast ~18% annual sales growth through FY30. As the 
company grows, its channel mix should shift to its own website and stores, driving gross 
margin expansion. Plus, we also anticipate the company is able to leverage fixed costs. 
Both factors combine to help ONON achieve a 20.9% EBITDA margin by FY30E. We think 
ONON's EPS growth would justify a ~29x P/E and we apply this multiple to our CHF 2.10 
FY28E EPS to derive our $73 price target (FX 1.22 CHF/USD).
DOWNSIDE ($17): We forecast a ~16% 5-yr. EPS CAGR (FY25-30E). In our downside 
case, we assume ONON is unable to effectively mitigate the impacts of US tariffs. In this 
scenario, global sportswear demand slows as inflation proves not transitory. Plus, 
ONON's innovation gains limited traction with consumers, particularly in the 
performance running category. This, along with global macro challenges, and an

Valuation Method and Risk Statement

products are primarily manufactured by third party partners. Any disruption in On’s global 
supply chain would be a risk for the shares. Similarly, On’s products are primarily sold through 
third party retailers that have significant influence over end-market sales of the company’s 
products. Further, the athletic apparel and footwear industry is subject to volatility due to 
fashion cycles. Any deterioration in On’s ability to maintain a rapid pace of bringing 
compelling new product innovation to market would also be a risk for the stock. Other risks 
include slowing growth in China, global competition, and low visibility related to global 
consumer spending levels.
