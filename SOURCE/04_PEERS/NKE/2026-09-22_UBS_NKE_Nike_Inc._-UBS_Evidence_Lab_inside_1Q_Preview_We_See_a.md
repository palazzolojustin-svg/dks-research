We expect Nike's stock price to fall due to weak guidance:
Our channel checks suggest Nike's global sales growth trend has deteriorated over the 
last 3 months. We expect Nike to deliver a 5c 1Q27 EPS miss and give an implied 2Q27 
EPS outlook of 31-43c, below the Street's 53c view. We also see risk NKE mgmt. uses the 
event to reset FY27 expectations lower ahead of its November investor day.  Sentiment is 
bearish, yet our conversations with investors suggest the market underestimates the 
magnitude of the downward EPS revisions this print is likely to cause. We think Nike's Q1 
report will cause Street EPS estimates to drop and market sentiment to weaken.  The 
options market is pricing in an +/- 8.0% jump over the event vs. a 6.7% historical avg. 
move. We agree with the options market's view. 
Nike sentiment is bearish, in our view: 
UBS Quant Team's crowding data shows Nike is short-crowded relative to peers and its 
history (Fig. 23-24). Plus,  short interest has risen 20 bps since NKE reported Q4 EPS to 
6.4%, a new 5-yr. peak. Lastly, NKE's FY1 P/E is now 21x vs. its 34x past 5-yr. avg. These 
datapoints mirror our conversations with investors indicating sentiment is bearish.  We 
believe the "bars" for the event are: 1) Q1 sales down MSD% y/y; 2) Q2 sales guidance 
of -MSD% y/y; and 3) NKE lowers its CY26 sales growth outlook to down MSD% y/y 
from -LSD-MSD% previously.  With respect to FY27, the buyside consensus EPS estimate 
is around $1.55.  Thus if Nike gives an FY27 outlook which implies EPS below $1.55, then 
we think the stock price will fall.  We note our new FY27 EPS estimate is $1.30.  
Our checks suggest weak global sales and margin trends during 1Q27:
We believe Nike’s Q1 US DTC sales growth rate likely declined ~MSD% y/y and  falls 
short of the Street's -0.4% view (Figs. 93-98). In Europe, we believe EMEA DTC sales 
trends were also pressured during Q1 and likely come in below the cons. -6.7% 
EMEA DTC sales growth forecast  (Figs. 83-84).  Plus, we believe underlying trends in the 
EU wholesale channel continue to be under pressure and are underperforming peers 
(link). Finally, we believe Nike's China sales trends continue to be pressured (Figs. 58-61, 
link). Lastly, UBS pricing data suggests promotional levels of Nike product in its DTC 
channel over Q1 were elevated y/y (Figs. 38-41). Please see more data inside this report.
Valuation: We lower our price target 13% to $42 and our rating is Neutral:
Our $42 PT is based our 21x our $2.00 FY29 EPSe. Our 13% FY29 EPSe reduction drives 
our PT change.  Our multiples analysis indicates a $42 PT puts NKE's valuation in-line 
with peers on P/E, P/Sales, & FCF yield. While we see a  negative catalyst over the event, 
we  rate NKE Neutral since we expect NKE to perform in-line with peers over the NTM.

Q: Can Nike deliver +HSD% sales growth?
Not in the next two years, in our view, but maybe by FY30E. Nike will probably lose market share as it 
resets its business over the next few years. We believe Nike leaned into its lifestyle/fashion business 
too much and now it needs to rebalance its assortment back into more athletic performance 
products. This will likely take a few years to occur and means Nike may lose share in the lifestyle 
component of its business. We forecast Nike's revenue declining -3% y/y  in FY27, but growing +3% 
in FY28 and +4% in FY29, respectively.
Q: How high can Nike's EBIT margin climb?
We think Nike's EBIT margin will hover around the HSD% range over the long-term. The issue is 
Nike's attempt to reset its business back into athletic performance products should require significant 
investment within SG&A. Plus, Nike will likely experience material margin headwinds as its sales mix 
shifts towards performance and wholesale sales over the NTM. There is no easy way for Nike's EBIT 
margin to rebound, in our view. Nike will likely have to return to strong sales growth in order to 
releverage and this process could take a very long time.
Q: Is athleisure still a strong trend?
Yes. We forecast a +MSD% global CAGR, with developing markets contributing a disproportionate 
share of the gains. Athleisure benefits from the ongoing trends toward comfortable and casual attire, 
as well as the global trend toward health and wellness.
We rate Nike Neutral. We believe Nike is undergoing a major business reset that will take time to play 
out. We think this reset will limit upward EPS revision potential and probably weigh on Nike's P/E. We 
still believe Nike has the brand strength and balance sheet to ultimately return to +MSD% sales 
growth rates and a 10% EBIT margin over the long term. We forecast a ~3.5% 5-yr. EPS CAGR (FY26-
FY31E). We believe this growth rate, plus the fact Nike remains the #1 brand in the industry in terms 
of market share, justify a 21x P/E. We see a balanced upside/downside skew.
UBS Evidence Lab survey and pricing data reveal the Nike brand currently has #1 in mindshare globally 
and the company has significant room to reduce promotions.
 Our reverse DCF analysis suggests the market price assumes L-MSD% annual sales growth post 
FY27E and roughly 10-40 bps of annual EBIT margin expansion.

Sales: We model 1Q27E revenues of $11.19B, down 4.5% vs. last year and down -
1.7% on a 2-yr. basis. FX is expected to be a neutral impact to Q1E sales growth.  We 
anticipate momentum in Nike's performance business to persist, supported by strong 
momentum within Global Football around the summer '26 world cup. At the same time, 
Nike mgmt. noted it is reducing its inventory buys, reducing future sell-in. Mgmt. noted 
during the second half of its 4Q26, the company saw weaker traffic and sell through 
trends across its direct business and in sell-through trends at wholesale. Further, we 
expect ongoing pressure within Nike's sportswear and Jordan businesses. From a 
regional perspective, within EMEA, mgmt. noted trends in the region is expected to 
continue to be pressured  by elevated promotional levels, Middle East disruption, and 
ongoing softness in the sportswear category. Within Greater China, we note Nike is 
taking a more aggressive approach  in managing the sell-in of inventory in the region in 
order to foster a healthier marketplace ecosystem. We expect this strategic decision to 
continue to have a pronounced adverse impact on NKE’s topline performance in Greater 
China. Please see more detail below:

North America: We forecast 1Q27 North America sales down 0.1% y/y on a 
reported basis. We forecast FX to be a 20 bps headwind to reported revenue. This 
is equivalent to $5.015B in sales and 44.8% of revenue. We believe North America 
DTC sales are under pressure due to challenges with innovation and brand heat, 
particularly within the lifestyle segment. Web traffic data suggests US visits are up 
37%, on average during Q1, and Canadian visits are up 31% y/y (Fig. 85). We note 
this is a sequential improvement q/q for both geographies. We note visits during 
4Q rose +9% in the US and fell 16% in Canada.  Plus, Google searches for Nike in 
the US showed deceleration q/q with US searches rising 31% y/y, vs. 61% growth 
in 1Q.  Within North American secondary footwear market platforms, price 
premiums have fallen for the Nike and Jordan brands over recent months (Figs. 14-
20). Plus, Nike's Converse brand has the highest price premium, as of August  2026 
(Fig. 13).  Based on industry data, we expect DTC sales to be down ~MSD% y/y as 
Nike pulls back on core franchises, particularly Dunks, primarily in its DTC channel 
(Figs. 93-98).

EU Speciality Retail channel checks: We spoke with a senior leader of a 
large European specialty retailer. Our main takeaway was Nike is likely losing 
market share, and the turnaround is taking longer than expected. The expert is 
cautious on the outlook for Nike and believes the company's challenges extend 
beyond a typical product cycle issue. While running trends have improved and 
certain performance franchises are generating positive results, the expert 
believes gains in running are insufficient to offset continued weakness in 
lifestyle categories. The expert noted Nike continues to lose share to 
competitors including adidas, Hoka, On, and Salomon, particularly among 
younger consumers.The expert also highlighted concerns around execution in 
areas such as innovation, marketing and supply chain. Additionally, the expert 
expressed concern that Nike has become one of the most promotional brands 
in the market and noted increasing promotional activity could pressure brand 
equity over time. The expert also observed broader distribution expansion 
across channels that Nike historically avoided, creating further concerns around 
exclusivity and long-term brand positioning. Overall, the expert believes Nike's 
turnaround is taking longer than expected and sees limited evidence of a near-
term inflection in performance. Please see more takeaways here: link.

China Athletic Wear industry channel checks: Our main takeaways with an 
industry expert suggest Nike sales growth rates in China have been weak and a 
full recovery likely takes more time. The expert noted Nike and Jordan have 
been the weakest-performing major global sportswear brands in China 
recently. The expert believes sales trends deteriorated through the summer, 
with declines remaining in the -DD% range and further decelerating into 
September MTD. According to the expert, Nike's challenges are largely 
idiosyncratic.  Nike has less new product innovation in the performance side of 
this business y/y. At the same time, the company has reduced distributor 
participation in e-commerce channels, scaled back promotional support, and 
focused on improving pricing integrity. While these actions may improve the 
long-term health of the business, they are adding to near-term sales pressure. 
Inventory levels remain somewhat elevated, though the expert noted 
conditions improved between July and August. Looking ahead, Nike's recovery 
is expected to take time and will likely depend on improved product 
innovation, cleaner inventory levels, and successful execution of Nike's revised 
distribution strategy, in the expert's view. Please see more takeaways here: link.

Quant Crowding Factor Insights
Crowding Score
What's crowding? Crowding is too many of the same type of market participants holding the same or similar positions, in this 
case, a stock. Crowding can occur on the long and the short side of names. A crowding score serves as a risk control indicator. A 
high crowding score in either direction could pose a risk as there is a high chance investor positioning starts to go in the opposite 
direction. We note the crowding scores have no imposed maximum or minimum values, but the range tends to be from "-30" to 
"+30" due to the scaling and combination of inputs. In other words, the higher the score on the positive side implies the stock is 
more long-crowded. Conversely, the lower the score on the negative side implies the stock is more crowded on the short side.
Please see more information on crowding scores and crowding momentum here: Report Link.

Crowding Momentum
What's crowding momentum? Three-factor crowding momentum combines three stats: 1) Crowding score; 2) change in 
crowding, and 3) 1-month momentum. On the long side, the most crowded names that are becoming more crowded coupled 
with recent poor relative performance should do the best. On the short side, the most short crowded names that are becoming 
more short and have had relatively good recent performance should do the worst. Essentially, longs tend to outperform and shorts 
underperform. Stocks with positive momentum get a "+1" score. Stocks with negative momentum get a "-1" score. Most of the 
stocks in our Softlines coverage universe have neither positive nor negative momentum. These stocks get a "0" score.

UPSIDE ($74): We forecast a ~14% 5-year upside case EPS CAGR (FY26-31E). In this 
case, we assume demand growth accelerates in China as Nike's effort to drive more 
innovation is a success and stimulates better demand in the region. Nike's North America 
business grows at a +HSD% rate as successful new product introductions drive a surge in 
demand in this market as well. Better-than-expected sales growth allows Nike to leverage 
expenses in a bigger way than anticipated. We forecast $3.10 in FY29E upside case EPS. 
Our upside case EPS algorithm is ~5% sales growth, ~60 bps of annual operating margin 
expansion, and a ~2% annual share count reduction. We apply a ~24x upside case P/E 
multiple in this scenario as we think the market pushes the multiple higher once it sees 
Nike's sales growth rate inflecting and on a path back to M-HSD% growth.
BASE ($42): We forecast a ~3.5% 5-year EPS CAGR (FY26-31E), led by ~2.5% annual 
revenue growth, ~5 bps operating margin expansion per year, and a ~-1% annual share 
count reduction. Nike is now a turnaround stock. We believe Nike has leaned into its 
fashion products too much over the past few yeas and as a result needs to reset its sales 
mix in order to restore its image as the world's best sports brand. We believe this will 
require patience, discipline and major investment. Ongoing investment and tariffs likely 
limit margin upside. In our view, Nike will need to invest not only in its product 
assortment, but also in marketing and IT. We estimate operating margins hover in the 
HSD% range. We see the stock as worth 21x P/E in this scenario.
DOWNSIDE ($11): We forecast a -~6.5% 5-year downside case EPS CAGR (FY26-31E). 
In this scenario, we assume Nike's rebalancing takes much longer than expected to occur. 
The issue is Nike discovers that it not only lacks enough performance products, but also 
its brand isn't resonating with consumers the way it used to. As a result brands like On 
and Hoka take major market share from Nike. In this scenario, Nike's sales have to fall 
further vs. our base case before a true reset can take place, particularly within its Greater 
China region. Plus, weak sales result in more fixed cost deleverage than expected and 
require Nike to invest even more in brand marketing in order to engineer a turnaround. 
Sales fall ~-7.5% in FY27E, fall -2% in FY28E, and grow at +LSD% in FY29E and beyond. 
Nike's operating margin hovers in the ~MSD-HSD% range. We forecast $1.25 in FY29 
downside case EPS. Our EPS algorithm here is ~-1.5% sales declines, roughly 40 bps 
annual EBIT margin contraction, and a roughly flat share count. We apply a ~9x downside 
case P/E multiple.

due to fashion cycles. Any deterioration in Nike’s ability to maintain a rapid pace of bringing 
compelling new product innovation to market would also be a risk for the stock.
