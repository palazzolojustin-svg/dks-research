Nike downgraded by BofA as channel and China
pressures weigh on recovery
Author: Ian Lyall  /  First published: 15:34 25 September 2026
Bank of America (BofA) has downgraded Nike Inc (NYSE:NKE, XETRA:NKE) to 'underperform', citing worsening channel
and macroeconomic pressures.
The bank cut its price objective to $30 from $47, based on a price-to-earnings multiple of 16 times, down from 22 times
and in line with the peer average.
BofA reduced its earnings per share (EPS) estimates for the 2027 and 2028 fiscal years by 11% and 12%, respectively.
The bank now expects negative sales growth through the 2027 financial year, reversing its previous forecast of a spring
recovery.
Its 2027 fiscal EPS estimate sits 14% below Visible Alpha consensus.
BofA also lowered Nike's income rating to 8 from 7, reflecting a dividend payout ratio above 100%.
North American wholesale sales, previously a source of strength, grew 14% in the 2026 fiscal, compared with flat total
sales growth.
Sell-through is lagging sell-in in some cases, driven by declines in classic styles and new launches that are missing
expectations.
BofA said this puts forward order books at risk as retailers become less willing to bet on new products until they prove
successful.
The bank expects North American wholesale sales to begin declining in the second quarter and remain challenged
through the rest of the 2027 fiscal.
In China, Nike's reduction of partner online sales is likely to cause promotional pressure through the second quarter.
BofA's Luxury Goods team reported weak sports demand during a China field trip, with product newness failing to
resonate and excess inventory building amid low demand.
Nike shares are down 44% year to date, compared with a 12% gain for the S&P 500. Its shares gave back around 0.7%
on Friday morning.
BofA said it would watch for growing product innovation and stabilizing trends in China before becoming more positive on
the stock.
The bank acknowledged some "green shoots" in product innovation but said these have been outweighed by weakness
in larger casual categories.
