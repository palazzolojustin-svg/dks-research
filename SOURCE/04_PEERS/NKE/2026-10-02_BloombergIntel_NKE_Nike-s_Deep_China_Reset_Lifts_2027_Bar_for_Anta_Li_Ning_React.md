Nike's Deep China Reset Lifts 2027 Bar for Anta, Li Ning: React
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- RECENT EVENT REACTION: Nike's 26% revenue decline in Greater China and its
forecast of continued sales deterioration through fiscal 2027 underscore a mixed near-term backdrop
for the country's sportswear market. Its decision to cut unprofitable digital distribution, reduce
discounting and refresh aged physical storefronts could rationalize promotional discount intensity
over time. The shift would benefit domestic leaders Anta and Li Ning by fostering healthier industry
pricing and lowering Nike-led inventory clearance, but also raises their execution bar. Both Anta and Li
Ning will need to continue to localize products, sharpen brand positioning, upgrade retail experiences
and sustain innovation in running and other performance categories as Nike steps up investment in
these same areas. (10/01/26)
Anta Chases Market Share With Pricier Labels: Equity Outlook
THESIS: Anta is poised to gain market share in China even if consumer sentiment on the mainland
remains lackluster into 2027. The company is leveraging tailwinds in sports participation through
high-end brands like Descente and Kolon Sport, as well as Arc'teryx, Salomon and Wilson through its
stake in Amer Sports. If regulators approve the planned purchase of a stake in Puma, Anta will add yet
another globally recognized premium asset to its multi-brand portfolio. Anta continues to sharpen
product appeal by tailoring products closely to local tastes, while maintaining its core Anta brand as
an affordable alternative, priced at roughly half the cost of Nike and Adidas. (08/26/26)

Steeper-than-Expected 4Q Drag From China
Nike's guidance for a steeper-than-expected fiscal 4Q revenue drop in Greater China contradicted
expectations for a sequentially narrower decline from 3Q, which fared better than analysts
anticipated. This reinforces our view the company's mainland China reset will remain a near-term
sales drag as it cuts sell-in, reduces discounts and cleans up digital channels. These steps can help
profitability bottom before revenue, but suggest Nike's recovery in China will be slower and more
product-dependent than consensus' projections.
Nike's report that running, tennis, golf and ACG (outdoor) sales in China rose is encouraging. But it
suggests peers such as Anta, Puma, Li Ning and Amer Sport, which rely on these categories, could face
greater pressure from Nike as the US-listed firm invests more on the segments. (03/31/26)

Online Sales Underperform Without Discounts
Nike's effort to rebuild a premium online position in China could weigh on sales as rivals lean more
aggressively into promotional e-commerce events, especially on major marketplaces. Shang Zhi Zhen
data show Nike's January-February online markdown at 12%, the lowest among multi-sport brands
such as Adidas, Anta, Fila and Li Ning. If this strategy holds, Nike will need more innovative,
performance-led products that resonate with Chinese consumers, particularly if discretionary
spending remains selective.
Pou Sheng, one of Nike’s key distributors in China, reported flat sales but narrower discounting
through February from a year earlier. The company attributed the narrower discounts to lower
inventory burden after buybacks by partners last year, with Nike likely among them. (03/31/26)

Outdoor Segment to Get More Crowded
Nike's introduction of ACG in China targets the same outdoor tailwind as Anta, which is pushing for
sales of its Descente, Kolon Sport and Jack Wolfskin labels to surpass both its namesake brand and
Fila in 2026. With only one ACG store opened in February and a second slated for May 2026, it is still
too early to assess whether Nike is gaining traction or how effectively it can compete.
Nike’s push to tap rising outdoor participation through higher ACG investment faces intensifying
competition from Anta’s outdoor trio and Amer Sports’ Arc’teryx and Salomon, following the category’s
strong 2020-25 expansion. Early Sang Zhi Zhen-based data tracked by Bloomberg Intelligence show
average online sales growth for six major outdoor brands on China’s leading e-tailers slowing to 12%
through February, from about 35% in 2025. (03/31/26)

Anta-Puma Crowds Running Race
Anta’s Puma Bet Risks Dividend Trim to Support Running Expansion
Contributing Analysts Poonam Goyal (Retail)
Anta's €1.5 billion Puma investment will likely avoid straining its liquidity, but softer margins, a
volatile macro backdrop and heavier investment in running -- a battleground where Nike and Adidas
fight Hoka, On and Saucony -- will probably justify a temporary payout cut to preserve balance-sheet
flexibility. Anta's April 24 circular clarifies that the cost of the investment is based on 2027 revenue
and Ebitda expectations, reinforcing its bet on Puma's turnaround. (04/27/26)
Bet on Puma's 2027 Turnaround
Anta's anchoring of its €1.5 billion Puma investment to consensus estimates of the German firm's
2027 revenue and Ebitda implies a below-industry EV/sales multiple. The EV/Ebitda valuation also
trails peers such as Nike, Amer Sports and On, which have strong traction on the mainland. This
signals Anta's confidence in Puma's turnaround next year and the willingness to look beyond
disruption from potential demand volatility from geopolitical shocks and particularly Puma's 2026
"reset" or corporate spring cleaning.
With the transaction expected to become unconditional only in 4Q, Anta will probably avoid absorbing
most of Puma’s guided €50-€150 million Ebit loss in 2026. It enters after much of the wholesale
cleanup, range cuts and headcount reduction has flowed through Puma's financials. (04/27/26)

Sportswear Firms' China Exposure, Valuations
Success Depends on Puma Regaining Share
Puma's ability to regain market share in running will be crucial for Anta to maximize the return on its
29.06% stake in the Frankfurt-listed label. It won't be cheap -- winning back consumers from
challengers like On, Hoka and Wolverine's Saucony while fending off Nike and Adidas will require
heavier marketing spending, including on endorsements. A global reset should strengthen Puma's
brand equity and help revive sales momentum, particularly in the greater China region, where it
generates 7% of its global sales. Anta's application of its mainland retail and omnichannel playbook
while keeping Puma distinct from its other brands will determine the deal's success.
More evidence of progress should emerge after Puma, which aims to achieve above-industry sales
growth and healthy profits in 2027, completes its reset this year. (04/27/26)

Anta's Expanding Brand Portfolio
Dividend Flexibility Warranted Amid Uncertainty
Anta's April 24 pro forma figures -- assuming the purchase was completed on Dec. 31, 2025 -- show
that it can self-fund the Puma investment. The central question is whether it should. Softer margins,
heavier brand investment and a more volatile macro backdrop will probably justify a temporary payout
trim to preserve balance-sheet flexibility. A reduction would give Anta more room to fund China capex,
the growth of newer labels such as Maia Active and Jack Wolfskin, and Puma integration if demand
shocks, FX swings or geopolitical risks intensify.
The group generated about 16 billion yuan of free cash flow in 2025 and ended the year with roughly
32 billion yuan of net cash, with more than 75% held in bank fixed deposits. Even at a 50% payout ratio,
Anta should be able to support more than 7 billion yuan of dividends in 2026. (04/27/26)

Anta's Dividend Faces Overhang After Self-Funded Puma Purchase
Anta might lower its dividend payout below its three-year average of about 50% this year if the Puma
purchase closes before 4Q. Approvals are expected to take more than six months. Puma will likely
remain unprofitable for a second straight year as it increases global restructuring efforts over the
next eight months, though consensus sees the loss narrowing from 2025. Anta will need to equity-
account its share of Puma's losses and one-offs, diluting 2026 earnings.
Anta’s self-funded payment of 12.4 billion yuan for the purchase will absorb at least 75% of annual
operating cash inflow, driving more conservative capital allocation. Liquidity should stay ample, but
dividends may be more flexible in the near term. In 2019 Anta cut its payout ratio to 30.7% while
funding its Amer Sports deal. (04/27/26)
Anta Dividend Drivers
Puma's China Rebound Depends on Speed of Reset
Puma's ability to meet consensus' greater China growth of 8% in 2027 will depend on the pacing of its
anticipated shift toward a direct-to-customer (DTC)-led model in the market, following Anta's
acquisition of a 29% stake in the firm. Management expects wholesale partners in the region to likely
withhold new store openings due to this strategic pivot, according to its FY2025 earnings call. This
appears set to restrict wholesale sell-in this year.
Countering this wholesale weakness will likely require Puma to hike spending on marketing and to
expand its directly-operated retail network in China to accelerate growth in the DTC channel. As a
result, Puma will likely struggle to raise margins beyond pre-pandemic levels in the next nine months.
DTC revenue accounted for around 70% of Puma's China sales in 2025. (04/27/26)

Leveraging 2026 Winter Olympic
Anta, Fila Winter Olympics Halo Can Support China, Overseas Push
Anta is positioned to leverage heightened awareness of its namesake label and Fila brands following
the Milano Cortina 2026 Olympics to drive sales of higher-margin technical sportswear in China. We
expect the halo effect to improve the efficiency of marketing spending, keeping the cost-to-sales
ratio this year below consensus estimates of 9.7%, even as the firm expands beyond the mainland.
(02/23/26)
Winning Winter Olympic Spotlight With Fila
Anta's visibility at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics matched or exceeded that of Team China's
official apparel sponsor, Li Ning. All five of China's gold medalists competed in Anta- and Fila-branded
kits. Exposure extended to official Olympic proceedings, with International Olympic Committee
members also wearing Anta uniforms. Additionally, Fila-sponsored Team Netherlands rose to third in
gold-medal rankings, up from sixth in 2022.
This high visibility -- shown on every podium close-up and replay, amplified by a threefold jump in
social media engagement on the Olympics channels compared with Beijing 2022 -- provides a platform
for Anta to convert its brands' placement into premium product sales in China and support Anta's
international expansion. (02/23/26)
Milano Cortina 2026: Ranking by Gold Medals
Anta, Li Ning Could Tame Rise in Marketing Cost Ratio
Anta and Li Ning will probably contain increases in their marketing-cost-to-sales ratios if they
translate Olympic-driven brand momentum into greater demand for premium technical products.
Stronger pricing power and reduced reliance on discounting will likely allow both companies to convert
their labels' visibility into higher average selling prices and gross margins, improving operating
leverage even as absolute marketing spending remains elevated. (02/23/26)

Holiday Boost to Winter Olympics Buzz in China
China's nine-day Lunar New Year holiday, which coincided with the Winter Olympics, likely lifted
mainland viewership and social-media buzz, as time off work offset the Italy-China time difference and

Our forecasts have yet to factor in a potential 10-basis-point interest-rate cut and 25-bp cut to

Optimizing Selling Channels Can Fuel Beauty Growth
Contributing Analysts BI China Consumer Team
China's June retail sales in cosmetics expanded 12.6% year-on-year, the fastest growth in the past 20
months, vs. a 1% uptick in overall retail sales, signaling resilient beauty demand among mainland
shoppers so far in 2026. This brought 1H growth in the category up to 6.3%, making it likely that
cosmetics sales will rise by our forecast of 2.7% this year, after jumping more than 5% last year.
As China's cosmetics market matures, brands will need sharper innovation and more effective
engagement in the channels that matter most, especially online, to drive repeat purchases and
defend market share. The recovery of Hainan duty-free sales this year is likely to support global
premium brands' sales. (07/19/26)
Retail-Sales Growth (Percent)
Sustaining Online Spending Remains Critical
China's digital-commerce operators are unlikely to materially scale back incentives in 2026 if
consumer sentiment stays lackluster. Over the last 12 months, major platforms including Alibaba,
Meituan and JD.com have been intensifying a structural push to lock in habitual usage through on-
demand convenience and expedited delivery. Sustaining this behavior will likely require continued
discounts, and these companies are set to add more perks as they push agentic applications and
tools designed to cement new consumption habits.
China's online retail sales of physical goods expanded 3.6% year-on-year in June, but spending on
online services fell 1.5% -- the category's first decline on record -- after platforms pulled back
promotional subsidies from a high base. This suggests service-category volume growth will likely
decelerate through 2H as discount intensity normalizes. Unlike physical goods, where purchases can
be deferred or trade-in incentives banked for later use, on-demand services (food delivery, travel,
local services) see usage frequency drop almost immediately once cash-back and voucher support
fades.
This poses a disproportionate risk to platforms most reliant on subsidized volume generation --
notably Meituan, whose instant-retail and food-delivery order frequency has been propped up by
aggressive voucher use, and Trip.com, where travel-booking growth has leaned on promotional pricing
to offset still-cautious discretionary spending. (07/19/26)
