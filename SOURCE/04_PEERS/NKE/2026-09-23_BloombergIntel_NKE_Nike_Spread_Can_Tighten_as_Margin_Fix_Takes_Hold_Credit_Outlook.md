Nike Spread Can Tighten as Margin Fix Takes Hold: Credit Outlook
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- THESIS: Nike's curve may tighten toward equal- or better-rated retailers,
with the 10-year currently wide of its peer regression curve even as the 2030 and long-dated bonds
trade inside direct comparables. Nike's A2/A+ credit profile, $9 billion of liquidity and gross-margin
expansion beginning in fiscal 1Q27 support the potential convergence. (09/23/26)
Credit Considerations
Credit Checklist
Nike Can Outrun Headwinds in Fiscal 2027
Nike's cash and short-term investments of $9 billion exceed $8 billion of funded debt, while $3 billion
of committed facilities are undrawn. Its $1 billion November 2026 and $1 billion March 2027 maturities
are readily fundable, though Nike hasn't disclosed whether it will repay or refinance them. Fiscal 2026
revenue was flat on an as-reported basis, and down 2% currency-neutral. Operating cash flow fell 22%
to $2.9 billion and free cash flow was about $2.2 billion, with a $684 million tariff receivable
depressing year-end conversion, before it was substantially collected.
Nike's credit profile is supported by its leading position in the global athletic footwear and apparel
markets, strong geographic diversity and digital sales. (09/23/26)

Credit Drivers
Nike Can Keep Winning Despite Headwinds, $11 Billion of Debt
Even with pressured fiscal 2026 results, Nike's robust credit metrics and strong liquidity should enable
it to withstand near-term headwinds and maintain its lead in global sportswear. Nike has made
progress on clearing inventory in its wholesale channel and bringing new products and innovations to
market. But the company faces lingering tariff costs and consumer uncertainty as its turnaround
continues. (09/22/26)
Credit Metrics Solid Despite Profit Pressure
Even with debt well above pre-pandemic levels, most of Nike's credit metrics remain solid relative to
single A retail peers. Its credit profile is further supported by superior global brand recognition, strong
products and geographic sales diversity. Nike leads Adidas in global sportswear with a 14% market
share in 2025 vs. 8.7% for Adidas. Cash and short-term investments of $9 billion exceeded $8 billion of
funded debt at May 31, leaving a funded-debt net-cash position before $3.1 billion of operating-lease
liabilities. Two committed facilities totaling $3 billion and a $3 billion commercial-paper program were
undrawn. The near-term maturity stack consists of $1 billion in November 2026 and $1 billion in March
2027, followed by $1.5 billion in 2030. Nike's liquidity makes the 2026-27 maturities manageable,
though it hasn't disclosed whether they will be repaid or refinanced.
Adidas' adjusted net leverage totaled 1.6x as of 2Q (vs. a target of below 2x). Ralph Lauren's net
leverage usually trends below 1x. Target's net leverage was 1.4x at the end of fiscal 2Q26. (09/22/26)

Cash Flow Softens as Capital Returns Recede
Nike has historically generated strong cash flow and maintains a conservative financial policy relative
to peers, helping support its credit profile despite demand uncertainty and strategic execution risk.
Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow fell to $2.87 billion from $3.70 billion as working capital used $1.68
billion, including a $684 million tariff receivable that was substantially collected after year-end. Free
cash flow was about $2.18 billion after $684 million of capital spending. Nike paid $2.41 billion of
dividends and repurchased $123 million of stock, with no repurchases in 4Q. The dividend exceeded
free cash flow, though subsequent tariff collections and $9 billion of liquidity limit near-term funding
pressure. A return to large buybacks before the 2026-27 maturities are addressed would weaken
balance-sheet flexibility.

Brand Scale, Mix Cushion Turnaround
Nike's credit profile and A2/A+ ratings from Moody's and S&P are supported by its commanding brand
presence in the sports footwear and apparel market. The company's significant share in global sports
footwear (23%) and sports apparel (7.9%) as of 2025 is driven by its product offerings, athletic
partnerships and digital strategy. Its market share remains ahead of Adidas' 12% in footwear and 6.3%
in apparel. Geographic diversity (as seen on FA PGEO <GO>) also enhances Nike's credit profile.
Fiscal 2026 Nike Brand revenue was about 45% North America, 28% EMEA, 14% APLA and 13% Greater
China, while about 61% came through wholesale and 39% via Nike Direct. Wholesale revenue rose 4%
currency-neutral, while Direct fell 8% as Digital dropped 12% and store sales declined 4%. Performance
categories are gaining momentum, though management expects Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear to
remain negative in fiscal 2027 before improving in 2H. Brand scale preserves recovery capacity, and
full-price sell through determines when it converts to earnings. (09/22/26)

Nike Leads Adidas in Working-Capital Management
Adidas lags behind Nike in key working-capital management metrics. Nike maintains higher inventory
turnover than Adidas, reflecting its ability to better stock inventory types and quantities to meet
demand. Nike also boasts a lower cash-conversion cycle than Adidas, underscoring its stronger
position in the supply chain and enabling it to invest in other business areas.
Nike's cash conversion weakened in fiscal 2026 as accounts receivable consumed $1.21 billion and
working-capital changes used $1.68 billion, partly because $684 million of tariff recoveries remained
receivable at year-end. Substantially all of the receivable was collected after year-end, which should
improve fiscal 2027 cash conversion. EMEA inventory rose by low double digits, while Greater China
inventory fell by double digits as Nike reduced sell-in and promotions. (09/22/26)

Valuation
Nike Discount to Retail Curve Persists for $6 Billion of Bonds
The regression curve for Nike's $6 billion of bonds in the investment grade Bloomberg US Corporate
Bond Index trades wide of equal- or better-rated retail peers, though select maturities trade at a
premium to Target, Home Depot and Amazon.com. Despite uncertainty about consumer demand and
execution-risk headwinds, the spread gap can narrow as gross-margin expansion begins in fiscal 1Q27,
supply-chain actions lift fiscal 2027 profitability and the November 2026 maturity is addressed.
(09/17/26)
Some Nike Bonds Trading Wide, Long Bonds Tight to Peers

Earnings Review
Nike Revenue Reset Extends Into Fiscal 2028: Earnings Outlook
Contributing Analysts Poonam Goyal (Retail) & Sydney Goodman (Retail)
Post-1Q Earnings Outlook: Nike's reset is likely to keep sales pressured into fiscal 2028 as
management cuts supply in sportswear and Jordan and cleans up Greater China's digital marketplace,
even as the performance portfolio keeps growing. Fiscal 2027 revenue guidance calls for a high-
single-digit decline, with 2Q facing about 400 bps of added pressure from EMEA Cyber Week and North
America wholesale comparisons. Gross margin should remain pressured by discounts, mix, fixed-cost
deleverage and higher input costs, partly offset by supply-chain savings and better full-price
realization. China's digital cleanup is expected to take multiple seasons.
Nike expects its new cost savings program, Pace, to lower its cost base by about $2.5 billion, though
most savings are expected in fiscal 2029-30 and reinvestment could limit near-term profit upside.
(10/01/26)
