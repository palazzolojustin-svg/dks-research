Our Call
With changes to the China Digital Marketplace starting in 2027, we once again lower 
numbers beyond the current guidance window. While the strategies do make sense, there 
remains more Qs than As on the direction of the model. Stay sidelined.
 
Changing The Go-To-Market In China. Larger changes to the China market were 
announced this week. Topsports and Pou Sheng both issued individual press releases 
indicating that Nike officially gave notice "that the Group’s existing online sales of Nike’s 
products in mainland China will terminate completely with effect from January 1, 2027." 
Topsports indicated that Nike Digital sales were 22% of total revenue, while Pou Sheng 
indicated they were 15% of total revenue (and insignificant to profit).
 
Taking A Stab At The Impact. While we don't have the full picture today, we are 
estimating the impact to NKE revenue from the announced actions to be $750M-800M 
(or ~25% of NKE's China Wholesale revs). We model the incremental ~25% headwind 
to the region/channel from 3Q27 through 2Q28 - translating to a ~150-200bps 
consolidated sales impact and driving SG&A deleverage. This is partially offset by a higher 
GM as the eliminated revenue stream carries a materially lower GM profile.
 
Cutting Estimates. We are lowering estimates based on the recent news that Nike will be 
pulling digital sales to third-party sellers in China, specifically Top Sport and Pou Sheng. 
Our updated EPS estimates for FY27/FY28 are now $1.62/$1.80 (from $1.65 and $1.88 
prior) - driven by the sharp reduction in China wholesale revenue beginning in 3Q27. This 
change introduces new downside beyond the company's previous calendar-year 2026 
guide, and sets up for more revision risk into fiscal 2H.
 
Many More Questions. While we understand and appreciate the decisions being made 
for the LT, the news still leaves us with more questions: 1) What is the total size of these 
changes to revenue and profitability for the region?; 2) How will this change impact the 
retail store allocation; 3) How will partners act between now and then regarding clearing 
and ordering inventory, and where does the unsold digital product go come January?; and
4) Is there more restructuring to come in the region?
 
Why is NKE Doing This? The China marketplace is not where it needs to be, and digital 
is a key component of this and where NKE is starting their work. NKE wants to create a 
more premium digital marketplace, a stronger retail experience and more locally created 
product (these revs have become completely unproductive over the past few years). This 
should help deliver a much more consistent, elevated Nike experience. This is not about 
reducing access, it's about reducing fragmentation.

NKE Strategic Changes in China Underway
What Happened?
Earlier in the week on July 21st, two of Nike's largest partners in the Greater China region (Topsports 
and Pou Sheng) both issued individual press releases (Topsports Press Release and Pou Sheng Press 
Release) indicating that Nike officially gave notice "that the Group’s existing online sales of Nike’s 
products in mainland China will terminate completely with effect from January 1, 2027." Additionally, 
Nike published an essay (How We’re Reimagining the Marketplace in Greater China to Better Serve 
Athletes) written by the current GM of Greater China, Cathy Sparks, highlighting the details behind the 
strategy for the region.
Why is NKE Doing This?
The China marketplace has clearly faced a difficult few years for Nike that stem back to 2021 and 
the controversy over the Xinjiang region and the subsequent rise of "guochao" - that is an increasing 
preference for domestic brands over Western brands. Notably, local competition (Anta and Li Ning) 
has taken significant share with competitive product often priced at a significant discount to Nike. 
Regional sales growth was once viewed as a consistent 20%+ piece of the business, with segment 
operating margins approaching 40%. That said, over the last two years, sales have declined by low-
teens and margins have collapsed relative to history - now approaching 20%.
Most importantly to the decision this week, the market has become increasingly fragmented and 
heavily promotional, stemming primarily from the digital channel. The prevalence of markdowns 
has further driven down brand perception and muddied the customer journey (often beginning online), 
and in-turn the revenue in these channels has become increasingly unproductive for Nike. Some of 
our checks further support this notion that the brand has become an option that is only bought on 
discount, partially due to competition, but additionally a lack of brand resonance in the region (such as 
athletes that are not culturally or currently relevant) and also a lack of clear innovation.
In order to reposition Nike as the premium offering, and be able to deliver consistent messaging on 
the brand, the move does make some sense to us, though it is likely going to take time to get there, 
and the potential run-off impacts introduce unknown risks. To that point, there are clearly more 
questions that need to be answered, and a few unknowns of what the impact will be: 1) What is the 
total size of these changes to revenue and profitability for the region? (there are surely other smaller 
distributors impacted, the total size of the revenue hit is unknown, and how much spend will come out to 
reduce the deleverage on these lost sales); 2) How will this change impact the retail store allocation (how 
will Topsport manage their Nike inventory in its retail channel which represents 65%+ of its business); 3)
How will partners act between now and then regarding clearing and ordering inventory, and where 
does the unsold digital product go come January? (do partners attempt to clear through the entire 
inventory between now and January - further deepening the promotions near-term - and does Nike need 
to help pay for the cleanup?); and 4) Is there more restructuring to come in the region? (do the stores 
eventually need to be rebased lower?)
While it can be argued that this is reminiscent of its push for DTC in NA, to us this seems less like 
trying to force the customer to shift channel, and more like trying to reset what has clearly become 
an unproductive part of the business that is currently a hindrance to the work they have already 
acknowledged needs to be done on the brand.
What is the Impact to China Digital Marketplace?
Topsports indicated that Nike digital sales represented 22% of its 2026-Year End revenue of ¥25.7B, 
or $850M using current rates. Pou Sheng indicated the Nike's digital sales represented 15% of its 
2026-Year End revenue of ¥17.1B, or $385M using current rates. Combined, this is ~$1.2B in retail 
sales for the two partners.
Assuming that these products carry a 60% IMU (both businesses reported ~35% gross margin),

consolidated NKE sales. This is a conservative estimate in our view, as it doesn't include any other 
partner, it doesn't assume any impact to the retail part of the business (Topsport digital for example 
is only 30-35% of the business), and arguably the IMU conversion might be underestimating the size

Investment Thesis
Key themes surrounding the company include:
1. Over The Hills and Far Away. Elliot Hill - a 30+ year veteran of the company - rejoined NKE as 
its CEO in late 2024. The immediate areas of focus for the company include 1) reigniting their 
culture by leading with sports, putting athletes at the center of the decisions again and pivoting 
away from lifestyle; 2) shifting the organizational structure to sports-led teams to accelerate 
the product portfolio across 10 different sports spanning footwear, apparel, equipment, and 
accessories; 3) investing back towards demand creation (now at 10%+ vs. 7-8% the last 5 years) 
and shifting spend from performance marketing to brand marketing (top of funnel); 4) Rebuilding 
the integrated marketplace (wholesale) after it was de-prioritized in the post-COVID era to focus 
on digital revenue while liquidating aged inventory to clear the channel for new innovations; and 5)
investing behind specialty channels including running and football (soccer) by investing behind the 
sales organization (EKIN's) to drive engagement and improve product positioning for full-priced 
selling. While Hill remains confident that the strategies enacted (collective, "Win Now") are the 
right ones, the timeline to these actions leading to sustainable and profitable growth and market 
expansion are taking significantly longer than anticipated.
2. North America Plugged... The cleanup of the classics business has no doubt been the largest 
revenue headwind of the last 12–24 months (representing a MSD-HSD total revenue headwind), 
with our math suggesting the company has cleared out a cumulative $4B of revenue tied to the 
category. At the same time, the region has grown for the past three quarters driven by outsized 
growth in wholesale as the brand returns to the channel in a big way. As the first region touched 
by the "Win Now" actions above, it is showing the earliest signs of positive results. The key debate 
for the region today is whether the business can continue to grow as it starts to lap significant 
wholesale sell-in given commentary on weakening sell-through trends on its 4Q26 earnings call 
following a step-down in overall demand in mid-April.
3. ...But International Springing New Leaks. After several years of very strong growth, China 
slowed materially post-COVID on a number of issues. Local companies like Anta and Li Ning 
have raised their own game in terms of sneaker technology, are able to price well below Nike—
and have benefited from a cultural shift to China-centric companies. At the same time newer 
competitors like On and Hoka have also captured share in China on the rise of trail and road 
running, at the expense of basketball. These companies' focus on sport/technology has led to 
lifestyle success as well—mirroring broader trends in athletic participation. Margin recapture 
potential is significant—with operating margins down (1,700)bps over the last five years and 
(950+)bps over the last three. Most notably, NKE announced that it will be ending digital sales on 
third party platforms starting in 2027, which is indicating that the region restructuring is going 
to weigh on performance through next year. In EMEA, inventory levels are significantly elevated 
(was +20% in 3Q26 against constant currency sales of -7% and remains elevated in 4Q26), and 
there remains significant uncertainty on the brand's ability to recapture lost share, particularly in 
specialty channels which have replaced the brand with competitors that are driving healthy and 
profitable sales.
4. Limited Catalysts Between Now and "Win Now" Actions Finishing (End of CY26) and "Sports 
Offense" (Spring 2027). The timeline to finish the Win Now actions by the end of calendar 
year-end 2026 and benefits from the Sports Offense actions hitting in Spring 2027 pushes out 
expectations for the next 12 months. While the analyst day in November 2026 may provide some 
much-needed clarity to the long-term outlook, it seems to us that investors are fighting an uphill 
battle to find any torque in the underlying earnings power - even if things go well, we see little 
upside above ~$2.00 in EPS Power in the out-years and see no major catalyst for the multiple to 
expand beyond its current levels above the peer group.

The Bull vs. Bear "Tug of War"
Below, we illustrate how we think the buyside is positioning its thesis—detailing the current Bull and 
Bear arguments on NKE shares.
Exhibit 2 - The Bull vs. Bear "Tug of War"
The Bull Case….
We believe that investors who are bullish on NKE shares are focused on: (1) favorable risk-reward at 
current levels—NKE remains one the strongest softlines brands in the space that, while experiencing 
some transitory challenges, still has the key long-term opportunities intact; (2) "new" CEO Elliot Hill 
is aggressively cleaning inventory and removing stale product from the market, (3) numbers have 
been reset for FY27 and sentiment remains low; and (4) with marketplace inventories eventually clean 
NKE can refocus on growth, now driven internally through sport—which allows for focused innovation, 
marketing and community connection.
The Bear Case….
On the other hand, investors who are bearish on NKE shares are likely focused on: (1) NT outlook to 
remain choppy—global demand is very different across geographies and each on separate timelines;
(2) China is structurally challenged with headwinds persisting longer than expected and beyond 
2027; (3) NT issues exacerbated by strong competitors such as ON and HOKA in the US and local 
brands in China (along with recent strength by Salomon and Arc'Teryx); (4) global sport and fitness has 
transitioned more towards trail running and outdoor sport relative to "indoor" sports where NKE has 
historically found success; and (5) valuation >20x NTM P/E remains elevated relative to peers who are 
actually growing earnings, and there is limited upside to future earnings power (especially given the 
company's shift to a more variable distribution model).
Financial Outlook
FY27E: We forecast FY27 EPS of $1.62 (down from $1.65) representing EPS growth of +2% 
(excluding the FY26 $0.51 EPS benefit from IEEPA refunds), driven by lower sales in China and SG&A 
deleverage, partially offset by stronger GM. We're projecting total sales -2.5% and gross margin down 
~110bps to 41.8%. We expect SGA to decline -0.9% in dollars and see 56bps of deleverage to 35.3%. 
As a result we forecast operating margin to decline -175bps to 6.5%.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
NIKE, Inc. (NKE)
Investment Thesis
We rate NKE Equal Weight. 1) While the North America business is improving as the headwind from classics dissipates, the international 
market is deteriorating and is likely to drag on results near term, 2) There are limited catalysts between now and "Win Now" actions ending 
by year end and "Sports Offense" impacting Spring 2027; and 3) We see little upside to out-year earnings and very little valuation support 
for what is proving to be a muted and prolonged recovery.
Target Price Valuation for NKE
Our $40 price target is based on a ~22.5x P/E on our CY27E EPS of $1.75. This is in line with the current consensus 22x NTM P/E and in 
line with our view of a longer road for a more muted sales and margin recovery.
Risks to Our Price Target and Rating for NKE
Downside risks: (1) new innovation (along w/ franchise reset) does not drive topline; (2) global demand remains tepid; (3) the transition 
back to wholesale from DTC causes more material headwinds to profits in the NT; and 4) International headwinds accelerate and 
necessitate a larger restructuring of the business. Upside risks include (1) a positive turn in Chinese consumption trends and (2) better-
than-expected performance in US clearance activity on both sales and margins.
