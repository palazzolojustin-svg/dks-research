Nike’s Weaker Results, Softer Outlook Risk to Turnaround: React
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- RECENT EVENT REACTION: While Nike's North America business continues to
improve, weakness elsewhere and a much softer fiscal 2027 outlook suggest the turnaround may
have stalled. Sales are expected to decline by a high-single-digit percentage in fiscal 2027 after fiscal
1Q revenue missed expectations, hurt notably by a mid-20% currency-neutral decline in Greater
China. Pace, Nike's new program that builds on its existing cost-savings plan, stands to generate $2.5
billion in savings through 2031. (10/01/26)
1. Nike Turnaround Underway; Focus Shifts to China: Equity Outlook
THESIS: Nike has made progress reducing unwanted domestic inventory, creating room for market-
share gains from new products and partnerships. China, sportswear and Jordan are underperforming
and need sharper execution to stop the sales slide. CEO Elliott Hill's "Win Now" strategy matters most
in China, with its focus on innovation, sports and fresh inventory. Shorter lead times, tighter
wholesale partnerships, strategic pricing and stronger digital engagement should help the company
respond faster to demand signals and reduce discounting. Calendar 2026 sales could fall by low- to
mid-single digits, with gross margin beginning to expand in fiscal 1Q. (09/15/26)

2. Nike's China Drag, North America Gains Shape Reset: 1Q Preview
Nike's fiscal 1Q could show an uneven recovery, with growth in North America partly offsetting
weakness in Greater China and softer trends in EMEA and Asia-Pacific and Latin America. Newer
product launches and wholesale momentum may support demand, but direct-to-consumer pressure

Commentary on the upcoming Caitlin Clark's signature shoe will also be important. (09/15/26)

3. Nike's Fiscal 2027 Sales Could Slip as Gross Margin Rebuilds

sportswear and inventory cleanup offsets strength in North America, its sports focus and new product

12. Nike Gross Margin Starts to Stabilize
Nike's gross margin is beginning to stabilize as discounting eases and inventory cleanup shifts the
business toward more full-price sales, though tariffs are a wild card. Fiscal 4Q gross margin was 49.2%,
up 890 bps, aided by a 900-bp IEEPA tariff-recovery benefit. Excluding that benefit, gross margin
would have been 40.2%, down 10 bps. Inventory was flat overall, with double-digit declines in Greater
China and high-single-digit growth in Asia-Pacific and Latin America (APLA), where cleanup efforts
continue. Management expects revenue growth to moderate over the next three quarters as it
tightens purchases and reduces sell-in, but gross-margin expansion should start in fiscal 1Q.
Supply-chain initiatives, lower discounting in North America and a greater mix of full-price sales may
support a fiscal 2027 margin recovery, partly offset by incremental tariff rates of 10-15%. (07/20/26)

19. Expanding Wholesale Can Drive Gains
Nike is elevating and revamping its wholesale offerings to differentiate itself and improve
assortments in a bid to win mind and wallet share. A greater focus on breadth of products by different
business segments can help attract new shoppers and expand the reach of its innovations. A
multibrand basketball concept at Foot Locker, HomeCourt, may help spur Nike and Jordan basketball
sales, while an elevated women's fitness concept at Dick's Sporting Goods could draw more female
shoppers.
Wholesale sales rose 1% in fiscal 4Q, with a 10% gain in North America as Nike's turnaround pays off with
a growing global order book in spring and summer. Wholesale sales in China were down 19% and will
take longer to rebound. In 2027, wholesale sales may dip slightly after rising in 2026. (07/16/26)

20. Reigniting Digital Strength Can Help
Nike's home-workout apps may aid loyalty, but engagement with shoppers through its mobile retail
apps is key to driving a higher share of spending and retention. Nike's membership tools create
personalized experiences for consumers on the app, though traffic still softened in fiscal 4Q, with
digital sales down 12%. Returning its digital offering to a more premium platform -- after half of the
channel had been promotional -- is starting to pay off and is key to longer-term gains. Nike's decision
to resume selling on Amazon.com after a six-year absence reflects a strategic effort to recapture
market share and broaden distribution.
Downloads and active users for the Nike app and SNKRS app, which offers limited, exclusive sneaker
styles that often sell out, have declined recently, based on Sensor Tower data, though June data saw
some strength at SNKRS as the World Cup aided. (07/16/26)
