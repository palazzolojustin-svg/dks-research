IG and HY Spread/Yield Movers
Aerospace
Bombardier's Bonds Bracing for Another Bout of Trade Turbulence
Contributing Analysts Matthew Geudtner (Credit)
Bombardier's strong bond performance at the start of 2026 faded after the Trump administration's
January threat to revoke aircraft certifications, citing Canada's perceived treatment of Gulfstream
jets. Despite Bombardier's sizable US footprint, its bonds could follow a similar pattern until greater
clarity emerges around US-Canada trade negotiations. The overhang ultimately dissipated upon
certification of the Gulfstream models. (09/14/26)
GE's $9 Billion Engine Still Hums, Defying War’s Contagion Risk
Contributing Analysts Matthew Geudtner (Credit)
GE's cash flow, potentially exceeding $9 billion this year and next, should keep benefitting from
resilient air-travel demand in IATA's latest release despite the war in Iran. That can extend the engine
maker's outlier status among aerospace and defense peers and support potential ratings upgrades
where positive outlooks are already in place. GE's pending purchase of CPP, intended to ease castings
bottlenecks, should provide additional support. (09/14/26)
Chemicals
European Chemicals Face Rhine and Power Costs in Tough 3Q
Contributing Analysts Timothy Riminton (European Basic Materials)
European chemical operations look set for a challenging 3Q, with substantial cost pressures due to
low Rhine river levels, as well as surging gas and power prices. The seasonally weak quarter for
volumes is likely to be exacerbated by the physical disruption of the river, as well as continuing
geopolitical uncertainty. Chemical margins haven't responded -- instead significantly deteriorating --
meaning prices aren't an offset to higher costs. Bulk and intermediate producers look most exposed,
while those with large US operations should at least see mid-cycle level earnings in that region. Any
boost from a tightening Asian market looks to be coming too late to support 3Q results. (09/15/26)
Communications
How AT&T, Charter, Comcast Bonds Could Overcome Fear of SpaceX
Contributing Analysts Stephen Flynn (Credit)
AT&T, Charter and Comcast notes are trading at wider spreads than peers and could outperform if
fears of a SpaceX-led spike in broadband and wireless competitive intensity are overblown. AT&T and
Charter are striving to reduce their leverage ratios after recent M&A pushed up debt, and Comcast
expects to maintain a strong balance sheet after the spinoff of its NBCUniversal subsidiary in mid-
2027. Excess returns for AT&T, Charter and Comcast bonds have been flat to negative this year,
lagging the Bloomberg US Corporate Bond index by 52-184 bps. (09/14/26)

4. Cinemark Momentum Gains on Box Office, Cash Flow: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Stephen Flynn (Credit) & Suchi Trivedi
THESIS: Cinemark's operating and financial momentum continues to strengthen as a recovering box
office and higher attendance support revenue, Ebitda and cash flow. There may be potential for
ratings upside, with Moody's positive outlook and leverage at the low end of management's 2-3x
target, while ample liquidity provides room for higher shareholder returns. Cinemark bonds
(B2/BB/BB-) trade modestly tighter than double-B consumer-discretionary peers, likely due to the
company's solid credit profile. (09/14/26)
Consumer
5. Ralph Lauren No One-Trick Pony in Luxury Retail: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Mike Campellone (Credit)
THESIS: Ralph Lauren's A3/A- rated bonds trade wide of peers and could compress as the company's
pricing power, conservative balance sheet and less price-sensitive shoppers underpin growth. These
strategies have supported margin gains and kept leverage low. With the company's bonds due in 2030
trading at spreads closer to BBB rated retail peers, spreads could narrow on the brand's resilient
customer base and the company's disciplined financial policy. (09/15/26)
6. Royal Caribbean Trades Range-Bound Since Upgrade: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Jody Lurie (Credit)
THESIS: Royal Caribbean's (Baa2/BBB) leverage may hold near 3x, but further delevering could take
longer than we initially expected due to fallout from the Iran war, energy-price volatility and inflation.
Solid advanced bookings and management's comments about onboard spending trends support cash
flow projections and will help Royal's bonds stay tight vs. similarly rated consumer bonds. This may be
offset by limited momentum for its credit metric and the sector, as debt cuts have taken a backseat
to shareholder rewards. Expansion into river cruises and land-based offerings may also weigh on
cash. (09/16/26)
7. McDonald's Weak US Traffic Challenges Spreads: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Jody Lurie (Credit) & Keerthana Kothapalli
THESIS: McDonald's bonds (Baa1/BBB+) trade about 25-30 bps tighter than the Bloomberg US triple-B
consumer cyclicals index, but softer consumer spending and weaker US traffic mean little room for
spread tightening. Elevated capital spending and shareholder returns could prevent deleveraging
below management's 3x target, so sustained recovery in US guest counts is the key spread catalyst.
Strong margins, franchise cash flows and balance-sheet positioning could provide a cushion. (09/18/26)

Transocean Refinancing Could Add Extra Lift to Unsecured Bonds
Contributing Analysts Spencer Cutter (Credit)
Even after a strong rally following the Feb. 9 announcement that it plans to acquire Valaris in a $6.2
billion all-stock deal, Transocean's unsecured bonds may have additional room to outperform. An
improving credit profile and offshore drilling market coupled with the tightest credit spreads in at
least seven years may enable Transocean to refinance its secured notes as well as Valaris' second lien
bonds once the merger closes. (09/11/26)
Cheniere Partners Sets Stage for $4.7 Billion Expansion Project
Contributing Analysts Spencer Cutter (Credit)
The signing of a $4.7 billion construction contract with Bechtel has set the stage for an expansion
project at Cheniere Energy Partners' Sabine Pass facility, with a final investment decision expected in
early 2027. Ebitda is expected to remain at about $3.8-$4 billion a year through 2028, though debt
incurred to fund the project's capex could pressure leverage until the expansion is completed.
(09/17/26)
Financials
Goldman Leads Record TLAC Bond Sales, Fueling Capital Markets
Contributing Analysts Arnold Kakuda (Credit) & Nicole Castelblanco (Credit)
Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead big US banks' $188 billion of senior total loss-absorbing
capacity (TLAC)-eligible debt issuance -- a record pace through September. This is stoking
persistently brisk trading and investment-banking activity, but the active debt sales have weighed on
their bond curves, which trade wider than those of rivals. (09/28/26)

Contributing Analysts Himanshu Bakshi (Credit)

the key risk now centered on regulatory validation rather than implementation. TD remains the best
capitalized among peers, with a 14.3% CET1 ratio, while its gross impaired-loan ratio is the lowest and
its allowance-for-credit-loss ratio exceeds most rivals. (09/15/26)

9. Boston Scientific's Debt Paydown Won't Come Easy: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Jean-Yves Coupin (Credit) & Keerthana Kothapalli
THESIS: Boston Scientific's credit profile could weaken as the $14.5 billion Penumbra acquisition
pushes up leverage toward mid-3x, just as organic growth slows and operating disruptions test
execution. Robust free cash flow and a 2.25-2.5x leverage target support a path back to mid-2x by
end-2027, though bonds may stay wider than peers as new issuance and execution risk linger.
(09/17/26)
10. Baxter's Bond Rally to Be Tested by Energy Costs: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Jean-Yves Coupin (Credit)
THESIS: Baxter bonds have outperformed peers, reacting to management's focus on debt reduction
to preserve its investment grade status. This is reflected in the valuation discount on its bonds with
coupon step-up, which indicate a low downgrade risk amid a recovery in its stock price from historic
lows in 1Q. Yet persistently high energy prices remains a key risk for Baxter's margins and cash flow
amid tight hospital budgets and heightened competition. Further pressure from these headwinds
could help curb the discount on these bonds relative to the rest of Baxter's capital structure.
(09/17/26)
Technology
11. $9 Trillion AI Question: Can Markets Fund the Capex Growth?
AI infrastructure spending could approach $9 trillion cumulatively by 2031 as hyperscalers keep
ratcheting up capex plans, yet this trajectory isn't certain, as outer-year consensus increasingly
assumes spending growth that looks difficult to sustain. Recent guidance reinforces our view that
2026 outlays could rise almost 100%, with pressure extending into 2027, even as weaker free cash
flow increases the need for financing. Deep markets for bonds, equities, leases, project finance and
customer prepayments should absorb the shortfall, keeping the build-out financeable, even if
spending ultimately undershoots current long-term expectations. (09/15/26)

13. Oracle Force Majeure Tests Timing, Not AI Demand: Credit Outlook
Contributing Analysts Suchi Trivedi
THESIS: Oracle's force majeure notice for Project Jupiter looks more like a timing issue than a sign of
weaker AI demand, supporting our view that it can maintain high grade ratings, and recent bond and
CDS widening may be overdone. Delayed payments could ease near-term cash burn and funding
needs, though a later start would push out revenue and Ebitda growth and raise uncertainty around
monetizing Oracle's AI investments. Its $664 billion of RPO provides strong visibility, but conversion
into revenue and cash flow is critical as heavy fiscal 2027 capex keeps free cash flow negative and
borrowing needs elevated.
Greater funding clarity, including likely debt issuance in 1H27, and continued operating improvement
could support spread tightening and relative outperformance vs. megacap hyperscaler tech peers as
execution risk recedes. (09/26/26)
Broadcom's AI-Fueled Rally May Eventually Lead to Improved Value
Contributing Analysts Suchi Trivedi
Broadcom's revenue diversity and strong cash flow support relative value vs. technology peers as the
company's rapid deleveraging improves credit quality. Booming AI demand should sustain cash
generation, providing Broadcom capacity to reduce leverage while funding larger shareholder
returns. Continued balance sheet improvement could support further ratings upgrades and spread
convergence toward technology peers that trade tighter. (09/17/26)

15. Covenants Under Stress – Serta, Aston Martin, Xerox: BI Webinar
Contributing Analysts Negisa Balluku (Litigation) & Philip Brendel (Credit)
An evolution in transaction structures and conflicting judicial interpretations are testing the meaning
and limits of creditor protections within debt documents while transforming covenant risk for
leveraged-finance investors. Bloomberg Intelligence Analysts Negisa Balluku, Phil Brendel and Alex
Diaz-Matos will examine Aston Martin, Xerox and Optimum Communications use of IP, joint ventures
and novel entity structures to raise financing, alongside recent court rulings in the Serta and Del
Monte cases concerning "pro rata" provisions, including Serta's approach to calculating damages
arising from the unraveling of liability-management transactions ("LMEs").
The discussion will explore the varied sources of flexibility in debt documentation in the face of
J.Crew blockers, Serta protections and other restrictions on LMEs. (09/18/26)
