Gen Z and Millennials' Affinity for Nike, DTC Purchases Endures
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Making up almost a quarter of the US population millennials are propelling
sneaker-market momentum, with over a third planning to boost spending, focusing heavily on Nike,
Adidas and New Balance. Bloomberg Intelligence's latest sneaker survey shows lifestyle and athletic
sneakers dominating Gen Z and millennials' planned purchases, driven by men who buy more
frequently. DTC remains important, but multibrand retailers are gaining popularity, driven by
convenience. (06/08/26)
1. Millennials, Gen Z Plan to Spend More on Sneakers
Though most consumers expect their sneaker spending to remain steady in the next six months, with
60% of overall respondents indicating no change, a significant portion of Gen Z and millennials plan to
increase their outlay on both athletic and lifestyle sneakers. Our survey of more than 1,000 US
consumers shows that over a third of millennials and Gen Z anticipate spending more than last year
on athletic and lifestyle sneakers. Male Gen Z shoppers lead, showing a greater intent to boost
spending than their female counterparts.
Planned sneaker spending in the next six months centers on shoes that cost $100-$250, the
preferred budget among all generations and genders. Younger men are more inclined to spend more
than $250, reflecting a willingness to invest in premium, performance or collectible sneakers.

3. Over 50% of Millennials Plan to Buy Nike
Nike remains the dominant planned sneaker purchase across nearly all demographics, particularly
among younger consumers. In the latest survey, 51% of millennials and 56% of Gen Z respondents said
they plan to buy Nike sneakers in the next six months, reinforcing the brand's strong cultural
relevance and broad appeal. Adidas remains a clear No. 2 among younger shoppers, while the No. 3
position varies by generation.
Performance-oriented brands also continue gaining traction. New Balance maintains broad cross-
generational appeal, Hoka continues benefiting from comfort and performance trends, and Asics
showed improving momentum vs. prior surveys, particularly among millennials. Meanwhile, Skechers
continues to skew older, with especially strong purchase intent among baby boomers. (06/08/26)

4. Emerging Fashion Trends Could Spur Sneaker Demand
As sneaker trends continue shifting toward lifestyle, versatility and fashion-driven silhouettes,
consumers remain focused on comfort and everyday wearability. Casual and lifestyle sneakers
continue to lead planned purchases across generations, with roughly half of Gen Z and millennial
consumers planning to buy a pair in the next six months. The data highlights sustained demand for
both lifestyle and performance footwear, with younger consumers driving category growth through
diverse preferences spanning athletic, casual and fashion-oriented styles.
Interest in fashion, dress and work shoes also remains elevated among millennials and Gen Z
consumers, reinforcing the growing opportunity for footwear brands to blend comfort, versatility and
style through slimmer profiles, mixed-use sneakers and other lifestyle designs. (06/08/26)

Replacement purchases continue to be the primary driver of sneaker demand, with nearly half of
consumers saying their last purchase was driven by the need to replace worn-out shoes. However,
younger consumers remain significantly more driven by style and trends. More than 37% of Gen Z and
millennial respondents said they purchased sneakers to acquire a style or color they didn't already

Older shoppers, particularly baby boomers, remain far more practical in purchasing behavior, with

Top Reasons for Buying (by Generation)
6. Millennials Are Driving Sneaker-Purchasing Volume
Gen Z and millennials continue to lead sneaker-purchase volume, though buying behavior has
moderated vs. earlier surveys. In the latest poll in May 2026, a little over 30% of Gen Z and 45% of
millennials said they bought four or more pairs in the past 12 months, well above Gen X and baby
boomers, where a majority purchased just one to three pairs annually. Millennials remain the
strongest volume buyers overall, with 11% acquiring seven or more pairs, reflecting stronger
engagement with both athletic- and lifestyle-footwear categories. Gen Z still shows high purchase
frequency, though the share of those buying more than seven pairs has fallen, suggesting more
selective spending behavior vs. 2025.
Women in all age cohorts generally buy fewer sneakers than men, though millennial women stand out,
with 32% purchasing 4-6 pairs. (06/08/26)
