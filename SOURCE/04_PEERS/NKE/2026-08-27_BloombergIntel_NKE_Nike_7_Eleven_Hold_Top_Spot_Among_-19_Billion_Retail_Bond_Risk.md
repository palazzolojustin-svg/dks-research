Nike, 7-Eleven Hold Top Spot Among $19 Billion Retail Bond Risk
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- The amount of retailer bonds facing downgrade risk has fallen to $19
billion this month, led by bonds issued by Nike, 7-Eleven, Genuine Parts and Kohl's. The volume of at-
risk retailer bonds has declined materially this year from a peak of almost $72 billion a year ago.
(08/27/26)
1. Easing Retail-Bond Risk, Rating Threats Linger for Some
Bonds of retailers facing negative rating action fell to $19.4 billion in August, about $1.7 billion lower
than last month's total after Moody's revised its outlook to stable from negative for VF Corp's Ba3
bond rating on Aug. 20. Moody's took similar rating outlook action for ARKO's B3 bond rating on Aug.
21 amid the company's improved credit metrics via debt reduction and earnings improvement.
QVC Group faced downgrades in April after it filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on April 16 amid the
company's elevated debt load and declining Ebitda. Prior to the bankruptcy announcement, the
company disclosed it had breached its 4.5x net leverage financial-covenant threshold. (08/26/26)

2. QVC, Saks, Among Notable Retail Bankruptcies of Past, Present
Consumer discretionary topped the 2025 list of sectors with the most bankruptcies, according to
Bloomberg data, driven by companies with high leverage, elevated debt-servicing costs and weaker
consumer demand -- pressures also seen in 2023 and 2024. Retailer bankruptcies over the past year
included Walker Edison Furniture, Saks Global Enterprises, with $3.4 billion in liabilities, Lycra, QVC
Group, West Marine and Sleep Number.
Excluding asset-based loan recoveries, par-weighted first lien recoveries for retail loan and bond
claims of at least $100 million averaged only about 49% from 2016-25, according to data from Fitch.
The retail and restaurants sector had a 34.2% bond recovery rate in 1987-2025, the sixth-lowest
among the 16 sectors assessed by S&P. (09/29/26)

3. High Yield's Mixed Ratings Bag
Revenue and cash-flow pressures amid elevated inflation have impacted most high yield retailers
over the past couple of years, and increased downgrade risk as credit metrics erode. S&P changed its
negative watch on Under Armour to a negative outlook in February as its credit metrics and
operating performance take longer to recover. VF's negative outlook from Moody's was resolved on
Aug. 20 as the company's credit metrics improved via debt repayment. While sales trends at Vans
continue to face pressure, Moody's expects VF's leverage to decline further over the next year. The
rater assumes VF will repay its $500 million bond maturing in April 2027 with internal sources.
Though Kohl's has a negative outlook from Fitch, Moody's changed its outlook to positive from stable
amid better-than-expected operating performance in 2025 and improved liquidity. (09/29/26)

4. High Grade Rating Tone Largely Stable, Some Outliers
Ratings pressure for investment grade retailers is largely due to execution missteps and prolonged
turnarounds. Nike was downgraded one notch to A2 at Moody's in November 2025, following a
downgrade at S&P in July 2025 to A+ from AA-. In April, S&P changed its outlook to negative. The sports
retailer's efforts to expand margins is facing an adverse macroeconomic environment. 7-Eleven's
negative outlook for its Baa2 rating from Moody's persists amid a difficult operating environment in
the US convenience store space.
Target was placed on negative outlook by Fitch in June 2025 as the retailer established an
"Acceleration Office" to hasten a growth revival, but the rater withdrew its rating for the company on
May 6. (09/29/26)
