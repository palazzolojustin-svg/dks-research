Nike Discount to Retail Curve Persists for $6 Billion of Bonds
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- The regression curve for Nike's $6 billion of bonds in the investment
grade Bloomberg US Corporate Bond Index trades wide of equal- or better-rated retail peers, though
select maturities trade at a premium to Target, Home Depot and Amazon.com. Despite uncertainty
about consumer demand and execution-risk headwinds, the spread gap can narrow as gross-margin
expansion begins in fiscal 1Q27, supply-chain actions lift fiscal 2027 profitability and the November
2026 maturity is addressed. (09/17/26)
1. Some Nike Bonds Trading Wide, Long Bonds Tight to Peers
The curve of Nike's index-eligible bonds (A2/A+) trades slightly wider than the regression curve of
single A and AA rated peers, with spreads at the 10-year point of the curve 6 bps wider vs. the
regression curve of peers. Though Nike's bond due in 2030 traded at a 20-bp discount to Target
(A2/A) in August 2024 -- a spread relationship we believe didn't reflect Nike's historically solid credit
profile -- Nike's bond now trades at a 2-bp premium. Nike's bonds maturing in 2046 and 2050 traded
wide of the peer curve earlier this year, but are now at a slight premium. Nike's bond due in 2050
trades 4 bps inside of Home Depot's (A2/A) similar maturity vs. a 6-bp discount in May 2026. (09/17/26)
