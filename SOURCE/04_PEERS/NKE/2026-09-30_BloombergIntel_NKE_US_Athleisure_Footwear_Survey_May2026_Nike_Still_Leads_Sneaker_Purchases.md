Nike can widen its lead as household budgets tighten and sneaker 
demand weakens, with BI survey data showing economic stress is 
hurting discretionary purchases more than brand preference. Lower
-
income consumers are under the most pressure, with 19% of those 
earning under $50,000 not expecting to buy footwear in the next six 
months and 38% waiting for discounts or sales, up from 31% a year ago. 
Nike remains the top planned purchase across income levels, 
demographics and occasions, suggesting its recent challenges reflect 
execution more than brand affinity. That sets it apart as spending slows, 
with the World Cup offering a chance to build further loyalty. Adidas 
remains No. 2, while New Balance and Hoka skew toward middle
- and 
higher -income shoppers, reinforcing the divide between premium 
demand and value
-seeking.

BI's survey of athleisure footwear purchasing and wearing behavior among US consumers, conducted jointly with 
Attest on May 6 -15, had a sample size of 1,191, of whom 681 purchased athletic sneakers in the past 12 months and 
469 bought lifestyle shoes. Factors such as geographical location, gender and age were controlled to keep sample
