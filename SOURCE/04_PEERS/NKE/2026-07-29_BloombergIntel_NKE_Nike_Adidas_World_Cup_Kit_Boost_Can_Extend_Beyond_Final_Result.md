Nike, Adidas World Cup Kit Boost Can Extend Beyond Final Result
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Nike and Adidas can turn World Cup kit dominance into post-tournament
soccer gains, with Adidas' €1.5 billion in event sales, topping its initial €1 billion expectation, and the
Spain-Argentina final all-Adidas matchup reinforcing demand. An increase in online searches for Nike's
USA kit adds North America momentum, while Puma's Morocco and Switzerland runs widened its
exposure. (07/29/26)
1. World Cup Kits Extend Nike, Adidas Lead
Nike and Adidas' World Cup kit dominance and product launches position both to drive brand
momentum and wallet share, with Adidas-backed Spain beating Adidas-sponsored Argentina in the
final. Adidas' bestselling national-team jerseys were Mexico and Argentina, though there was strong
demand for Spain gear after it won the title. Nike's on-field visibility came from England's third-place
finish and France's fourth, though Brazil's Round of 16 exit may have weighed on gains from its
highest-profile sponsorship. Nike's Kylian Mbappé and Erling Haaland and Adidas-sponsored Lionel
Messi and Lamine Yamal added player-led visibility, supporting kit and soccer-collection demand
alongside Adidas' jersey sales and online search momentum for Nike's USA gear.
Next year's women's world cup remains an added opportunity for athleisure brands to broaden their
reach in the sport. (07/29/26)

3. Adidas Sales, Nike Demand Extend Cup Lift
Adidas' World Cup sales upgrade and Nike's off-field demand suggest soccer momentum can extend
beyond tournament kits, especially in North America over the next few years. Adidas now expects
about €1.5 billion in event-related sales vs. more than €1 billion previously, with jerseys selling at 4x
the pace of the last World Cup and balls 2x. Its US denim jersey collection, inspired by the 1994 World
Cup, was also one of the summer's strongest apparel releases.
Nike had the top-searched kit in April-June, with USA averaging more than 14,000 weekly searches,
and the highest pre-tournament media-impact value, supporting soccer-collection awareness ahead
of its Germany kit switch in 2027. (07/29/26)

4. Puma Needs Fan Conversion After Cup Runs
Puma's World Cup exposure trailed Nike and Adidas, but Morocco and Switzerland's quarterfinal runs
gave the brand sharper visibility beyond its 11-team roster. Adidas stayed visible through the Trionda
official match ball and title-winning Spain kit, while Nike's reach was reinforced by England, France and
strong USA kit searches. Nike is also in exclusive talks with the Union of European Football Associations
for match-ball rights in 2027-31, which would end Adidas' Champions League run, and will outfit
Germany from 2027.
Puma still has room to gain if Future 8 and Ultra 5 Ultimate cleats, kit storytelling and distribution
convert World Cup awareness into repeat purchases by fans. (07/29/26)
