FY1Q27 Preview: The 5 Key
Debates
We believe there are 5 key debates investors need answers to
in order to assess progress to NKE's turn. NKE's turn is
following the classic "margins before sales" recovery, and we
think the path to return to sales growth will not be linear.

The 5 Key Debates. From our perspective, FY1Q27 is likely less about the headline revenue and
EPS results and more about evidence that Nike's "Sport Offense" strategy is beginning to
translate into cleaner marketplace inventories, improved wholesale productivity, and
stabilization in China. Management already guided FY1Q27 revenue down low-to-mid single
digits while expecting gross margins to inflect positive for the first time in the turnaround. We
believe investors are looking for evidence that the turnaround is progressing toward a more
sustainable trajectory. The key debates surround: 1) meaningful improvement in China, 2)
durable running momentum, 3) progress in Sportswear, 4) continued wholesale recovery, and 5)
a credible path to margin expansion. If investors receive positive signals across those areas,
confidence in a FY27-FY28 earnings recovery should improve meaningfully. If not, then the
current narrative of NKE remaining "stuck" in a longer and more painful turnaround while
competitors continue gaining share in key categories remains. Investors should expect "margins
before sales" with OM drivers below the top line and muted top line recovery.
Broad lifestyle athletic footwear slowdown, product newness is a must. For the past couple
of years, investors have debated whether lifestyle athletic footwear slowing was a Nike problem,
a consumer problem, or a macro problem. Lifestyle athletic is slowing and we believe it is a
product cycle problem with lack of innovation to drive urgency to purchase. DKS described a
lifestyle industry lack of newness impacting Foot Locker, driven by slowing legacy/retro lifestyle
product, weak launches, elevated inventories, and an increasingly promotional marketplace.
Importantly, performance running remains healthy at core Dick's Sporting Goods, which tells us
this is not a demand problem but a newness problem and that the footwear market, much like
apparel, is becoming increasingly "zero sum." DKS management explicitly cited slowing legacy
lifestyle silhouettes, fewer launches, retro fatigue, and MAP breaks from inventory buildup. At
Nike, Sportswear remains the weak point even as performance running inflects positive. We
await lifestyle newness from NKE, even though we believe operating margins may be near the
bottom, but investors are unlikely to underwrite a NKE turnaround until they see successful new
lifestyle franchise launches.

Greater China likely remains the largest downside to revenue. Greater China remains the
most significant drag and key friction point in the turnaround story with FY4Q26 revenues down
-12% reported (-17% CC) and EBIT down -20%, with no clear near-term inflection, despite the
company continuing to work on reducing inventory, controlling the use of promotions, and
ultimately improving the health of the marketplace. We acknowledge that the pace of progress
in the region is disappointing, as the reset, while necessary, is proving both deeper and longer
in duration, consistent with our prism curve analysis that positions Greater China firmly in the
“TRAP” phase (Figure 2). Management’s outlook reinforces this caution, guiding to continued
subdued trends in China and overall company sales down low-to-mid-single digits from FY4Q26
to FY1H27, with continued pressure on Sportswear and uneven global demand.
Further Greater China reset actions. The company recently announced that, beginning
January 2027, NKE plans to move its China digital sales toward official flagship destinations
(Tmall, JD.com and Douyin, plus Nike.com.cn and the Nike App) and away from partner
operated stores. Two retailers, Topsports and Pou Sheng, confirmed complete termination of
their China online NKE sales, which leads to near-term top line pressure due to the loss of
wholesale sales. However, it is important to note that the online-rights change does not begin
until CY27, so it should not have a direct impact on FY1H27 results. In our view, the action to
remove product from online partner websites will be a near-term headwind but over the long-
term it will reduce fragmentation, bring inventory/promotional control back to NKE, and elevate
the brand image.
Assessing near-term risk versus long-term opportunity. At current valuation levels, NKE
reflects a turnaround/restructuring story, replacing its historical positioning as a premium
global growth story. The key debate is not whether a turnaround is underway, but whether
margins get worse before getting better or NKE can return to growth with double-digit operating
margins. In our view, today's negative sentiment centers on two key issues: 1) structural
pressure in Greater China, and 2) competitive share loss in running and lifestyle. Thus, the path
to a NKE re-rating is dependent on clear, sequential evidence across a defined set of operating
metrics. If NKE can recover, then current stock levels represent a very attractive risk/reward
over a multi-year horizon. However, current data points suggest we are at the apex of
uncertainty with little investor appetite to underwrite NKE's resurgence.
A playbook for recovery. We believe that current NKE valuation discounts prolonged structural
Greater China weakness, margin pressure, and competitive share loss globally. Without
concrete evidence that the "Sport Offense" strategy is working, investor sentiment hinges on a
debatable narrative, which makes it difficult to prove or disprove NKE's fundamental turn. Key
tenets of our recovery thesis for NKE include: 1) North America early stabilization in wholesale
should drive DTC recovery; 2) Greater China stabilization is sufficient for NKE stock to work; 3)
sport-led innovation beyond running success can halo out to rebuild brand equity; and 4)
margins that are at-or-near trough with margin improvement before sales recovery.
Adjusting estimates; more GM visibility, less sales confidence; lowering PT to $48 (from
$52). Our FY27, FY28, FY29 adj. EPS estimates are $1.57, $1.94, and $2.37 (from $1.55, $1.90, and
$2.24), versus Bloomberg consensus estimates of $1.68, $2.12, and $2.48, respectively. We
adjust our estimates and increase our GM forecasts offset by lower sales, as we have less
conviction in a near-term top-line turn. Our PT of $48 (from $52) is based on 22x (from 25x),
applied to our CY28 EPS estimate of $2.17 (from $2.08). We lower our multiple given increasingly
slower turnaround progress on sportswear innovation and macro uncertainty. Our valuation
multiple of 22x remains a premium to the current NTM P/E multiple of 19.9x, as we believe the
company may be nearing trough gross margins, although we have less confidence in a
sustained return to positive sales growth in the near term.

The 5 Key Debates: Bull vs. Bear Evidence
Debate #1. Greater China remains the most important swing factor
Nike's FY4Q26 Greater China revenue declined 17% constant currency and EBIT fell 20%.
Management continues to characterize China as being in a deeper and longer reset phase with
no clear inflection point. Inventory clean-up, reduced promotions and store refresh efforts are
underway, but traction remains limited. Investors are increasingly questioning whether China is
a cyclical issue or a structural share loss to domestic champions.
Bull evidence:
• Constant-currency declines moderate meaningfully versus FY4Q26.
• Sequential improvement in sell-through and inventory.
• Commentary indicates improved traffic trends and premium positioning.
Bear evidence:
• Revenue deterioration remains double-digit.
• Continued reliance on markdowns.
• Further reductions in wholesale shipments to clean inventory.
Debate #2. Running remains Nike's strongest proof point
Nike Running has exceeded 20% growth for multiple quarters and management has highlighted
market share gains in North America and Europe. Vomero, Pegasus, Structure and post-recovery
Mind are clear examples of successful innovation translating into demand.
Bull evidence:
• Running growth remains >15%.
• Additional share gains versus run competitors.
• Positive commentary around Mind, Peg Premium and future innovation pipeline.
Bear evidence:
• Running growth decelerates sharply.
• Innovation benefits remain concentrated to a few products.
• Incremental competition from ONON and HOKA and other new players such as Salomon and
Altra weighs on market share.
Debate #3. Sportswear remains the biggest drag
Nike continues rightsizing Air Force 1, AJ1, and Dunk inventories. DKS recently stated that Foot
Locker is experiencing persistent weakness in legacy lifestyle categories and promotional
pressure across retro and lifestyle product remains elevated.
Bull evidence:
• Commentary that Air Force 1, Air Jordan 1 and Dunk inventory normalization is largely
complete.
• Air Max, lifestyle running silhouettes and women's product gain traction.
• Full-price sell-through improves.

Debate #4. Wholesale recovery vs. clean marketplace discipline
Nike has deliberately rebuilt relationships with wholesale partners. North America wholesale

Debate #5. Gross margin inflection

• Gross margin exceeds guidance.
• Lower promotional intensity.
• Product mix shifts toward performance and innovation.
Bear evidence:
• Gross margin misses despite easy comparisons.
• Additional markdown activity.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: NKE is Executing 2
Competing Turnarounds
"Situational awareness" and the PRISM cycle. When assessing highly complex, multi-faceted
turnarounds, we often pause and take a step back to reflect on a company's "situational
awareness." Those of you who follow our research know that our construct is called the Retail
PRISM Cycle (see Figure 1 and Figure 2). The construct has three distinct phases of recovery:
Phase 1 called Reset (aka the TRAP in our PRISM curve) where sales and margins are both under
pressure and management strategy is on defense; Phase 2 called Rebase (aka the ESCAPE in our
PRISM curve) where merchandise margins rebase and begin to recover and management
strategy turns to offense; and Phase 3 called Regrow (aka the HUNT in our PRISM curve), where
strong product, marketing, and brand recovery drive sales acceleration in concert with full-
price-driven merchandise margin expansion. This is the moment where profits and operating
margins can explode to the upside, driven by both leverage and GM. This is what we track
metrics toward to gauge a fully-expressed recovery.
North America in the "HUNT" Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
This phase may take place over multiple years as the company enters its sweet spot.

Scenario Analysis & Valuation Framework
Methodology. We anchor valuation to forward earnings power, not peak- or trough-cycle
profitability. We use a 22x NTM P/E multiple reflecting what we believe to be near-trough
margins and EPS. Historically, NKE has traded at a median multiple of 27.5x, 29.2x, and 29.1x on
a 3-, 5-, and 10-year median multiple basis, respectively.
Base Case Scenario. Our base case assumes modest flat to low single-digit top-line growth and
margin expansion as the driver of earnings growth, as North America continues to normalize
and China progresses through its reset.
 
Downside Scenario. Assumes prolonged China pressure with limited margin recovery. Reflects a
conservative multiple below long-term averages. We view this as a reasonable worst-case
outcome under current visibility, with downside increasingly constrained at these levels.
Upside Scenario. Assumes successful execution of regional resets. Margin recovery driven by
cleaner inventory, healthier wholesale dynamics, and DTC stabilization. Multiple re-rating
supported by renewed confidence in sustainable earnings growth.
