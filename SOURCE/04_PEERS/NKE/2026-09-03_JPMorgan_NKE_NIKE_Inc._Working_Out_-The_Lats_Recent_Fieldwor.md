Following our recent global channel work and lateral reports (DKS, JD Sports, Pou 
Sheng), we thought value-add to share our key takeaways by region and multi-year 
industry implications:
I. China = Increasing Promotional Intensity Beginning Ahead of January 
Online Sales Distribution Reset: Incremental by our recent global fieldwork – 
select Tier 1 online dealers, including YY Sports (owned by Pou Sheng) and 
Topsports have already begun inventory reduction actions to limit exposure of 
NKE brand inventory ahead of the January 1, 2027 termination of online 
distribution. Specifically, the start of the initial actions by Pou Sheng and 
Topsports started in the month of July by our recent fieldwork, with the 
clearance inventory focused primarily across the Nike lifestyle category and 
Jordan brand, inclusive of both apparel and footwear (although primarily 
footwear). Recall on actual results, Pou Sheng reported monthly sales -13.9% 
YOY in the month of July, moderating relative to -8.6% Y/Y in June, -2.7% Y/Y in 
May, and +0.1% in April.
•
Recall, our math points to an “unmitigated” forward revenue headwind to 
NKE’s Greater China business of $1B+ in annualized revenues (= ~20% of 
NKE’s FY26-end Greater China revenues) based on the online sales 
terminating completely for NKE at Topsports & Pou Sheng as two public 
disclosures. Representing a potential “mitigating” factor – online sales 
terminated from partner-operated sites could transfer or be recaptured on its 
official Nike “flagship” experiences with pace of innovation and local 
marketing/story telling dictating the magnitude by our work (given price has 
been a key driver behind the sales on partner-operated storefronts).
= Broad-Based Promotional China Marketplace by Our Work: With Pou 
Sheng and Topsports comprising the majority of sector market share in Greater 
China, our recent work points to a “knock-on effect” to nearly all retailers for 
sportswear brands in the region as well as other Sportswear brands having to 
react to the depth of discounts on Nike inventory to compete & drive sell-
throughs. Said differently, with clearance actions already underway ahead of the 
Jan 1, 2027 online distribution cutoff, our channel work points to an increasingly 
pressured backdrop in China and a sequential deceleration of sales performance 
into July/August across the industry (= worse than initial channel partners 
expecting bottoming sales performance across April/May). In addition, our recent 
work points to channel partners (i.e. Pou Sheng) reducing forward orderbooks 
with NKE and below-plan sell-through feedback on recent product launches 
within the region. Looking forward and representing a wildcard to watch – 
our work also points to competitive actions underway from both local brands 
and national brands (i.e. Adidas) to aggressively pursue market share 
donated by Nike online upon the official cutoff of Nike distribution online 
across select partners (outside of its own dedicated Flagship sites) on January 
1, 2027.

Trifecta of Current N/A Demand Constraints: On current demand within the athletic 
North America marketplace, JD Sports (covered by W. Liu) reported on 8/20 its 2Q (May-
July) trading update including a softer North America market with LFL -6.8% 
(moderating by 620bps vs. 1Q -0.6%), with management stating North America sales 
performance “reflects weaker core consumer sentiment, a slower quarter for high-heat 
footwear product, and deferred ‘back-to-school’ demand from July into the first half 
of August.” On the calendar, Labor Day timing is shifted to 9/7 this year (vs. 9/1 a year 
ago), broadly pushing back school start dates by one week. Recall, JD Sports North 
America consists of the following fascias: JD US, JD Canada, Hibbett, DTLR, Shoe 
Palace, Finish Line (standalone stores and .com) and Macy’s Finish Line concessions, 
with management noting JD US & JD Canada North America outperformance 
(comparable sales -1.1% in 2Q) supported by JD as relatively more multi-brand and 
apparel sales-weighted, while the total North America LFL -6.8% decline was pressured 
by underperformance at Hibbett, DTLR, Shoe Palace, and Finish Line (dotcom & legacy 
stores being wound-down and a source of promotional activity), in which we believe 
Nike lifestyle footwear was a key contributor by brand (w/ estimates pointing to NKE 
in aggregate as over 40% of JD’s total revenues).
•
N/A Marketplace Challenged Through “At Least” 4Q/Holiday: Furthermore on the 
8/25 DKS (covered by C. Horvers) 2Q (May-Jul) EPS Call, management cited, “As 
demand continued to shift during the quarter, inventory built up in parts of the industry, 
particularly within certain legacy footwear silhouettes and apparel franchises that 
simply aren’t resonating the way they once did. The inventory built up across the 
industry supply chains and across the retail marketplace, which led to an increasingly 
aggressive promotional environment.” By banner, management noted an impact across 
both DKS and FL, albeit to a greater degree at FL given higher exposure to legacy 
footwear silhouettes & greater dependence on launch & retro product (noting NKE 
brand as ~60% of FL’s revenue mix), while core DKS has a more diversified brand 
& category mix. To this point, DKS reported 2Q comparable sales at FL -3.6% Y/Y 
(moderating by -420bps vs. 1Q’s +0.6% comp result) and core DKS comparable sales 
of +4.9% in 2Q (moderating by -110bps vs. 1Q’s +6.0% comp result). As a result, DKS 
stated, “We’re taking action to shift the mix toward in-demand brands, and while we 
expect the launch calendar to be more favorable in the back half of the year, the quality 
of those launches will be critical. We expect the broader promotional environment, 
particularly around legacy silhouettes, to remain challenging at least through the 
fourth quarter” (= at least through NKE’s F3Q27).
•
Lifestyle Footwear Remains a Headwind, Only Partially Offset by Performance: 
Specifically by category, JD Sports & DKS both spoke to performance product 
outperformance relative to underperformance in lifestyle categories. Specifically, in 
addition to the slower quarter for high-heat product, JD Sports also noted that footwear 
performance specifically reflected tougher comparisons due to a shift in the product 
launch schedule from 1Q into 2Q in the prior year and ongoing softness in end-of-cycle 
footwear product lines (which we see as Lifestyle silhouettes), only partially offset by 
momentum in the performance-running category (i.e. NKE’s Vomero, in addition to 
other brands) & newer footwear styles. Similarly, DKS management stated, “I really do 
believe this is focused on the lifestyle legacy silhouettes that aren’t doing well. The 
performance aspects of the category and then some of the newness that Ed mentioned 
continued to do really well” with the Nike Vomero & Peg specifically referred to two 
strong performing performance running silhouettes. Recall, Nike Sportswear & Jordan 
Streetwear (inclusive of all Jordan non-performance product) represents 60% of 
Nike’s total revenue mix.

DKS spoke to a “very aggressive” promotional environment in EMEA on elevated 
inventory across the industry and an “even more cautious” consumer tied to the 
geopolitical environment (i.e. higher fuel costs). Further, and relative to North America, 
DKS cited, “The impact on EMEA is much greater than what’s going on in the U.S. 
It’s a more competitive market. It’s a much more promotional market than the U.S. 
right now. Based on fuel costs, the consumer is much more cautious in EMEA.” In 
addition, JD Sports noted Europe sales trends in 2Q “continued to be impacted by a 
subdued consumer environment, amidst a promotional market,” notably UK 
“underlying performance remained challenged, particularly in footwear.” Similar to 
North America, a key theme by our recent fieldwork across Europe is outperformance of 
performance-led product today (vs. lifestyle) creating an extended clearance of legacy 
lifestyle inventory.
•
Drilling down further by category & by brand – our work points to an increasing 
preference of consumers to shop multi-brand across sports (vs. one brand across 
all sports pre-pandemic), with brands dedicated to delivering innovation in specific 
performance-based categories (i.e. performance road running, trail running/hiking, 
tennis, padel & golf) outperforming “mega brands” (i.e. NKE) which still remain 
pressured by the majority of revenue mix concentrated on Lifestyle product.
Putting the pieces together & tying back to our NKE downgrade to Underweight (see 
our 8/4 Mgmt Access & Fieldwork = 2H27 & FY28 EPS ~20% Below Consensus) – we 
are modeling FY27 EPS of $1.55 (below Consensus at $1.72). Specifically, our 1H27 revenue 
forecast is aligned to management’s implied guidance and includes 1Q27 -3.9% Y/Y reported 
on our model (vs. mgmt’s -LSD-MSD% guidance & Street -2.8%) & 2Q27 revenues -5.9% 
Y/Y on our model (vs. Street -4.4%), reflecting a sequential deceleration vs. 1Q (as mgmt 
guided) based on a “multi-point headwind” in rank order of magnitude from (i) the lap of 
higher digital promotions in the prior year, notably flagging EMEA, and (ii) timing of N. 
America wholesale shipments. Into 2H27, we are modeling revenues -4.6% Y/Y (or 
nearly 500bps below the Street at Flat), and relative to our 1H27 revenues -4.9% Y/Y, 
with our model reflecting below-Consensus revenues in Greater China (online 
marketplace sales loss) and North America (store closure headwinds). Importantly, we 
see incremental potential downside risk (not embedded in our below Consensus model) 
from aforementioned laterals negatively impacting NKE’s 1Q27 (Jun-Aug) associated 
with lifestyle sneaker underperformance into Back-to-School, notably across North 
America and EMEA, and into 2Q/2H27 with knock-on inventory & promotional 
activity headwinds across each of N. America, EMEA, and Greater China.
IV. Taking a Step Back on the Industry – 2 Key Themes to Monitor
•
“Ankle Biters” Increasing Market Share Relative to NKE w/ “Authenticity” by 
Sport in Focus: Competitive structure in sports footwear has shifted from a single-leader 
market to a more fragmented share set, with Nike market share down ~300bps vs. 2019 
to ~19%—still the leading brand but less dominant than pre-pandemic. The “ankle biter” 
cohort (e.g., On, Hoka, Brooks) has moved from collectively <10% share in 2019 (with 
many sub-1%) to roughly ~17% today, closing the gap to Nike’s ~19%. This compresses 
Nike’s former >10 point share advantage versus the cohort to roughly ~200bps, 
indicating that smaller brands are now operating at scale rather than at the margin. 
To us, the central question is no longer whether niche brands can grow, but whether 
consumer behavior has structurally shifted toward lower brand loyalty and higher multi-
brand trial, making share recapture a harder, more durable challenge.

Sportswear Growth Normalization w/ Category Penetration 800bps Higher Today 
vs. Pre-Pandemic: Global Sports Apparel & Footwear is increasingly screening as a 
maturing category, with Euromonitor growth expected to run at +4.5% from FY25–
28 versus +6.2% pre-pandemic, and the forward outlook revised down by 
~120bps versus the forecast a year ago. Category “saturation” in the consumer wallet has 
risen meaningfully, with Sports Apparel & Footwear now ~24% of total Apparel & 
Footwear spend, up ~800bps versus ~16% in 2015, as activewear adoption has 
broadened beyond performance use-cases. Consistent with maturity, the category’s 
historical growth premium is compressing, narrowing to ~+130bps outperformance 
versus total apparel/footwear (from +410bps pre-pandemic). The largest driver of the 
downgrade to Euromonitor’s forward forecasts is North America, where the $167B TAM 
is now forecast to grow +3.0% in 2026/2027 (cut from +5.1% a year ago) and moderating 
versus 2015–19 (+6.5%). By 2028, North America’s sports vs. total apparel growth 
spread is expected to compress to ~30bps (vs. ~460bps pre-pandemic), alongside a high 
wallet share already at ~35%.
