Nike Can Keep Winning Despite Headwinds, $11 Billion of Debt
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Even with pressured fiscal 2026 results, Nike's robust credit metrics and
strong liquidity should enable it to withstand near-term headwinds and maintain its lead in global
sportswear. Nike has made progress on clearing inventory in its wholesale channel and bringing new
products and innovations to market. But the company faces lingering tariff costs and consumer
uncertainty as its turnaround continues. (09/22/26)
1. Credit Metrics Solid Despite Profit Pressure
Even with debt well above pre-pandemic levels, most of Nike's credit metrics remain solid relative to
single A retail peers. Its credit profile is further supported by superior global brand recognition,
strong products and geographic sales diversity. Nike leads Adidas in global sportswear with a 14%
market share in 2025 vs. 8.7% for Adidas. Cash and short-term investments of $9 billion exceeded $8
billion of funded debt at May 31, leaving a funded-debt net-cash position before $3.1 billion of
operating-lease liabilities. Two committed facilities totaling $3 billion and a $3 billion commercial-
paper program were undrawn. The near-term maturity stack consists of $1 billion in November 2026
and $1 billion in March 2027, followed by $1.5 billion in 2030. Nike's liquidity makes the 2026-27
maturities manageable, though it hasn't disclosed whether they will be repaid or refinanced.
Adidas' adjusted net leverage totaled 1.6x as of 2Q (vs. a target of below 2x). Ralph Lauren's net
leverage usually trends below 1x. Target's net leverage was 1.4x at the end of fiscal 2Q26. (09/22/26)

2. Cash Flow Softens as Capital Returns Recede
Nike has historically generated strong cash flow and maintains a conservative financial policy relative
to peers, helping support its credit profile despite demand uncertainty and strategic execution risk.
Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow fell to $2.87 billion from $3.70 billion as working capital used $1.68
billion, including a $684 million tariff receivable that was substantially collected after year-end. Free
cash flow was about $2.18 billion after $684 million of capital spending. Nike paid $2.41 billion of
dividends and repurchased $123 million of stock, with no repurchases in 4Q. The dividend exceeded
free cash flow, though subsequent tariff collections and $9 billion of liquidity limit near-term funding

3. Brand Scale, Mix Cushion Turnaround
Nike's credit profile and A2/A+ ratings from Moody's and S&P are supported by its commanding brand
presence in the sports footwear and apparel market. The company's significant share in global sports
footwear (23%) and sports apparel (7.9%) as of 2025 is driven by its product offerings, athletic
partnerships and digital strategy. Its market share remains ahead of Adidas' 12% in footwear and 6.3%
in apparel. Geographic diversity (as seen on FA PGEO <GO>) also enhances Nike's credit profile.
Fiscal 2026 Nike Brand revenue was about 45% North America, 28% EMEA, 14% APLA and 13% Greater
China, while about 61% came through wholesale and 39% via Nike Direct. Wholesale revenue rose 4%
currency-neutral, while Direct fell 8% as Digital dropped 12% and store sales declined 4%. Performance
categories are gaining momentum, though management expects Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear
to remain negative in fiscal 2027 before improving in 2H. Brand scale preserves recovery capacity,
and full-price sell through determines when it converts to earnings. (09/22/26)

4. Nike Leads Adidas in Working-Capital Management
Adidas lags behind Nike in key working-capital management metrics. Nike maintains higher inventory
turnover than Adidas, reflecting its ability to better stock inventory types and quantities to meet
demand. Nike also boasts a lower cash-conversion cycle than Adidas, underscoring its stronger
position in the supply chain and enabling it to invest in other business areas.
