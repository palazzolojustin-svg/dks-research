OUR MAIN TAKEAWAY
We remain cautious on NKE, and we're lowering estimates into fiscal 1Q27 EPS on 10/1 AMC.
We believe demand trends remain soft, inventory levels remain high, promotional activity
remains intense, and competitive dynamics remain unfavorable. We'd also note that two
negative developments emerged following the 4Q26 earnings call in late-June: 1) U.S. demand
trends worsened for back-to-school, leading to sales misses and guidance cuts at Dick's/Foot
Locker and JD Sports, and 2) two key wholesale accounts in China announced the pending
termination of online sales of Nike products. Given these dynamics, we're lowering our FY27/28
EPS forecasts to $1.64/$1.80 (from $1.74/$2.00) and we believe the "path of least resistance" for
the shares could remain downward, given the challenging environment and the expectation
that numbers keep moving lower.
North American Fundamentals Remain Pressured. While management is working hard to
reinvigorate brand heat, unfortunately it seems as though U.S. demand remains challenged. Credit
card data (tracking U.S. DTC sales) remains very week, launch product sell-throughs have been soft,
discounting has been pervasive (both in the wholesale channel and DTC), and the brand's social
media follower count is declining. Furthermore, amid the soft demand environment, NKE made the
questionable decision to significant increase sell-in to the North American wholesale channel (+14%
in FY26), which is likely leading to inventory overhangs that could stifle growth going forward.
Visibility In China Remains Low. This region has fallen on hard times (8 straight negative quarters),
with little visibility into a turnaround. Additionally, in an effort to clean up the marketplace, NKE has
informed two key multi-brand retailers in China (TopSports and YY Sports) that beginning in January
2027 they may no longer sell Nike products online. We estimate that this is a $550-$600 million revenue
headwind in CY27. Plus, the market in China overall remains challenging, as evidenced by recent -DD
sales declines at both of these retailers.
ONON's Investor Day Showed That Not All Is Bad In Athletic. Investor sentiment towards the
athletic space is about as low as we can remember it, particularly given recent struggles at bellwether
names such as NKE and LULU. However, we do continue to see pockets of strength in the athletic
space. Although ONON cut FY revenue guidance on the Q2 EPS call, they're still on track to grow
low-20's this year, with record-high gross margins. Plus, they laid out strong 3-year targets: high-teens
CAGR globally, low-to-mid teens in North America. As they continue to grow the door count both in
DTC (growing to 180 stores from 80 today) and wholesale (increasing door penetration in strategic
accounts from 50% to 75%), they may continue to gain market share, along with other smaller high-
growth brands (Hoka, Saucony, Altra, etc.)

NORTH AMERICAN FUNDAMENTALS REMAIN PRESSURED
While management is working diligently to reignite brand heat, we believe that demand trends remain muted globally. Beginning with
the domestic market, we continue to see signs of tepid demand for Nike products. One way we track demand is by looking at Bloomberg's
ALTD data, as their "Observed Sales" metric aims to track credit-card transactions in the U.S. DTC channel. Historically, the YoY change
in the ALTD data has an 85% correlation with Nike's North America DTC growth. Unfortunately for NKE following a deceleration in fiscal
Q4, trends did not improve at all in Q1, with both quarters down 17% YoY. Although the ALTD data might not be accurate in terms of
magnitude (e.g. the actual North America DTC decline in Q4 was -6% vs. ALTD -17%), it tends to be accurate in terms of direction. Since
the Street is forecasting North America DTC sales growth to improve QoQ (almost flattish YoY, as compared to Q4's -6%), investors could
be disappointed if actual sales growth doesn't improve in Q1. As far as QTD trends are concerned, we only have 2 weeks of ALTD data,
but so far we haven't seen a meaningful change in trajectory (-16% YoY).
Figure 1 - Consensus Assumes North America DTC Improves To Flattish In Q1, But Card-Tracking Data IS Still Weak
Source: Company reports, Bloomberg ALTD, and ConsensusMetrix
We're also concerned about recent sell-through trends we've seen for the Jordan sub-brand. Retro Jordan sneakers, which are predicated
on scarcity (supply intentionally restrained below demand) became over-saturated post-COVID, and NKE management has worked
diligently to tighten distribution of the brand. When the brand is performing at its best, the shoes are difficult to procure in primary
channels of distribution, and they end up trading on secondary-market platforms such as GOAT and StockX. And not only do they trade
on these platforms, they usually trade at a premium. However, during fiscal 4Q26, we saw poor performance of retro Jordans on secondary-
market platforms, and the trends actually got slightly worse in 1Q27. Prior to Q4, ~50%-60% of retro Jordans released in a given quarter had
commanded premiums in the resale market. This is below the 90%-100% rates that we saw pre-COVID and in the immediate aftermath
of the pandemic. But in 4Q26, it took another step downward, with only 28% of retro Jordans selling at resale premiums, and it worsened
even further to 26% in fiscal Q1 (including a brutal July, in which every retro Jordan release was soft). We think this is a discouraging sign,
as it could signal that the Jordan business requires more rationalization.

We've also seen a pickup in discounting on Nike.com. Every weekend, we visit Nike.com and count the number of sale items are on the
website, and then we compare it YoY. Early this calendar year, the number of sales items on Nike.com was generally tracking flattish (plus
or minus a few percentage points each week). However, during the Spring months, the breadth of discounting increased meaningfully,
which worsened further over the summer. Specifically, during fiscal Q3 (ended Feb'26) the number of sale SKUs on Nike.com declined 1%
YoY, but in Q4 the number of discounted SKUs increased 40% YoY and in Q1 it worsened to a 63% YoY increase.

necessitated bigger discounts YoY to move inventory units.

Additionally, trends are highly challenged in the wholesale channel, as we can see from disappointing recent results at NKE's strategic
partners. For example, DKS reported a disappointing comp decline of almost 4% at their Foot Locker segment (vs. a slight increase in
Q1) and they lowered FY Foot Locker comp guidance to a range of flat-to-down-2% vs. an original range of up-1%-to-3%. Additionally,
they lowered FY EBIT margin guidance by ~100bps (including a 50bps reduction at the core Dick's banner and larger cut at Foot Locker),
attributed to the highly promotional environment, which is expected to cause gross margin to decline at both segment. Furthermore, JD
Sports reported Q2 like-for-like sales in North America declined almost 7%, which was a significant deterioration vs. Q1's less-than-1%
decline. Additionally, they lowered FY guidance for pretax income by 50 million GBP, or ~6% at the midpoint.

We believe this promotional pressure is largely a function of NKE having too much inventory in the channel. In late-CY25, NKE made
the unwise decision to ship a lot of product into the North American wholesale channel, which appears to be backfiring. NKE has had
four consecutive quarters of double-digit sell-in growth in North America (+14% YoY over the LTM), and the November 2025 quarter was
the largest period of domestic wholesale sell-in during the company's long history. Essentially, over the LTM, NKE has shipped in $1.5
billion more wholesale product YoY, and when grossing that up to retail sales, it's about $3 billion of incremental product at retailers.
Essentially, NKE shipped a lot more product into the North American wholesale channel, and shortly thereafter retailer comp-sales trends
slowed. As a result, we don't believe NKE has sufficient demand at the moment to match the increased supply, which is resulting in excess
promotional activity at retailers.

Source: Company reports and Needham research
We also track discounting of the Nike brand at wholesale partners. Every week, we go on the websites of NKE's major wholesale accounts
across a variety of channels (sporting goods, sneaker specialty, run/outdoor specialty, family footwear, etc.) and we track the total number
of Nike SKUs available, as well as the number of SKUs that are discounted. We can then see what percentage of the Nike assortment is
discounted. During NKE's fiscal Q1, we saw over 30% of the Nike assortment on discount (average across 14 different retailers), which was
up from 25% of the assortment being discounted a year earlier (1Q26).

CHINA MARKET PIVOT PRESENTS INCREMENTAL RISK OVER THE NEXT 12-18 MONTHS
In late-July, NKE notified two of their key multi-brand wholesale partners in China, Topsports and YY Sports (owned by Pou Sheng Intl.)
that NKE's products may only be sold in stores beginning in calendar 2027. This is an effort to limit online distribution to select channels
(e.g. Nike's own website and certain digital marketplaces such as T-Mall), in order to strengthen pricing integrity in China.
While it may help clean up the Chinese marketplace in the long-run, it will likely result in a near-term sales headwind as those revenue
streams are terminated. TopSports stated that online sales of Nike products were ~22% of their fiscal 2025 sales. They generated 25.7
billion RMB of revenue last year, or ~$3.6 billion when converted to USD. 22% of that amount is ~$785 million, or just shy of $400 million
wholesale-equivalent. Meanwhile, Pou Sheng stated that online sales of the Nike brand were ~15% of Pou Sheng's total revenues in CY25.
Pou Sheng generated 17.1 billion RMB of revenue last year, or ~$2.4 billion when converted to USD. 15% of that amount is ~$355 million,
and the wholesale-equivalent revenue for NKE would thus be ~$180 million. Thus, they total headwind to Nike from these two terminations
is in the range of $550-$600 million of lost wholesale revenue, or almost 10% of NKE's total revenue in Greater China. Given NKE's global
footprint, this represents a ~1-point headwind to sales in calendar 2027.

VALUATION (PRICE TARGET: NA)
The stock is currently trading at a low-20's P/E on our FY27 EPS forecast of $1.64.
POTENTIAL UPSIDE DRIVERS
Potential upside drivers for NKE include:
■
The arrival of new CEO Elliott Hill could reinvigorate the brand to a greater degree than expected.
■
Reduced supply of over-distributed styles could lead to reduced discounting/markdowns, driving higher gross margins.
■
Some of the "green shoots" in the product assortment (Vomero, P-6000, Signature Basketball) could gain momentum and offset some
of the declines of over-distributed styles.
RISKS TO TARGET
NKE
Risks to the NKE story include:
■
It could longer than expected for new CEO Elliott Hill to effect change at the company, particularly given long lead times for new
product development.
■
The company has a large exposure to China (15% of revenues), which is a market that has become far more challenging in recent
quarters.
■
The ability to wean consumers off of discounts and get them to buy more at full-price could prove more challenging than expected,
which could either keep a lid on margins or negatively affect revenues.
