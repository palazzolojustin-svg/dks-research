Operational and Financial Highlights
Revenue
NIKE, Inc. revenue declined 4.3% YoY to $11.21 billion in Q1 FY2027,
versus $11.72 billion a year earlier. On a currency-neutral basis,
revenue declined 5%. The result was below the approximately $11.35
billion consensus estimate available ahead of the release. Diluted
EPS of $0.48 exceeded the $0.44 consensus estimate.
NIKE Brand revenue declined 4% to $10.95 billion, while Converse
revenue fell 28% to $263 million. NIKE Brand wholesale revenue
declined only 1% to $6.80 billion, while NIKE Direct revenue declined
8% reported and 9% on a currency-neutral basis to $4.14 billion. The
decline in NIKE Direct reflected a 13% decline in NIKE Brand Digital
sales and a 5% decline in NIKE-owned store sales.
Geographic Performance
North America
North America remained the strongest major geographic market,
with revenue increasing 2% YoY to $5.13 billion. Footwear revenue
increased 1%, while apparel increased 6%. Equipment declined 8%.
Management highlighted running, football and basketball as key
contributors to performance growth. Basketball, tennis and golf also
posted double-digit growth in selected categories.
The channel mix was particularly notable: North American wholesale
revenue increased 9%, while NIKE Direct declined 6%. This indicates
that wholesale momentum continues to offset weakness in the direct
channel.
EMEA
EMEA revenue declined 5% to $3.18 billion. Footwear remained the
key drag, declining 11%, while apparel increased 5% and equipment
increased 5%. The result indicates a continued divergence between
the performance and lifestyle portions of the portfolio.
NIKE Direct revenue declined 12% in the region, while wholesale
revenue declined only 1%.

Greater China
Greater China remains the principal area of concern. Revenue
declined 22% on a reported basis and 26% on a currency-neutral
basis, to $1.18 billion. Footwear declined 22% reported / 26%
currency-neutral, while apparel declined 23% / 27%, respectively.
Wholesale revenue declined 28% reported and 31% currency-
neutral. NIKE Direct revenue declined 13% reported and 18%
currency-neutral.
Greater China now represents approximately 10.8% of NIKE Brand
revenue, down materially from historical levels.
Management characterized the market reset as a multi-season
process. Nike is reducing online distribution that it considers
unprofitable or dilutive to the brand, concentrating digital activity
around fewer flagship experiences on platforms including Tmall,
JD.com and Douyin, and investing in physical retail and locally
developed product.
Management also highlighted continued growth in running in China,
but emphasized that the broader marketplace reset will continue to
create near-term revenue and profitability pressure.
Asia Pacific & Latin America
Revenue declined 2% reported but was broadly flat on a currency-
neutral basis, at $1.46 billion.
This remains a comparatively stable region, with apparel increasing
1% reported and 4% currency-neutral, while footwear declined 3%
reported and 1% currency-neutral.
Channel Dynamics
The quarter demonstrated a clear divergence between wholesale and
direct channels.
Total NIKE Brand wholesale revenue declined only 1%, compared
with an 8% reported decline in NIKE Direct. The strongest wholesale
performance was in North America, where revenue increased 9%.
By contrast, NIKE Direct declined across all major regions: 6% in
North America, 12% in EMEA, 13% in Greater China and 4% in APLA.
The distinction between digital and total direct revenue is important.
Nike reported a 13% global decline in NIKE Brand Digital sales, but
the geographic figures above refer to NIKE Direct, which includes
both digital and NIKE-owned stores.

Our View
The Q1 FY2027 report produced a mixed set of signals.
On the negative side, Greater China deteriorated further, EMEA
remained under pressure, and management's FY2027 revenue
guidance implies a substantially deeper decline than previously
anticipated. The expected Q2 revenue headwind and the statement
that EBIT will decline faster than revenue indicate that the company
is entering a period of significant earnings pressure.
At the same time, several operating indicators are constructive.
North America returned to 2% revenue growth, performance
categories continued to expand at a high-single-digit rate, and Nike
demonstrated the ability to protect gross margin through supply-
chain and cost-management initiatives despite continued revenue
pressure.
The key issue is therefore increasingly the quality and sustainability of
the recovery, rather than simply whether Nike can stabilize quarterly
earnings.
Greater China remains a structural challenge. The region's share of
NIKE Brand revenue has fallen to approximately 11%, reducing its
direct impact on consolidated growth, but the magnitude and duration
of the reset remain important indicators of Nike's brand health.
Management does not expect a rapid recovery and has explicitly
indicated that marketplace and digital restructuring will take multiple
seasons.
The wholesale channel also requires monitoring. North American
wholesale growth is encouraging, but the continued decline in NIKE
Direct indicates that Nike is still working through consumer, product
and distribution challenges.
The Pace program could provide a meaningful structural
improvement in the cost base and operating model. However, the
timing is important: the largest savings are expected later in the
program, while the company is likely to absorb restructuring charges
and revenue pressure before the benefits are fully realized.
The balance sheet remains a key financial strength. Nike has
approximately $0.5 billion of net cash on a financial-debt basis,
substantial liquidity and a long history of shareholder distributions.
Management's explicit commitment to maintaining and ultimately
growing the dividend provides additional visibility on capital
allocation.
Valuation
Following our review of quarterly results, we are lowering our Target
Price to $51 per share and maintain our Hold rating. Our target price
is based on a 5-year discounted cash flow (DCF) model using the
free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) approach.

Freedom Broker’s Analyst Rating Calculation Disclosure
The Analyst Rating (“AR”) is calculated as follows:
Analyst Rating (AR) = Expected Price Return / Annualized Historical Volatility
1. Expected Price Return
Expected Price Return is calculated using the following formula:
Expected Price Return = (Price Target / Current Price) − 1
This figure represents the percentage price appreciation or decline implied by the analyst’s 12-month price target relative to the stock’s current
market price.
2. Annualized Historical Volatility
Annualized Historical Volatility is calculated as follows:
Annualized Historical Volatility = Standard Deviation of Daily Logarithmic Returns × √252
For securities with at least 12 months of trading history, the calculation uses daily logarithmic returns over the preceding 12 months. If fewer than
12 months of trading history are available, the calculation uses all available daily price history. Volatility calculated using a shorter trading history
may be less representative of the security’s longer-term trading behavior.
Daily logarithmic returns are calculated as the natural logarithm of the ratio of the current trading day’s closing price to the preceding trading
day’s closing price:
Daily Logarithmic Return = ln(Current Closing Price / Previous Closing Price)
The standard deviation of daily logarithmic returns is annualized by multiplying it by the square root of 252, representing the approximate number
of trading days in a year.
Once calculated, the AR expresses the expected price return implied by the analyst’s price target as a proportion of the stock’s historical annualized
volatility. The AR does not represent the probability that the stock will reach its price target. Historical volatility, particularly when calculated using
a shorter trading history, may not be indicative of future volatility.
