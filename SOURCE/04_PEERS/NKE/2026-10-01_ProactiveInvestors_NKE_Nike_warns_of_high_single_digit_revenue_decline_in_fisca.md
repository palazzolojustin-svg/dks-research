Nike warns of high-single-digit revenue decline in
fiscal 2027
Author: Angela Harmantas  /  First published: 20:27 1 October 2026
Nike Inc (NYSE:NKE, XETRA:NKE) forecast fiscal 2027 adjusted earnings well below Wall Street expectations on
Thursday, sending its shares down 5.8% after the bell. 
The sportswear maker expects adjusted earnings per share of $1.15 to $1.35, against analyst estimates of $1.65. It also
expects revenue to decline by a high-single-digit percentage and an effective tax rate in the mid-20% range. 
First-quarter revenue fell 4% to $11.21 billion, missing estimates of $11.32 billion. Earnings per share were $.048, ahead
of the $0.43 expected, while net income slipped 2% to $0.7 billion. Gross margin widened 60 basis points to 42.8%,
topping the 42.2% estimate, helped by lower warehousing and logistics costs. 
Nike Direct revenue dropped 8% to $4.1 billion, below the $4.4 billion estimate, with digital sales down 13% and Nike-
owned stores down 5%. Wholesale revenue fell 1% to $6.8 billion, in line with estimates. 
Greater China was the weak spot, with revenue down 22% to $1.18 billion versus the $1.3 billion expected. EMEA
declined 5% to $3.18 billion. North America grew 2% to $5.13 billion, beating the $5.1 billion estimate, while APLA fell
2% to $1.46 billion, above the $1.4 billion estimate. 
Converse revenue plunged 28% to $263 million, short of the $296 million estimate. 
Nike also introduced "Pace," an operating model overhaul that includes modernizing its global supply chain, building a
new campus in India and realigning to three geographies. The company expects Pace to deliver about $2.5 billion in
cumulative savings through fiscal 2031, against about $1 billion in pre-tax charges. Adjusted EPS guidance excludes
about $0.15 of Pace-related restructuring expenses. 
"The Sport Offense is driving measurable progress across our performance business, and we introduced Pace to help us
accelerate and scale that momentum across NIKE," said CEO Elliott Hill. 
"We have more work to do in NIKE Sportswear, Jordan Brand and Greater China, and we're taking deliberate actions to
strengthen those businesses the right way for the long-term," Hill said.
