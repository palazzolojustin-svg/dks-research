I. “Win Now” Decisions Made Through CY26 = Financial Impact In 2H27 & 
Into FY28. A key clarification from our recent mgmt access (& filings) - the 
financial impact of “Win Now” decisions made through the end of calendar year 
2026 will linger and impact NKE’s P&L in 2H27 and into FY28. Recall, “Win 
Now” was initially launched in December ’24 under CEO Hill (post CEO hire in 
Oct 24) as a set of priorities to (i) refocus NKE’s culture on Sport & growth, (ii) 
create emotional connections with consumers with distinct storytelling behind the 
NKE brand, (iii) accelerate an innovative product portfolio across fields of play, 
(iv) elevate and grow the marketplace, and (v) win on the ground with localized 
connections. Importantly to address these priorities, management has made 
changes across the leadership team, business structure (shifting to the Sport-
Offense construct vs. Gender construct prior), supply chain, and marketplace.
Put differently, mgmt’s clarification on financial impact through 2H27 (& 
into FY28) from “Win Now” decisions made through CY26 (including 7/21 
China reset decision & July ’26 ongoing N. America store closure 
announcements) supports recent filings language including the 7/15 10-K: 
“our reportable operating segments are at different stages of progress, and we 
expect to complete these actions by the end of December 2026. The timing of 
financial impacts has varied and will continue to vary by segment. North 
America has made the most progress against these actions, while Greater China 
and Converse will take more time.”

III. Digging Deeper Into 6 Key Investor Pivot Points By Our Work (= Likely Investor 
Day Topics):
Pivot Point #1: Is Global Sports Apparel & Footwear “Mature” as a Category?
•
Global Sports Apparel & Footwear is increasingly screening as a maturing category, with 
Euromonitor growth expected to run at +4.5% from FY25–28 versus +6.2% pre-
pandemic, and the forward outlook revised down by ~120bps versus the forecast a year 
ago. Category “saturation” in the consumer wallet has risen meaningfully, with Sports 
Apparel & Footwear now ~24% of total Apparel & Footwear spend, up ~800bps 
versus ~16% in 2015, as activewear adoption has broadened beyond performance use-
cases. Consistent with maturity, the category’s historical growth premium is 
compressing, narrowing to ~+130bps outperformance versus total apparel/footwear 
(from +410bps pre-pandemic). The largest driver of the downgrade to Euromonitor’s 
forward forecasts is North America, where the $167B TAM is now forecast to grow 
+3.0% in 2026/2027 (cut from +5.1% a year ago) and moderating versus 2015–19 
(+6.5%). By 2028, North America’s sports vs. total apparel growth spread compresses to 
~30bps (vs. ~460bps pre-pandemic), alongside a high wallet share already at ~35%.

Pivot Point #2: Have the “Ankle Biters” Now Become More Formidable Competitors?
•
Competitive structure in sports footwear has shifted from a single-leader market to a more 
fragmented share set, with Nike market share down ~300bps vs. 2019 to ~19% - still 
the leading brand but less dominant than pre-pandemic. The “ankle biter” cohort (e.g., 
On, Hoka, Brooks) has moved from collectively <10% share in 2019 (with many sub-
1%) to roughly ~17% today, closing the gap to Nike’s ~19%. This compresses Nike’s 
former >10 point share advantage versus the cohort to roughly ~200bps, indicating 
that smaller brands are now operating at scale rather than at the margin. The central 
question is no longer whether niche brands can grow, but whether consumer behavior has 
structurally shifted toward lower brand loyalty and higher multi-brand trial, making share 
recapture a harder, more durable challenge.

Pivot Point #3: What Is “Wrong” in China and What Is the Path Forward in the Region?
•
China represents the largest documented share-loss region, with Nike brand market

pandemic with China representing ~25% of Nike segment profit and delivering >38% 
operating margins as of FY19, but today it contributes ~12% of segment profit with 
margins down >1,000bps to ~21.9%, implying roughly a ~50% reduction in EBIT 
dollars versus peak and elevating the importance of both share stabilization and margin 
rebuilding.

Pivot Point #5: Is Nike’s Legacy Marketing Strategy Still Effective to Reach Today’s 
Consumer?
•
Within Nike’s marketing budget (~10% of sales on Demand Creation), we expect 
marketing “mix” to be of increasing focus as NKE evaluates concentration in athlete

conversion path (i.e. mid-funnel/influencer reach). The strategic tradeoff is 
increasingly defined as “owning the athlete” versus “owning the community” in our 
view with evidence that localized influencer-led activations can drive relevance and

marketed through localized creators including a micro-influencer with a small follower 
base but high cultural fit, illustrating how product storytelling can be built via community 
credibility. In China, Adidas’ viral “Urban Errands” campaign shows the power of speed 
and localization: a translation-driven meme cycle was converted into brand exposure 
within days through a rapid, tailored response (product, ambassador amplification, store-
level execution). The broader implication is that marketing effectiveness is more 
dependent on agility, localization, and mid-funnel engagement than on legacy scale 
dynamics alone.

NIKE, Inc. (Underweight; Price Target: $40.00)
Investment Thesis
NKE is the global athletic market leader with diversification across product categories, 
geographies, and distribution, and while NKE has begun to realize initial greenshoots from 
its Sport Offense strategy within North America and the running category, the balance of 
the portfolio including International regions (EMEA, Greater China, and APLA) continue 
to face actions to reset the marketplace, and sell-through results remain challenged globally, 
resulting in an elongated timeline for the model to reach an inflection to revenue growth and 
a return to double-digit operating margins. As a result, our go-forward estimates remain 
below Consensus, with incremental headwinds associated with N/A DTC store closures and 
Greater China online distribution changes incremental risk to Consensus numbers in our 
view.
Valuation
We establish our Dec ’27 Price Target of $40 (vs. Dec ‘26 PT of $47 prior) based on ~20.5x 
our CY28E EPS of $1.97 supported by our linear and peer regression analysis. Specifically, 
our 20.5x multiple represents a discount relative to NKE’s 27x pre-pandemic 3-yr average, 
with our steady-state model profile reflective of a growth algorithm & margin profile below 
that of pre-pandemic. 
Risks to Rating and Price Target
The economic climate, particularly the employment picture, can affect consumer spending 
and the sportswear industry. A  greater-than-expected uptick in household spending could 
cause sales trends to accelerate above our current assumptions rendering our estimates too 
low. Roughly 50% of the company’s revenues are generated outside of the United States,
