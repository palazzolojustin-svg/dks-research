1. Nike Loyalty Sets It Up to Win in Weak Sneaker Market: BI Survey
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Nike can widen its lead as household
budgets tighten and sneaker demand weakens, with BI survey data
showing economic stress is hurting discretionary purchases more than
brand preference. Lower-income consumers are under the most
pressure, with 19% of those earning under $50,000 not expecting to

execution more than brand affinity. That sets it apart as spending slows, with the World Cup offering a
chance to build further loyalty. Adidas remains No. 2, while New Balance and Hoka skew toward
middle- and higher-income shoppers, reinforcing the divide between premium demand and value-
seeking. (06/08/26)

2. Nike Maintains Global Lead Despite Rising Competition
Contributing Analysts Sydney Goodman (Retail)
Nike remains the undisputed leader in the global footwear market, despite softer performance since
the pandemic and a loss of market share in recent years. Its scale, brand strength and consumer
loyalty underpin its dominance over our surveys in the past two years. In the most recent survey,
Nike was most frequently cited as the go-to brand for both athletic performance and everyday
lifestyle footwear in the US. It also ranked first among respondents' future purchase intentions, with
almost half planning to buy Nike shoes in the next six months, compared with 53% last year. Purchase
intent was similarly resilient, though 58% of respondents said they bought Nike shoes in the past year,
down from 59% last year, indicating modest pressure from tariffs, higher gas prices and softer sneaker
spending overall. (06/08/26)

5. Gas Prices Pressure Middle-Income Earners' Spending
Contributing Analysts Sydney Goodman (Retail)
Higher gas prices and inflation are pressuring spending by households earning $50,000-$150,000,
with these shoppers most likely to buy fewer items and curb discretionary purchases as more of their
budgets shift to needs. Consumers earning under $50,000 are showing a greater tendency to delay
purchases and make fewer store trips. That aligns with Walmart's commentary that higher-income
households are still spending as fuel costs and affordability pressure weigh more heavily on lower-
income shoppers. Households earning at least $150,000 appear the most insulated, with the largest
share reporting no change in behavior.
Consumers earning less than $50,000 are also more likely to forgo shoe purchases in the next six
months, at 19% vs. 13% overall, and to wait for sales or discounts, at 38% vs. 30%. (06/08/26)

8. Methodology and Data Collection
Contributing Analysts Sydney Goodman (Retail)
BI's survey of athleisure footwear purchasing and wearing behavior among US consumers, conducted
jointly with Attest on May 6-15, had a sample size of 1,191, of whom 681 purchased athletic sneakers
in the past 12 months and 469 bought lifestyle shoes. Factors such as geographical location, gender
and age were controlled to keep sample averages similar to US demographics. About 13% were 18-24
years old, 23% were 25-34, 22% were 35-44 and 40% were 45 and older. Women made up 51% and men
49%.
The survey's goal was to capture a comprehensive understanding of sneaker-buying behavior and
future purchasing intent across a broad consumer base. (06/08/26)

10. Affluent Shoppers Still the Key Drivers of Sneaker Spending
Higher-income consumers continue to fuel sneaker-spending growth despite a more cautious
economic backdrop. Though spending intentions remain relatively stable across income cohorts,
affluent shoppers continue to show the greatest willingness to trade up and pay more for footwear,
supporting demand for premium, performance and fashion-oriented brands. The findings suggest
higher-income households remain relatively insulated from broader economic pressures, while
consumers earning less are becoming more selective and value conscious in their purchasing
decisions. As a result, brands with strong premium positioning may continue to outperform, even as
overall consumer demand becomes increasingly polarized by income level. (06/08/26)
Future Spending Intentions
11. Promotions, Discounts Key for Low-Income Cohort
Sneaker purchases remain primarily driven by replacement needs across income cohorts, though
motivations diverge meaningfully by income level. Higher-income consumers show greater interest in
style, social influence and occasion-based purchases, with more than 21% of respondents earning
above $100,000 citing social-media influence or purchases tied to travel, vacations or specific
events. Consumers earning less are far more promotion-driven, with nearly one-third of respondents
making less than $50,000 saying their most recent purchase was motivated by discounts or sales. The
results suggest footwear increasingly serves as both a functional and fashion purchase, depending on
income level. (06/08/26)

21. Younger Sneaker Buyers Blend Function With Fashion
Replacement purchases continue to be the primary driver of sneaker demand, with nearly half of
consumers saying their last purchase was driven by the need to replace worn-out shoes. However,
younger consumers remain significantly more driven by style and trends. More than 37% of Gen Z and
millennial respondents said they purchased sneakers to acquire a style or color they didn't already
own, reinforcing the shift toward fashion-oriented and lifestyle footwear. Millennials also lead in
impulse-driven purchasing, particularly around promotions and shopping activity, while social-media
and peer influence remain disproportionately important for younger consumers.
Older shoppers, particularly baby boomers, remain far more practical in purchasing behavior, with
activity heavily concentrated around replacement needs. (06/08/26)

22. Millennials Are Driving Sneaker-Purchasing Volume
Gen Z and millennials continue to lead sneaker-purchase volume, though buying behavior has
moderated vs. earlier surveys. In the latest poll in May 2026, a little over 30% of Gen Z and 45% of

engagement with both athletic- and lifestyle-footwear categories. Gen Z still shows high purchase

Women in all age cohorts generally buy fewer sneakers than men, though millennial women stand out,
