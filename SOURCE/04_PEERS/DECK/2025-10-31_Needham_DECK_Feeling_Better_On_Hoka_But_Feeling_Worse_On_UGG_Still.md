Feeling Better On Hoka, But Feeling Worse On UGG;
Still At Buy, But Lower PT
OUR CALL
DECK appears poised for another negative investor response to earnings, with shares down in
the 10/23 aftermarket despite a 2Q26 beat. The issues largely stem from the UGG brand, which
saw a major deterioration of DTC trends, overshadowing positivity around Hoka (accelerating
DTC trends, solid wholesale order books, etc.). Though we feel better about Hoka than we did
earlier in the year, we're getting concerned about the UGG brand, as they may be hitting a wall
after several strong years. As a result, we're lowering our price target to $113 from $128, while
tweaking our FY26/27 EPS to $$6.36/$7.00 (from $6.28/$7.10). For the quarter, revenue growth
of 9% was ~100bps above guidance, while EPS of $1.82 was well above guidance of $1.50-$1.55.
They introduced FY26 guidance, calling for EPS of $6.30-$6.39 (in line with Street at $6.33).
TOM'S TAKEAWAYS
Tom's Takeaway #1: Hoka DTC Acceleration Bodes Well. Hoka was up 11% in Q2, with wholesale
up 13% and DTC up 8%. Notably, DTC accelerated from +3% growth each of the past two quarters,
which we view as encouraging. Management noted that strength was driven by new styles in Clifton,
Bondi, and Arahi which are experiencing strong sell-through (up DD+ in the wholesale channel).
Encouragingly, the Spring '26 order book remains strong (up YoY, with a benefit from new styles in the
Mach franchise) and early feedback on the Fall '26 order book is positive. FY Hoka guidance is for a
low-teens increase, implying 2H +LDD, which could prove conservative if DTC continues to accelerate.
Tom's Takeaway #2: UGG Growing Strongly At Wholesale, But DTC Faces Pressure. UGG was
up 10% in Q2, driven by a 17% increase in the wholesale business, as wholesale partners took
shipments earlier given inventory shortages last Holiday season. However, after 2 straight quarters
of -LSD declines in UGG DTC, the channel worsened to -10% in Q2. They attributed the decline to
cannibalization by wholesale, as better in-stock availability in the channel siphoned sales away from
DTC. They also cited an incrementally cautious U.S. consumer, who is seeking to shop across brands
(exacerbating the shift to wholesale). Encouragingly, they noted a solid consumer response to some
new styles, including the Zora ballet flat which debuted late in the quarter, and we highlighted its initial
success in our earnings preview last week. It remains to be seen if this and other new styles can drive
upside to UGG in 2H. They expect the brand to grow LSD-MSD for the FY, implying 2H is flat to down
LSD.
Tom's Takeaway #3: Expecting 2H Margin Pressure. Gross margin is planned down ~300bps in
2H, of which 200-250bps is due to tariffs (net of mitigation), and the remainder is due to normalizing
markdown levels. They handily beat gross margin each of the past 2 quarters (Q2 +30bps YoY vs.
guidance down ~200-250bps), so we would hope that this outlook is conservative.

At a Glance
OUR INVESTMENT THESIS
We think DECK is one of the highest-quality companies in our coverage, with an impressive multi-year track record of performance, two
of the strongest brands in the industry (Hoka and UGG), a strong management team and a fortress balance sheet.
BULL CASE ASSUMPTIONS
With the benefit of a slew of new products,
as well as channel fill in the wholesale
channel, Hoka growth could re accelerate
to the 15%-20% range the next few
years. Additionally, given international
momentum and new product launches
domestically, UGG could generate MSD-
HSD revenue growth. If margins are
sustained in the low-20's, then calendar
2027 EPS could be ~$8.50 and the share
price could reach ~$170 (20x P/E on FY27E
EPS ).
BASE CASE ASSUMPTIONS
With headwinds from slowing growth and
tariffs, we expect FY26 EPS to be roughly
flattish vs. FY25. We forecast UGG +LSD-
MSD and Hoka growth in the low-teens
range. Between tariffs and other margin
headwinds, we forecast ~200bps of gross
margin erosion and EBIT margin erosion.
We believe the company can resume +DD
EPS growth in FY27, driven by MSD-HSD
revenue growth, modest margin erosion
(wrap-around of tariffs), and buyback. Our
$113 price target is based on a mid-teens
P/E on calendar FY27 EPS of ~$7.50.
BEAR CASE ASSUMPTIONS
Against difficult compares and a tough
macro environment, UGG growth could
flat-line, while Hoka could slow to
single-digit growth (mainly driven by
new distribution). Tariffs plus normalized
discounting levels could push margins
down to the high-teens. If so, EPS growth
would slow dramatically, and calendar
2027 EPS could be $5.00, and the share
price could fall to $65 (low-teens P/E on
FY27E EPS).
COMPANY DESCRIPTION
DECK owns a portfolio of global footwear brands, led by the UGG and Hoka brands.

VALUATION (PRICE TARGET: $113.00)
Our $113 price target is based on a mid-teens P/E on calendar 2027 EPS of $7.50.
POTENTIAL UPSIDE DRIVERS
The company could do better than we expect if:
■
Hoka gains brand awareness and retailer shelf space faster than we expect.
■
UGG is able to continue rolling out successful new products.
■
Recent levels of full-price selling are maintained.
■
The company accelerates the pace of share repurchases.
RISKS TO TARGET
DECK
Potential downside risks include:
■
Hoka operates in a highly competitive athletic footwear market.
■
Hoka could run out of new channels of distribution, slowing potential growth.
■
UGG growth could stall if new product introductions fail to resonate with consumers.
■
Margins could come under pressure if growth slows and more discounting/markdowns are necessary.
