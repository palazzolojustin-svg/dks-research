FY3Q26 Review: Back on Track;
Reiterate OW
In FY3Q26 DECK beat across the board posting EPS of $3.33
(vs. cons. $2.76). The beat was driven by better sales, GM, and
SG&A leverage. The company raised its FY26 guidance for
sales, GM, OM, and EPS, with the FY3Q25 beat offset by
modestly tempered FY4Q25 guidance.
Back on Track; Reiterate OW. We are encouraged by the FY3Q26 beat as the company delivered
on all fronts seeing +7.1% topline growth, sequential GM expansion despite tariffs, and
meaningful SG&A leverage, which all led to a a significant beat on the quarter posting EPS of
$3.33 (versus consensus of $2.76). DECK has recovered from prior missteps earlier in the year,
such as channel and inventory management, delivering strong FY3Q26 results as management
remained disciplined on its marketplace management initiatives and the core fundamentals of
the business. We are encouraged by positive sales growth within its DTC channel as both HOKA
and UGG DTC sales growth outpaced wholesale, but are even more encouraged by a return to
growth within HOKA U.S. DTC (we estimate it was positive MSD). Management highlighted
strong execution during the holiday season at both brands driven by product innovation, an
elevated consumer experience, and successful brand activations and marketing campaigns,
which all led to higher full price selling and growth across all channels and regions. We view
product innovation as the key for continued topline growth and margin expansion and we
believe DECK has the right products in place and down the pipeline at both the UGG and HOKA
brands to continue the momentum over the long-term. However, the entire FY3Q26 beat did not
flow through to the full-year. As a reminder, brand manufacturers tend to operate in "half years"
versus quarters. As such, FY4Q26 is expected to see slower sales with UGG sales to be flat as it
shifted orders from FY4Q26 into FY3Q26, GM to contract 200bps due to tariffs, and SG&A to
deleverage as it invests further behind its business. We remain confident in shares of DECK as: 1)
wholesale demand for product increases; 2) a strong pipeline of product innovation within
multiple categories at both its UGG and HOKA brands; 3) ample opportunity to continue to build
both domestically and internationally; 4) the opportunity for continued full-price selling, which
should support both the top line and earnings; and 5) multiple levers to continue to gain global
market share at both of its brands. As such, we reiterate our Overweight rating. In reaction in the
aftermarket, DECK shares traded +13.0% versus the S&P 500 flat.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: DECK in the "HUNT" phase
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.

Tariff Exposure Update. The company now anticipates a smaller tariff impact than previously
assumed for FY26. FY3Q26 GM was better than expected, driven primarily by a lower than
expected tariff impact due to the timing of inventory flows and the benefit of lower-tariff
inventory in the pipeline. However, management indicated that FY4Q26 GM will face the largest
tariff impact in FY26, citing an expected 200bps headwind entirely from tariffs, as the full 20%
tariff burden flows through the P&L. Despite this, management lowered its FY26 outlook for the
unmitigated tariff headwind to $110mn (from prior $150mn) and the net tariff impact to
$25mn (from implied prior range of $55mn to $75mn). Management attributed this to better
than expected price action benefits and favorable timing of inventory sold. We expect pressure
from tariffs to roll in FY1Q27, FY2Q27, and to a lesser extent FY3Q27. 
Prior tariff commentary. The company now expects the unmitigated tariff impact on FY26 to
be ~$150mm, with their mitigation efforts for the fiscal year to offset approximately $75mm to
$95mm of this pressure, down from previously guided estimate of ~$185mm of unmitigated
impact from the previous quarter. The company noted that it saw a delay in the net headwind of
tariffs due to pulling forward inventory shipments before the increased tariff rates were
implemented and as a result, it did not experience a meaningful negative impact in FY2Q26.
However, management stated that this will be unique to FY2Q26 and their expectation of net
tariff headwinds in FY2H26 remain unchanged. The company's mitigation tactics remain in line
with FY1Q26 commentary, including: 1) select, strategic, and staggered price increases and 2)
partial cost-sharing negotiations with factory partners. Recall, related to sourcing, less than 5%
of its footwear is produced in China, some of which would not be routed to the U.S. The majority
if its production is coming from Southeast Asia, primarily Vietnam at about 80%.
