Strong FY26 Sets Stage for Robust LRP Algorithm. DECK closed FY26 with strong performance
in FY4Q26, delivering revenue of $1.12bn (+10% y/y), driven by continued strength at HOKA
(+14.5%) and solid execution at UGG (+9.2%), with OM of 23.1% and record EPS of $7.02, which
underscore the durability of the model. HOKA remains a key growth driver, with brand
awareness meaningfully expanding in the U.S. and internationally, benefit from deeper
franchise breadth, a healthy wholesale order book, and continued momentum in the DTC
channel. UGG execution continues to improve with its 365 strategy, men’s penetration, and
growth in sneakers and sandals supporting the brand to become all-season, while broadening
the consumer base. While we acknowledge the near-term macro headwinds from tariffs and
Middle East related logistics/cost impacts, we see these as manageable, with limited structural
impact given sourcing flexibility, pricing power, and disciplined inventory and channel
management. Furthermore, management introduced a compelling multi-year framework
calling for HSD revenue CAGR from FY28-FY30, LDD growth at HOKA, MSD growth at UGG, and
LDD EPS growth, supported by faster international and DTC expansion. We remain confident in
the shares of DECK on: 1) increased wholesale demand for product; 2) a strong pipeline of
product innovation within multiple categories at both its UGG and HOKA brands; 3) ample
opportunity to continue to build both domestically and internationally; 4) brand commitment
to full-price selling, which should support both the top line and earnings; and 5) multiple levers
to continue to gain global market share at both of its brands. As such, we reiterate our
Overweight rating. In reaction on 5/21/26, in the aftermarket, DECK shares traded -0.6% versus
the S&P 500 flat.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: DECK in the "HUNT" phase
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.

Tariff Exposure
Tariff Exposure Update. FY4Q26 GM expanded +90bps y/y despite tariff headwinds, with higher
full-price selling across UGG and HOKA, favorable FX, reduced freight costs, and favorable
product and channel mix driving the increase. For FY26, GM decreased -20bps, including an
80bps headwind from tariffs partially offset by 60bps of underlying GM expansion from
favorable product mix and lower freight costs. Guidance assumes the current 10% tariff rate
remains in effect for the duration of FY27, with management citing they are pursuing tariff
refunds but have not incorporated any refund amount into guidance. The company paid around
$120mn in IEEPA tariffs, but noted that once it receives the money back, the company will factor
in cost sharing arrangements with partners. FY1Q27 GM is expected to be down y/y primarily
due to tariffs, with slight positive offsets from favorable channel mix and favorable FX. 
Prior Tariff Commentary. The company now anticipates a smaller tariff impact than previously
assumed for FY26. FY3Q26 GM was better than expected, driven primarily by a lower than
expected tariff impact due to the timing of inventory flows and the benefit of lower-tariff
inventory in the pipeline. However, management indicated that FY4Q26 GM will face the largest
tariff impact in FY26, citing an expected 200bps headwind entirely from tariffs, as the full 20%
tariff burden flows through the P&L. Despite this, management lowered its FY26 outlook for the
unmitigated tariff headwind to $110mn (from prior $150mn) and the net tariff impact to
$25mn (from implied prior range of $55mn to $75mn). Management attributed this to better-
than-expected price action benefits and favorable timing of inventory sold. We expect pressure
from tariffs to roll in FY1Q27, FY2Q27, and to a lesser extent FY3Q27.
