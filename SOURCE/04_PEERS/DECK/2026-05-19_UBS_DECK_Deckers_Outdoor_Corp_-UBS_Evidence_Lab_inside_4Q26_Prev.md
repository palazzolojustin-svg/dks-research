a Balanced Upside/Downside Skew
We doubt DECK's Q4 report changes sentiment or the stock's P/E much:
We think Hoka had solid sales momentum through March and believe this sets DECK up 
to deliver a 10c Q4 EPS beat. At the same time, our view is trends have modestly 
softened QTD and anticipate this drives DECK to deliver FY27 EPS guidance of $7.05-
$7.20. We believe the market expects a similar outcome and thus don't anticipate 
DECK's 4Q report to change the stock's P/E much. This is why we see a balanced upside/
downside skew over the event.  The options market is pricing in a +/- 9.7% jump vs. a 
7.8% historical avg. move. We agree with the options market's view. 
DECK sentiment leans bearish, in our view:  
UBS Quant team's  data suggests DECK's crowding score has fallen significantly over the 
last 3 months and is now below its 5-yr. avg.  (Fig. 9,10). Plus, DECK's short interest has 
risen 100 bps since it reported Q3 EPS to 3.1%. Lastly, DECK's stock price has fallen 
19% over the last 3 months, underperforming the S&P 500 by 2,800 bps. These 
datapoints and our conversations with investors suggest sentiment around DECK leans 
bearish.  In addition to the FY27 EPS guide as discussed above, we believe the "bars" for 
the event are: 1) 4Q Hoka sales +Mid-teens% and UGG sales +LSD% y/y; 2) FY27 Hoka 
brand sales guidance of +10% and UGG FY27 sales up +LSD% y/y. 
Our checks show solid 4Q Hoka trends, but softer signals for 1QTD: 
Industry data indicates Hoka's 4Q US DTC observed sales rose 9% y/y, accelerating over 
1,800 bps vs. Q3 trends (Fig. 62). However 1QTD data is trending -8% y/y. Industry 
online traffic data shows visits to Hoka's US website rose 7% y/y, down from 19% 
growth in Q3, and 1QTD slowed to -8% y/y declines in April (Fig. 53). Lastly, we believe 
Hoka's 4Q EU DTC sales trends were modestly  softer y/y in Feb-March (Fig. 48). For 
UGG, industry data suggests its US DTC observed sales rose 2% y/y over Q4, improving 
from -3% declines in Q3. Plus, 1QTD UGG data is trending +12% y/y (Fig. 64-65). Other 
industry web traffic data also shows strong UGG trends continuing in April (Fig. 56).  
Valuation: We lower our price target 10% to $145; Buy:
Our $145 PT is based on 18x  $8.05 FY28 EPSe. We lower the P/E used to value DECK to 
18x from 20x given greater macro risk around new geopolitical tensions. Our multiples 
analysis indicates a $145 PT puts DECK's valuation in line with peers in terms of P/E, P/
Sales, and FCF yield. While we see a balanced upside/downside skew around the event, 
we believe DECK has underappreciated LT growth potential and thus rate DECK Buy.

Q: How much can the global footwear industry grow?
We expect it to rise at a 3% to 4% CAGR, highly skewed to sports footwear. We believe athleisure 
should continue driving this category at a compelling mid- to high-single-digit annual pace, 
underpinned by leading industry players like Nike pushing for innovation and prevailing themes like 
casualization and healthy lifestyles. Deckers should benefit from this trend as athleisure covers sports 
(i.e. HOKA brand) and comfort shoes (i.e. UGG).
Q:  Can  Hoka return to delivering +DD% direct-to-consumer (DTC) channel sales growth?
Yes. While choppiness in the transition from the Bondi 9/Clifton 10 franchises in the US and price 
increases related to new US tariff policies likely weigh on US  DTC sales growth in FY26, we believe 
Hoka DTC sales growth rate will accelerate in FY27 to the +LDD% range as Hoka expands further into 
the training, lifestyle, and apparel categories, and continues to expand its global retail footprint. This 
+LDD% rate reflects +MSD% growth in the US and greater than +20% growth in international 
markets, led by very robust growth in APAC. 
 Can DECK deliver higher operating margin expansion?
Yes. We expect EBIT margins hover in the 22-23% through FY30E.  DECK's margins should benefit 
from a mix shift to DTC given higher digital sales and SG&A leverage in the medium term as HOKA 
gains scale, partially offset by tariff-related headwinds.
We rate DECK Buy.  We think Deckers' sales and EPS outlook justify a high valuation. We estimate its 
high-growth brand, HOKA, drives a ~13% 5-year consolidated top-line CAGR and believe this makes 
DECK one of Softlines' best growth stocks. Our quant analysis shows growth stocks have 
outperformed within the Softlines space over the past 25 years and we think the trend will continue. 
We see a solid LT upside/downside skew.
UBS Evidence Lab Google search and social media analyses support our bullish view of the company's 
ability to grow sales. Meanwhile, Euromonitor data points to  a 3% to 4% 5-year CAGR for the 
footwear industry, underpinned by a stronger sports footwear category (+8%) outlook.
Our reverse DCF analysis suggests the market is pricing in a +MSD% 5-year EPS CAGR, below our

Quant Crowding Factor Insights
Crowding Score
What's crowding? Crowding is too many of the same type of market participants holding the same or similar positions, in this 
case, a stock. Crowding can occur on the long an d the short side of names. A crowding score serves as a risk control indicator. A 
high crowding score in either direction could pose a risk as there is a high chance investor positioning starts to go in the opposite 
direction. We note the crowding scores have no imposed maximum or minimum values, but the range tends to be from "-30" to 
"+30" due to the scaling and combination of inputs. In other words, the higher the score on the positive side implies the stock is 
more long-crowded. Conversely, the lower on the negative side implies the stock is more crowded on the short side. 
Please see more information on crowding scores and crowding momentum here: Report Link.

Crowding Momentum
What's crowding momentum? Three-factor crowding momentum combines three stats: 1) Crowding score; 2) change in 
crowding, and 3) 1-month momentum. On the long side, the most crowded names that are becoming more crowded coupled 
with recent poor relative performance should do the best. On the short side, the most short crowded names that are becoming 
more short and have had relatively good recent performance should do the worst. Essentially, longs tend to outperform and shorts 
underperform. Stocks with positive momentum get a "+1" score. Stocks with negative momentum get a "-1" score. Most of the 
stocks in our Softlines coverage universe have neither positive nor negative momentum. These stocks get a "0" score.

UPSIDE ($223): We forecast a ~16.5% 5-year upside case EPS CAGR (FY25-FY30E). We 
see an upside scenario where DECK delivers low-double-digit revenue growth in the UGG 
brand, supported by int'l expansion in China and post its European marketplace reset. 
Plus, its new product styles outside the classic winter boot strongly resonate with younger 
consumers and the brand becomes an all-season product. This expansion along with a 
faster-than-expected rollout of HOKA through the DTC channel yields an incremental 
~430 bps in total sales annual growth vs. our base case. HOKA and DTC growth translate 
into higher gross margins given the positive sales mix shift. This leads to an operating 
margin of ~24.5% by FY30E and the market rewards this profitable growth with a ~22x 
P/E valuation.
BASE ($145):  We forecast a ~9.5% 5-year EPS CAGR (FY25-FY30E). We assume topline 
remains strong in FY26E and grows at a ~9% annual rate thereafter. The algorithm is 
roughly ~35 bps annual operating margin contraction, stabilizing at ~22% by FY30E, as 
well as ~3% share count reduction due to buybacks. DECK should benefit from robust 
growth of HOKA as it rebounds in its US DTC channel and evolves into a lifestyle 
footwear brand from being purely focused on running. Meanwhile, we anticipate 
DECK's UGG brand sales to rise at a ~6% CAGR. We anticipate tariffs pressure gross 
margins over the NTM, but we believe gross margins should expand beyond 1HFY27 as 
structural sales mix shifts to the DTC channel vs. wholesale's lower margins, and HOKA's 
profitability increases. We think DECK's ~9.5% EPS growth outlook justifies an ~18x P/E, 
which we apply to our $8.05 FY28E EPS to derive our $145 price target.
DOWNSIDE ($53):  We forecast a -1% 5-year EPS CAGR (FY25-FY30E) in our downside 
case. This scenario assumes the US consumer spending environment softens significantly 
in CY26-27 that results in revenues growing ~6.5% y/y in FY26E and +MSD% annual 
revenue growth thereafter with much lower operating margins. Our downside scenario 
implies a negative mix shift given higher exposure to less profitable channels, such as 
wholesale and distributors. The UGG brand loses share in the US and abroad as the 
comfort fashion trend evolves into something less favorable for the brand and its new 
launches do not engage consumers. Hoka brand share gains slow as consumer trends 
shift towards lower profile silhouettes and weigh on Hoka's Clifton and Bondi franchises. 
Plus, elevating competitive pressures weigh on Hoka's key road running business. The 
weakness in volumes results in an increase in promotional activity to drive sales, hurting 
gross margins while the company deleverages SG&A.  Our downside scenario assumes -
130 bps operating margin decreases annually in this scenario. We anticipate DECK trades 
at ~10x P/E in our downside scenario.

UBS Evidence Lab methodology
Instagram
UBS Evidence Lab tracks page and post-level data from Instagram pages of leading 
brands and influencers. Metrics are based on the aggregation of the official Instagram 
pages for a given brand or influencer. Key metrics include page followers, posts, and 
interactions (likes, comments, and video views) on posts. Follower counts are presented 
based on the date of collection and interaction metrics are presented across multiple 
timescales including 4-week rolling sum, monthly, and quarterly. This report leverages 
the following UBS Evidence Lab asset: Global Brand Tracker [ Instagram ].
WeChat
UBS Evidence Lab tracks post-level data from WeChat official accounts of leading 
brands. Metrics are based on the aggregation of posts on the WeChat official account of 
a given brand. Key metrics include reads, WOWs, likes and posts, presented monthly 
and quarterly based on the date of the posts. Time series included in this analysis dates 
from January 2015.
Google 
UBS Evidence Lab used Google search data to frame interest and activity in various 
software platforms. Google Trends enables users to understand changes in the 
popularity of a search query over time in a particular geography and relative to a limited 
set of additional terms. UBS Evidence Lab utilizes a proprietary procedure to normalize 
Google search data across an unlimited number of search terms and geographies. This 
report leverages the following UBS Evidence Lab asset: Global Sportswear & Athleisure 
Brands Search Monitor [ Google ].

Company Description
Deckers Outdoor Corp designs, markets, and distributes mainly footwear, as well as apparel 
and accessories, for lifestyle and high-performance activities. The company distributes its 
products through the direct-to-consumer channel (including company-operated stores and 
eCommerce) and wholesale. Its offerings include brands such as UGG, HOKA, Koolaburra, 
Sanuk, Teva, and others. As of March 31, 2023, it operates 52 retail stores in the US. 
Internationally, it operates 112 retail stores in Austria, Belgium, Canada, China, France, 
Germany, Japan, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and the UK.
Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Our price target is derived from a multiple analysis, supported by a DCF valuation as a 
validation method. Several downside risks to consider: (1) The health of the consumer. Brands 
are reliant on the state of the macro environment and any deterioration in consumer 
spending and/ or unemployment could negatively impact sales and margins. (2) Fashion risk/ 
inability to execute on merchandise strategy. Brands are inherently exposed to fashion risk 
due to ongoing shifts in trends and customer preferences. The company's success has hinged 
upon its ability to forecast fashion trends and appropriately translate the looks for its target 
customer. If it finds itself unable to predict and/ or interpret changes in trends, sales and 
margins could suffer. (3) Inventory risk. The company's ability to manage inventory levels is 
important to be the health of margins and earnings. If sales are worse than expected and 
inventories are aggressively purchased relative to actual sales trends, the company will likely 
need to take incremental markdowns to clear remaining product, which could pressure gross 
margins.
