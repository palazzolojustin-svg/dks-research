The Art of Channel Management...More Shoes on More Feet. Brand manufacturers have the
challenge of balancing competing DTC and wholesale distribution channels. In the early stage of
growth, both channels grow in tandem as building brand awareness drives accelerating market
share gains in both channels simultaneously. However, as a company matures there are periods
of time where one channel expands faster than the other. In DECK's case, wholesale expansion
is currently negatively impacting DTC growth, as new competing points of distribution enter the
market. This is temporary and not structural, and as such, we reiterate our Overweight view of
DECK over a longer horizon. The most important point is that DECK is "putting more shoes on
more feet" and gaining global market share in both UGG and HOKA brands. In reaction in the
aftermarket, DECK shares traded -12.3% versus the S&P 500 flat. 
FY2Q26 beats, while FY26 guidance disappoints. In FY2Q26 DECK beat on sales, GM, and EPS
of $1.82 (cons. $1.58). The beat was primarily driven by GM due to price increases and less than
expected tariff pressure. The company reinstated full year FY26 guidance with sales coming in
below consensus, while EPS at the midpoint came in slightly ahead. Once again, wholesale and
international drove the majority of the sales growth, while the US and DTC remained under
pressure. Specifically, we note that the wholesale channel was the predominant driver of the
sales growth in FY2Q26 with some timing of shipments benefiting FY2Q26. The DTC channel,
particularly in the US, will remain pressured throughout FY2H26, though we are encouraged by
the fact that the company expects to see sequential improvement from FY3Q26 to FY4Q26.
Additionally, it is important to note that the near-term tariff headwinds are expected to put
pressure on GM combined with increased SG&A as a % of sales throughout the remainder of the
year. Management did highlight strength during the back to school season and they anticipate a
strong holiday season, though they expressed caution around the health of the US consumer as
higher prices and inflation could put pressure on demand. However, despite the near-term
headwinds, we remain confident in shares of DECK as: 1) wholesale demand for product

Tariff Exposure Update. The company now expects the unmitigated tariff impact on FY26 to be
~$150mm, with their mitigation efforts for the fiscal year to offset approximately $75mm to
$95mm of this pressure, down from previously guided estimate of ~$185mm of unmitigated
impact from the previous quarter. The company noted that it saw a delay in the net headwind of
tariffs due to pulling forward inventory shipments before the increased tariff rates were
implemented and as a result, it did not experience a meaningful negative impact in FY2Q26.
However, management stated that this will be unique to FY2Q26 and their expectation of net
tariff headwinds in FY2H26 remain unchanged. The company's mitigation tactics remain in line
with FY1Q26 commentary, including: 1) select, strategic, and staggered price increases and 2)
partial cost-sharing negotiations with factory partners. Recall, related to sourcing, less than 5%
of its footwear is produced in China, some of which would not be routed to the U.S. The majority
if its production is coming from Southeast Asia, primarily Vietnam at about 80%. 
Quarter in Review. Adj. EPS of $1.82 beat consensus of $1.58, driven by slightly better sales and
GM. Sales of $1.43bn came in ahead of consensus of $1.42bn, as wholesale came in better than
expected with sales of $1.04bn (vs. cons. of $972mm), partially offset by lower DTC sales of
$395mm (vs. cons. of $432mm). Gross margin of 56.2% beat consensus of 53.9% primarily due
to a benefit from favorable product mix and price increases combined with a delay in the impact
of tariffs that management said would be unique to the second quarter. SG&A rate of 33.4%
matched consensus of 33.4%. Management reinstated full fiscal year 2026 guidance, with
revenue expected to be ~$5.35bn (vs. cons. of $5.45bn), with HOKA expected to increase low-
teens and UGG expected to increase by LSD to MSD. Gross margin is expected to be ~56% (vs.
cons. of 55.8%), with SG&A rate of 34.5% (vs. cons. of 34.6%), operating margin of 21.5% (vs.
cons. of 21.2%), and adj. EPS in the range of $6.30 to $6.39 (vs. cons. of $6.33). 
Inventory. Based on our proprietary inventory analysis, DECK has posted one quarter of
negative sales-to-inventory growth. In FY2Q26 the Inventory Management Spread ("IM Spread")
was (93) basis points and worsened from 734 in the prior quarter. DECK's Gross Margin Return
on Inventory ("GMROI") has improved for one quarter, and its Operating Margin Return on
Inventory ("OMROI") has worsened for two consecutive quarters.
Adjusting estimates and lowering our PT to $113 (from $141). Our FY26, FY27, and FY28 adj.
EPS estimates are $6.34, $6.86, and $7.66 (from $6.34, $7.08, and $8.01), respectively. For FY26,
we are lowering our sales and gross margin assumptions, while slightly flowing through the
FY2Q26 beat. We lower our FY27 and FY28 estimates due to lower sales and GM, though partially
offset by lower SG&A dollars. Our new PT of $113 (from $141) is based on an NTM P/E multiple of
15x (from 18x) applied to our CY27E adj. EPS of $7.53 (from $7.86). We lower our multiple 3 turns
to be more in line with the current NTM P/E multiple of 15.4x and due to headwinds from tariffs
and macro uncertainty.

Proprietary PRISM Curve
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.

Prior tariff commentary. Based on recent tariff updates, which includes an increase on
Vietnam from 10% to 20%, management increased its expectations for its an unmitigated
impact to COGS of $185mn for FY26, up from prior $150mn. To partially mitigate the impact, the
company has implemented selective initial price increases and plans to phase in additional
pricing actions throughout FY26, aiming to recapture up to $75mn. Despite mitigation efforts,
management does not expect these actions to fully offset the incremental costs from tariffs and
anticipates margin pressure in 2H26. 
Barclays Tariff "QuikCalc" Model assumptions. We have updated our Tariff "QuikCalc" model
analysis, which now assumes global tariff assumptions effective 8/7/25. Our prior tariff analysis
had assumed 30% China and 10% Rest of World (excluding Mexico and Canada). Note our
estimate of Tariff on Goods Imported to the US is a gross unmitigated amount for a full year, and
we calculate the price increase necessary to mitigate full tariff cost (absent other mitigation
strategies). Our updated tariff calculator under 8/7/25 effective rates, suggests that for DECK,
total average unit cost will increase by 12.8% with pass-through to the U.S. market if only
increasing prices to fully offset costs by 8.4% (see Figure 4).
