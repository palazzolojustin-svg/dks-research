Reports Of DECK's Demise Have Been Greatly
Exaggerated; Strong Q3 Beat-&-Raise
OUR CALL
While 2025 was a year to forget for DECK shareholders, 2026 is off to a much better start,
with a strong beat-and-raise in fiscal 3Q26. With DTC trends accelerating at both brands,
wholesale channel demand remaining strong, long-term distribution growth opportunities for
Hoka, conservative Q4 guidance, and a fortress balance sheet that is now being used more
aggressively (via buyback), we see a compelling multi-year growth opportunity here. We're
raising our FY26/27 EPS forecasts to $6.85/$7.48 (from $6.36/$7.00) and our price target to $138
(from $115). For the quarter, revenue growth of 7% and EPS of $3.33 were well above Street at
2% and $2.77, respectively. FY26 guidance was raised, now calling for sales growth of 8%-9% (vs.
7% prev.) and EPS of $6.80-$6.85 (vs. $6.30-$6.39 prev.).
TOM'S TAKEAWAYS
Tom’s Takeaway #1: Hoka Accelerates Nicely, Especially DTC. Hoka grew 19% in Q3, solidly above
consensus +11%. Importalty, DTC showed a very sharp acceleration, growing +19% (positive both
in the U.S. and overseas) from +8% in Q2. This was the first time in 4 quarters that DTC outgrew
wholesale, though wholesale was similarly strong at +18%. They noted particularly strong demand in
the European wholesale channel, where they're averaging 90% sell-through rates. For Q4 Hoka was
guided up 13%-14%, which could prove conservative given that it's the easiest compare of the year.
Tom's Takeaway #2: Hoka Still Has Robust Distribution Potential. Hoka is only in about half the
targeted sporting goods doors in the U.S., and only ~40% penetrated in Europe. In athletic-specialty,
they're only in ~25% of doors in the U.S. and less than 20% of doors in Europe. And though run specialty
is fairly saturated in the U.S., they're only ~80% penetrated in Europe.
Tom’s Takeaway #3: UGG Fears Ease as DTC Rebounds. UGG grew 5% in Q3, vs. consensus flat,
with similar growth rates in both DTC and wholesale. The DTC performance is particularly notable
following Q2's disappointing -10%. Q4 is planned flat, which could prove conservative.
Tom’s Takeaway #4: Full-Price Sell-Through Rates Drive Better-Than-Expected Margins. Gross
margin was down 50bps in the quarter, which was much better than consensus down 290bps due
to lower-than-expected impacts from tariffs and markdowns. They guided to 200bps of gross margin
erosion in Q4, which may prove conservative given lean inventories and resurgent momentum at the
brands. Q4 guidance also implies SG&A growth accelerates to ~15% (vs. +4% in Q3), which may be
another source of EPS upside.
Tom's Takeaway #5: Stepping On The Gas Pedal On Buyback. DECK spent nearly $350 million on
share buyback in Q3 (all-time quarterly high), and they plan to spend at least another $200 million in
Q4, surpassing $1 billion in buyback for the year. With over $2 billion in cash, no debt, and nearly $1
billion in FCF generation, DECK can afford to get more aggressive on buyback.

At a Glance
OUR INVESTMENT THESIS
We think DECK is one of the highest-quality companies in our coverage, with an impressive multi-year track record of performance, two
of the strongest brands in the industry (Hoka and UGG), a strong management team and a fortress balance sheet.
BULL CASE ASSUMPTIONS
With the benefit of a slew of new products,
as well as channel fill in the wholesale
channel, Hoka growth could re accelerate
to the 15%-20% range the next few
years. Additionally, given international
momentum and new product launches
domestically, UGG could generate MSD-
HSD revenue growth. If margins are
sustained in the low-20's, then calendar
2027 EPS could be ~$9.00 and the share
price could reach ~$180 (20x P/E on FY27E
EPS ).
BASE CASE ASSUMPTIONS
We expect FY26 EPS to come in at the
high end of guidance, implying +HSD
EPS growth. We believe the company can
grow EPS at a similar growth rate next
year, driven by MSD-HSD revenue growth,
modest margin erosion (wrap-around of
tariffs), and buyback. We expect EPS to
grow +DD in FY28, resulting in EPS power
of $8.00-$8.50 in fiscal 2028 (Mar'28). Our
$138 price target is based on a high-teens
P/E on calendar FY27 EPS of ~$8.15.
BEAR CASE ASSUMPTIONS
Against difficult compares and a tough
macro environment, UGG growth could
flat-line, while Hoka could slow to
single-digit growth (mainly driven by
new distribution). Tariffs plus normalized
discounting levels could push margins
down to the high-teens. If so, EPS growth
would slow dramatically, and calendar
2027 EPS could be $6.00, and the share
price could fall to ~$80 (low-teens P/E on
FY27E EPS).
COMPANY DESCRIPTION
DECK owns a portfolio of global footwear brands, led by the UGG and Hoka brands.

VALUATION (PRICE TARGET: $138.00)
Our $138 price target is based on a high-teens P/E on calendar 2027 EPS of ~$8.15.
POTENTIAL UPSIDE DRIVERS
The company could do better than we expect if:
■
Hoka gains brand awareness and retailer shelf space faster than we expect.
■
UGG is able to continue rolling out successful new products.
■
Recent levels of full-price selling are maintained.
■
The company accelerates the pace of share repurchases.
RISKS TO TARGET
DECK
Potential downside risks include:
■
Hoka operates in a highly competitive athletic footwear market.
■
Hoka could run out of new channels of distribution, slowing potential growth.
■
UGG growth could stall if new product introductions fail to resonate with consumers.
■
Margins could come under pressure if growth slows and more discounting/markdowns are necessary.
