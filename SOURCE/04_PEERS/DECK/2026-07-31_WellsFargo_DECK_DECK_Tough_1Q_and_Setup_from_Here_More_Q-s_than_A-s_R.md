Our Call
Remain UW, PT lower to $85. 1Q print adds more fuel to Bear Case and likely keeps Bulls 
from engaging despite valuation + lack of neg revisions. Another quarter where we believe 
there are more questions than answers around the direction of the story.
 
Thoughts on 1Q. Three key takes we'd flag: 1) Smallest beat in several years (revs and 
EPS), 2) a below Street guide for 2Q (numbers coming down) & 3) a heavily 2H weighted 
outlook at HOKA (following a rev miss in 1Q). 2H now appears to carry revs risk, and the 
outlook appears heavily new distribution driven. While mgmt stated much of this is timing 
related, we were surprised by the amount of volume expected to fall into 2H, which 
investors likely poke at. PT $85 on 11x (unchanged) new CY27 EPS.
 
HOKA Thoughts. Full-year outlook remains unchanged, but the main negative issue to 
poke at is the channel trajectory through year-end in our view. 1) 1Q revenue came in 
below consensus ($703M vs. $707M); with DTC +17% and wholesale +3%, driven by 
international; 2) new models drove over half of the growth, including Pro concept; 3)
2Q guide for +HSD is below consensus and 2H embeds acceleration driven by timing in 
international wholesale shipments.
 
UGG Thoughts. UGG 1Q was in-line with balanced channel dynamics delivering against 
its +MSD algo. 1) Continued progress on 365 efforts with continued strength in classic 
styles, but also newer investments in fashion, apparel, casual footwear, sneakers, and 
sandals (Minimel, Golden collection); 2) strength in men's (still ~15% of business) drove 
the majority of growth; 3) healthier assortments and better sell-through driving better 
margin and allowing for reinvestment in marketing.
 
2Q Guide Below Consensus. 2Q guide is below Street ($1.73-$1.78 vs. $1.90). Bulls likely 
view it as conservative, but with the 1Q top-line miss, expect Bears to view this as a visible 
deterioration in HOKA demand NT. 1) HOKA growth for +HSD is below consensus (+14%) 
against the easiest compare of the year (+11%); 2) GM guided down from tariffs and 
rising freight (despite the 1Q beat against a similar guide); 3) SG&A still elevated on new 
hires, marketing, rent, and technology.
 
Acceleration Implied For 2H. While the 2H timing shift of international wholesale was 
communicated last Q (but not quantified), the optics of the updated guide will likely 
draw scrutiny from Bears - HOKA missed 1Q revs, 2Q was guided lower, and the delta 
to maintain the FY is now an acceleration in 2H (12% to 13%). Outside this timing shift, 
management is confident in the underlying demand for the brand consistent across both 
halves, with continued execution from UGG into fall/winter season.

Company Description
Deckers Outdoor Corporation, based in Goleta, CA, is a leading 
marketer of footwear and related accessories and apparel. Its 
brands are UGG and HOKA. The company sells its products through 
domestic and international retailers (including national department 
stores, chain and specialty stores) as well as its own directly-
operated stores and global ecommerce websites.

Investment Thesis
Key themes surrounding the company include:
1. HOKA Long-term Opportunity. Acquired 10+ years ago (and generating just $3-5M in sales), 
HOKA has been one of the better growth stories in the Softlines space for several years, growing from 
just over $200M in revenues pre-COVID to just over $2B+ in revenues in FY25 — a +50% growth 
CAGR over the past several years. During this time, HOKA established its dominance in the technical 
running category and made its mark on fashion with its unique silhouette, fresh colorways, and larger 
foam heel. They currently have a Top 3 share position in the specialty run channel, and they have 
penetrated the authentic runner and expanded well beyond towards more casual joggers/runners 
and even a lifestyle consumer. While HOKA still sees the opportunity to add "billions" to revenue we 
do note growth in the brand has slowed in FY26 as the US business has slowed on tough compares 
and increased competition, with DTC no longer a growth vehicle and more reliance needed outside 
the US as well (neither detail favorable for investors). We view FY27 as likely to have a more balanced 
mix of channel and geography growth, but with international still outpacing domestic (especially in 
2H). Further, our industry checks indicate that there remains opportunity (and risk) for the HOKA 
brand to embrace its lifestyle customer, but to date has not done enough to address this. The primary 
difference being through introducing more product beyond the super-cushioned platform run shoe - 
with a more robust product launch calendar (and away from the 1x new model every year). High level, 
competition now has replicated what made HOKA great, and in our view the brand needs to show it 
can come to market with a product that allows it to break away from the pack on its core shoe.
2. Expanding UGG's TAM. The company has been working to make UGG a less seasonal business and 
go after a male customer. This was evidenced by the extension of product into sneakers, all-weather 
boots, and more apparel, with the company noting progress in its 365 initiative, the growth from the 
Lowmel franchise expanding to lifestyle sneaker. The shift in strategy seems to be working, with the 
brand growing Mid-Teens in FY24/FY25 and HSD in FY26, though the algorithm remains for it to be 
an MSD growth business long term.
3. Sustaining That Growth Becoming More Difficult. Despite the multi-year track record, concerns 
on the durability of growth intensified in 2026, with headwinds on difficult channel comparisons 
(tougher DTC comps, more wholesale fill-in), a resurgent Nike Run in the US, and a 1H26-weighted 
launch calendar of new models against higher inventory of old models. Brand growth has been choppy, 
slowing to +11% as of 2Q26, HOKA accelerating to +19% in 3Q26, +15% in 4Q, and again decelerating 
to +8% in 4Q. Bears also point to potential issues around new product release schedules, which 
are focused more on the core runner and less so on the casual customer, who has become a much 
bigger part of the product mix. While growth in International has been solid, we do see potential risk 
stemming from actions Nike is taking in order to recapture lost share in specialty run. To us, it is more 
than coincidental that Nike saw significant growth in specialty run in North America throughout 2025 
at the same time that HOKA began to focus on wholesale fill-in with decelerating DTC trends. While 
DECK was able to drive growth through international in 2025, Nike has now turned its attention to 
correcting its issues overseas, looking to reclaim lost market share, and rationalizing the marketplace - 
which may create a more negative promotional operating environment for a longer period of time.
4. Margins Well Above Industry. With operating margin near 23.1% for FY26, well above the industry 
group and despite a relatively difficult year on the top line, investors are debating DECK's go-forward 
margin (i.e., have margins peaked and will growth require additional investment in the face of an 
increasingly competitive market - marginal revenue has a higher marginal cost). The guide for FY27 
calls for contraction to just over 21.5% certainly has some conservatism in it, but we view the risk/
reward as more weighed to the downside rather than additional expansion from here. We do see 
potential benefits near term, including the reversal of tariffs and the company's ability to manage 
costs down and price up new models, but excess margins may face continued long-term pressure as 
reinvestment is likely necessary to sustain the top-line growth.

The Bull Case….We believe that investors who are bullish on DECK shares are focused on (1) Hoka 
recaptures its magic through technical investments and accelerates growth back above the +15-20% 
range, (2) ongoing margin headwinds are largely tariff-related that is being worked through, and 
channel mix becomes less of a headwind, (3) UGG direction is still healthy, as the brand has innovated 
beyond the classics and manages inventory and (4) valuation now provides better risk/reward.
The Bear Case….On the other hand, we believe that investors who are bearish on DECK shares believe
(1) Hoka brand has plateaued and competitive pressures will revert growth rates downward, (2) Hoka 
brand margins are normalizing and will likely fall over a multi-year period as the brand matures and as 
it moves deeper into wholesale (vs. DTC) and (3) UGG is likewise over-earning and will be hard-pressed 
to sustain current levels of profitability, also based on channel mix.
Financial Outlook
FY27E: Our FY27E EPS of $7.45 (from $7.40) represents a 6% increase y/y. We are projecting total CC 
sales to increase 7%. We are projecting GM down ~90bps, to 56.8%, and we expect SG&A to grow +9% 
in dollars, to 35.2%. As a result, we expect operating margin to contract ~150bps, to 21.6%.
FY28E: Our FY28E EPS estimate of $7.65 (from $7.90) represents EPS +3% y/y. We are projecting 
total CC sales +4%. We are projecting GM down ~40bps, to 56.5%, and we expect SG&A to grow +6% 
in dollars, to 35.8%. As a result, we expect operating margin to contract ~90bps, to 20.7%.
