7. Ugg Expanding Beyond Seasonal Core, Winning With Men
Under new president, Anne Spangenberg, who was previously Nike's merchant chief, Ugg has
developed a more robust year-round product assortment in the past two years, and could build on
this momentum to boost sales by low-double digits in fiscal 2026. In 1H, sales may outpace consensus'
mid-single digits as shoulder-season styles like Lowmel, Goldenstar and Tasman are resonating
globally, with many out of stock during our channel checks.
Ugg is also making headway in men's and we expect that momentum to accelerate given recent
initiatives, like the first male-focused campaign in over five years featuring Post Malone, who
previously had a long-standing collaboration with rival Crocs. Men's and apparel are key catalysts for
Ugg to double sales in the long term. (08/27/25)

8. DTC Momentum May Build as Launches Drive Traffic
New products could unlock further direct-to-consumer upside for Hoka and Ugg in 1H as refreshes
fuel digital engagement. Ugg.com traffic surged 184% in June, according to Similarweb, suggesting
the brand's push into shoulder-season styles is resonating and helping it gain relevance beyond cold-
weather boots. Key styles like Goldenstar and Lowmel appear to be driving strong sell-through and
broader adoption. The sharp rise in traffic may also reflect stronger engagement from men following
recent targeted campaigns.
Hoka.com traffic rose 33% in April, coinciding with the launch of the Clifton 10, one of its top-selling
and most recognizable names. The spike suggests upgraded models are drawing new and returning
customers, even as some US consumers shift to wholesale to try on newer silhouettes. (07/21/25)

9. Deckers Trades at Discount to Historical Valuation

10. Limited Exposure to China; Premium Positioning May Aid
Deckers will likely navigate the changing tariff landscape well as it has minimal exposure to China and

leverage in negotiations with wholesale partners and manufacturers to help offset some of the drag

20. Gross Margin May Slow, But Still Structurally Stronger
Though Deckers' gross margin may ease in fiscal 2026 due to tariffs, freight and a wholesale-skewed
mix, the brand's margin structure remains materially stronger than it was three years ago. Since fiscal
2023, gross margin has expanded by over 800 bps, led by dramatic gains at Ugg, a more favorable
brand mix as Hoka scales up, and tighter channel and inventory management. Ugg's gross margin has
risen 11 percentage points, surpassing Hoka's in 2025 due to higher average selling prices, improved
product mix and sustained full-price selling.
This year, promotional activity and cost pressures may weigh modestly, but price increases and
premium positioning are expected to cushion the impact. Structural shifts in mix and brand discipline
suggest margins can remain well above pre-2023 levels, even in a more normalized environment.
(10/03/25)

21. EBIT Margin May Ease From Peak as Investments Grow
Deckers' Ebit margin is likely to decline modestly from the record 23.6% reached in fiscal 2025, muted
by tariff-driven cost pressures and SG&A investments. Operating expenses rose 11% in 1Q as it ramped
up brand-building campaigns, retail expansion and logistics upgrades in EMEA. Though these
investments may lift SG&A as a percentage of sales near term, they're necessary for driving long-term
growth.
With a disciplined cost structure and strong gross margin foundation, Deckers remains among the
most profitable companies in the category. Its premium positioning and scarcity model continues to
drive robust gross margin, particularly at Ugg. Since fiscal 2023, Ugg's Ebit margin has risen nearly 10
percentage points to 39.6%, largely driven by robust full-price top-line growth. (10/03/25)

22. Channel Mix Shift Weighs Near Term; DTC a Margin Lever
Expanding Deckers' more profitable direct-to-consumer channel remains key to supporting
structurally strong margins in the long term, though a shift toward wholesale in the near term may
curb gains. Both Hoka and Ugg are experiencing stronger wholesale growth, driven by elevated sell-
in, door expansion, and consumers seeking to try on new styles. In 1Q, Hoka wholesale rose 30% vs.
just 3% for DTC and UGG saw a 30% wholesale gain, while DTC fell 1%. Though this shift is creating margin
headwinds in fiscal 2026, both brands should remain supported by strong full-price sell-through in
both channels.
As new customers convert to repeat purchasers and store expansion supports greater engagement,
we expect DTC to recover and return to a path toward management’s long-term goal of a 50/50
channel mix. (10/03/25)
