Our Call
Raise 4Q/1Q/FY27 EPS above Street, PT to $115. While we have been concerned on 
HOKA's trajectory since the start of 2025, today we give DECK credit as the brand 
appears to have stabilized product-flow/margin/DTC demand (driving NT confidence 
higher).
 
Thoughts Into 4Q. We are building NT confidence that the worst of the negative catalysts 
are behind DECK - lapping difficult channel dynamics from LY, a rebalanced launch 
calendar for '26 and an incrementally less negative narrative on the resurgence of Nike 
Running. Our checks indicate that HOKA growth remains strong post holiday, and we see 
potential for a narrative shift should DTC stay robust while keeping brand margins healthy 
(HOKA GMs +20bps in 3Q).
 
Fewer Growing Pains. FY26 faced multiple headwinds at HOKA - 1) optically difficult 
channel dynamics (slowing DTC + higher W/S sell-in), 2) missteps on launch calendar and
3) Nike's resurgence in US running. We expect more balanced channel/geo into FY27 
(still higher internationally), and believe the new launch cadence should allow a cleaner 
transition and fewer inventory pressures. Net/net, HOKA DTC growth +DD (and pos in 
US) that began in 3Q should sustain in 4Q and into FY27.
 
Is All Growth Valued Equal? The key question we wrestle with for DECK - what is a +MSD-
HSD growth business with above-industry operating margins and growth predominantly 
driven by wholesale/international expansion worth? While DECK has ample white space, 
we do not see them currently being rewarded for what is viewed as lower-quality growth 
(global wholesale fill-in). Should DTC trends continue their acceleration from 3Q, there 
could be potential upside for the multiple from just ~14x today.
 
Raise EPS Above Street. Our model changes are driven primarily by higher sales at both 
HOKA and UGG, higher GM estimates and more SGA leverage. Most notably, we raise 
4Q HOKA DTC to +15% (from +10%) taking HOKA revs +15% (above guide) as we believe 
demand trends have sustained momentum from holiday. Our 4Q EPS moves to $0.86 
(from $0.72) - above Street, while our 1Q/FY27 EPS moves to $0.96/$7.45 (from $0.92/
$6.98) - again above Street. Our PT of $115 (from $110) is based on 14-15x our CY27 
EPS.
 
Remain EW, but See Fewer Headwinds. Our main take on DECK is that while we have 
leaned more cautious over the past 12+ months, today we lean a bit more positive. While 
last year had multiple growing pains (plus a resurgence of Nike Running in the US), we now 
see DTC upside + fewer headwinds into FY27. While we expect fewer/smaller beats as the 
company sits at industry-leading operating margin (22%+), the durability of growth in 
both channels and in the US remains critical to the story from here.

Company Description
Deckers Outdoor Corporation, based in Goleta, CA, is a leading 
marketer of footwear and related accessories and apparel. Its 
brands are UGG Australia, Hoka One One, and Teva. The company 
sells its products through domestic and international retailers 
(including national department stores, chain and specialty stores) 
as well as its own directly-operated stores and global ecommerce 
websites.

Investment Thesis
Key themes surrounding the company include:
1. HOKA Long-term Opportunity. Acquired 10+ years ago (and generating just $3-5M in sales), 
HOKA has been one of the better growth stories in the Softlines space for several years, growing 
from just over $200M in revenues pre-COVID to just over $2B+ in revenues in FY25 — a +50% 
growth CAGR over the past several years. During this time, HOKA established its dominance in the 
technical running category and made its mark on fashion with its unique silhouette, fresh colorways, 
and larger foam heel. They currently have a Top 3 share position in the specialty run channel and hey 
have penetrated the authentic runner, and expanded well beyond towards more casual joggers/runners 
and even a lifestyle consumer. While HOKA still sees the opportunity to add "billions" to revenue we do 
note growth in the brand has slowed in FY26 as the US business has slowed on tough compares and 
increased competition, with DTC no longer a growth vehicle and more reliance needed outside the US 
as well (neither detail favorable for investors). We view FY27 as likely to have a more balanced mix of 
channel and geography growth, but with international still outpacing domestic.
2. Expanding UGG's TAM. The company has been working to make UGG a less seasonal business and 
go after a male customer. This was evidenced by the extension of product into sneakers, all-weather 
boots, and more apparel, with the company noting progress in its 365 initiative, the growth from 
the Lowmel franchise expanding to lifestyle sneaker (doubling growth in 3Q26). The shift in strategy 
seems to be working, with the brand growing Mid-Teens in FY24/FY25 and HSD in FY26, though the 
algorithm remains for it to be an MSD growth business long-term.
3. Sustaining That Growth Becoming More Difficult. Despite the multi-year track record, concerns 
on the durability of growth intensified in 2026, with headwinds on difficult channel comparisons 
(tougher DTC comps, more wholesale fill-in), a resurgent Nike Run in the US, and a 1H26 weighted 
launch calendar of new models against higher inventory of old models. While brand growth has slowed 
to +11% as of 2Q26, HOKA accelerated to +19% in 3Q26. Bears also point to potential issues around 
new product release schedules, which are focused more on the core runner and less so on the casual 
customer, who has become a much bigger part of the product mix. While growth in International has 
been solid, this is masked by softness in the US, and in our view, the stock is not being rewarded in the 
multiple for what investors view as lower-quality sales growth (more wholesale sell-in in international 
markets).
4. Margins Well Above Industry. With operating margin near 23% for FY26E, well above the industry 
group and despite a relatively difficult year on the top line, investors are debating DECK's go-forward 
margin (i.e., have margins peaked and will growth require additional investment in the face of an 
increasingly competitive market - marginal revenue has a higher marginal cost). We do see potential 
benefits near-term, including the reversal of tariffs and the company's ability to manage costs down 
and price up new models, but excess margins may face continued long-term pressure.

The Bull Case….We believe that investors who are bullish on DECK shares are focused on (1) Hoka 
recaptures its magic through technical investments and accelerates growth back above the +15-20% 
range, (2) margin headwinds in 2H26 and 1H27 are largely tariff-related and one-time in nature, and 
channel mix becomes less of a headwind, (3) UGG direction is still healthy, as the brand has innovated 
beyond the classics and manages inventory and (4) valuation now provides better risk/reward.
The Bear Case….On the other hand, we believe that investors who are bearish on DECK shares believe
(1) Hoka brand has plateaued and competitive pressures will revert growth rates downward, (2) Hoka 
brand margins are normalizing as the brand matures and as it moves deeper into wholesale (vs. DTC) 
and (3) UGG is likewise over-earning and will be hard-pressed to sustain current levels of profitability, 
also based on channel mix.
Financial Outlook
FY26E: Our FY26E EPS estimate of $6.94 represents EPS +10% YoY. We are projecting total CC sales 
+7%. We are projecting gross margin down ~65bps, to 57.2%, and we expect SG&A to grow +9% in 
dollars, to 34.3%. As a result, we expect operating margin to contract ~70bps, to 22.9%.
FY27E: Our FY27E EPS of $7.45 represents a 7% increase YoY. We are projecting total CC sales to 
increase 7%. We are projecting gross margin down ~30bps, to 56.9%, and we expect SG&A to grow 
+8% in dollars, to 34.7%. As a result, we expect operating margin to contract ~70bps, to 22.2%.
