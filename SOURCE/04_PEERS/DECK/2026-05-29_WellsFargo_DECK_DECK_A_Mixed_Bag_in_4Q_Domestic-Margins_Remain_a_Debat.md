Our Call
4Q results are okay, but overall low-quality capping the multiple. An in-line view for HOKA 
and upside from UGG w/ LT algo in-print, vs. contracting margin and an EPS outlook 
bolstered by buybacks. The debate continues on domestic/margin - remain UW.
 
Thoughts on 4Q. Results were mixed - 1) 4Q beat on sales w/in-line HOKA and 
UGG upside, GM strength partially offset by SG&A deleverage on the pull forward of 
investments; 2) guide usually conservative, but implies flattish EBIT on contracting margin 
with EPS offset on share repurchases; 3) A lack of domestic growth continues to keep 
flags raised with business being driven by wholesale expansion. Overall, results remain 
okay, but are of low quality and keep the multiple under pressure.
 
Additional Thoughts. Our recent downgrade was predicated on 1) our view of more 
attractive relative opportunities in apparel; 2) cracks evident from domestic decelerating 
at HOKA; 3) the risk that margins have peaked and will likely come down from here in 
an increasingly competitive backdrop; and 4) little valuation support from here. The 1Q 
guide does little to alleviate concerns against difficult W/S compares, and though DECK 
commits against its growth algo, we see less upside through FY27.
 
HOKA Takes. 1) results were in-line (4Q growth of +14.5% vs. guide 13-14%), with 18% 
DTC and 13% W/S, while the 1Q outlook for HSD growth on a tough W/S compare was 
below expectations; 2) progress continues on building out beyond core Bondi/Clifton (6 
models ~$100M each); 3) brand awareness becoming more full (60% vs. 50% last year 
US, 40% vs. 30% intl.); 4) W/S expansion at sporting goods/specialty athletic; 5) DTC 
expansion with 20-25 stores/yr.
 
UGG Takes. 1) results outperformed (4Q growth of +9% vs. guide of flat), with 8% DTC 
and 10% W/S, and an outlook for 1Q in-line with FY algo for MSD; 2) new products are 
working, with half the brand's growth from Lowmel and Golden collection for the year;
3) men's contributed to over 20% of the growth in the year; 4) channel growth should be 
more balanced through the year. Net-net, UGG is performing well and executing against 
plan.
 
FY27 Guide & LT Algo. Guidance for FY27 includes +HSD top line and 160bps of op 
margin contraction. Implying flattish EBIT. While the company notoriously is conservative 
w/ the outlook, the inclusion of share repurchases to drive EPS growth is a change (FY24 
and FY25 did not include buybacks). Additionally, the 2028-2030 framework for +LDD 
HOKA and +MSD UGG is in-line with how mgmt historically viewed the brands, but the op 
margin of 20+ leaves the door open for further contraction from here.

Company Description
Deckers Outdoor Corporation, based in Goleta, CA, is a leading 
marketer of footwear and related accessories and apparel. Its 
brands are UGG Australia, Hoka One One, and Teva. The company 
sells its products through domestic and international retailers 
(including national department stores, chain and specialty stores) 
as well as its own directly-operated stores and global ecommerce 
websites.

Investment Thesis
Key themes surrounding the company include:
1. HOKA Long-term Opportunity. Acquired 10+ years ago (and generating just $3-5M in sales), 
HOKA have been one of the better growth stories in the Softlines space for several years, growing 
from just over $200M in revenues pre-COVID to just over $2B+ in revenues in FY25 — a +50% growth 
CAGR over the past several years. During this time, HOKA established its dominance in the technical 
running category and made its mark on fashion with its unique silhouette, fresh colorways, and larger 
foam heel. They currently have a Top 3 share position in the specialty run channel, and they have 
penetrated the authentic runner and expanded well beyond towards more casual joggers/runners 
and even a lifestyle consumer. While HOKA still sees the opportunity to add "billions" to revenue we 
do note growth in the brand has slowed in FY26 as the US business has slowed on tough compares 
and increased competition, with DTC no longer a growth vehicle and more reliance needed outside 
the US as well (neither detail favorable for investors). We view FY27 as likely to have a more balanced 
mix of channel and geography growth, but with international still outpacing domestic. Further, our 
industry checks indicate that there remains opportunity (and risk) for the HOKA brand to embrace 
its lifestyle customer, but to date has not done enough to address this. The primary difference being 
through introducing more product beyond the super-cushioned platform run shoe - with a more 
robust product launch calendar (and away from the 1x new model every year). High level, competition 
now has replicated what made HOKA great, and the brand needs to show it can come to market with a 
product that allows it to break away from the pack on its core shoe.
2. Expanding UGG's TAM. The company has been working to make UGG a less seasonal business and 
go after a male customer. This was evidenced by the extension of product into sneakers, all-weather 
boots, and more apparel, with the company noting progress in its 365 initiative, the growth from the 
Lowmel franchise expanding to lifestyle sneaker. The shift in strategy seems to be working, with the 
brand growing Mid-Teens in FY24/FY25 and HSD in FY26, though the algorithm remains for it to be 
an MSD growth business long-term.
3. Sustaining That Growth Becoming More Difficult. Despite the multi-year track record, concerns 
on the durability of growth intensified in 2026, with headwinds on difficult channel comparisons 
(tougher DTC comps, more wholesale fill-in), a resurgent Nike Run in the US, and a 1H26-weighted 
launch calendar of new models against higher inventory of old models. Brand growth has been choppy, 
slowing to +11% as of 2Q26, HOKA accelerating to +19% in 3Q26, and again decelerating to +15% 
in 4Q. Bears also point to potential issues around new product release schedules, which are focused 
more on the core runner and less so on the casual customer, who has become a much bigger part of 
the product mix. While growth in International has been solid, we do see potential risk stemming from 
actions Nike is taking in order to recapture lost share in specialty run. To us, it is more than coincidental 
that Nike saw significant growth in specialty run in North America throughout 2025 at the same time 
that HOKA began to focus on wholesale fill-in with decelerating DTC trends. While DECK was able to 
drive growth through international in 2025, Nike has now turned its attention to correcting its issues 
overseas, looking to reclaim lost market share, and rationalizing the marketplace - which may create a 
more negative promotional operating environment for a longer period of time.
4. Margins Well Above Industry. With operating margin near 23.1% for FY26, well above the industry 
group and despite a relatively difficult year on the top line, investors are debating DECK's go-forward 
margin (i.e., have margins peaked and will growth require additional investment in the face of an 
increasingly competitive market - marginal revenue has a higher marginal cost). The guide for FY27 
for contraction of 160bps to 21.5% certainly has some conservatism in it, but we view the risk/reward 
as more weighed to the downside rather than additional expansion from here. We do see potential 
benefits near-term, including the reversal of tariffs and the company's ability to manage costs down 
and price up new models, but excess margins may face continued long-term pressure as reinvestment 
is likely necessary to sustain the top-line growth.

The Bull Case….We believe that investors who are bullish on DECK shares are focused on (1) Hoka 
recaptures its magic through technical investments and accelerates growth back above the +15-20% 
range, (2) margin headwinds in 2H26 and 1H27 are largely tariff-related and one-time in nature, and 
channel mix becomes less of a headwind, (3) UGG direction is still healthy, as the brand has innovated 
beyond the classics and manages inventory and (4) valuation now provides better risk/reward.
The Bear Case….On the other hand, we believe that investors who are bearish on DECK shares believe
(1) Hoka brand has plateaued and competitive pressures will revert growth rates downward, (2) Hoka 
brand margins are normalizing and will likely fall over a multi-year period as the brand matures and as 
it moves deeper into wholesale (vs. DTC) and (3) UGG is likewise over-earning and will be hard-pressed 
to sustain current levels of profitability, also based on channel mix.
Financial Outlook
FY27E: Our FY27E EPS of $7.40 (from $7.36) represents a 5% increase y/y. We are projecting total 
CC sales to increase 7%. We are projecting GM down ~110bps, to 56.6%, and we expect SG&A to grow 
+9% in dollars, to 35.1%. As a result, we expect operating margin to contract ~160bps, to 21.5%.
FY28E: Our FY28E EPS estimate of $7.90 (from $7.65) represents EPS +7% y/y. We are projecting 
total CC sales +5%. We are projecting GM down ~10bps, to 56.5%, and we expect SG&A to grow +6% 
in dollars, to 35.4%. As a result, we expect operating margin to contract ~45bps, to 21.0%.
