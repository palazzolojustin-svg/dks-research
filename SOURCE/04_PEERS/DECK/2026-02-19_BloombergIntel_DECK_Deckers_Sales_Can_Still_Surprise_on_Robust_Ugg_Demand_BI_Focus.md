Deckers Sales Can Still Surprise on Robust Ugg Demand: BI Focus
(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Updating Focus Idea: Deckers Outdoor's 7.1% sales increase in 3Q25 was
well above consensus’ 2% gain amid outperformance at Ugg and Hoka, affirming our thesis that key
franchise upgrades, along with Ugg's growing year-round relevance, can propel fiscal 2026 sales by at
least the low-double digits. Hoka is expected to rise by the midteens for the year, based on guidance,
in line with our analysis and ahead of prior low-teen consensus. Raised full-year sales guidance for
8.6% growth can still prove conservative, as it assumes Ugg is flat in 4Q, despite delivering 4.9% growth
in 3Q, and we see potential upside from colder weather and winter storms.
Original Thesis (July 2025): Deckers Outdoor's product-innovation pipeline for Ugg and Hoka could
temper investor concerns about momentum slowing at both brands, particularly the latter, and fiscal
2026 sales growth may top consensus by 200-300 bps, based on our analysis. Last year was a
transitional one for Hoka, but we believe key franchise upgrades plus Ugg's rising year-round
relevance can drive Decker's fiscal 2026 sales up by low-double digits. (02/19/26)
1. Product Innovation, Expansion Key to Better Sales
Deckers' robust innovation pipeline, with updates to key Hoka franchises and the expansion of Ugg
into year-round products and men's, can boost revenue by at least 10% in fiscal 2026 vs. consensus'
high-single digits, our analysis shows. Ugg's growing relevance outside of its core -- sandals, clogs and
lifestyle sneakers -- has helped boost reported year-to-date sales 8%, in line with our initial
expectations for growth and ahead of analysts' low-single-digit gain. More momentum in 4Q could
push sales up nearly 8% for the year, based on our scenario analysis.
Hoka has outperformed expectations year to date, rising 16%, reaffirming our thesis for high-teens
brand growth vs. consensus' low-to-midteens, as product newness, key shoe updates and added
distribution points abroad aid. (02/19/26)

3. Global Growth Levers, Strong Brand Momentum Aid Hoka
Hoka is well positioned to sustain midteen expansion over the next several years as it expands
internationally, broadens into adjacent categories, and expands direct-to-consumer. The brand is
underpenetrated globally, with just a 1.1% share of the $179 billion sports-footwear market, leaving
ample room for for gains. Strength in core franchises like Clifton, Bondi and Arahi, alongside
innovation in trail and racing and disciplined wholesale expansion, supports durable share growth.
A more strategic launch cadence, with less overlap of major updates and cleaner marketplace
inventory, helped reaccelerate Hoka’s sales growth in fiscal 2026, leading to raised annual guidance
from low-to-midteen gains, in line with our original expectations. Consensus moved toward 15% from
low-double digits at initiation. (02/19/26)

4. Ugg Expands Beyond Seasonal Core, Winning With Men
Under new president Anne Spangenberg, Ugg has developed a more robust year-round product
assortment, reducing reliance on its traditional cold-weather core and positioning it for market-share
gains. It's making headway in men's, and we expect that momentum to accelerate given recent
initiatives like the first male-focused campaign in over five years featuring Post Malone, who
previously had a long-standing collaboration with rival Crocs. Men's and apparel are key catalysts to
doubling Ugg sales in the long term.
Shoulder-season styles like Lowmel and Tasman are resonating globally, helping drive 1H sales up 12%,
in line with our expectations and ahead of consensus' 7%. We believe colder weather in 4Q will boost
sales ahead of guidance for flat growth and drive high-single-digit gains for the year. (02/19/26)

5. DTC Can Reaccelerate as Marketplace Normalizes
Direct to consumer has trailed wholesale, yet much of the pressure appears to be structural rather
than demand-driven. Earlier wholesale shipments and expanded door counts, particularly for Ugg,
shifted traffic toward retail partners, while Hoka's prior model transitions temporarily disrupted
digital momentum. Importantly, Hoka's US DTC inflected positively following cleaner inventory
positioning, tighter franchise launch spacing and its enhanced membership program, which can lift
revenue per consumer and multicategory purchasing.
For UGG, softer DTC trends reflect stronger wholesale in-stocks and a consumer preference for
multibrand shopping, and not brand fatigue. As product newness resonates, DTC could modestly
outperform wholesale in 4Q as management leverages owned channels to manage launches. (02/19/26)

6. Deckers Trades at Discount to Historical Valuation
Deckers trades at a 15% discount to peers and a 25% discount to its five-year average, despite
consistently beating EPS expectations and maintaining a high margin. Its stock dropped 49.3% in 2025.
Though company shares are up 9.3% year to date on the heels of a strong 3Q25 earnings report in
late January, its forward 12-month price-to-earnings ratio of 16.2x is well below its five-year average
of 21.6x.
Deckers' adjusted EPS has beaten consensus the past 17 consecutive quarters. (02/19/26)
Blended Forward PE
7. Limited Exposure to China, Premium Positioning May Aid
Deckers will likely navigate the changing tariff landscape well given its minimal exposure to China and
premium pricing for Ugg and Hoka, which would permit less elasticity if the company decides to pass
additional costs to customers. Its scale and full-price sell-through could also provide leverage in
negotiations with wholesale partners and manufacturers to help offset some of the drag from added
levies.
Deckers sources less than 5% of goods from China, with the rest from South East Asia, primarily
Vietnam. (02/19/26)
