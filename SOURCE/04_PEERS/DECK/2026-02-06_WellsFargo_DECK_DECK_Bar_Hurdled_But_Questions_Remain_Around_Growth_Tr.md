Our Call
Solid 3Q beat (incl the inflection of UGG DTC) driving stock up, but bears likely remain 
focused on a lack of US growth, core GM pressure, and growing competitive headwinds in 
the athletic space. Remain EW, raise PT to $110 (from $95), ~16x FY27 EPS.
 
3Q Thoughts. Bulls satisfied by more substantial 3Q beat with both Hoka and UGG above 
estimates and ongoing better-than-expected tariff mitigation through strong pricing 
power. That said, we view bears likely remain cautious off 1) a lack of US growth - which 
went from +DD in FY25 to -LSD in 1H and remained muted in 3Q, 2) a lack of core (non-
tariff) GM upside, with promos still up YoY, and 3) a notable slowdown in UGG wholesale 
growth - despite benefitting from a 4Q to 3Q shipment timing shift.
 
Hoka Beats. Hoka revs were +18%, above the Street (+11%) with strength in both 
wholesale and DTC. DTC continued to sequentially accelerate (to +19% from +8% in 2Q) 
despite relatively similar compares - with management highlighting 1) a return to healthy 
growth in the US with a meaningful improvement in online customer acquisition, and 2) a 
positive response to their membership program with benefits in revenue per transaction 
and customer units, in addition to greater multi-category purchasing.
 
UGG More Mixed. While UGG revs did beat the Street (+5% vs. approx flat), wholesale 
still saw a material decel (to +4% from +17% in 2Q) despite benefitting from a 4Q to 3Q 
shipment timing shift and improved in-stock positions for partners. On the upside, DTC 
returned to growth (+5%) despite slightly tougher compares, with the channel benefitting 
from meaningful growth in aggregate membership, email subscribers, and retained 
consumers - all as UGG continues to boost interest in product launches.
 
More on Channel Mix. Management has continued to speak towards a more balanced 
channel mix - and coming out of 3Q highlighted 1) a LT target of improving DTC 
performance over several years (with benefits from changes to membership program), 
and 2) DECK is balancing wholesale demand by fulfilling a little earlier and addressing late 
season demand with DTC (implemented TY with management noting they will maintain 
that go-forward).
 
Thoughts on Outlook. 4Q rev plan came in below the St with headwinds from a shipment 
timing shift in UGG (Hoka planned +13-14% and UGG approx flat). Meanwhile, though 
GM implied plan came in slightly ahead of the Street (54.2% vs. 54.0%) as DECK continues 
to benefit from better-than-expected tariff mitigation from pricing action and favorable 
inventory sale timing - with GM headwinds of ~200bps in 4Q (and FY26 net tariff impact 
down to $25M from $55-75M) - core is still expected to delev ~50bps.

Company Description
Deckers Outdoor Corporation, based in Goleta, CA, is a leading 
marketer of footwear and related accessories and apparel. Its 
brands are UGG Australia, Hoka One One, and Teva. The company 
sells its products through domestic and international retailers 
(including national department stores, chain and specialty stores) 
as well as its own directly-operated stores and global ecommerce 
websites.

Investment Thesis
Key themes surrounding the company include:
1. Hoka Growth Has Been Impressive. Acquired 10+ years ago (and generating just $3-5M in sales), 
Hoka has been one of the better growth stories in the Softlines space for several years, growing from 
just over $200M in revenues pre-COVID to just over $2B+ in revenues in FY25 — a +50% growth 
CAGR over the past several years. During this time, Hoka established its dominance in the technical 
running category and made its mark on fashion with its unique silhouette, fresh colorways, and 
larger foam heel. They currently have a Top 3 share position in the specialty run channel and hey have 
penetrated the authentic runner, and expanded well beyond towards more casual joggers/runners 
and even a lifestyle consumer. While Hoka still sees the opportunity to add "billions" to revenue we do 
note growth in the brand has slowed in FY26 as the US business has slowed on tough compares and 
increased competition, with DTC no longer a growth vehicle and more reliance needed outside the US 
as well (neither detail favorable for investors).
2. Sustaining That Growth Becoming More Difficult. Despite the multi-year track record, concerns 
that began to emerge in 3Q25 with growth "slowing" to +24% (from +30-35%) have intensified to 
date in FY26. Brand growth has slowed further from +24% in 3Q25 to +20% in 1Q26 and then to 
+11% as of 2Q26. The most recent outlook suggests growth in the +LDD range for 2H26. The primary 
culprit is a slowdown in US DTC growth as compares continue to get more difficult and the road 
and trail running category has become more crowded. Bears also point to potential issues around 
new product release schedules, which are focused more on the core runner and less so on the casual 
customer, who has become a much bigger part of the product mix. While growth in International has 
been solid, this is masked by softness in the US.
3. DECK Approaching Margin Inflection? DECK's margin profile and thus sustainable EPS power is 
on the other side of this coin. Hoka's margin growth (from 5% to 25%+) helped drive DECK's overall 
margin from 12% to 24% in FY25 and EPS from ~$1.00 to $6.00+. However, DECK guided to margin 
pressure in FY26 stemming from catalysts in both GM and SGA, and is not guiding EPS up YoY for 
the first time since Hoka launched. To be certain, tariffs are playing a role in the margin pressure 
(approximately $85M, mitigated in FY26 / 2H26), but we believe investors are potentially debating 
DECK's go-forward growth rate and whether Hoka has matured — and what that implies for margins 
— or whether the slowdown can be reversed with newer, more exciting product and a turnaround in 
channel mix which has leaned more towards wholesale in recent quarters.

The Bull Case….We believe that investors who are bullish on DECK shares are focused on (1) Hoka 
recaptures its magic through technical investments and accelerates growth back above the 20% range 
- especially as compares have eased some, (2) margin headwinds in 2H26 and 1H27 are largely tariff-
related and one-time in nature, and channel mix becomes less of a headwind, (3) UGG direction still 
healthy, as the brand has innovated beyond the classics and manages inventory and (4) valuation now 
provides better risk/reward.
The Bear Case….On the other hand, we believe that investors who are bearish on DECK shares believe
(1) Hoka brand has plateaued and competitive pressures will revert growth rates downward, (2) Hoka 
brand margins are normalizing as the brand matures and as it moves deeper into wholesale (vs. DTC) 
and (3) UGG is likewise over-earning and will be hard-pressed to sustain current levels of profitability, 
also based on channel mix.
Financial Outlook
FY26E: Our FY26E EPS estimate of $6.80 represents EPS +7% YoY. We are projecting total CC sales 
+7%. We are projecting gross margin down ~80bps, to 57.0%, and we expect SG&A to grow +10% in 
dollars, to 34.5%. As a result, we expect operating margin to contract ~110bps, to 22.5%.
FY27E: Our FY27E EPS of $6.98 represents a 3% increase YoY. We are projecting total CC sales to 
increase 4%. We are projecting gross margin down ~90bps, to 56.2%, and we expect SG&A to grow 
+6% in dollars, to 35.1%. As a result, we expect operating margin to contract ~150bps, to 21.0%.

aggressive pace.
