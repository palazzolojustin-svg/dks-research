Why We See 57% Price Target Upside and 
Continue to Rate DECK Buy
Solid EPS growth and P/E expansion should be key drivers:
We see a very good opportunity to buy shares in a growth company we believe is 
significantly undervalued by the market. DECK's solid 4Q26  report increases our 
conviction in this view. We expect DECK to positively surprise the market over the NTM 
due to its Hoka and UGG brands growing revenue better than expected. We think this 
will cause the market to realize DECK should grow sales and EPS at +HSD%/+LDD% 5-
yr. CAGRs, sending DECK's FY2 P/E to 17x from 14x currently.
Our view is different from the market's in 2 key areas:
1. DECK has strong sales and EPS growth potential. The market believes DECK's 
Hoka brand has little growth potential. Yet, the company introduced a new LT 
Hoka revenue guide pointing to a +LDD% 4-yr. CAGR. The market doesn't 
appreciate Hoka plays in a large and growing global athletic footwear industry 
where it has only modest market share. Plus, the company bought back $1.08B of 
stock in FY26 (7% of the share count), up from $567M in FY25. We believe $1B+ 
is the new annual buyback rate.  We model 9%/12.5% 5-yr. sales/EPS CAGRs. 
2. Both Hoka and Ugg have strong brand equity. The market is likely focused on a 2H-
weighted FY27 guide and view it negatively. The market will likely say this shows 
DECK's brands are losing momentum. We disagree. The market is overlooking two 
very important financial metrics: 1) gross margin and 2) inventory. DECK's 4Q26 
gross margin increased 85 bps y/y despite tariffs and tough compares. DECK's GM
% has jumped 740 bps over the last three years. Plus, inventory ended 4Q26 down 
2% y/y despite a +5% 1Q27 sales growth guide and tariffs. These metrics indicate 
to us both Hoka and Ugg remain very healthy brands. 
Our FY27 EPS estimate rises 3% and our FY28-FY29 EPS estimates rise ~4-8%: 
We now anticipate a smaller GM% headwind related to freight  and product input cost 
pressures versus our prior view. Additionally, we lower our share count forecast given 
mgmt guidance.  These are the main drivers of the 3% FY27 EPSe increase. Our FY28-
FY29 EPS estimates rise ~4-8% given a higher FY27 EPS base plus a new ~$1B  annual 
share buyback forecast vs. ~$400M prior. Please see more detail inside this report.
Valuation: We raise our PT 11% to $161; Our rating is Buy: 
Our $161 PT is based on ~17x our $9.70 FY29 EPSe. We are rolling our valuation 
forward to FY29 from FY28 since DECK is starting a new fiscal year. This causes us to use 
a slightly lower P/E from 18x since more of the growth story has now played out, in our 
view. Our multiples analysis puts DECK's valuation in-line with peers in terms of P/E, P/
Sales, and FCF yield (Fig. 5). Our DCF analysis also supports a $161 valuation (Fig. 8).

Q: How much can the global footwear industry grow?
We expect it to rise at a 3% to 4% CAGR, highly skewed to sports footwear. Athleisure should 
continue driving this category at a compelling mid- to high-single-digit annual pace, underpinned by 
leading industry players like Nike pushing for innovation and prevailing themes like casualization and 
healthy lifestyles. Deckers should benefit from this trend as athleisure covers sports (i.e. HOKA brand) 
and comfort shoes (i.e. UGG).
Q:  Can  Hoka return to delivering +DD% direct-to-consumer (DTC) channel sales growth?
Yes. While choppiness in the transition from the Bondi 9/Clifton 10 franchises in the US and price 
increases related to new US tariff policies weighed on US  DTC sales growth in FY26, we believe Hoka 
DTC sales growth rate will accelerate in FY27 to the +LDD% range as Hoka expands further into the 
training, lifestyle, and apparel categories, and continues to expand its global retail footprint. This 
+LDD% rate reflects +MSD% growth in the US and greater than +20% growth in international 
markets, led by very robust growth in APAC. 
Q:  Can DECK continue to deliver industry-leading operating margins?
Yes. We expect EBIT margins hover in the 22% range through FY30E.  DECK's margins should benefit 
from a mix shift to DTC given higher digital sales and SG&A leverage in the medium term as HOKA 
gains scale, partially offset by tariff-related headwinds.
We rate DECK Buy.  We think Deckers' sales and EPS outlook justify a high valuation. We estimate its 
high-growth brand, HOKA, drives a ~13% 5-year consolidated top-line CAGR and believe this makes 
DECK one of Softlines' best growth stocks. Our quant analysis shows growth stocks have 
outperformed within the Softlines space over the past 25 years and we think the trend will continue. 
We see a solid LT upside/downside skew.
UBS Evidence Lab Google search and social media analyses support our bullish view of the company's 
ability to grow sales. Meanwhile, Euromonitor data points to  a 3% to 4% 5-year CAGR for the 
footwear industry, underpinned by a stronger sports footwear category (+8%) outlook.

4Q26 Earnings Review
DECK reported 4Q26 EPS of 96c, 13c above consensus: Total revenues rose 9.6% y/y 
to $1.119B. DECK's 4Q26 sales growth rate was ~330 bps ahead of the consensus 
estimate. By brand, Hoka sales rose 14.5% y/y, and UGG sales rose 9.2% y/y. Gross 
margin expanded 85 bps y/y to 57.6%, 260 bps ahead of the Street's 55.0% estimate. 
SG&A dollars rose 20.2% y/y to $488M, and this led to a 43.6% SG&A rate, implying 
~385 bps of deleverage y/y. DECK's operating margin contracted 300 bps y/y to 14.0%, 
~140 bps above the Street's forecast. Inventory declined -1.7% y/y to $487M.

Modeling Detail
Sales: We model 1Q27E sales up 7.6% y/y to $1.038B (vs. $1.05B prior). We anticipate 
that HOKA will be the primary driver of sales growth as the brand benefits from strong 
brand heat, primarily in international markets, new product launches, and widening 
consumer awareness from its global marketing campaigns, partially offset by 
unfavorable timing shifts impacting its wholesale sales growth. Thus by channel, we 
anticipate Direct-to-Consumer sales growth to significantly outpace wholesale sales 
growth during the quarter. On the other hand, we anticipate sales growth driven by the 
Hoka and UGG brands to be partially offset by annualizing the impact of DECK's phase 
out of the Koolaburra brand, which begun during DECK's 2Q26. We note DECK mgmt. 
noted Koolaburra generated $65M in revenues during FY25, primarily within the US 
wholesale channel. For the full year, we estimate 8.4% FY27 revenue growth to $5.93B. 
Here are the main considerations in our forecast:

Hoka's underlying demand should remain solid in Q1, particularly in 
international markets. Hoka's net sales grew +14.5% in 4Q26, driven by +18% 
Direct-to-Consumer growth and +13% growth in wholesale revenue. Further, 
DECK mgmt. noted it expects Hoka Q1 US sales to be positive y/y during the 
quarter, as well as solid growth in International markets. These data points cause 
us to believe underlying demand for the Hoka brand continues to be strong on a 
global basis. Plus, we believe continuing to launch global marketing campaigns 
surrounding new product launches should further spread brand awareness in 
international markets. We forecast Hoka delivering +17% Direct-to-Consumer 
sales growth and +6.7% wholesale sales growth during Q1. From a wholesale 
perspective, we note DECK mgmt. noted during its 4Q26 conference call Hoka is 
experiencing three key wholesale timing shifts expected to impact its Q1 
wholesale growth: 1) lapping last year's earlier shipments related to its 
EMEA warehouse transition; 2) delayed distributor shipments to within APAC; and 
3) lower shipments of the outgoing Clifton 10 model in preparation for the Clifton 
11 launch in July. For the full year, we forecast Hoka delivering +14% y/y  DTC sales 
growth and 13% y/y wholesale sales growth. 

UGG underlying demand should continue to be strong in Q1. UGG's net 
sales rose 9.2% during 4Q26, driven by +13% growth in DTC revenue and +4% 
growth in wholesale sales. Looking to Q1, we anticipate UGG brand revenues 
benefit from growth in international markets, including strength in the China and 
EMEA regions. We believe UGG's solid global brand strength positions the brand 
well for growth going forward across key "hero" categories such as the Tasman 
and Ultra mini franchises, as well as gains around newness, including the Lowmel 
family and the Golden Collection. By channel, we forecast UGG Q1 wholesale 
sales up 3.1% y/y to $192M and DTC sales growth to rise 7.1%, driven by 6% 
growth on a constant currency basis and ~110 bps favorable FX impacts. We note 
our wholesale sales growth forecast also contemplates timing shift impact related 
to lapping last year's earlier shipments related to its EMEA warehouse transition. 
For FY27, we estimate UGG DTC sales rise 4.7% to $1.36B  and wholesale sales 
rise 4.4% y/y to $1.50B. 

By brand, we model UGG's 1Q27E sales rise 4.3% y/y to $276M, HOKA rising 
10.2% y/y to $720M, and Others brands revenue down 9.7% y/y to $42M. For the 
full year, we model FY27 UGG sales growing 4.6% y/y to $2.864B, HOKA rising 
13.4% y/y to $2.93B, and Other brands revenue down 9.9% y/y to $132M. 
Gross Margin: We anticipate -160 bps y/y 1Q27 gross margin contraction to 54.2%. 
We anticipate  tariff-related cost pressures as the primary headwind to Q1 gross margin. 
We note our forecast assumes ~100 bps of gross margin pressure related to tariffs. On 
the other hand, we expect this to be partially offset by favorable FX rates, favorable 
geographic mix as we anticipate international sales to outpace sales trends in the US, 
and brand mix benefits to DECK's higher margin Hoka business. Further, we model 
DECK's Direct-to-Consumer business as a higher share of Q1 sales vs. 1Q26 and thus 
anticipate GM% tailwinds related to favorable channel mix-shifts to DECK's higher-
margin DTC business. Therefore, we forecast -160 bps y/y 1Q27 gross margin 
contraction to 54.2%. For the full year, we model FY27E gross margin contracting -75 
bps y/y to 56.9%.

Our 5-yr. EPS algorithm uses FY26 (ending March 2026) as a base.
UPSIDE ($256): We forecast a ~19.5% 5-year upside case EPS CAGR (FY26-FY31E). We 
see an upside scenario where DECK delivers low-double-digit revenue growth in the UGG 
brand, supported by int'l expansion in China and post its European marketplace reset. 
Plus, its new product styles outside the classic winter boot strongly resonate with younger 
consumers and the brand becomes an all-season product. This expansion along with a 
faster-than-expected rollout of HOKA through the DTC channel yields an incremental 
~440 bps in total sales annual growth vs. our base case. HOKA and DTC growth translate 
into higher gross margins given the positive sales mix shift. This leads to an operating 
margin of ~24.5% by FY31E and the market rewards this profitable growth with a ~21x 
P/E valuation.
BASE ($161):  We forecast a ~12.5% 5-year EPS CAGR (FY26-FY31E). We assume topline 
remains strong in FY27E and grows at a ~9% annual rate thereafter. The algorithm is 
roughly ~25 bps annual operating margin contraction, stabilizing at ~22% by FY31E, as 
well as ~2.5% share count reduction due to buybacks. DECK should benefit from robust 
growth of HOKA as it rebounds in its US DTC channel and evolves into a lifestyle 
footwear brand from being purely focused on running. Meanwhile, we anticipate 
DECK's UGG brand sales to rise at a ~6% CAGR. We anticipate tariffs pressure gross 
margins over the NTM, but we believe gross margins should expand beyond FY27 as 
structural sales mix shifts to the DTC channel vs. wholesale's lower margins, and HOKA's 
profitability increases. We think DECK's ~12.5% EPS growth outlook justifies a ~17x P/E, 
which we apply to our $9.70 FY29E EPS to derive our $161 price target.
DOWNSIDE ($44):  We forecast a ~1% 5-year EPS CAGR (FY26-FY31E) in our downside 
case. This scenario assumes the US consumer spending environment softens significantly 
in 2H26-27 that results in revenues growing ~5% y/y in FY27E and +MSD% annual 
revenue growth thereafter with much lower operating margins. Our downside scenario 
implies a negative mix shift given higher exposure to less profitable channels, such as 
wholesale and distributors. The UGG brand loses share in the US and abroad as the 
comfort fashion trend evolves into something less favorable for the brand and its new 
launches do not engage consumers. Hoka brand share gains slow as consumer trends 
shift towards lower profile silhouettes and weigh on Hoka's Clifton and Bondi franchises. 
Plus, elevating competitive pressures weigh on Hoka's key road running business. The 
weakness in volumes results in an increase in promotional activity to drive sales, hurting 
gross margins while the company deleverages SG&A.  Our downside scenario assumes -
115 bps operating margin decreases annually in this scenario. We anticipate DECK trades 
at ~7x P/E in our downside scenario.

Company Description
Deckers Outdoor Corp designs, markets, and distributes mainly footwear, as well as apparel 
and accessories, for lifestyle and high-performance activities. The company distributes its 
products through the direct-to-consumer channel (including company-operated stores and 
eCommerce) and wholesale. Its offerings include brands such as UGG, HOKA, Koolaburra, 
Sanuk, Teva, and others. As of March 31, 2023, it operates 52 retail stores in the US. 
Internationally, it operates 112 retail stores in Austria, Belgium, Canada, China, France, 
Germany, Japan, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and the UK.
Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Our price target is derived from a multiple analysis, supported by a DCF valuation as a 
validation method. Several downside risks to consider: (1) The health of the consumer. Brands 
are reliant on the state of the macro environment and any deterioration in consumer 
spending and/ or unemployment could negatively impact sales and margins. (2) Fashion risk/ 
inability to execute on merchandise strategy. Brands are inherently exposed to fashion risk 
due to ongoing shifts in trends and customer preferences. The company's success has hinged 
upon its ability to forecast fashion trends and appropriately translate the looks for its target 
customer. If it finds itself unable to predict and/ or interpret changes in trends, sales and 
margins could suffer. (3) Inventory risk. The company's ability to manage inventory levels is 
important to be the health of margins and earnings. If sales are worse than expected and 
inventories are aggressively purchased relative to actual sales trends, the company will likely 
need to take incremental markdowns to clear remaining product, which could pressure gross 
margins.
