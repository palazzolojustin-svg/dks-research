# Foot Locker, Inc. (FL) — Standalone Financial History FY2019–FY2025 and the "Lace Up" Plan

**Purpose:** Pre-acquisition baseline for modeling the Foot Locker segment inside DICK'S Sporting Goods (DKS). DKS closed the acquisition on 2025-09-08. All figures are copied from FL's own SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K earnings releases and the 2023-03-20 Investor Day materials) converted to markdown from EDGAR. Where a number is derived (not printed in a filing) it is marked **[derived]**.

**Fiscal calendar:** FL's fiscal year ends the Saturday nearest January 31 (same as DKS). "FY2024" = 52 weeks ended 2025-02-01; "FY2023" = 53 weeks ended 2024-02-03; "FY2022" = 52 weeks ended 2023-01-28; "FY2021" = 52 weeks ended 2022-01-29; "FY2020" = 52 weeks ended 2021-01-30; "FY2019" = 52 weeks ended 2020-02-01. FL labels fiscal years by the calendar year in which they begin (FL's "2024" = FY2024).

**Source directory:** `C:\Users\jharp\OneDrive\Desktop\DKS - CLAUDE CODE\04_PEERS\FL\sec_filings\` (10-Ks and 10-Qs) and `...\sec_filings\8-K\` (earnings releases and Investor Day exhibits). Source file names are given per table. Companion file (sell-side view, not duplicated here): `FL_Foot_Locker_PreAcquisition_Baseline_from_Barclays.md`; reconciliation points are noted in Section 9.

**Non-GAAP definition change (important for FY2021 comparisons):** Effective Q1 FY2022, FL began excluding *all* gains/losses on minority investments from non-GAAP earnings (previously only GOAT fair-value gains were excluded). FY2021 adjusted figures were recast in the FY2022 10-K: adjusted EBIT $1,120M → $1,049M; adjusted EBIT margin 12.5% → 11.7%; adjusted net income $807M → $755M; adjusted diluted EPS $7.77 → $7.27; ROIC 17.4% → 16.4%. ## 5. Margin bridge: management's explanations by year

### 5a. Gross margin rate bridge (10-K "Gross Margin" tables)

| | FY2020 | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin rate | 28.9% | 34.4% | 31.9% | 27.7% | 28.9% |
| Change (bps) | (290) | +550 | (250) | (420) | +120 |
| Merchandise margin rate | (340) | +450 | (240) | (340) | +100 ("cost of merchandise" effect) |
| Occupancy & buyers' compensation rate | +50 | +100 | (10) | (80) | +20 |

Management commentary (short quotes):
- **FY2021** (FY2021 10-K): "a higher merchandise margin rate since we were significantly less promotional than a year ago, due to increased demand and relatively short supply of certain merchandise." COVID rent abatements: "$16 million of rent savings ... in 2021, as compared with $67 million in the prior year."
- **FY2022** (FY2022 10-K): "a lower merchandise margin rate due to higher markdowns versus historically-low levels last year. The occupancy rate deleverage reflected the effect of prior year rent abatements related to COVID-19. Excluding the benefit from the prior year abatement, the rate would have decreased by 10 basis points." Q1 FY2022 10-Q: "higher supply chain costs and slightly higher markdowns versus historically-low levels." Q4 FY2022 release: "(290) bps ... driven mainly by higher markdowns on increased promotional activity across the industry."
- **FY2023** (FY2023 10-K): "higher promotional activity in the current marketplace and markdowns recorded to reduce overall inventory levels and improve aging. Other factors that negatively affected the rate were higher cost of merchandise and increased shrink. The occupancy rate deleverage reflected the fixed nature of these costs in relation to the decline in sales." Q1 FY2023 release: "higher markdowns compared to historically low levels in the prior year, and occupancy deleverage, as well as an increase in theft-related shrink." Q2/Q3 FY2023: "increase in promotional activity, which included higher markdowns, as well as occupancy deleverage and higher shrink."
- **FY2024** (FY2024 10-K): "we were less promotional in 2024 despite the highly promotional marketplace and the liquidation of inventories in the geographies that we are closing. The markdown pressure was partially offset by vendor allowances, which improved our gross margin rate by 20 basis points ... the cost of the [loyalty] program improvement pressured the gross margin rate by 10 basis points for the year. The leverage in the occupancy and buyers' compensation rate was primarily related to rent renegotiations and our ongoing optimization of our store portfolio." Q2 FY2024: non-recurring FLX charge reduced sales by $11M and GM by 40 bps. Q3 FY2024: "+230 bps ... led by reduced markdown levels ... below expectations given an elevated promotional environment." Q4 FY2024: "+300 bps ... sequentially improved merchandise margin recapture trends ... Occupancy, as a percent of sales, was flat."
- **FY2025 H1** (releases): Q1 "(40) bps: merchandise margins decreased by 10 bps, while occupancy as a percentage of sales increased by 30 bps"; Q2 "(50) bps: merchandise margins decreased by 50 bps, while occupancy ... was flat."

### 5b. SG&A rate and EBIT margin explanations

- **FY2021** SG&A 20.7% (−30 bps): "higher sales in the current year, partially offset by the effect of prior-year COVID-19 payroll subsidies [$16M in 2021 vs $71M in 2020] and higher incentive compensation [+$14M]." Division profit 13.0% vs 6.5%: "expense leverage and coupled with gross margin improvement."
- **FY2022** SG&A 21.8% (+110 bps): "higher labor costs, information technology and support expenses, and COVID-19 related matters in the prior year ... Our newly acquired businesses contributed $104 million to SG&A." Corporate expense +$22M on IT. Division profit 9.5%: "both sales channels experiencing declines in gross margin and deleveraging expenses."
- **FY2023** SG&A 22.7% (+90 bps): "deleverage from the decline in sales, coupled with pressures from inflation and investments in front-line wages and technology ... Partially offsetting these increases were lower incentive compensation expense ... and savings from our cost optimization program." Corporate expense fell $109M to $42M on "an increase in the allocation of corporate expense to the banners in 2023 and lower incentive compensation." Division profit 3.2%: "cost-cutting program benefits were not enough to offset the decline in sales."
- **FY2024** SG&A 24.1% (+140 bps): "investments in technology and brand-building [NBA partnership], as well as higher inflation and incentive compensation expense ... partially offset by savings from the cost optimization program, store closures, and ongoing expense discipline." Division profit 3.1%: "decline in store sales coupled with higher SG&A expenses, despite our cost-cutting program benefits and improved gross margin rate."
- **FY2025 H1**: Q1 SG&A 25.6% (+100 bps): "underlying deleverage on the sales decline and investments in technology"; SG&A dollars −0.7%. Q2 SG&A 25.3% (+20 bps): dollars −1.7%, "benefits from ongoing expense discipline, including our cost optimization program, partially offset by our investments in technology."
- **Investor Day 2022→2023 EBIT bridge** (FL_8-K_2023-03-20_EX-99-2, slide "2023 EBIT margins down as near-term pressures offset cost saves"): 7.9% (FY2022) → 5.7% (FY2023 guidance mid-point, 52-week): −1.8 pts "Early 2023" promotional pressure, −1.0 pts technology, −1.0 pts wages, +1.6 pts cost savings. Actual FY2023 adjusted EBIT margin: 2.6%.
- **Cost optimization program:** target raised from $200M to $350M at Investor Day ("$150M to fuel investments, $200M to the bottom line"; timing 2022 ~10%, 2023 ~40%, 2024/2025 ~50%; roughly half SG&A/overhead/procurement and half merchandise margin/occupancy). Dashboard readings: $20M in 1Q24, $80M for 2024 (1Q24 deck); $35M in 4Q24, $100M cumulative for 2024 vs $350M 2026 target (4Q24 deck).

## 7. Store strategy

### 7a. Closures and banner exits by year (see Section 1c for the full tables)

- **FY2021:** 391 closures, dominated by Footaction (199 closed; ~63 converted to Foot Locker/Champs/Kids Foot Locker); Lady Foot Locker 21; impairment review of ~100 underperforming stores in Q4 ($26M charge on ~55 stores). Runners Point (Germany) shutdown completed in FY2020.
- **FY2022:** 247 closures (Foot Locker U.S. 100, Champs 42, Footaction 39 — wind-down "substantially completed"); Eastbay Team Sales sold Q2 FY2022 ($47M proceeds); Eastbay consumer business closed late 2022; Q4 impairment review of ~142 underperforming stores incl. 70 Sidestep ($53M charge).
- **FY2023:** 270 closures (Champs 83, Sidestep 78 — banner exit, Foot Locker Asia 20 — Hong Kong/Macau exit, Singapore/Malaysia converted to license; atmos U.S. closed Q4). 69 new-concept stores opened; 242 Community/House of Play/Power stores at year-end.
- **FY2024:** 139 closures "as part of the Lace Up strategic plan to power up our portfolio" (Foot Locker U.S. 47, Foot Locker Europe 39, KFL 23, Champs 21); South Korea, Denmark, Norway, Sweden closures and Greece/Romania sale announced Aug 2024; HQ relocation from New York to St. Petersburg, FL announced (late 2025). Reorganization costs $26M; impairment $32M incl. New York HQ.
- **FY2025 H1:** 67 closures (56 in Q1 incl. all stores in the six exited countries; 11 in Q2); stores operated in 20 countries at Aug 2, 2025.

### 7b. "Reimagined" and "Refresh" programs (FY2024 10-K; 2025-03-05 release and deck; FY2025 releases)

- "As of February 1, 2025, we have 8 'Reimagined' concept stores across North America, Europe, Asia, and Australia, including one licensed store in India." First Reimagined store opened in Wayne, NJ (Q1 FY2024); 7 opened in FY2024; "80 additional Reimagined stores planned" for 2025 "primarily through conversions or relocations"; 11 opened in Q2 FY2025 including the first 2 Champs Sports Reimagined stores. Claimed economics: "~$4 to ~$5 million Year 1 Sales, ~20% Year 1 EBITDA Margin."
- Refresh: 407 refreshes in FY2024 (13 / 67 / 167 / 160 by quarter); 69 in Q1 and 52 in Q2 FY2025; ~300 planned for 2025. "At year end 44% of our Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, and Champs Sports gross store square footage was at current brand standard"; target "~2/3 of our global Foot Locker and Kids Foot Locker footage to be at brand standard by end of 2026." Claimed productivity: "+Low- to Mid-Single Digit Sales Productivity Lift ... +Mid-Single Digit Merchandise Profit Dollar Lift ... Average Capital Expenditure per Refresh: ~$150K; Payback period: ~2–3 years; Cash on Cash Returns ~35%–45%."
- Earlier concepts: 62 Community/Power stores at Jan 29, 2022; 242 at Feb 3, 2024. The FY2024 10-K no longer reports this count.

### 7c. Mall vs. off-mall

"Growing our off-mall presence has favorably affected our occupancy costs" (FY2024 10-K). WSS is "100% off-mall" (Investor Day). Investor Day target >50% by 2026. ## 9. Caveats, gaps and reconciliation to the Barclays baseline

1. **No regional profit.** FL reports one segment; operating income by North America / EMEA / Asia Pacific is not disclosed anywhere. Modeling regional margins requires assumptions (e.g., Investor Day comments that Asia lost ~$30M and Sidestep ~$10M per year; FY2024 10-K that off-mall lowers occupancy).
2. **Banner-level data limits.** Sales by banner are available FY2020–FY2024 and quarterly from Q4 FY2021; banner comps only from Q4 FY2022 (10-Qs for FY2022 Q1–Q3 give region comps only). Store counts by banner for FYE2019 are not in the files (total only). Banner-level profit is not disclosed (except one-off WSS/atmos division-profit contributions in FY2022).
3. **Non-GAAP recasts.** FY2021 adjusted EBIT/EPS were recast in FY2022; FY2020/FY2021 income from operations, division profit and other income were restated for the licensing-revenue reclassification. The table shows both original and restated values.
4. **53-week FY2023** distorts FY2023 vs FY2024 growth ($98M of sales; $0.11–$0.15 EPS); FY2024 Q4 sales fell 5.8% with a +2.6% comp.
5. **Garbled or image-only extractions.** (a) FY2022 10-K "Total sales" comparable row shows DTC comps in prior-year columns. (b) FY2022 Q1–Q3 10-Q region tables lost their header rows (columns are "Sales at constant currencies, excluding acquisitions" and "Comparable sales"; reconstructed from the Q2/Q3 headers). (c) The Investor Day presentation (EX-99-2) is slide text run together; numbers were reconstructed from context and the "30% vs ~35% off-mall" base figure appears both ways on different slides. (d) FL_8-K_2024-03-06_EX-99-2 (Q4 FY2023 deck) is image-only — no text — so the March 2024 capital-allocation and target-timing detail comes solely from the press release. (e) Lace Up dashboard figures for 2Q24 are jumbled in extraction (loyalty 22% → 24%, off-mall 36% → 40%, new concepts 12% → 17%, digital 15.5% → 15.9% reconstructed by column order).
6. **Definitions differ across documents.** "Digital % of sales" on Lace Up dashboards (e.g., 21.8% in 4Q24) does not match the 10-K "direct-to-customer % of sales" (18.2% FY2024); loyalty penetration jumped from 27% (3Q24) to 49% (4Q24) after the FLX relaunch, likely a definitional change — neither is reconciled in filings. Investor Day Nike figures are % of *sales*; 10-K figures are % of *purchases*.
7. **Nike vendors beyond Nike** — never quantified; "next largest vendors" is not disclosed.
8. **Quarterly adjusted EBIT** is not published; the quarterly EBIT margin column is GAAP income from operations ÷ sales. Q4 gross margin and SG&A rates are derived from release income statements (cost of sales line).
9. **FY2019 inventory turns**, FY2019 constant-currency growth, FY2019 banner sales and FY2019 operating lease liabilities were not pulled (not in the five-year summaries provided).
