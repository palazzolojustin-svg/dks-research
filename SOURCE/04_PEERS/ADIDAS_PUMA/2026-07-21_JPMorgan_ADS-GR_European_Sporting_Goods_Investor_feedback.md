In this note, we summarize recent investor feedback on adidas, On, Puma and JD 
Sports following the reinstatement of our ratings two weeks ago (adidas 
Overweight, On Overweight, Puma Neutral, JD Sports Neutral – see our note 
here). Over the last two weeks, we have met with a broad set of investors. While 
sentiment towards the sector remains cautious, investors generally agree that a 
slower Nike (covered by Matthew Boss) recovery could extend the window for 
adidas and On to gain share, supported by solid brand momentum. The key 
pushback is duration. In our view, both adidas and On trade at meaningful discounts 
due to a lack of conviction that either can sustain strong growth in 2027 (or over 
the mid-term). We expect both to be multi-year growth stories, driven by continued 
focus on brand building, product innovation, and regional growth opportunities. 
Into H2 2026 and 2027, as external headwinds (notably FX and tariffs) fade, and 
as operating leverage kicks in, we expect margin uplift to further accelerate 
earnings growth. We remain Overweight on adidas and On.
•
adidas: 
•
The pushback: Investors broadly expect a strong Q2, helped by a major 
World Cup-driven apparel boost (JPMe: 14% Q2 cFX revenue growth, 
including c.6 pts from World Cup-related jersey demand; the buyside “bar” 
seems to be slightly higher than our estimates). The key concern, however, 
is that the market may look through Q2 strength given the perceived “one-
off” element, with tougher comparisons in 2027. Investors also worry 
growth could slow from Q3 as footwear momentum decelerates, especially 
if the Terrace franchise volume is scaled down. More broadly, investors 
question what product engines can support growth beyond 2026 – 
particularly if apparel is not positioned to be a sustained growth driver. 
Investors also asked about Investors Day expectations.
•
Our view: We expect adidas to be more than a Q2 story and model 8%+ 
topline growth in H2 26 and FY27, driven by consistent innovation and 
execution across lifestyle and performance. In lifestyle, investor 
discussions have focused on finding the next hero product, but we see two 
underappreciated drivers: (1) the longer runway for current heroes – 
especially Samba (Figure 3Search trends for Samba show an extended life span since reaching a peak in April 2024) and (2) a broader portfolio of smaller but 
growing franchises. Into H2 2026/FY27, we expect adidas to extend the 
Samba playbook to Stan Smith by modernizing a classic franchise through 
refreshed designs, materials, and collaborations. In performance, we 
expect growth to be led by running supported by the brand halo from the 
sub-2 marathon successes, with additional opportunities across football, 
training and basketball. By region, we continue to see significant upside 
outside Europe, where the “local for local” playbook should allow adidas 
to take additional market share. On World Cup 2026, while historically 
event sales mostly came from jerseys, we note that adidas has broadened 
the product assortment this year (for instance, “Lifestyle collection”/ 
“Blokecore”/ “Jeseys redefined” are featured under “FIFA World Cup 26” 
pages in most adidas websites across Europe and North America). The 
upside hinges on adidas’s ability to convert the additional traffic from the

On: 
•
The pushback: Investors who remain skeptical argue that as long as revenue growth 
continues to decelerate, the multiple may struggle to re-rate – citing Lululemon 
(covered by Matthew Boss), and Hoka (under Deckers, not covered), whose 
multiples de-rated following weaker North America sales growth. Near-term focus 
is on Q2: investors are sensitive to any topline softness, especially after management 
flagged slower wholesale sell-in expectations in Q2, as the company prepares for 
major product launches in Q3. Looking further out, investors want a clearer growth 
framework for the next phase – specifically, which levers (categories, geographies, 
channels) can drive topline growth in a sustainable way. A recurring concern is that 
management could guide to a more conservative 3-year sales CAGR (below current 
sell-side expectations of c.20%) during its upcoming Investors Day in September, 
reinforcing the deceleration narrative. Investors also asked whether margin targets 
could be pressured if On increases investment to enter incremental sports (e.g. 
football). Several noted that a “top-line miss with a profit beat” is unlikely to change 
sentiment near-term.
•
Our view: With shares trading at 16x P/E, the deceleration concerns look overdone. 
We continue to expect topline growth to be sustained at a 20%+ rate over the next three 
years, driven by strong growth in Asia (we model CAGR of c.35%) and apparel (c. 
40%), in contrast to the concerns that growth will decelerate to mid-teens (or lower). 
We also think the market is underappreciating On’s brand-building efforts and its 
ability to sustain a premium lifestyle positioning that captures the “feel-good” 
demand. We see similarities between On and Ralph Lauren (see our note here), and 
note that On recently hired Alice Delahunt (former Ralph Lauren/Burberry) to lead 
global DTC. We also note that margin upside is less discussed during our investor 
discussions. Our Q2 and FY26 adj. EBITDA are 9% and 7% ahead of consensus, 
respectively, and we continue to see strong margin uplift potential – near term from 
GM and medium term also from operating leverage. We expect the upcoming quarters 
to reassure investors of growth drivers, and the upcoming Investors Day to set a 
clearer multi-year roadmap, both supportive of a multiple re-rate. We remain OW and 
keep On on Positive Catalyst Watch into Q2 results.  
•
Puma: 
•
The feedback: Investor views remain polarized. Bulls see a potential multi-year 
reset/turnaround setup and point to prior successful playbooks in the sector ("Could 
Puma be the next adidas?"), including the possibility of upside in Greater China 
supported by Anta following its 29% stake purchase. They also see any guidance 
upgrade (or a smaller-than-feared EBIT loss versus guidance) as a near-term catalyst. 
Bears cite soft channel reads and limited visibility on product “newness” driving a 
top-line inflection. They are also cautious on execution risk with the ongoing 
turnaround.
•

JD Sports: 
•
The feedback: Investors largely agree that the valuation looks attractive, but see 
limited near-term catalysts for a re-rating – particularly if Nike’s recovery takes 
longer than expected. Investors cited strong free cash flow, share buybacks, and 
management's focus on execution and productivity. That said, the setup remains 
challenging if macro conditions remain soft and JD’s core customers remain under 
pressure. Investors also highlighted JD’s exposure to Nike’s lifestyle franchises that 
are still in transition, which could constrain top-line acceleration in the near term.
•
Our view: In our view, the key for JD Sports’s equity story into the rest of the year 
is whether self-help can effectively offset major external headwinds. Q1-27 sales 
momentum still looks soft, and notably weaker than US peers, affected by JD’s 
exposure to the UK and Europe, where consumer sentiment remains subdued. The 
promotional environment and the sizable exposure to Nike will continue to weigh on 
JD’s profit outlook. On the other hand, the expectations have been largely reset and 
the valuation is not demanding. We remain Neutral as we do not see an inflection in 
profit growth in the near term.
