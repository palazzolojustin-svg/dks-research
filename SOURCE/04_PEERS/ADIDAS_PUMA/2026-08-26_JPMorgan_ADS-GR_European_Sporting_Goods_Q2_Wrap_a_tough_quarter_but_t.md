Q2 has been a tough reporting season for the European Sporting Goods companies. 
Limited guidance upgrades (and a few downgrades) have weighed on the sector, 
with adidas -12%, On -20%, Puma -5% and JD Sports -14% on their results days. 
Growth is slowing as brands prioritise full-price DTC sales in a highly promotional 
market. On the other hand, a DTC-led growth model comes with a cost, especially 
as brands stepped up marketing and activations to drive online and store traffic. Our 
discussions with investors so far clearly indicate concerns about a structural 
slowdown in sector profit growth. While we do not expect market conditions to 
improve materially over the next two quarters, there is limited evidence to 
conclude that there is a structural slowdown in footwear demand. Meanwhile, JD 
Sports’ update yesterday suggests that inventory remains controlled and both 
brands and retailers look disciplined on supply. We see scope for wholesale order 
growth to re-accelerate into Q4-26 and 2027 as newness builds (notably at adidas 
and On), and upcoming CMDs should remind investors that newness pipelines 
remain solid into the new year. Meanwhile, expectations have reset into Q3 
following recent results. We remain OW on adidas and On. 
•
What we learned from JD Sports’ Q2 trading update yesterday: The 
surprise came from the significantly softer sell through in North America, a 
market that used to be described as more resilient than the UK and Europe. JD 
attributed the weaker sales to 1) a soft consumer environment, 2) ongoing 
footwear product cycle evolution, and 3) back to school timing. That said, 
inventory remains “well controlled” and the FCF guidance was maintained 
despite a lower profit guidance and unchanged Capex guidance, implying 
additional cash release from working capital management. Management also 
called out “encouraging momentum in performance-based running and newer 
footwear styles.” 
•
How promotional is the market? A quick look at the Online channel: We 
ran a small “discount breadth” screen (share of products on markdown) across 
brand.com and key multi-brand sites (JD Sports, Foot Locker) in the US, UK 
and France (see Figure 3adidas, Hoka and New Balance are among the least discounted at brand.com in the US to Figure 11Fot Locker, on the other hand, is visibly more promotional in France). Discounts appear broad-based across 
channels, suggesting consumers have ample choice before trading up to full-
price. On and adidas screen among the least discounted on multi-brand 
platforms, although markdown breadth is modestly higher on their own 
brand.com sites. This aligns with both companies’ messaging around 
controlled wholesale sell-in to protect full-price. It may also indicate a greater 
need to clear inventory in owned channels: Q2-end inventories appear more 
elevated for both versus previous quarters.

growth is becoming more expensive, particularly for On and adidas. While one-off 
factors including World Cup related investment played a role, our analysis shows that the 
higher DTC mix (and the lack of wholesale driven Opex leverage) is likely a main driver 
of the Q2 EBIT miss. Notably, looking back across the last five Q2 periods, we note that 
DTC mix in Q2 2026 is at / near all-time highs for adidas, Puma and On (Figure 12Q2-26 saw one of the highest DTC mixes acros brands ). While

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
adidas Group (Overweight; Price Target: €230.00)
Investment Thesis
While adidas is still delivering solid double-digit top-line growth and margin growth, there 
have been increasing questions about sales and earnings upgrade upside.The Nike 
comeback, the potential slowdown of Terrace as it enters the second half of its life cycle, and 
tariffs are likely to delay the trajectory of adidas’ market share growth. Our view is that these 
concerns are more than priced into the current valuation, while the market may have 
overlooked growth drivers elsewhere, including additional upside from the running and 
apparel categories, as well as the upcoming World Cup. When it comes to EBIT margins, 
we note that incremental growth in apparel, adidas’ momentum in Greater China and other 
emerging markets, and disciplined cost control could offset potential weakness from the 
important US market. Our analysis indicates that the risk/reward is asymmetric, which 
justifies a share re-rating to the upside. We rate adidas Overweight.
Valuation
We use a three-stage DCF (model forecasts + mid-term + fade to terminal) and assume 4.5% 
mid-term growth/ 2% terminal growth, 11% mid-term and terminal EBIT margin, and 9.0% 
WACC.
