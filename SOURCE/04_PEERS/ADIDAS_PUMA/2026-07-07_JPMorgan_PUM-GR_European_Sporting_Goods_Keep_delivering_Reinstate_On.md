asymmetry for adidas, On, Puma and JD Sports, leveraging data, discussions with

(covered by Matthew R. Boss, see his thoughts here following Nike’s Q4 results

their high growth phase”. We expect a consistent delivery of earnings growth over 
the coming quarters to support an upward re-rating of both companies.  On the other 
hand, we continue to see execution risk in the turnaround at Puma and JD Sports,

H2 26 Outlook 
The set up for European Sporting Goods remains dynamic. Earlier this year, JD Sports, 
in particular, noted “muted” market in 2026 with 0-1% overall demand growth for 
sporting goods and athleisure as consumers become more cautious and selective in 
response to continued inflation pressures. We expect broadly similar trends in H2 
2026 to what we saw in H1. We expect the promotional environment widely flagged by 
brands and retailers to continue into Q3. That said, the recovery in global supply chains, 
falling oil prices following progress made in the Middle East, better consumer sentiment 
as macros improve, and the ongoing World Cup are set to boost earnings prospects for 
sports brands and retailers. A slower-than-expected Nike comeback extends the window 
for adidas, On and Puma to gain or recover market share, even though competition 
remains intense across the space.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
adidas Group (Overweight; Price Target: €230.00)
Investment Thesis
While adidas is still delivering solid double-digit top-line growth and margin growth, there 
have been increasing questions about sales and earnings upgrade upside.The Nike 
comeback, the potential slowdown of Terrace as it enters the second half of its life cycle, and 
tariffs are likely to delay the trajectory of adidas’ market share growth. Our view is that these 
concerns are more than priced into the current valuation, while the market may have 
overlooked growth drivers elsewhere, including additional upside from the running and 
apparel categories, as well as the upcoming World Cup. When it comes to EBIT margins, 
we note that incremental growth in apparel, adidas’ momentum in Greater China and other 
emerging markets, and disciplined cost control could offset potential weakness from the 
important US market. Our analysis indicates that the risk/reward is asymmetric, which 
justifies a share re-rating to the upside. We rate adidas Overweight.
Valuation
We use a three-stage DCF (model forecasts + mid-term + fade to terminal) and assume 4.5% 
mid-term growth/ 2% terminal growth, 11% mid-term and terminal EBIT margin, and 9.0% 
WACC. 
Risks to Rating and Price Target
Key downside risks to our Overweight rating and price target include:
•
Weaker momentum from new franchises: adidas’ upcoming launches in running, 
Originals and other franchises may not turn out to be as successful, and as such may not 
be able to sustain growth should the very successful Terrace franchise begins to slow 
down quickly. 
•
Nike competition: Nike’s turnaround could be faster than expected, which may slow 
down adidas’ market share recovery, especially in the wholesale space. 
•
North American consumer strength: President Trump's tariffs may lead to broad-
based inflation in the US, negatively affecting consumer sentiment. As such, growth 
could slow in this very important region, leading to strong operating deleveraging. 
•
Consumer weakness: A weaker consumer environment in Europe, Greater China and 
other markets could impact demand and, depending on the suddenness of a a decline in 
sales, the potential build-up of inventories (given long lead times). 
•
FX exposure: adidas is a global business and is therefore exposed to fluctuations in FX 
rates, which can have a negative impact on the group.

We think the concerns over CEO change are overdone
On’s share price was down 10% upon the news of the exit of Martin Hoffmann, a well-re-
garded CEO who has been with On for the last 13 years (first as CFO, then joint CEO, then 
sole CEO). This comes just one year after the exit of former co-CEO Marc Maurer. While 
"continuity" is a word frequently cited by founders David Allemann and Casper Coppetti 
in analyst calls, the uncertainty that follows this change makes some investors think twice 
as they model growth over the next 3-5 years. Founder returns usually follow a bottleneck, 
a major challenge, or a significant strategy pivot, and the return of founders brings mixed 
results. We take the comfort from the fact that both co-founders sit on the Board of 
Directors and have been closely connected with the company throughout Hoffman’s 
tenure. We think the return of the founders signals closer execution oversight, which 
is probably exactly what is needed for On's next stage of growth.
Markets outside the U.S. still represent significant opportunities 
We still see significant growth drivers in markets such as China, where On is currently still 
under-penetrated. On only has own-retail stores in Tier 1 cities, while its wholesale exposure 
in lower tier cities is also at an early stage. While overall consumer demand in China remains 
lackluster, we view the premium sportswear segment as one of the most attractive sub-sec-
tors, driven by a growing focus on value (as middle class consumers trade down from luxu-
ry to premium brands) and on health (as participation in running, outdoor sports and niche 
sports accelerates, especially in higher tier cities). As such, we think On sits in an attractive 
category with substantial potential to expand over the next 1-3 years. Meanwhile, our dis-
cussions with industry experts indicate that On has taken a patient approach and has been 
selective in terms of new store locations and partners to work with.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
On Holding (Overweight; Price Target: $51.00)
Investment Thesis
On is a premium Swiss sports brand founded in 2010 that primarily sells footwear (>95% 
of sales). On operates in an attractive industry with substantial barriers to entry and high 
growth prospects underpinned by health and casualisation tailwinds. On has successfully 
broken into the category with a distinct premium product rooted in performance and 
technology. Importantly, we think On is striking a balance between its performance roots 
(crucial for credibility and in the sportswear space) and lifestyle (paramount for growth). 
We see On as early in its growth life-cycle and well positioned to capitalise on the sector’s 
strong tailwinds through its strong product offering. We rate On Overweight.
Valuation
Our Dec-27 PT is based on a three-stage DCF (model forecasts + mid-term + fade to 
terminal) and assumes 15% medium-term growth/4% terminal growth, 15.5% medium-
term/15.5% terminal EBIT margin and a 10.3% WACC.  
Risks to Rating and Price Target
•
Highly competitive landscape: On is competing with notable players in the sportswear 
category including the likes of Nike, adidas, Hoka and Brooks. The Nike comeback, in 
particular, may delay On’s expansion into the wholesale space. 
•
President Trump’s tariffs may lead to broad-based inflation in the US, hence hurting US 
consumer demand, including the growth of On. 
•
Multi-channel distribution: The brand’s multi-channel approach (c.59% wholesale, 
c.41% retail) adds complexity and risk to the business model.
•
Fashion risk: Many of On’s shoes have a similar look and feel to them, which leads to 
a risk that this style may go out of fashion.
•
Inventory risk and markdown potential: On is carrying elevated inventory levels, which 
could result in higher-than-expected promotional intensity, impacting margins and the 
brand overall.
•
Changes in discretionary spending: The premium footwear category remains somewhat 
discretionary in nature.
•
Management structure: On has an unusual and complex leadership structure with co-
CEOs and multiple founders on the board.
•
Ambassadors: Key partners, including co-entrepreneur, Roger Federer, Zendaya, and 
other athletes. If On were to lose its celebrity endorsers, and in particular Federer, the 
brand and its business could be adversely impacted.

Anta (covered by Qian Yao) brings a strong track record of turning around challenged 
brands, with industry-leading expertise in marketing, retail execution, and supply chain 
management in China. This is exactly what Puma needs in China. Recall that while China 
contributed only 7% of sales and 4% profit to Puma in 2025, it used to be a much more 
meaningful region in terms of P&L contribution: In 2019, for instance, China contributed 
14% sales and 24% EBIT to Puma (arguably, China was structurally a more profitable mar-
ket for international brands before BCI in 2021). Puma's sales in China (FY25: €488m) are 
considerably smaller than peers adidas (€3.6bn), Anta brand (c. €4.5bn) and Fila brand (c. 
€3.7bn, managed by Anta). From 2019 to 2025, Anta and Filan brands grew at a low teens 
CAGR while Amer group brands grew even faster, taking market share from peers. A suc-
cessful turnaround by Anta thus could see Puma more than double its P&L in China. In the 
short to medium term, we expect Puma and Anta to focus on the low hanging fruit for Puma 
in China, through establishing a clear brand positioning, improving distribution and retail 
execution, and accelerating product speed to market.    
Why are we not bullish yet?     
While shrinking to grow is a necessary strategy, uncertainty around the "grow" remains 
high. Anta alone cannot fix close to 95% of Puma business outside China. In addition, a 
China-focused turnaround is also a multi-year journey with strategic and execution risks. 
Notably, there are many differences between Amer Sports, a successful turnaround by Anta, 
and Puma. While the brands under Amer are niche premium brands largely unknown to 
Chinese consumers when they were bought by Anta, Puma today is a well-established mass 
market brand with high global awareness but a brand rebuilding challenge. Therefore, we 
note Anta will need to update its playbook when it comes to reinvigorating Puma in China. 
When it comes to valuation, the €35 price paid by Anta, while a very bullish signal from a 
strategic investor, perhaps also sets a short-term ceiling for share prices, especially consid-
ering that the €35 to be paid by Anta also reflects a premium for management access, influ-
ence and control, which is strategically valuable for Anta in its quest for global expansion. 
We expect -9% sales decline in Q2, and €-61m EBIT loss 
We expect the ongoing reset initiatives to lead to uneven quarter-over-quarter growth this 
year. Q2, in particular, is likely to be a trough due to the timing of sell-in and clean-ups. We 
expect Q2 sales to be down -9% at constant FX, driven by the Americas (-10%, including 
North America -18%) and EMEA (-11%). This is primarily driven by 1) the ongoing reset 
initiatives, with lower sell-in to mass merchant accounts, 2) less boost from clean-up sales 
versus Q1, and 3) softer consumer demand across EMEA and the Americas. We expect 
APAC sales to be less impacted, -3%.  
We expect gross margin to come in at 47%, up 80bps yoy, mostly reflecting a boost from 
fewer promotions & release from inventory reserve. We model Opex to decline 7% yoy, 
reflecting cost savings from the reset initiatives offset by higher DTC related costs and mar-
keting investment. We expect adjusted EBIT to come in at a loss of €-48m and reported EBIT 
to come in at a loss of €-61m.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
JD Sports (Neutral; Price Target: 90p)
Investment Thesis
JD Sports is the largest Sports Fashion retailer globally, with now the US representing c.40% 
of JD group sales. Following a busy period of acquisitions, JD Sports’ key priority is now 
on execution, especially to drive cost efficiency and to improve margins. In the near term, 
the promotional environment and the sizable exposure to Nike will continue to weigh on 
JD's profit outlook, although the bar today is lower too. In response to the headwinds, 
management stressed "controlling the controllables," announcing five strategic priorities in 
an effort to grow the business while preserving profitability ("controlled price 
investments"). The focus has shifted from chasing growth to improving execution, 
generating cash and improving shareholder returns. We remain Neutral as we do not see an 
inflection in profit growth in the near term. 
Valuation
Our Jan-28 PT is based on a three-stage DCF (model forecasts + mid-term + fade to terminal) 
and assumes 2.0% mid-term growth, 0.5% terminal growth, 9.5% terminal EBIT margin 
and a 9.8% WACC. 
Risks to Rating and Price Target
Upside risk 
Faster-than-expected diversification of shelf space to boost JD’s topline 
Nike returns to growth faster than expected 
Strong execution leads to faster margin improvement in Europe and globally 
Strong cash flow generation and additional share buyback
Downside risk 
Slower-than-expected diversification of shelf space 
Nike remains weaker for longer 
Inflation and other macro challenges affect consumer sentiment and reduce the 
spending power of JD’s core customer group
