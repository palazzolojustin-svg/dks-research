PM’s take: Overall, given high consternation into the print, we believe that DKS 
posted better-than-feared results with core comps +3.1%, a tick above our 
previewed +2.5% bar, with core gross margin in line (vs. concerns for a miss), with 
FL non-GAAP operating loss of ~$6MM in line with guidance for a slight decline. 
Mgmt took the clear tact of stoic confidence on the call (and in our follow-up, see 
below) regarding core business momentum, guidance conservatism, and the FL 
opportunity (closing fewer stores, “Fast Break” test format results better than 
expected and rolling to ~250 locations in 2026).  Like many other retailers, DKS 
took a prudent approach to guidance, including reflecting recent uncertainty from 
geopolitical events into the range, and also pushed down the first half. Overall, 
should geopolitical concerns fade, the DKS story could shine, especially with clean 
inventory FL (so the margin turnaround narrative could expand), momentum in the 
business even before World Cup, and the topline turnaround for FL in 2H26.  
Key model emphasis points from our CFO and separate group follow-ups. 
Given the intensity of DKS model post the FL deal, especially with 4Q earnings, 
we thought it would be helpful to pull out key model takes to the top of the note. 
(1) Mgmt noted that weather was net immaterial in 4Q with all months comping 
positive in the core business.  (2) From a guidance perspective, recent geopolitical 
events added an extra layer of prudence into the outlook from a range perspective.  
In addition, when pushed on traffic trends, DKS cited the two-year trend being 
stable, strength across all categories and consumer cohorts, and its typical 
prudence as reasons to be confident in the outlook.  FL comps are expected to inflect 
in 2H due to the ~250 Fast Break store remodels (touching US and some Europe) 
as well as improved vendor allocations given how brands are aligned with DKS to 
improve FL’s core assortment.  Year 2 comps at House of Sports are “modestly 
positive.”  (3) Merch margin was “not down” in 4Q for core Dick’s with mix, 
GameChanger, and Media all modest contributors (GC is seasonally highest in 
spring and fall while Media has been an ongoing benefit/not new YOY). Core DKS 
gross margin is forecasted flat to up slightly given House of Sports cost headwinds 
and a new DC this year are expected to be offset by the benefits from 
GameChanger, Media, and buying synergies (the latter starting in 2H26 similar to 
FL with FL gross margin up for the year). (4) DKS is forecasting $90MM of pre-
opening in 2026 driven by opening costs for both 2026 and 2027 House of Sports 
stores. Cadence-wise, the dollars are relatively even over the halves but 
concentrated in 2Q/3Q.  (5) Operating margin, and operating income are expected 
to be down in 1H26 for core Dick’s, with 2Q influenced by higher sales from World 
Cup but also opportunistic advertising investments.  (6) Capex is likely at the high 
end of $1.0-1.5B in 2027.

Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation
Investment Thesis
DKS is executing at a high level, has strongly improved its 
relationships with vendors and is benefitting from casualization 
and healthy living trends. On the other hand, we worry about the 
footwear cycle slowing, while any potential softness in the core 
DKS business will make improving FL even more important in 
2026 from an investment perspective.  We think this process will 
take time, especially with its lower income consumer exposure 
in the US and global retail footprint.  Hence, we maintain our 
Neutral rating.  
Valuation
Our Dec 26 price target is $228 and based on ~14x our 2027 EPS 
estimate, which is in the range of its historical FY1 valuation, 
and equivalent to ~8x EV/EBITDA.

(2) Core DKS strength with all months positive in the qtr; 1H guided > 2H on World 
Cup timing. Indeed, 4Q core DKS posted a +3.1% comp, below our +3.5% estimate but 
above the Street’s +2.1% (per Consensus Metrix) and a tick above our previewed +2.5% 
bar driven by ticket +4.4%, partially offset by a 1.3% decline in traffic (though +0.7% on 
a 2Y stack basis). DKS referenced the overall consumer environment as relatively 
unchanged and cited growth in all key categories (apparel, footwear, hardlines) and gains 
across income cohorts. Cadence-wise, all months were positive. Looking ahead, DKS 
expects +2-4% core comps for the year, albeit slightly 1H-weighted due to the timing of 
the World Cup (though we note profitability will be 2H-weighted due to cadence of 
investments). We are now modeling +3.4% 1Q comp,+4.2% 1H comp, +2.5% 2H comp, 
and +3.3% FY26 comp for core DKS. 
•
(3) FL exceeding initial expectations with positive inflection coming around BTS. FL 
posted a -3.4% comp, better than the down M-HSD guide and above the -6.7% consensus 
estimate and -6.5% JPMe, with the company citing better-than-expected topline driven 
by the Fast Break initiative (see below). We expect inventory rationalization/markdown 
activity also contributed, as well as a renewed strength in basketball cited by key vendors 
such as NKE and JD Sports (see Figures 9-10 for key vendor laterals). Looking to FY26, 
mgmt guided +1-3% FL comp driven by both the Fast Break stores and the overall Foot 
Locker banner, with the positive inflection (both topline and profitability) expected 
around BTS (recall, DKS expects to touch the full FL assortment by BTS). Thus, we are 
now modeling -3.0% 1Q comp and +1.5% FY26 comp for Foot Locker. 
•
(4) Fast Break stores outcomping core DKS with improving margins; DKS reduces 
expected closures given strong reaction.  Last qtr, FL launched an 11 store reset test, 
removing ~30% of unproductive styles in these stores and enhancing/renovating store 
formats to be similar to DKS stores (clearer storytelling/better presentation), which saw 
positive store comps meaningfully higher than the DKS business as well as “strong” GM 
improvement. More specifically, the test locations were broad based across urban, 
suburban, high volume and low volume stores, as well as some in Europe, with the 
company adding 10 more Fast Break stores in the LA area ahead of the NBA All-Star 
Game in mid-February. As a result of the early success, (i) DKS plans to roll out 250 Fast 
Break locations (across US and Europe, weighted domestically) by back-to-school and 
(ii) DKS reduced its initial FL closure list as a result of the early Fast Break success. We 
recently visited the Times Square test store and our findings included (i) lack of clearance, 
(ii) elevated footwear and apparel assortments (especially the latter) that resembles a 
Dick’s Sporting Goods location, and (iii) improved organization with SKUs organized not 
only by gender but also by sport (e.g., running and basketball). See Figures 3-4 for store 
photos. 
•
(5) GM expansion on merch margins; DKS guided flat to up with FL expansion 
expected. Specifically, 4Q core DKS gross margin of 35.6% expanded ~70 bps YOY and 
was in-line with the Street (vs. concerns for a miss), driven by the factors noted above. 
Like TSCO/BBY/sporting goods vendors, DKS spoke to heightened promotionality 
during the holidays, which we believe was offset by ongoing strength in GameChanger 
and Dick’s Media Network (though GameChanger seasonality did create less of a 
tailwind in 4Q). On FL, gross margin was 25.2%, impacted by 1,000-bp headwind (in-
line with expectations and ~350-bp impact to consolidated GM) to right-size inventory.

bringing DKS to a grand total of 67 locations at the end of 2027. This is slightly below 
the initial 75-100 HoS by 2027 guide. DKS also expects to open 22 Field House locations 
and 15 Golf Galaxy performance centers in 2026. (3) The 6th DC remains on track to open 
this year. Recall, DKS is constructing a distribution center spanning 800,000 square feet 
in Fort Worth, TX to build upon its 50-store presence in the state and improve product 
availability in the region of the US (a negative for ASO).
