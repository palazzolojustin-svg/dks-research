Taking This in Stride
DKS’ 2Q was a reset of the near-term, but underlying earnings power remains. 
DICK’S 2Q results were consequential, with consolidated adjusted EPS of $3.53 and full-
year EPS guidance reduced to $11.00-$12.00 from $13.50-$14.50, but the underlying 
core franchise remains healthy. The DICK’S banner generated a 4.9% comp, gained 
market share, increased transactions and average ticket, and outperformed the sporting 
goods industry by roughly 200 bps. The earnings reset is primarily tied to excess legacy 
footwear inventory, heightened promotional activity and the Foot Locker business, 
where expected operating profit of $110-$150 million became a projected loss of $40-
$80 million. We maintain our Buy rating and believe shares can rise over the next 12 
months as investors gain confidence that current margin pressure reflects a temporary 
inventory and product-cycle adjustment rather than a permanent reduction in earnings 
power.
Our estimate revisions isolate the near-term margin reset while preserving a 
path to earnings recovery. We reduce FY26E EPS to $11.50 from $13.96, FY27E to 
$12.95 from $16.75 and FY28E to $14.25 from $19.17, reflecting lower Foot Locker 
sales, greater promotional pressure, and a slower operating-margin recovery. However, 
scenario analysis indicates meaningful asymmetry: our $178 base case assumes 2.5% 
DICK’S comp growth, 3.9% total sales growth and a 7.5% operating margin in CY'28, 
versus $210 in an upside case and $90 in a downside case. The work reinforces that the 
key debate is the duration of the footwear correction and Foot Locker losses, not the 
health of the core DICK’S business.

Dick’s Sporting Goods delivered one of the more consequential and nuanced 
quarters recently across retail.
While the headline narrative centers on a meaningful reset in marketplace economics, 
we believe the more important takeaway is that the earnings revision appears largely 
attributable to an industry-wide inventory dislocation and near-term Foot Locker 
pressures, rather than any deterioration in the underlying health of the DICK’S franchise. 
In our view, the quarter reinforced a distinction that the market will probably 
increasingly focus on in the coming quarters: the core DICK’S business continues to 
execute at a high level even as portions of the broader athletic marketplace work 
through a significant correction.
One of the most notable aspects of the quarter was the divergence between sales 
momentum and profitability. The DICK’S banner generated a 4.9% comp, gained 
market share, increased both transactions and average ticket, and continued to 
outperform the broader sporting goods industry by roughly 200 bps. Importantly, 
management maintained its full-year sales outlook for the segment, which is still 
expected to comp up 2.5%-4% for the year. This implies 2-3% same-store sales growth 
in the back half of 2026 despite an increasingly promotional environment. Viewed 
through that lens, the operating backdrop appears considerably more resilient than the 
earnings revision alone would suggest.
DKS continues to see strength in its customer base as it navigates product-cycle 
headwinds.
The underlying challenge appears to stem from a substantial footwear and apparel 
inventory correction rather than a broad-based deterioration in consumer demand. The 
company repeatedly emphasized that consumers have not pulled back from athletic 
footwear as a category. Instead, spending patterns have shifted away from mature 
lifestyle franchises and toward newer performance-oriented products, innovation-
driven launches, and emerging brands. As demand for several legacy franchises slowed, 
inventory accumulated across brands, wholesale channels, and retailers, ultimately 
creating the conditions for widespread promotional activity throughout the 
marketplace.
The distinction is critical. DICK’S continues to frame the current environment as a supply-
side and product-cycle adjustment rather than a demand-driven downturn. The 
company continues to report strength across running, performance footwear, team 
sports, outdoor, soccer, UGG, Birkenstock, and several newer apparel concepts. Said 
differently, consumers remain engaged and willing to spend; however, they are 
demonstrating far greater selectivity regarding product, innovation, and brand relevance 
than they have in prior years.
Management’s strategic response is particularly noteworthy. Rather than prioritizing 
near-term margin protection, DICK’S is deliberately investing in price and 
competitiveness. Throughout the call, management characterized the current 
environment as a market-share opportunity, arguing that maintaining value leadership 
during periods of industry stress often represents one of the highest-return investments 
available. This approach is consistent with DICK’S historical playbook. During prior 
periods of disruption, the company has repeatedly leveraged its scale, balance sheet 
strength, vendor relationships, and operational capabilities while weaker competitors 
and channels faced increasing pressure. 
The company is extending this playbook where it is seeking to increase investments in 
areas like advertising and wages at a time when sales are declining. These factors, along 
with a significant amount of promotional intensity is what led the swing in profitability 
of this segment from a $110mm to $150mm gain to a -$40mm to -$80mm loss. In other 
words, DKS is expecting a $200mm swing in sales to lead to a $200mm swing in profits 
for FL, but this includes some deeper investments that it otherwise had not factored in. 
The implication is that earnings may be deferred rather than structurally impaired. The 
gross margin pressure is real and could persist through year-end, but management 
appears increasingly confident that once excess inventory is cleared from the system, 
DKS will emerge with stronger customer relationships, enhanced competitive

The Foot Locker acquisition is clearly the primary source of the market's concern 
and the principal reason guidance moved lower.
The magnitude of the revision was significant. Foot Locker moved from an expected 
operating profit of $110-$150 million to an operating loss of $40-$80 million. However, 
the company’s commentary suggested that multiple headwinds are occurring 
simultaneously rather than a single fundamental breakdown in the business model. Foot 
Locker remains disproportionately exposed to the exact categories experiencing the 
greatest pressure, particularly legacy athletic footwear silhouettes and launch-driven 
product cycles. We interpret this as including products such as Air Force 1s, Dunks, 
Jordan 1s, and even Sambas. At the same time, launch productivity fell short of 
expectations during the quarter, while the EMEA region remains materially more 
promotional and economically challenged than management had anticipated entering 
the year.
There are still constructive elements of the story. 
Despite the near-term challenges, the company’s messaging around the strategic 
rationale for the acquisition remained consistent. The company continues to view Foot 
Locker as a unique global platform that deepens relationships with key brand partners, 
expands its reach within the athletic ecosystem, and creates long-term opportunities 
that would have been difficult to replicate organically. Investors may continue to debate 
the timing of the transaction, but management showed little indication that its 
conviction in the long-term strategic thesis has weakened.
Perhaps the most notable aspect of the call was the continued evidence that DICK’S 
long-term growth engines are gaining traction. House of Sport, Field House, 
GameChanger, and the DICK’S Media Network all appear to be performing well and 
increasingly contributing to profitability. These assets are important because they 
diversify earnings away from traditional retail economics while creating a broader 
ecosystem centered around sports participation, athlete engagement, and community 
involvement. Over time, that ecosystem could evolve into one of the company's most 
durable competitive advantages and represent an increasingly differentiated source of 
value creation relative to traditional retailers. 
Further, investments made between 2022 and 2024 are now paying dividends. For 
example, the DKS Media Network has now reached profitability. The company noted 
that DMN should  be a meaningful future growth and profit contributor, reinforcing the 
idea that DICK's is developing higher-margin revenue streams beyond traditional retail. 
GameChanger continues to stand out as a significant hidden asset and was among the 
more encouraging topics discussed outside of the core DKS business. Management 
reiterated expectations for 30%-40% CAGR growth and described the platform as 
highly profitable, cash-generative, and EBITDA accretive. In our view, it remains one of 
the fastest-growing and most attractive assets within the broader portfolio.
We believe the central debate now shifts from whether DICK’S is executing to how 
quickly the footwear and apparel correction normalizes. If management is correct that 
the current environment is primarily driven by inventory imbalances and product-cycle 
dynamics, then the earnings pressure should prove temporary. In that scenario, this 
period may ultimately be remembered less as the beginning of a structural slowdown 
and more as a deliberate margin sacrifice that strengthened DICK’S competitive position 
while the broader industry retrenched.
The stock reaction will likely hinge on investor confidence in that normalization timeline. 
Near-term estimates almost certainly need to move lower, particularly given Foot Locker 
and EMEA pressures. However, the call reinforced our view that the underlying health of 
the DICK’S franchise remains intact and that the company's long-term earnings power 
may prove more resilient than the revised guidance initially implies.

Looking ahead, we believe DKS shares can work from here, although the path is unlikely 
to be linear. The most important near-term debate centers on the athletic footwear 
category, where demand appears to be normalizing following several years of unusually 
strong lifestyle-driven consumption. Importantly, we do not view this as a one- or two-
quarter adjustment. The footwear cycle could remain challenged into 2027 as 
consumers work through elevated levels of lifestyle product penetration and brands 
reset innovation pipelines, product assortments, and launch calendars.
As a result, DKS will likely need other categories to help offset moderation in demand for 
part of the footwear complex. While this creates a somewhat more challenging growth 
backdrop, we continue to believe the company is well positioned given its diversified 
assortment, strong vendor relationships, differentiated customer experience, and 
demonstrated ability to gain market share across a variety of environments.
At the same time, we believe the market may be under-appreciating the flexibility 
embedded within FL's store portfolio. Across North America, the segment operates 
roughly 1,600 stores that generate approximately $3.0 million in annual sales per 
location. If the company further rationalizes the fleet over time and recaptures even 
30% of the sales associated with every 100 stores closed, the retained revenue could 
translate to $90mm to $100mm of incremental share for DKS stores. This could 
contribute approximately 60bp to 80bps to the core business. Moreover, we would 
expect that revenue to flow through at a high incremental margin, likely in the 40%+ 
range, which could add roughly $0.30 to EPS.
In our view, this represents an underappreciated pathway toward margin preservation 
and earnings growth even in a slower demand environment. Combined with DKS’s 
strong balance sheet, disciplined capital allocation framework, and demonstrated 
operational flexibility, the company appears to have multiple levers available to navigate 
a prolonged footwear slowdown while continuing to create shareholder value.
Valuation: Risk-reward skews favorably
Our $178 price target (was $275) is based on ~12.5x our revised CY'28 EPS estimate. 
We have rolled our valuation framework forward and now apply ~12.5x our CY'28 EPS 
estimate of $14.25 (our prior price target reflected ~16x our prior CY'27 EPS estimate). 
We are lowering our estimates following DKS's revised 2026 guidance and our 
expectation for greater operating margin pressure in the out-years. Importantly, our 
~12.5x multiple remains within 1 standard deviation of the company's 5-yr average on 
an NTM basis. We believe this valuation appropriately reflects both the near-term 
uncertainty and our view that the fundamental health of the DICK'S franchise and the 
company's long-term earnings power remain intact.
What happened in 2Q results?
We believe DKS's 2Q results and updated guidance illustrate the extent to which the 
company has been affected by the challenging conditions currently facing the athletic 
footwear and apparel marketplace. While the core business continued to gain market 
share and deliver healthy top-line growth, both segments experienced margin pressure 
as management prioritized competitiveness and long-term positioning over near-term 
profitability.

Operating Margins: The core DICK'S segment delivered an operating margin of 
12.6% during the quarter, reflecting a modest 42 bp year-over-year decline. While 
the segment benefited from broad-based demand and sales growth, profitability 
came under pressure as competitive dynamics shifted meaningfully throughout 
the quarter. As discussed previously, portions of the athletic footwear and apparel 
marketplace became increasingly promotional, prompting the company to make 
targeted pricing investments to preserve competitiveness and protect market 
share. Due to promotional margin headwinds and substantial turnaround 
investments, the Foot Locker business recorded an operating loss of $31.90 million 
during the quarter, translating to a 124 bp year-over-year operating margin decline 
to -1.8%. The results underscore the combined impact of a highly promotional 
retail environment and the company's ongoing efforts to reposition and improve 
the Foot Locker banner.

As for SG&A, the integration of the newly acquired Foot Locker segment added 
approximately $477 million to consolidated SG&A expenses during the quarter. 
To support the long-term turnaround of the business, management continued 
investing behind the platform, including the execution of Foot Locker's first 
major brand marketing campaign in over 10 years and increased payroll 
investments for store associates. Meanwhile, DICK'S segment SG&A 
deleveraged due to substantial investments in world cup marketing initiatives, 
as well as continued spending to enhance both digital and in-store 
experiences. Management also highlighted broader macroeconomic and 
geopolitical pressures that affected fuel, supply chain, healthcare, and other 
operating costs.

Tariffs: DKS received approximately ~$60mm of tariff refunds in 2Q, although 
only ~$21mm was included in quarterly results related to merchandise sold in 1H, 
with $19mm attributed to DKS and $2mm attributed to FL. Roughly ~$38mm 
related to the prior year and was excluded from non-GAAP adjusted EPS. 
Management indicated that the vast majority of refunds have now been received 
and expects only a de minimis amount in 2H. Importantly, the company has largely 
reinvested these benefits into the business to remain competitively priced while 
helping offset ongoing fuel, supply chain, and other inflationary cost pressures.

EPS: Taken together, the company reported adjusted 2Q EPS of $3.53 on a 
consolidated basis (UBSe pre-print of $3.60 and pre-print consensus of $3.76).
FL's transformation faces a near term setback but sees a path to get back on 
track.
Much of the market’s focus centered on Foot Locker's weaker-than-expected 2Q 
performance and the path toward recovery. Management attributed the shortfall 
primarily to a sharp slowdown in legacy lifestyle footwear, weaker launch productivity, 
and fewer launches in 2Q relative to 1Q. Investors are attempting to determine whether 
these issues are Nike-specific or reflective of a broader shift in consumer preferences 
toward innovation, performance, and newer silhouettes. Management's view is that the 
challenge extends beyond Nike and is more closely tied to consumers' desire for 
newness. The company also acknowledged that Foot Locker lacked sufficient exposure 
to several of the categories and products currently driving demand. While management 
expects some improvement in 4Q relative to 3Q, it also acknowledged that promotional 
pressure remains elevated and visibility remains somewhat limited.
What are bulls saying after the print?
The bulls believe a substantial portion of the bad news is already reflected in the stock, 
particularly given that shares trade below 13x EPS and remain below levels observed at 
the time of the Foot Locker acquisition. Additionally, many investors view the revised 
guidance as significantly de-risked and more achievable. Bulls see several potential 
drivers of improvement, including Foot Locker-specific operational fixes, a stronger 
product launch pipeline, reduced exposure to challenged lifestyle categories over time, 
improving credit card data, and the upcoming back-to-school season. Under this 
framework, investors believe the current valuation offers meaningful upside if execution 
improves and demand trends stabilize, with many modeling FY'27 EPS of approximately 
~$13.00.

What are bears saying after the print?
The bears remain skeptical that the challenges facing Foot Locker—particularly around 
lifestyle franchises such as Air Force 1s, Jordans, Dunks, and Sambas—will dissipate next 
year or beyond. They question whether Foot Locker can generate meaningful 
improvement if broader lifestyle demand remains weak, arguing that the business could 
become a "black hole" that absorbs earnings generated elsewhere within the DICK'S 
enterprise. Some investors draw parallels to Family Dollar's impact on Dollar Tree. More 
broadly, bears view the core DKS business as moderating and believe the quarter served 
as a reminder of the inherent volatility within the model. Under this view, the market 
may ultimately assign a lower multiple to the DICK'S enterprise on a more permanent 
basis. Bears are modeling roughly $10.00 of EPS for FY'27.
How to think about DKS’s updated guidance?
DKS updated its guidance to reflect a more cautious outlook heading into the second 
half of the year as a result of the marketplace conditions encountered during 2Q. 
Notably, management identified promotionality as the single largest swing factor 
between the low and high ends of the guidance range. Below, we highlight the most 
important considerations by segment and line item.

DKS sales: The company reiterated DKS comp guidance of 2.5%-4.0%. 
Importantly, growth continued to outpace the broader industry by nearly 200 bps, 
reinforcing the company's ability to strengthen its leadership position and gain 
market share. Management remains confident in the core business, and 
expectations have not materially changed despite the more promotional 
environment. The company intends to continue executing through differentiated 
athlete experiences, strong brand partnerships, and the long-term investments it 
has made across the platform.

FL sales: FL comp guidance was lowered to -2.0% to 0% (vs. +1.5%-3.0% 
previously). Foot Locker faces several headwinds, including an increasingly 
promotional marketplace, excess inventory in legacy footwear silhouettes, and 
fewer product launches than originally anticipated. Additionally, management 
now expects the EMEA business to require a longer recovery period than North 
America. Within EMEA, consumers have become more cautious amid geopolitical 
tensions and elevated fuel costs, contributing to an industry-wide build-up of 
excess inventory.

DKS Margins: DKS OM guidance was lowered to 10.6%-10.9% (vs. 11.0%-
11.4% previously; UBSe 11.1%).

The company continues to expect modest gross margin pressure due to a more 
promotional environment through the remainder of the year, as well as higher 
expected fuel and supply chain costs.

From a cadence perspective, management expects the gross margin pressure 
to be most pronounced in 3Q relative to 4Q.

The company also expects SG&A deleverage for the full year, including 
approximately 50 bps of deleverage at the midpoint in 3Q.

FL Margins: FL OM guidance was lowered to a loss of -$80mm to -$40mm (vs. a 
profit of $110mm-$150mm previously), implying an OM range of -1.1% to -0.5% 
(vs. ~1.4%-1.9% previously; UBSe -0.9%).

Management lowered its Foot Locker outlook to reflect the same footwear-
related pressures experienced during 2Q, along with continued challenges 
within the EMEA segment.

EPS: Consolidated non-GAAP EPS guidance was lowered to $11.00-$12.00 (vs. 
$13.50-$14.50 previously; UBSe $11.50). In addition, the company increased its 
full-year tax rate assumption to approximately ~29% (up roughly ~200 bps from 
the prior ~27%). The higher tax rate is driven by foreign earnings mix and 
purchase accounting adjustments. Management quantified the resulting EPS 
headwind at approximately $0.35 for the full year.
