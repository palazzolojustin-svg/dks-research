1. Dick's Bond Yielding 6.5% After Foot Locker M&A: Credit Outlook
THESIS: Dick's Sporting Goods' (Baa2/BBB) bonds trade wide to similarly rated peers following its
acquisition of Foot Locker last year, with the 2052 bond yielding 6.5%. The discount likely reflects
integration and execution risks rather than a decline in credit quality, given Dick's solid balance sheet
and post-transaction credit metrics. The company financed the deal mostly through equity, with the
remainder covered by $223 million of cash on hand. (07/28/26)

2. Dick's Maintains Mid-BBB Profile Post-M&A, Execution Risks Exist
Dick's Baa2/BBB credit ratings are supported by historically low leverage and a disciplined financial
policy. Ratings stayed intact following the Foot Locker acquisition in September 2025, given the
transaction was financed with stock and cash on hand, limiting the strain on the balance sheet. Still,
Foot Locker's post-Covid inventory missteps and underperforming stores have prompted a review of
unproductive assets, raising the execution risk.
The combined entity has a $300 billion total addressable market across a broader set of consumer

Dick’s Sprints to Integrate Foot Locker, Faces Some Hurdles
Dick's Sporting Goods acquired Foot Locker in September 2025 in a majority-stock transaction.
Though Dick's credit metrics and mid-BBB credit ratings are stable, Foot Locker requires some
transformation of inventory following post-Covid missteps as well as the closing of underperforming

3. Positive S&P Outlook for Upgrade to BBB+ May Be Tested
The acquisition of Foot Locker, which closed in September 2025, didn't inflate Dick's leverage as most
of the purchase was funded with stock, not additional debt. Moody's resolved its review for downgrade
on Dick's last September as it expects the sports retailer to maintain a conservative financial policy.
But Dick's positive outlook at S&P on the expectation of a smooth integration of Foot Locker could be
at risk on the latter's wrong post-Covid inventory and underperforming stores. Foot Locker inventory
optimization actions resulted in a $43 million charge incurred in fiscal 1Q. The anticipated $100-$125
million of deal-related synergies could take longer to realize. (07/23/26)

5. Dick's Credit Quality at Forefront Despite Foot Locker M&A
Dick's credit metrics are solid following comparable-sales growth in fiscal 2025, driven by rising
average ticket and transactions. The company recorded a 4.1% increase in comparable sales in fiscal
1Q26. Liquidity of $3 billion as of 1Q includes $998 million of cash and $1.97 billion undrawn on its
unsecured revolving credit facility. Dick's used $223 million of cash-on-hand to finance its acquisition
of Foot Locker and exchanged 95.5% of Foot Locker's $400 million senior notes due in 2029 for
$381.932 million of Dick's-issued notes maturing October 2029.
For fiscal 2026, Dick's expects net capital spending of $1.4 billion, up from $976 million in 2025. The
company had $28.2 million remaining under its 2021 share-repurchase authorization as of May 2 and
authorized a new 5-year buyback program of up to $3 billion in March 2025. (07/23/26)

7. Store Expansion Brings Opportunity, Challenges
Contributing Analysts Lindsay Dutch (Consumer Hardlines)
Foot Locker's 2,400 stores and international presence offer opportunities and challenges for Dick's.
On the positive side, the company's small 850-store fleet rapidly expands, broadening customer
reach and creating a real estate footprint that would take decades to build organically. Yet Foot
Locker's smaller, on-mall sites likely need reinvestment and reimagination that may require different
execution than the Dick's House of Sport or Field House concepts. Touching over 2,000 stores will
also take time.
Dick's square footage is otherwise rising slowly as the vast majority of House of Sport and Field House
openings replace namesake locations. Still, these concepts are drawing customers and fueling bigger
baskets amid steady sports-driven demand. (08/26/26)

10. Dick's Foot Locker Slump Deepens Deal Concerns: Earnings Outlook
Contributing Analysts Lindsay Dutch (Consumer Hardlines)
Post-2Q Earnings Outlook: Dick's Sporting Goods' 2Q earnings miss and lowered outlook for Foot
Locker sales and segment profit deepens skepticism about the acquisition's merits. Dick's promised a
turnaround of Foot Locker by the back-to-school shopping season, yet the 3.6% same-store sales
decline in 2Q shows it's failing to deliver as tighter discretionary budgets pressure lifestyle footwear
into seasonally strong 2H. Foot Locker's disappointing same-store sales contrasts with the legacy
business' 4.9% increase, showing that sports-driven demand is steady. Foot Locker's greater exposure
to lifestyle trends, brand innovation and discretionary spending dilutes the differentiation that set
Dick's apart.
Foot Locker is also weighing on profitability. Rising costs and the need to increase discounts,
especially at Foot Locker, led to an 18% drop in the full-year EPS projection. (08/25/26)
