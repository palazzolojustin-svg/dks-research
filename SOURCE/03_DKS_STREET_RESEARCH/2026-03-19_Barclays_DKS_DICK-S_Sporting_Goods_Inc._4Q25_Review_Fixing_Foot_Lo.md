DICK'S Sporting Goods, Inc.
4Q25 Review: Fixing Foot Locker
Adj. EPS matched consensus, driven by a sales beat offset by
a GM miss. The core DICK'S business posted +3.1% comp
growth, and while FL comps remained negative, inventory
cleanup progressed faster than expected, allowing a shift to
the next phase of the FL turnaround: store fleet improvement.
Fixing Foot Locker; "cleaning out the garage" is complete and FL ready to progress
towards 3Q26 inflection. DKS's 4Q25 results showed ongoing solid performance out of the core
DICK’S business, with +3.1% comp growth healthy (albeit moderating), supported by growth
across categories. Foot Locker (FL), meanwhile, remained in negative territory with a -3.4%
comp sales decline, though FL guidance expects sequential improvement throughout FY26 with
positive comps for the year (up +1% to +3%). Importantly, the company highlighted that FL's
"clean out the garage" inventory clean-up phase is now finished and it is shifting to the next
phase of the FL turnaround: a store fleet refresh dubbed "Fast Break." Following a successful
pilot launch of Fast Break in 11 stores that drove "very strong positive comps, actually
meaningfully exceeding the DICK'S business," the company plans to scale to roughly ~250
locations by back-to-school season. These stores have light capex/refresh investment, with the
main driver being tightened merchandising and curating the offering for the consumer. Taking a
step back, while consolidated margins are expected to be pressured in 1H26 due to investment
timing, management expects expansion in 2H26 as synergies materialize and Foot Locker
performance improves. We reiterate our Overweight rating on DKS and continue to have a
positive view on the core DICK'S banner taking market share in the sporting goods sector. We
are also more confident on the visibility into the Foot Locker turnaround and accretion in 3Q26
following the completion of the inventory reset and early success of the Fast Break initiative. We
believe the combined DKS-FL entity will benefit from: 1) improved bargaining power with
emerging and existing branded partners; 2) secular trends in health, wellness, and expanding
athletics across all ages and demographics; and 3) new experiential formats including House of
Sport and Golf Galaxy, enabling DKS to own the category. Following the earnings release BMO
on 3/12/26, DKS shares closed +1.0% (SPX -1.5%).

environment but did not provide updated commentary on tariff impacts this quarter. Guidance
embeds tariff rates before the Supreme court ruling. Tariffs were not cited as a material driver of
either 4Q25 performance or FY26 guidance.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: DKS in "HUNT" Phase
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.

Tariff Exposure
Tariff Exposure Update. Management acknowledged a dynamic macro and geopolitical
environment but did not provide updated commentary on tariff impacts this quarter. Tariffs
were not cited as a material driver of either 4Q25 performance or FY26 guidance.
Prior tariff commentary. During the quarter, DKS noted that it effectively absorbed tariff costs
within its merch margin, still managing to deliver +5bps of merchandise margin expansion
despite tariffs and a slightly unfavorable product mix. This performance is consistent with prior
management commentary that tariff impacts in 2H25 would be modest. DKS’s updated 3Q25
guidance fully reflects all tariffs currently in effect, and management reiterated confidence in its
ability to navigate the evolving macroeconomic environment.
