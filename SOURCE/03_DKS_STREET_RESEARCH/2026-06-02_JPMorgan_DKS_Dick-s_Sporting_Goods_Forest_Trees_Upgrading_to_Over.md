Catalyst Watch is our near-term conviction indicator for our 
equity coverage universe. It is an indicator our research 
analysts can apply to a stock in their coverage into a specific 
event in order to articulate near-term positivity or caution 
based on a specific catalyst. As such, it can differ from the 
analysts’ published ratings, which are relative to their 
coverage universe and six to 12 months in duration. 
Expecting the burden of proof to quickly shift to the bears with 1Q providing 
strong support for the bull case. We placed DKS on Positive Catalyst Watch into-
the-print on the expectation of better-than-expected sales at DKS and FL, with the 
latter flipping to positive comps and operating profits where the Street was 
negative and we were above. All of this proved to be true, but the quarter was 
blemished by no upward EPS revision on tax clipping $0.14 out of EPS in 1Q and 
$0.25 from the updated guide for the year (so the low end of sales and op profit were 
raised but no EPS benefit), alongside a disappointing gross margin performance at 
core DKS (50 bps below the Street and down 36 bps YOY). Take a generally 
negative view on current credit card data, which was no doubt impacted by weather 
in the Northeast, especially on golf which peaks in mix in 2Q/June, and voila, the 
stock traded down, albeit modestly.  
Looking ahead, however, we believe the core bull case was affirmed and we are 
a warm weekend away (and a meeting with mgmt) from the bears flipping to 
bulls, especially in light of the World Cup ahead and how FL’s turn to positive 
comps/op profit occurred with little change to its assortment, other than 
having clean inventory. Indeed, Chairman Ed Stack’s merchandising touch (and 
his 50 year relationship with vendors) will start to be seen across FL stores for back 
to school (BTS), with the media brand relaunch alongside, and the “clean out the 
garage” FL clearance lap in 4Q. We also point to an expected “change in trajectory” 
in the core DKS gross margin in 2Q as tariffed inventory has been largely sold 
through with better 2H sales leverage in the core as well (another sticking point in 
the long-term DKS story). Our 2026 and 2027 EPS estimates are now $14.33 and 
$17.08, respectively (vs. $14.25 and $16.20, respectively, per Consensus Metrix 
into the print).  Our updated $270 Dec 26 price target based on 16x 2027E PE 
(equivalent to 9x EV/EBITDA) both one turn above the historical average, but in 
the range, given the potential for upward revisions vs. the Street.  Please also see 
our detailed highlights from our meetings with DKS mgmt, including Chairman Ed 
Stack, at our April conference.

Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation
Investment Thesis
Big picture, DKS is executing at a high level, with the company 
well positioned to benefit from wellness and sports trends, 
250th Anniversary, 2026 World Cup, and 2028 Olympics. 
Indeed, core Dick’s strength continues with House of Sport, 
leading assortment enabling full-price selling, and alternative 
profit pools. Simultaneously, the Foot Locker turnaround is off 
to a good start with DKS already achieving positive comps/op 
profit at FL despite little change to the assortment, with strength 
continuing on remodels, clean inventory, BTS assortment, and 
improved vendor relations. As such, we are optimistic on 
accelerating results in the back half and into 2027. Thus, we are 
upgrading the stock to Overweight from Neutral.  
Valuation
Our updated $270 Dec 26 price target is based on 16x 2027E PE 
(equivalent to 9x EV/EBITDA) both one turn above the 
historical average, but in the range, given the potential for 
upward revisions vs. the Street.

DKS still expects 1H-weighted comps due to the timing of the World Cup and prudence 
(though we note profitability will be 2H-weighted as per below), with the low end of the 
+2.5% to +4.0%  guide raised to reflect 1Q strength and continued confidence in the 
business. We are now modeling +5.0% 2Q comp and +3.9% FY26 comp for core DKS. 
See Figure 17. 
•
(3) FL turns positive with further growth coming around BTS. For the first time since 
4Q20, Foot Locker posted a positive comp (+0.6%), surpassing the Street’s -1.3% and vs. 
our +1.0%e, driven by Foot Locker U.S. banner +6.4% and North America +1.4% 
(partially offset by international comp -1.7%), with the improvement attributed to clean 
inventories (suggesting sales were AUR driven) and some lift from Fast Break success 
(+DD SSS; Figures 12-13). Looking to FY26, mgmt raised the low end of the FY26 comps 
guide on 1Q strength and continued confidence in the FL turnaround, now expecting a 
+1.5% to +3.0% comp range with SSS 2H weighted as DKS will touch the full FL 
assortment by BTS. Thus, we are now modeling +1.0% 2Q comp and +2.4% FY26 comp 
for Foot Locker. See Figure 18. 
•
(4) GM expecting a “change of trajectory” in 2Q with FL benefitting in 2H from 
better merchandising and 4Q clearance laps. 1Q core DKS gross margin of 36.3% was 
down 36 bps YOY, missing the Street’s 36.8%, with mix (collectibles/trading cards 
higher AUR but lower margin rate), tariff costs (given pre-SCOTUS inventory receipts), 
and SC expenses (fuel up YOY and Forth Worth DC opening at tail end of the qtr) the 
drivers. On FL, gross margin expanded ~100 bps YOY to 27.9%, primarily driven by 
occupancy leverage from store closures in addition to merchandise margin expansion 
from cleaner inventory (4Q’s “clean out the garage”) with “meaningful” improvements 
at the ~100 Fast Break locations. Notably, GM has yet to include buying synergies but 
will in 2H. Gross and merchandise margin expansion is expected for FY26 for both core 
DKS and Foot Locker.  At DKS, tariff headwinds abating, moderated supply chain costs, 
buying synergies in 2H, and accelerated Media Network and Gamechanger tailwinds 
support improvement and a “change in trajectory” starting in 2Q.  FL benefits from 
elevated clearance (especially in 4Q), occupancy leverage, and higher heat/margin 
product in 2H following the BTS assortment and beyond reflecting Chairman Ed Stack’s 
merchandising prowess. On 2H, we note that fuel headwinds are not contemplated in the 
guide, although neither are tariff refunds or benefits from recapturing the shared tariff 
pain from vendors (DKS is not the importer of record for most of its sales). We note DKS 
originally expected gross margin to expand ~75 bps last year but cut that on 2Q with 
tariffs (and ended the year only +40 bps on both direct imports and shared pain, in our 
view).
•

majority (~86%) of the dollar increase attributed to the FL consolidation. Looking ahead,
