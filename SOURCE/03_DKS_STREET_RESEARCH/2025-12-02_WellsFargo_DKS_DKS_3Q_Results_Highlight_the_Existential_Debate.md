Our Call
FL results vs. strong core DKS results highlight ongoing debate: bulls are LT oriented/
focused on value creation via execution, bears on heavy lifting expected (NT) and 
questions on current trends in lifestyle. PT (15x '26E EPS) to $225 from $220.
 
Mgmt Callback Takes. 1) The 4Q FL GM guide (1000)-(1500)bps is GAAP and includes 
obsolecense & write-downs, 2) while it is DKS's intent to exit 4Q clean (FL invs), work 
could extend into 1H26, 3) 4Q FL op inc guide of "slightly negative" is pro forma and 
excludes charges, 4) DKS is looking to build FL leadership through a combo of industry 
experts and operational experts, 5) while B2S was perhaps more promotional (YoY) than 
expected, DKS sees the holiday promo environment as similar to last yr.
 
Core DKS Again Solid. DKS continues to execute at a very high level (7 straight qtrs 
of SSS 4%+) across all facets of the business (ecomm again outpaced the overall biz), 
underpinned by a strong product assortment. Core DKS posted comps +5.7% (vs St 
+3.7%) and DKS-only EPS beat St by +$0.09 (+3.5%). That said, core DKS GM does 
look like a miss at 36% vs St 36.4% though was in line with mgmt expectations and DKS 
discussed 1) a more promo B2S, 2) product shift to HG in golf, and 3) some mix shift.
 
FL Was "Inconclusive." With the acq having closed on 9/08 DKS only reported the stub 
period for FL (through the Oct Q close) - thus, questions remain especially as B2S was 
excluded from reported numbers. That said, pro forma SSS decelerated to (4.7%) from 
(2%), with International down (10%) and NA (2.6%). DKS guided FL comps down (HSD) to 
(MSD)% in 4Q25. The reported pro forma GM of ~23% compares to 29.7% in 3Q24, and 
we believe guide implies 4Q core GM down (500)bps (vs GAAP -1000 to -1500bps).
 
What 3Q Says About the Bull Case. Discipline, merch execution & product access were 
the key FL issues DKS highlighted, and this is where bulls are focused, arguing it's exactly 
these traits DKS brings to the table. For example, DKS argues it's not only the shift in 
lifestyle from bball to running that's affecting FL comps but also (perhaps more so) the 
assortment prior FL mgmt had bought. This was compounded by a stale presentation. 
Bulls see NT fixes that help bridge to the B2S '26 inflection.
 
What 3Q Says About Bear Case. DKS announced $500-$750M of resturcturing charges 
and 1000-1500bps of 4Q GM pressure to "clean the garage." Mgmt also suggested 
no real inflection in pro froma sales trends before 2H26 (1H26 inv - poor quality - was 
already bought) excluding the impact of store / inv rationalization. We believe most of 
these updates were below expectations and highlight many of the bear case arguments 
(NT focused). Fashion trends in lifestyle is the key (unsettled) MT-LT question.

Investment Thesis
Key themes surrounding the name include:
1. Can DKS Fix Foot Locker? On May 15, DKS announced the acquisition of Foot Locker for $24/
share, or approximately $2.3B. At the time, DKS outlined 5 key tenants behind the proposed 
acquisition of FL; these include: 1) the opportunity to move from a purely domestic to global 
operator, 2) the ability to serve a broader variety of customer sets through multiple concepts,
3) the ability to strengthen its relationships with key suppliers, 4) a broader omni-channel reach, 
and 5) potential synergy opportunities of $100-$125mm. Synergies are mostly in COGS with 
plans to run the businesses separately. While mgmt plans to run the biz separately, we would note 
historical synergies closer to 2%, or $150mm based on ~$7.5b in FL revs suggesting slight upside 
optionality. DKS has repeated that the deal would be accretive in FY26, with cost synergies in the 
medium term achieved through procurement and direct sourcing efficiencies (COGS). While DKS 
again repeated this on the 3Q25 call, the company was a bit less direct in that regard on the 2Q25 
call, discussing a) the timing of synergies and b) the performance of the core FL business, and said 
"there are lots of moving pieces," with "more work to be done." Updates from the 3Q call include 
1,000-1,500bps of GM headwind in 4Q25 as DKS "cleans out the garage" and $500-$750M of 
restructuring charges, including lease terminations and inventory rationalization.
2. Executing at a High Level. DKS continues to execute at a very high level (7 straight qts of SSS 
4%+ as of 3Q25) across all facets of the business: in-store, online (ecomm continues to outpace 
the business), and digital customer interaction through Game Changer and Dicks Media Network
—all underpinned by a strong product assortment. DKS is also leaning into the success of their 
new store formats (Field House from 26 to 42 and House of Sport from 19 to 35 YoY). The newer 
concepts, mostly remodels of existing locations, bring DKS closer to hardcore athletes, provides 
a differentiated experience, unlocks better ("exciting") real estate, and provides access to better 
product from both larger, global suppliers, and smaller "boutique" suppliers. At the core DSG 
stores, DKS's continued share gains help them secure access to premium, unique product, and 
the upside to SSS has allowed DKS to re-invest in the stores through training and technology. 
For DKS, it really does come down to the blocking and tackling. If the recent rebound in sport 
participation continues, we believe DKS would be the prime beneficiary.
3. Youth Sports Participation Figures Cut Both Ways. The data here for DKS cuts both ways. 
On the one hand, youth sport participation is up YoY across boys and girls, but data is mixed 
and inconsistent over the medium to longer term. While the NSGA suggests high school sport 
participation hit a record high in 2023-2024, and the SFIA reports that participation rates recently 
hit the highest level since 2015, other studies have shown a drop in participation rates, from the 
50% range to the 40% range for boys, between 2013 and 2023, and from 56% to 50% for girls 
from 2017 to 2023. The potential for further retracement in sport participation levels to those 
several years ago is a key bull case argument. We believe bears could argue that participation rates 
are fluctuating but generally biased down over the LT.

The Bull Case… We believe bulls view (1) FL results and synergies coming in "better than feared";
(2) the potential that DKS acquired FL at almost trough levels on potentially trough profitability,
(3) excellent execution on the base business, now more consistently, and (4) potential for further 
retracement in sport participation levels to those several years ago.
The Bear Case… On the other hand, we believe bears are concerned that (1) the FL acquisition 
becomes an albatross given the myriad of issues DKS needs to address and fix, (2) the margin outlook 
becomes less visible following multiple years of growth post-COVID, (3) footwear innovation / 
newness gets pushed out, and (4) cumulative macro headwinds from tariffs pressures discretionary 
spend.
Financial Outlook
FY25: Our FY25 EPS of $12.98 represents EPS -7.6%. We project total sales up 26.8% on comps up 
2.5%. We expect gross margin -180bps to 34.1% and 56bps of SG&A deleverage, resulting in operating 
margins down (235)bps to 8.8%. All estimates are impacted by the mid-year (9/08) acquisition of Foot 
Locker.
FY26: Our FY26E EPS estimate of $15.03 represents EPS +15.8%. We project total sales up 30% on 
comps up 1.7%. We expect gross margin down 69bps to 33.4% and 39bps of SG&A leverage, plus 
synergies of ~$94M, resulting in operating margin contraction of 80bps to 8.0%. All estimates are 
impacted by the 2025 mid-year (9/08/25) acquisition of Foot Locker..
