DKS is scheduled to report 4Q earnings this Thursday (3/12) before the 
market open (typically at 7AM EST) with a conference call to follow at 10AM 
EST.  
Overall, we are maintaining our above the Street 4Q forecasts with +3.5% core 
Dick’s comps vs. +2.1% per Consensus Metrix (guidance implies roughly flat to 
+2%) with FL at -6.5%, essentially in-line with guidance and marginally above 
consensus. Our core DKS EPS forecast is $4.15, above the implied EPS guidance 
of $3.72-4.02, with our FL operating income +9MM vs. guidance for slightly 
negative dollars. The overall "consensus" EPS on both Consensus Metrix and 
Bloomberg appear to be skewed by estimates that perhaps reflect non-GAAP 
impacts (e.g., the 1000-1500 bps of FL merch margin pressure). Building it 
bottoms-up, we believe true consensus 4Q25 EPS is ~$3.50 vs. our $3.60 
(combined company). See Figure 1 for full guidance details. 
Da bulls. Bulls build their point of view based on (1) low core DKS comp bar 
(+2.5%); (2) a durable footwear cycle with DKS now the key retailer for vendors 
(see Figures 6-9) to fuel high heat product through, alongside a stimulated 
consumer (see Figures 10-11) and the World Cup; (3) clean FL inventories that 
create upward bias to the Street's profitability forecasts (get back a good chunk of 
the 500-600 bps of pre-deal FL merch margin degradation overtime) with 
Chairman/Founder Ed Stack's merchandising prowess and strong vendor 
relationships fueling sales optimism against easy compares in 2H when he will 
have influenced the assortment; (4) low guidance bar (i.e., just higher than 2025); 
and (5) risk/reward skewing higher at $200 (many see DKS as a $180-230 
stock). We view the FL deal through the lens of Porters Five Forces as a means to 
improve the negotiating leverage with key vendors, particularly Nike, who we 
believe has acted irrationally in the past (expanding and contracting distribution 
during good/bad times) and has significant structural margin incentives to drive 
sales to its own website/stores. Additionally, the deal does extend DKS customer 
reach to a more basketball/fashion oriented, relatively lower income consumer in 
the US while FL's execution has been poor and creates a large margin recovery 
story.  
Da bears. Bears build their point of view based on decelerating comps against 
tougher comparisons ahead (partly tariff-inflation driven), a core DKS gross 
margin miss (Street 35.6%), and slight risk to the "FL accretive to EPS" guide. On 
sales, DKS faced two years of tough weather comparisons, plus the widespread 
storm in January that hit a large swath of its stores. Snow in February is typically 
bad for discretionary sales as well, which likely indicates a tougher start to the year. 
Historically, a surefire way to de-rate a retail stock, especially a softlines-esque 
name like DKS, is to miss comp and gross margin. Bears believe that DKS 
valuation could break to 6x EV/EBITDA (while bulls see DKS as a structural 
winner that should value in the 7-8x range).

Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation
Investment Thesis
DKS is executing at a high level, has strongly improved its 
relationships with vendors and is benefitting from casualization 
and healthy living trends. On the other hand, we worry about the 
footwear cycle slowing, while any potential softness in the core 
DKS business will make improving FL even more important in 
2026 from an investment perspective.  We think this process will 
take time, especially with its lower income consumer exposure 
in the US and global retail footprint.  Hence, we maintain our 
Neutral rating.  
Valuation
Our Dec 26 price target is $228 and based on ~13.5x our 2027 
EPS estimate, which is in the range of its historical FY1 
valuation, and equivalent to ~7x EV/EBITDA.

Our view. We do worry that the comp bar is low, though surely the pullback in the stock has 
alleviated some of the bearishness (so not as crowded a short as it was $20 higher). That said, 
it was a promotional holiday, and we saw elasticity go up in many goods categories as 2026 
progressed, which concerns us, particularly in the mid-price point footwear category (while 
we also saw brands become more promotional as well; see Figures 13-16 for DKS/FL 
Promos). Looking at the stock, it is trading at a low 7x EV/EBITDA on 2026 consensus (vs 
the 6-8x bull/bear range noted above). This suggests a strong reaction up or down would 
require both sales and gross margin to vary in the same direction relative to expectations.
•
Core DKS by the numbers. For 4Q25, we are now modeling +3.5% comp, 35.7% gross 
margin (up 70 bps YOY), and 10.9% operating margin vs. the Street’s +2.1% comp, 
35.6% gross margin, and 10.8% operating margin. For 2026, we are now modeling +2.7% 
comp, 36.7% gross margin (up 80 bps YOY), and 11.2% operating margin vs. the Street’s 
+2.8% comp, 36.6% gross margin, and 11.2% operating margin per Consensus Metrix 
into-the-print. 
•
FL by the numbers. For 4Q25, we are now modeling -6.5% comp, 25.2% gross margin 
(up 40 bps YOY), and 0.4% operating margin vs. the Street’s -6.7% comp, 20.2% gross 
margin, and -2.6% operating margin. For 2026, we are now modeling -0.4% comp, 27.3% 
gross margin, and 26.1% operating margin vs. the Street’s flat comp, 28.0% gross margin, 
and 2.0% operating margin per Consensus Metrix into-the-print. 
•
Combined company by the numbers. For 4Q25, we are now modeling 32.1% gross 
margin, 7.3% operating margin, and $3.60 EPS vs. the Street’s 30.3% gross margin, 6.1% 
operating margin, and our expectation of $3.50 true consensus EPS (see above). For 2026, 
we are now modeling 33.6% gross margin, 7.8% operating margin, and $14.68 EPS vs. 
the Street’s 33.7% gross margin and 8.2% operating margin.
