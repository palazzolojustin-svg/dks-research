Our Call
We see more good than bad in the 1Q print: FL US is comping positive, the fast-break 
initiative is driving tangible results that give credibility to the BTS positive inflection, and 
margin pressure should alleviate at core DSG through the year.
 
Thoughts on 1Q. The most important takeaway from the print to us was the strength 
in FL US comps and evidence that the Fast Break initiatives are showing sustained 
improvement (comping double-digit) with another 150 to go before BTS—all before the 
new merchandise assortment arrives in the back half. While some may be disappointed in 
flow-through on the strong DSG comp, we view it as 1) transitory given the GM tailwinds 
through the year and 2) not a surprise given the reinvestment in marketing.
 
Mgmt Callback Takes. 1) Expect DSG to lever on SG&A in 2H; 2) DSG margin flow-
through a function of increased investment in marketing; 3) Merch margin to expand 
sequentially, Media Network and Gamechanger acceleration drive GM higher; 4) DSG 
comp performance broad based (not just trading cards); 5) FL Fast Break improvement 
was more weighted towards footwear; and 6) FL Europe earlier in improvement process, 
but initiatives but should accelerate from here.
 
Racking Up Fast Breaks at FL. The turn at FL remains most critical to the DKS story—
with the significant 2H positive inflection in both comp and margin at FL. DKS provided 
some early proof points that support the strategy; 1) a 6.4% FL US comp and 2) fast 
break stores comping double-digits (completed 100 of planned 250), despite a 30% 
SKU reduction. If DKS can drive FL result before new fall merchandise arrives, we have 
increasing confidence that it should improve with better product.
 
Flow-Through At DSG Likely To Remain A Focus. Despite posting a 6% comp at 
core DSG (5.5% ticket, 0.5% transaction), core OM contracted 66bps, with both GM 
contraction driven by freight, fixed cost deleverage (new DC opened), and mix (trading 
cards) and higher SG&A on previously communicated ramped investments ahead of the 
World Cup. 2Q will be the low point for flow-through with more marketing investments, 
but mgmt is confident in sequential improvement through 2H.
 
Reinvesting In The Business. DSG is executing at a high level while reinvesting behind its 
retail growth initiatives (House of Sport, Field House), growing adjacent margin enhancing 
businesses (expanding vertical brands, growing Dick's Media Network, enhancing the 
Game Changer app), and driving traffic with its loyalty program and category adjacencies 
(collectibles, trading cards). We view the headwinds in 1Q as transitory as the company 
prudently manages margin for LT growth.

Investment Thesis
Key themes surrounding the name include:
1. Can DKS Fix Foot Locker? On May 15, DKS announced the acquisition of Foot Locker for $24/
share, or approximately $2.3B. At the time, DKS outlined 5 key tenants behind the proposed 
acquisition of FL; these include: 1) the opportunity to move from a purely domestic to global 
operator, 2) the ability to serve a broader variety of customer sets through multiple concepts, 3)
the ability to strengthen its relationships with key suppliers, 4) a broader omni-channel reach, and
5) potential synergy opportunities of $100-$125mm (mostly in COGS). While mgmt plans to run 
the businesses separately, we would note historical synergies closer to 2%, or $150mm based on 
~$7.5b in FL revs suggesting slight upside optionality. DKS has repeated that the deal would be 
accretive in FY26, with cost synergies in the medium term achieved through procurement and 
direct sourcing efficiencies (COGS). As of 1Q26, DKS remains committed to the $100M-$125M 
of cost synergies, with a portion reflected in its 2026 outlook for the business, while expecting 
improvement in 2H26 driven by the "Fast Break" initiative (essentially re-merchandising, clearing 
out 30% of non-performing SKUs) being rolled out into a larger portion of the FL store fleet. 
Having completed 100 of the planned 250 stores, the company is seeing improving trends, with 
Fast Break stores comping double-digit positive.
2. Executing at a High Level. DKS continues to execute at a very high level (15 straight qtrs of LSD/
MSD SSS as of 1Q26) across all facets of the business: in-store, online (ecomm continues to 
outpace the business), and digital customer interaction through Game Changer and Dicks Media 
Network—all underpinned by a strong product assortment. DKS is also leaning into the success 
of their new store formats (Field House from 26 to 51 and House of Sport from 19 to 35). The 
newer concepts, mostly remodels of existing locations, bring DKS closer to hardcore athletes, 
provides a differentiated experience, unlocks better ("exciting") real estate, and provides access 
to better product from both larger, global suppliers, and smaller "boutique" suppliers. At the core 
DSG stores, DKS's continued share gains help them secure access to premium, unique product, 
and the upside to SSS has allowed DKS to re-invest in the stores through training and technology. 
For DKS, it really does come down to the blocking and tackling. If the recent rebound in sport 
participation continues, we believe DKS would be the prime beneficiary.
3. Youth Sports Participation Figures Cut Both Ways. The data here for DKS cuts both ways. 
On the one hand, youth sport participation is up YoY across boys and girls, but data is mixed 
and inconsistent over the medium to longer term. While the NSGA suggests high school sport 
participation hit a record high in 2023-2024, and the SFIA reports that participation rates recently 
hit the highest level since 2015, other studies have shown a drop in participation rates, from the 
50% range to the 40% range for boys, between 2013 and 2023, and from 56% to 50% for girls 
from 2017 to 2023. The potential for further retracement in sport participation levels to those 
several years ago is a key bull case argument. We believe bears could argue that participation rates 
are fluctuating but generally biased down over the LT.

The Bull Case… We believe bulls view (1) FL results and synergies coming in "better than feared";
(2) the potential that DKS acquired FL at almost trough levels on potentially trough profitability,

newness gets pushed out.
Financial Outlook
FY26: Our FY26E EPS estimate of $14.20 (from $14.08) is driven by stronger gross margin and a 
lower share count, partially offset by lower sales and a higher tax rate. We project core Dick's Sporting 
Goods SSS of 3.3% and Foot Locker SSS of 1.7%. We expect gross margin down 66bps to 33.5% and 
4bps of SG&A leverage, resulting in operating margin contraction of 70bps to 8.1%.
FY27: Our FY27 EPS of $16.50 (from $16.00) is driven by stronger gross margin and a lower share 
count. We project core Dick's Sporting Goods SSS of 2.1% and Foot Locker SSS of 1.4%. We expect 
gross margin +51bps to 34.0% and 25bps of SG&A leverage, resulting in operating margins up 76bps 
to 8.9%.

We are Equal Weight on shares of DKS. While core Dick's Sporting Goods performance and positioning remains strong, we believe the

Target Price Valuation for DKS
Our price target of $220 applies ~13x our FY27 EPS estimate of $16.50, slightly above the historical average ~12x given continued 
momentum in the model, driven by new store formats, offset by risks on the acquisition of Foot Locker.
Risks to Our Price Target and Rating for DKS
Upside risks include: (1) comps move back to +MSD run rate; and (2) merch margins remain sticky; (3) Acquisition of FL and execution of 
synergies drives EPS upside and further expands the TAM opportunity of DKS internationally.
Downside risks (1) comps decelerate and/or turn negative; (2) EBIT margin gains reverse; (3) Acquisition of FL proves to be long-term 
dilutive or takes longer to turn.
