Our Call
While remaining OW, the 2Q print was as bad as it gets. Big picture, legacy footwear 
challenges have deteriorated and all signs point to a much more challenged athletic 
footwear backdrop into 2H (which will weigh on the entire sub-sector).
 
Thoughts on 2Q. DKS signaled that the athletic footwear backdrop is not improving, 
rather deteriorating further. This "hangover" in legacy footwear will weigh on 2H margins 
for both banners (more FL vs. DSG) - as DKS attempts to navigate what has become an 
overly promotional category with a flooded marketplace that lacks core innovation. For 
context of how dramatic today was, following their FY guide down, we now view a 2027 
Bull Case EPS as below where the Bear Case sat prior.
 
Other Shoe Drops Harder. There has clearly been a material change in athletic footwear 
over the Summer against the expectation that things would get better by now. The 
unhealthy marketplace is necessitating aggressive promotions to work through excess 
inventory (with MAP breaks and some retailers cutting well below MAP). This was much
worse than expected (w/ neg laterals to brands, esp NKE) and gives Bears more evidence 
that FL will take longer to turn while flow-through can't be managed.
 
Core DSG "OK". Comp of +4.9% was in line and core GM's expanded (ex refunds), but 
the promo backdrop is necessitating participation to remain competitive and driving 
down the margin outlook for the year. 2H GMs are now planned down (3Q weighted)
as DSG looks to take share - cutting their FY margin plan by ~50bps. While the legacy 
footwear issue is more tethered to FL, DSG is likewise impacted. The good news was DSG
1) maintained comp guide and 2) plans to show expense scale exiting the FY.
 
FL Turn "Kicked" Out. Comps -3.6% were below plan and drives a material 2H profit 
cut. Several factors: Launch calendar was weak, launches that did occur didn't resonate, 
athletic inventories are elevated and EMEA has worsened. FL is taking the pain on 
clearance, while also continuing to invest. FL revs/EBIT were both cut by ~$200M
(implies 100% flow-through on lost sales) - which leads to more concern on margin 
guardrails as they manage the weak category.
 
2H Goes From Inflection Story To Cleanup Story As Numbers Come Down Significantly.
2026 EPS guide of $11.00-$12.00 is a ~20% cut at mid pt, w/ negative comps now 
expected at FL, w/ GM pressure at both banners driven by promos (most pronounced in 
3Q - implying a downbeat B2S view). FL is now expected to lose money, and it is hard to 
have visibility into expansion next yr w/o seeing improvements in the underlying athletic 
footwear category itself. PT to $185 (from $240) on 13.5x $13.70 FY27 EPS.

Investment Thesis
Key themes surrounding the name include:
1) Foot Locker Recovery Can Drive Meaningful Upside. In our view, getting comfortable that FL 
is fixable is the gating item for many investors today, especially given the ongoing weakness in the 
marketplace and ensuing cleanup. In our work, we come away incrementally more positive that 
there is potential upside, and a relatively modest lift can deliver meaningful EPS upside. 1) The 
strategic rationale for buying FL made sense, bringing DSG's leading omni-channel and merchandising 
capabilities and brand relationships to FL addresses FL's biggest weaknesses - this is evidenced by the 
early results at the Fast Break initiatives comping +DD with improving merchandise margin; 2) Nike's 
reduction in wholesale allocation at FL created a nearly ~1,000bps impact to sales, and we estimate 
a nearly ~490bps impact to OM (over half of the deterioration alone) - this issue is clearly reversing;
3) While still difficult to reconcile whether Nike can return to healthy brand growth in NA, especially 
given commentary on deteriorating sell-through trends starting in April of this year, we are more 
comfortable that the trajectory is at least improving from trough levels; and 4) We don't need a home 
run at FL to drive meaningful upside to DKS estimates - and a return of OM to > 7% (still below pre-
COVID levels) has the potential to drive DKS EPS north of $20.00, all else equal. While the math 
of margin recovery at FL still is compelling to us over the long term, the deterioration in the legacy 
athletic footwear market over the summer of 2026 and guide down for another year of negative 
margins pushes out the timeline for this, and will call into question some of the more structural issues 
facing the business today in our view.
2) Core Dick's Sporting Goods Remains an Attractive Business. We still like the core DSG business 
as the established category leader for all things sport. While their success today is rarely a topic of 
debate among investors, we believe the health of the business today is highly durable and built on 
nearly a decade of work. 1) We see a +LSD underlying comp at DSG coupled with the continued 
expansion of House of Sport and Field House relocations and renovations delivering attractive +LSD/
+MSD sales growth with both ticket and traffic; 2) Omni-channel capabilities built from in-housing 
capabilities starting back in 2017 remain unmatched in the space and will be difficult to replicate, 
driving market share while protecting margins; 3) Developed partnership with brands have structurally 
shifted the balance of power in DSG's favor, ensuring it remains the partner of choice for brands while 
they are still in the attractive part of their lifecycle and selling full-price; 4) Store productivity metrics 
are still trending positively, driving stronger occupancy leverage over the long term while enabling 
reinvestment behind SG&A; and 5) Margins that are comfortably in the LDD range as a result of the 
above, with underlying improvements allowing for continued reinvestment to expand its competitive 
moat (Fun Fact: DKS's CapEx guide of ~$1B for the DSG banner is nearly 5x the $200M-$240M 
guided by ASO for this year, over 300bps higher as a % of segment sales).
3) An Abundance of Opportunities Working in Unison. While the multiple initiatives at DSG may 
seem independent of each other, we believe they all have the makings for a very interesting flywheel 
and are more strategically intertwined than the company gets credit for. This includes: 1) a unique 
set of store concepts that each serve a distinct purpose in the overall portfolio (House of Sport, Field 
House, Going, Going, Gone!, and Golf Galaxy), 2) direct access to the customers involved in youth 
sports that spend the most in the category through GameChanger; 3) a growing loyalty program and 
potential for recurring subscription revenue; and 4) DICK's Media Network that is well positioned to 
attract more investment from brands (highly profitable "advertising" revenue). These initiatives are 
still early, but they are all building towards the same goals: driving sales with athletes (customers), 
expanding the competitive moat, and making DSG an indispensable partner in the value chain for 
vendors.

The Bull vs. Bear “Tug of War”
Below we illustrate how we think the buyside is positioning its thesis—detailing the current Bull and 
Bear arguments on DKS shares.
Exhibit 1 - The Bull vs. Bear "Tug of War"
The Bull Case… We believe bulls view (1) the potential that DKS acquired FL at almost trough levels 
on potentially trough profitability, and better merchandising and vendor relationships and synergies 
can drive profitability higher on reset numbers for 2026; (2) consistent execution on the base business 
which is operating at sustainably higher productivity levels, and (3) commanding market share position 
with optionality for profitable growth in Dick's Media Network and Game Changer app supporting 
operating income.
The Bear Case… On the other hand, we believe bears are concerned that (1) the FL acquisition is 
proving to be an albatross given the myriad of issues DKS needs to address and a footwear market 
that has taken a significant turn for the worse, (2) the margin outlook becomes less visible given 
remodels included in comp and high levels of investment holding back leverage, and (3) footwear 
innovation and newness fail to materialize, and the business is now over-exposed to a Nike turnaround.
Financial Outlook
FY26: Our FY26E EPS estimate is $11.70. We project core Dick's Sporting Goods SSS of 3.5% and 
Foot Locker SSS of -1.6%. We expect gross margin down 139bps to 32.8% and 51bps of SG&A 
deleverage, resulting in operating margin contraction of 191bps to 6.9%.
FY27: Our FY27 EPS estimate is $13.70. We project core Dick's Sporting Goods SSS of 4.2% and Foot 
Locker SSS of 1.1%. We expect gross margin +57bps to 33.4% and 19bps of SG&A leverage, resulting 
in operating margins up 75bps to 7.7%.
