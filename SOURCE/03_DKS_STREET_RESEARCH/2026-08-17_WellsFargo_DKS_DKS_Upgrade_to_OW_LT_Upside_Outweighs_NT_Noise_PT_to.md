Our Call
Upgrade to OW. While NT trends appear squishy at best (we model 2Q below Street), at 
current levels we are buyers of the risk/reward and are firm believers in the developing 
multi-year story - led by recovery at FL. PT to $240 on 14-15x 2027 P/E.
 
3 Keys to Our Call. 1) Multi-year margin upside at FL (from 1-2%, we see a return to 7-8% 
margins on the table) - driven by better allocation/merchandising; 2) Execution at DSG 
- which is in a stronger position today and in the early-innings of building a compelling 
fly-wheel (high ROI investment cycle) and 3) DKS as the best way to play Nike's eventual 
product turnaround (where checks point to bullish early reads on Spring 2027). We see 
potential FY28 EPS Power at $20.00+ (vs. Street $18.69).
 
The New Star of US Sport. Core DSG remains the category leader with structurally 
stronger sales productivity, margins and returns. We see +MSD sales growth on +LSD 
underlying comp (ex-relocations and GameChanger) - with House of Sport and Field 
House remodels earning ~30%/~40% 4-Wall ROIC and adding +200-275bps to comp. 
GameChanger, paid loyalty and DICK's Media Network are an under-appreciated flywheel 
in the early innings that cements status as the gatekeeper for athletic brands in the US.
 
Cleaner Play on a Nike Turnaround. For investors that believe in an eventual Nike 
recovery (but can't find comfort with the ongoing resets in their international operations),
we view DKS as the cleanest way to invest in a healthier NA market. While it remains 
a topic of debate, our industry checks indicate that NA demand from retailers is 
incrementally more positive on innovation and product hitting for Spring 2027, and we 
see DKS as having the most concentrated benefit (and at just ~12x P/E).
 
2Q Squishy at Best, Bad at Worst. It's evident NT results should be weak vs. prior plan 
with commentary from lateral reads speaking to deteriorating sell-through trends and a 
highly promotional environment. Our 2Q estimates are below Street - almost entirely due 
to FL (we model comps -3.5% vs. Street +0.4%) driving EPS of $3.72 (below Street's $3.77). 
That said, the go-forward outlook on B2S, FL inflection timing and profit levers in 2H will 
be more critical to story than the actual 2Q results.
 
What Bulls Need to Hear. Factors we deem most relevant to the stock's next move: 1)
B2S trends at FL (inc comp/margin from ~250 FB doors); 2) DSG trajectory post WC 
(particularly footwear, traffic vs. ticket), and flow-through against investments and 3) 2H 
profit levers that can be pulled if B2S demand falls short (can they hold "E"?). If DKS can 
hold earnings through slowing trends (and keep inflection hopes alive albeit pushed out), 
Bulls can re-engage with the story at depressed multiples.
 
PT of $220 ($200 prior), based on ~13x FY27EPS.

We Are More Positive on DKS. We Upgrade shares of DKS to Overweight. The three main reasons 
we are positive on DKS: 1) FL can drive meaningful upside to estimates as DKS is bringing highly 
complementary operational and merchandising capabilities that directly address the historic 
weaknesses at the FL business, re-establishing healthier brand relationships and allocations that have 
driven at least half of the earnings deterioration over the last 4 years, which we think should clear 
relatively low expectations for margin recovery and drive meaningful EPS upside; 2) The core DICK's 
Sporting Goods business remains attractive as the category leader, delivering +LSD/+MSD growth 
with a highly defensible moat in omni-channel capabilities, merchandising and partnerships with 
brands that have driven sustainable improvements in store productivity, margins, and returns; and 3)
The more work we have done on the multiple initiatives at DKS (expanding the fleet of stores with 
unique concepts, the expansion into paid loyalty, the GameChanger app, and DICK's Media Network), 
the more compelled we are that these are working together in driving value and engagement with the 
highest-value customers, breaking apart from competition, and further cementing its position as the 
partner of choice in the US for sporting goods and athletic brands.
There are considerable risks to this view, and the story has no doubt become increasingly complex 
to analyze - but therein lies the opportunity. We believe the most critical points of contention are: 1)
Continued skepticism on FL with both structural concerns on the need to rationalize the store base 
and pressure on lower-income consumers, as well as near-term concerns on the durability of Fast 
Break initiatives into 2H26, especially as Nike speaks to deteriorating sell-through at wholesale and 
ongoing weakness in lifestyle athletic footwear; 2) Concerns on the core DSG business which has 
become increasingly difficult to dissect as the strategy evolved, reporting methodologies changed, and 
investments into the unproven initiatives hold back earnings flow-through on otherwise strong sales 
growth; and 3) Risks to the combined entity given a history of failed M&A in scaled US retail, added 
complexity from incorporating an international business, and a much higher concentration to footwear 
as a category and to Nike.
While the stock has recently retrenched on concerns that the overall business is slowing (especially 
footwear) following the World Cup in the US, we believe the pullback presents a more compelling 
opportunity for investors with longer-duration that want exposure to the NA athletic market without 
being fully exposed to the rise and fall of individual brands. Our PT of $240 is based on 14.5x applied to 
our CY27 EPS estimate of $16.50.
Top Three Reasons We Are Positive DKS
1) Foot Locker Recovery Can Drive Meaningful Upside. In our view, getting comfortable that FL 
is fixable is the gating item for many investors today. In our work, we come away incrementally 
more positive that there is potential upside, and a relatively modest lift can deliver meaningful EPS 
upside. 1) The strategic rationale for buying FL made sense, bringing DSG's leading omni-channel and 
merchandising capabilities and brand relationships to FL addresses FL's biggest weaknesses - this is 
evidenced by the early results at the Fast Break initiatives comping +DD with improving merchandise 
margin; 2) Nike's reduction in wholesale allocation at FL created a nearly ~1,000bps impact to sales, 
and we estimate a nearly ~490bps impact to OM (over half of the deterioration alone) - this issue is 
clearly reversing; 3) While still difficult to reconcile whether Nike can return to healthy brand growth 
in NA, especially given commentary on deteriorating sell-through trends starting in April of this year, 
we are more comfortable that the trajectory is at least improving from trough levels; and 4) We don't 
need a home run at FL to drive meaningful upside to DKS estimates - and a return of OM to > 7% (still 
below pre-COVID levels) has the potential to drive DKS EPS north of $20.00 (Street $18.69) in 
FY28, all else equal.
2) Core Dick's Sporting Goods Remains an Attractive Business. We still like the core DSG business 
as the established category leader for all things sport. While their success today is rarely a topic of 
debate among investors, we believe the health of the business today is highly durable and built on 
nearly a decade of work. 1) We see a +LSD underlying comp at DSG coupled with the continued 
expansion of House of Sport and Field House relocations and renovations delivering attractive +LSD/
+MSD sales growth with both ticket and traffic; 2) Omni-channel capabilities built from in-housing 
capabilities starting back in 2017 remain unmatched in the space and will be difficult to replicate, 
driving market share while protecting margins; 3) Developed partnership with brands has structurally 
shifted the balance of power in DSG's favor, ensuring it remains the partner of choice for brands 
while they are still in the attractive part of their lifecycle and selling full-price; 4) Store productivity 
metrics are still trending positively, driving stronger occupancy leverage over the long-term while 
enabling reinvestment behind SG&A; and 5) Margins that are comfortably in the 11%-12% range as a

Foot Locker Recovery Can Drive Meaningful Upside
In May 2025, DKS announced its acquisition of FL with expectations to be accretive to EPS in the first 
full FY post-close (FY26) and deliver $100-$125M of cost synergies in the medium-term, and has 
very quickly moved to clear out unproductive inventory and make investments into re-assorted stores 
that are delivering positive results in a relatively quick time. The recovery at FL is a fairly binary event - 
either the problems with FL were mostly attributable to poor merchandising and vendor relationships, 
in which case it is now in capable hands that can quickly turn it around, or the problem is structural, in 
which case this might be a multi-year fix for DKS that will both cost more, drag on overall results and 
distract from the core DSG opportunity.
Our view is the truth will wind up somewhere in-between, but to us, we see it as more likely 
that FL improves meaningfully off a multi-decade low level of earnings - which is the right bet 
at current levels (just ~12x P/E today). Nike is clearly the big point of contention for investors 
today, as it historically has been a significant driver of both growth and margin to the FL business, 
and while we believe the allocation problem that started in 2022-2023 is largely resolved (which 
independently should be a lift to the FL business), the current trajectory of the brand with negative 
DTC and deteriorating sell-through trends remains a big question mark today. That said, we see the 
range of potential outcomes as skewed to the upside from here, as current estimates only embed FL 
returning to approximately half its historic margin levels.

representing a $2.5B EV (or ~6.4x trailing EV/EBITDA). At the time of the acquisition, DKS discussed 
intentions for FL to be accretive to EPS in the first full fiscal year post-close (FY26) and to deliver 
$100M-$125M of cost synergies in the medium term. The rationale at the time included: 1) create

A Historical Analysis of Nike's Importance to Foot Locker. Aside from the obvious that Nike 
represents over half of FL's business, we would highlight its importance from a historical perspective. 
Over the last almost two decades, Nike went from 56% of the business in 2007 to 75% of the business 
in 2020. Following 2020, Nike declined to 59% in 2024 (last disclosed), and we estimate 57% in 2025. 
At the same time, FL expanded its penetration with the next 4 brands from 16% in 2020 to 26% 
in 2024, and we estimate 28% in 2025 (Exhibit 3). Looking at FL comp sales growth, the impact of 
Nike became increasingly evident as growth between the two was highly related (Exhibit 4). In the 
19 years since 2007, FL has comped positive 11 years, and only 3 of those years were they able to 
do so while Nike was declining (Exhibit 5). Additionally, FL's GM performance was highly related to 
Nike penetration (Exhibit 6), with expansion again positively related to Nike growth (driven by both 
merchandise margin and occupancy leverage on sales). All of this is to say - if Nike returns to growth 
at Foot Locker (whether simply by increasing allocation or organic growth), we believe it will more 
likely than not be a positive for underlying comp sales as well as the ability to improve margin.

What Happened Over the Last 5 Years? There are two distinct factors that contributed to FL 
struggles with Nike: 1) The initial decision by Nike to reduce allocation to FL (2021-2023) and 2)
The subsequent deterioration of Nike's business in North America necessitating a massive reset of 
supply and reversion back to wholesale (2024-Today). The first factor is clearly reversed or at least 
not a headwind today, as Nike has committed to a more holistic view of the marketplace including its 
partners, though we doubt the penetration at FL drifts much higher than 60% due to diversification 
efforts (it isn't a good thing to be too dependent on one brand). The second factor leads to the big two 
questions today (which we address below in the key risks to DKS) - 1) can Nike return to generating 
growth in NA, and 2) if Nike can't, then can FL still work by growing with other brands through better 
presentation and merchandising (where DKS management excels).
The High Heat Shoe Dropped. On FL's 4Q21 earnings call February 22, 2022, FL announced that 
Nike's accelerated strategic shift to DTC would result in Nike's mix of sales declining by 10 percentage 
points (from 70% to 60%), which equated to approximately $900M of sales, beginning in the 4Q22. 
Fast-forward to the end of 2023, which had a full year of the reduced allocation, and Nike's penetration 
at FL was still 65%, representing a sales decline of approximately $800M from 2021. This mirrored 
the decline in the overall FL business, which also declined by approximately $800M. Our industry 
checks indicate that this was primarily driven by reducing the allocation of high-heat product in 
half, partially offset by infilling other Nike product.
Why Was High-Heat Important? High-heat products were a critical component of Foot Locker's 
business. 1) they drove sales by a) selling at a higher price point and b) driving traffic both online and 
in-store; 2) they had a higher merchandise margin business that a) was rarely marked-down and b) was 
almost always marked for Return-To-Vendor (RTV) when inventory was leftover, and 3) the sales drove 
leverage on both occupancy and SG&A, and the loss led to significant deterioration of both.
Our checks within the industry further point to the run-off impacts of Nike dislodging FL from the 
distribution chain - the loss of "high-heat" or "launch/atomic" product. Before the change in strategies 
at Nike, FL was typically allocated ~40% of "high heat product" (if Nike was selling 1 million pairs, FL 
would get 400k), and this was cut in half to ~20%. An even more exclusive part of this was "launch" 
or "atomic", which FL had an approximate 7-8% allocation from Nike. The high-heat product would 
generally sell through at full price, and if marked-down, it would typically be a LSD amount (vs. the rest

We are comfortable with the trajectory of the underlying DSG business, with attractive growth and 
market share gains, a highly defensible moat with omni-channel and brand relationships, an attractive,

for continued reinvestment and market share opportunities.

Going, Going, Gone! Keeps the Core Clean. The Going, Going, Gone! (GGG) concept is a strategically

throughout the fleet by eliminating the clearance racks that used to be prevalent throughout the core 
stores. Additionally, DKS was able to use GGG to assist in clearing FL inventory (though investors will

both footwear and Nike. While these are all real risks, we see offsetting factors to each: 1) DKS bought

power between retailer and brand further in DKS's favor, and Nike clearly needs DKS's help today more

Threading the Needle on M&A
The overall DKS story is not straightforward, and for the DKS stock to work the company will need 
to deliver on both FL and DSG. If FL deteriorates, there is concern that the business is structurally 
flawed, and the acquisition looks like an albatross on what was otherwise an attractive business. If DSG 
deteriorates, there is additional worry that the business will not be able to support the growth and 
investments necessary to turn FL. Buying DKS requires believing that both of these banners can 
work at the same time.
The primary concerns for the combined entity are: 1) scaled M&A in US retail rarely works and may 
become a distraction for the DKS management team; 2) the international business adds complexity; 
and 3) the acquisition doubles down and increases exposure to both footwear and Nike.
Skepticism on M&A. Scaled M&A in retail has a very spotty track record, with synergies rarely 
materializing as planned, particularly when a share gainer acquires its share donor. Investors will point 
to Dollar Tree and Family Dollar as a key example. We don't think the comparison is entirely fair, as
1) the sporting goods category itself is healthier than the discount consumables category, 2) the 
market share in sporting goods is more consolidated than the consumables market, and not as easily 
substitutable in mass channels, and 3) the price paid for the business was arguably on trough level of 
FL earnings (DLTR acquired Family Dollar for nearly $9B at ~11x EV/EBITDA vs. DKS acquiring FL for 
$2.5B at ~6x EV/EBITDA).
International Business Brings Complexity. FL also brings international risk, ~30% of last reported 
sales in 2024 came from international markets. There are certainly risks that this acquisition brings 
increased complexity in running a global supply chain. However, there are also opportunities as this 
may help DKS lay the groundwork for future international expansion of DSG, while also expanding 
capabilities to better partner with its vendor base globally. UK-based JD Sports has made significant 
inroads in apparel/footwear in the US organically and through the acquisition of Hibbett, and 
strategically this allows DKS to also compete globally.
Overexposure to Footwear and Nike. While DSG has shifted towards softlines over the last decade, 
the acquisition of FL massively accelerates their exposure to footwear and Nike. We estimate that 
the combined consolidated DKS business is ~50% footwear (from ~30% at DSG alone), with Nike 
now representing ~37% of merchandise sales (from 25% at DSG alone). Risks arise from over-
concentration with Nike (clearly evidenced from what happened at FL) as the direction of the brand 
can dictate overall performance at the retailer to a greater degree. Conversely, the combination 
increases negotiating power with brands - as evidenced by the $100-$125M of procurement 
synergies that are likely to benefit DSG. All-in, we think the acquisition actually tilts the balance of 
power between retailer and brand further in DKS's favor, and Nike clearly needs DKS's help today more 
than ever in building and maintaining a healthy US marketplace, which can result in the absolute best 
allocation of product and stronger vendor allowances.

1. 1) Foot Locker Recovery Can Drive Meaningful Upside. In our view, getting comfortable that FL 
is fixable is the gating item for many investors today. In our work, we come away incrementally

from trough levels; and 4) We don't need a home run at FL to drive meaningful upside to DKS

DKS EPS north of $20.00 (Street $18.69) in FY28, all else equal.
