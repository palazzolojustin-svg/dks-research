PM’s take: Foot Locker performance drives higher uncertainty, as expected.  
Cleaning up the inventory is a key first step, especially with accretion being 
mostly fundamentally driven.  DKS results were marked by strong sales 
performance in the core (+5.7%), a rare acceleration in our coverage vs. 2Q, but 
the second straight quarter of lackluster flowthrough (core DKS op profit down 
modestly again), with FL touching all the (negative) bases (comp miss, significant 
gross margin compression, and massive clearance expected in 4Q).  In addition, the 
expected deal accretion in 2026 (vs. the updated 2025 core DKS EPS guide of 
$14.25-14.55) is predicated largely on fundamental improvement in the FL 
business, vs. our expectation of a substantial buyback to offset the 10MM share 
deal dilution, led by clean inventory and better merchandising (with the full 
assortment touched by back to school 2026 = 2H weighted).  Mgmt resoundingly 
affirmed their confidence in the FL turnaround and that the brands are fully on 
board (NKE was 59% of FL merchandise in 2024) with Chairman/“founder” Ed 
Stack’s relationships the key “what you need to believe in” factor.  Cleaning up the 
inventory should represent a strong first step in the story, though we remain 
concerned that the footwear cycle is slowing and the portfolio effect of the global 
store brand footprint raises the bar for the stock.
Key takeaways from the call and our follow-up with mgmt:
•
(1) Turnaround all about better product and clean inventory; still 
expecting deal accretive in FY26 but no big ASR. DKS closed the FL 
acquisition on September 8th, with 3Q results reflecting the eight weeks 
post-acquisition. Mgmt reiterated that FL strayed away from “Retail 101” 
fundamentals, having not reacted quickly enough when its largest vendor 
(NKE, 59% of 2024 FL merchandise vs. 71% in 2019) pivoted to a DTC 
model, which left FL with too much stale inventory. Despite a lower than 
expected FL comp (-4.7% vs. -3%e), DKS continues to expect the deal to 
be accretive to DKS 2026 EPS (relative to the $14.25-14.55 2025 guidance 
base) with the turnaround largely built on better product and cleaner 
inventories (led by upgraded mgmt, including hiring the former CEO of 
Aldi to run international), along with a relatively modest lift from closing 
underperforming stores and $100-125MM synergies overtime (which 
mgmt sees as “very” achievable).  We had modeled a 4.5MM share repo in 
4Q25 to offset a large portion of the ~10MM share deal dilution, but 
management believes it will be far more fundamentally driven (with repo 
opportunistic).  We also note the tax rate is expected to be a modest 
headwind to accretion in 2026.  Longer-term, mgmt cited DKS pre-COVID 
op margin of 9-10% as the goal (vs. 2.5% in 2024).  Importantly, 2026 BTS 
is expected to be the turning point for the FL business (noting DKS will have 
full control of inventory buys in 3Q26). See our full FL deep dive HERE.

Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation
Investment Thesis
DKS is executing at a high level, has strongly improved its 
relationships with vendors and is benefitting from casualization 
and healthy living trends. On the other hand, we worry about the 
footwear cycle slowing, while any potential softness in the core 
DKS business will make improving FL even more important in 
2026 from an investment perspective.  We think this process will 
take time, especially with its lower income consumer exposure 
in the US and global retail footprint.  Hence, we maintain our 
Neutral rating.  
Valuation
Our Dec 26 price target is $228 and based on ~13x our 2027 EPS 
estimate, which is in the range of its historical FY1 valuation, 
and equivalent to ~7x EV/EBITDA.

(3) Core DKS comp beats on assortment with strength expected in the holidays; 
FL comp misses but BTS excluded from results. Core DKS comp came in at +5.7%, 
ahead of our Street-high +5.0%, the Street’s +3.4%, and +5-5.5% buyside 
expectations, per our work, driven by ticket +4.4% (modest price increases from 
tariffs, per mgmt) and traffic +1.3% with growth across all key categories (in 
particular golf where both equipment and apparel outperformed). DKS spoke to 
ongoing differentiation in its product assortment, which led to a robust BTS and 
strong positioning for the upcoming holiday season as well. Notably, the company is 
expanding into trading cards and collectibles, which has been met with favorable 
customer response thus far. Despite the strength in core DKS, FL comp came in at 
-4.7% vs. our -3% and buyside expectations down 3-4%, per our work, with North 
America comps -2.6% and International -10.2%. We note that the acquisition closed 
on September 8th, which in turn excludes a “profitable BTS season” from results. We 
are now modeling 4Q, FY25, and FY26 comps of +3.5%/+4.6%/+2.7% for core DKS 
and -6.5%/-4.1%/-0.4% for FL. 
•
(4) GM expansion led by core DKS offset by FL markdowns. 3Q core DKS gross 
margin expanded ~30 bps YOY, led by ongoing merch margin expansion (+5 bps 
including the net effect of mix, similar promos YOY, advertising, and Game Changer), 
partially offset by tariffs and unfavorable business mix (higher penetration of 
licensing business). On a consolidated basis, 3Q gross margin was down ~260 bps 
YOY, with the decline entirely due to FL (-520 bps YOY) as the company began 
marking down inventory in late 3Q. Looking ahead, DKS expects gross margin 
expansion in the core DKS business for the full year and in 4Q with 2H gross margin 
expansion higher than 1H on the alternate profit pools, a similar promotional 
backdrop in 2H YOY, and a 30-bp storm donation lap in 4Q (we are modeling core 
DKS GM up 60 bps YOY in 4Q).We are now modeling combined company 2025/2026 
GM of 34.0%/33.4%, with core DKS 36.3%/36.7% and FL 25.0%/26.2%, 
respectively.
•

the business over the long term. On a consolidated basis, SG&A as a % of sales

DKS SG&A to deleverage in FY25 on continued investments, essentially offsetting 
gross margin expansion and resulting in operating margin of 11.1% (+10 bps YOY).
