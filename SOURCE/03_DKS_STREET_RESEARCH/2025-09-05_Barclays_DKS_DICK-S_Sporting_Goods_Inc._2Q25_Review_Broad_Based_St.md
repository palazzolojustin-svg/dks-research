2Q25 Review: Broad-Based
Strength; Core DKS Continues to
Capture Share
DKS 2Q25 results beat on sales, GM, SG&A, and EPS. We are
encouraged by the +5.0% comp growth (consensus
+3.1%) driven by both average ticket +4.1% and transactions
+0.9%, reaffirming our view of DKS's dominant positioning
within its space.
Broad-Based Strength; Core DKS Continues to Capture Share. 2Q25 results beat across the
board, with EPS of $4.38 ahead of consensus of $4.30. Comparable sales growth of +5.0%
marked DKS's sixth  straight quarter of healthy mid-single-digit comp growth as the company
continues to capture market share. Management noted growth was balanced across all key
categories, being fueled by ticket growth +4.1% and transaction growth +0.9%. EPS guidance for
FY25 was increased to $13.90 - $14.50 from prior of $13.80 - $14.40 due to 2Q25 upside and
better visibility on 2H25 results. Some key offsets pressured guidance, including higher
projected tax rate which weighed on the EPS guide by $0.19, along with a diminished view of
gross margin expansion as the company pulled its language calling for "~75bps of GM
expansion", now seeing just "expansion." Going forward DKS intends to invest in its core
business from a position of strength, targeting higher investments in digital, stores, and
marketing. With the FL merger expected to close September 8th, we highlight our thoughts on
the combined DKS-FL entity which we believe will benefit from: 1) strengthened relationships
and bargaining power with key brand partners for the combined entity, 2) complementary real
estate portfolios between DKS's large-format and suburban-skewing stores vs. FL's smaller-
format and urban-center stores, 3) outlined cost synergies of $100mn to $125mn, with EPS
accretion expected in FY26, a view which was reiterated on the 2Q25 call, and 4) a foothold into
international markets. Following the earnings release on 8/28/25, DKS shares closed -4.8%
(SPX +0.3%).
Tariff Exposure Update. DKS noted minimal tariff impacts in 2Q25 with some impacts expected
in 2H25 incorporated into guidance. The company has already observed price increases being
pushed through by brand partners, and has not seen a corresponding demand slowdown on
these items. Recall, DKS's vertical brands (aka "direct imports") were 13% of revenue in
FY24. DKS does not disclose the precise percentages for manufacturing mix by country for this
13%, but indicated that exposure is negligible from Mexico and Canada, and that the company
has already significantly diversified away from China in recent years. The remaining ~87% of
revenue is exposed to tariffs indirectly through pass-through costs.

Quarter in review. Adj. EPS of $4.38 beat consensus of $4.30, driven by higher sales of $3.65bn
(consensus $3.61bn) as comp sales grew +5.0% (consensus +3.1%) driven by an increases in
average ticket (+4.1%) and transactions (+0.9%). SG&A rate was 24.0% (consensus of 24.3%) due
to the comp sales beat, as investments in digital, stores, and marketing meaningfully increased
and drove deleverage y/y. GM of 37.1% was slightly ahead (consensus of 37.0%) due to slight
merch margin expansion. FY25 guidance was increased on sales and EPS, with management
now seeing adj. EPS in the range of $13.90 - $14.50 (from prior $13.80 - $14.40 and vs. cons.
$14.36); net sales in the range of $13.75bn - $13.95bn (from prior $13.6bn - $13.9bn and vs. cons
$13.93bn); comp sales in the range of +2.0% to +3.5% (from prior +1% to +3% and vs. cons.
+2.9%); and OM of 11.1% at the mid-point or +10bps at the high end (unchanged from prior and
vs. cons. 11.0%) consisting of: gross margin to expand (from prior of expand +75bps at the mid-
point and vs. cons. +60bps) and SG&A to deleverage offsetting GM expansion, as the company
continues to invest in digital, stores, and marketing.
Inventory. Based on our proprietary inventory analysis, DKS has posted five consecutive
quarters of negative sales-to-inventory growth. In FY2Q25 the Inventory Management Spread
("IM Spread") was (433) basis points and improved from (919) basis points in the prior quarter.
DKS's Gross Margin Return on Inventory ("GMROI") has worsened for four consecutive quarters,
and its Operating Margin Return on Inventory ("OMROI") has worsened for four consecutive
quarters.
Adjusting estimates and rolling out PT to $246 (from $232). We increase our FY25 adj. EPS
estimate to $14.45 (from $14.42) due to the 2Q25 beat. We reiterate our FY26 and FY27 adj. EPS
estimates of $15.49 (unchanged) and $16.41 (unchanged), respectively, on slightly reduced GM
estimates as we layer in higher tariff rates and note the implied reduction to the GM guidance.
This is offset by our faster sales growth assumptions. Our PT of $246 (from $232) is based on an
15x multiple (unchanged) applied to our FY27 adj. EPS estimate of $16.41 (from FY26 adj. EPS
estimate of $15.49). We roll forward our PT year to CY27 from CY26. Our valuation multiple is ~2x
above the current consensus CY27E P/E of ~13.1x as we see the gap widening on category
dominance and future impacts of new initiatives.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: DKS in "HUNT" Mode
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.

Tariff Exposure Update. DKS noted minimal tariff impacts in 2Q25 with some impacts expected
in 2H25 incorporated into guidance. The company has already observed price increases being
pushed through by brand partners, and has not seen a corresponding demand slowdown on
these items.
Prior tariff commentary. DKS's vertical brands (aka "direct imports") were 13% of revenue in
FY24. DKS does not disclose the precise percentages for manufacturing mix by country for this
13%, but management indicated that exposure is negligible from Mexico and Canada, and that
the company has already significantly diversified away from China in recent years. The
remaining ~87% of revenue is exposed to tariffs indirectly through pass-through costs, but we
highlight 1) DKS management said that many of its suppliers have already diversified their
supply chains and 2) DKS is well positioned to push back on pass-through cost increases due to
its status as a choice retailer giving it improved bargaining power with both emerging and
existing branded partners.
Barclays Tariff "QuikCalc" Model assumptions. We have updated our Tariff "QuikCalc" model
analysis, which now assumes global tariff assumptions effective 8/7/25. Our prior tariff analysis
had assumed 30% China and 10% Rest of World (excluding Mexico and Canada). Note our
estimate of Tariff on Goods Imported to the US is a gross unmitigated amount for a full year, and
we calculate the price increase necessary to mitigate full tariff cost (absent other mitigation
strategies). Our updated tariff calculator under 8/7/25 effective rates, suggests that for DKS,
total average unit cost will increase by 15.8% with pass-through to the U.S. market only
increasing prices by 6.9% (see Figure 8).
