3Q25 Review: Core DKS
Outperforms as FL Resets
Results for the core DICK'S business beat on sales and EPS,
fueled by +5.7% comp growth (consensus +3.5%). The strong
start to holiday has continued into 4Q25, albeit assuming a
slight q/q moderation, and we note core DICK'S comp
guidance was increased for FY25.
Core DKS Outperforms as FL Resets. In its first earnings report as a combined entity, DKS 3Q25
results highlighted the performance divergence between the DICK'S core business and the Foot
Locker banner. Core DICK'S results were solid, beating on sales and EPS driven by +5.7% comp
growth (consensus +3.5%) fueled by both average ticket +4.4% and transactions +1.3%. Foot
Locker, meanwhile, posted a -4.7% comp sales decline, and its 4Q25 guidance calls for sharper
headwinds ahead due to 1) comp sales down mid- to high-single digits and 2) gross margin to be
down between 1,000bps to 1,500bps. While the headwinds impacting 4Q25 are outsized, we see
the actions as necessary pain-points which will enable the company to quickly clear
unproductive inventory via markdowns and the jobber-channel, with the ultimate aim of being
clean for 2026. While this will likely push promotions to deeper levels in the near-term, we
continue to have a positive long-term view on better promotional levels across the total
sporting goods landscape post-merger, as we expect Foot Locker to be more disciplined under
the DKS umbrella. We reiterate our Overweight rating on DKS and further highlight our thoughts
on the combined DKS-FL entity which we believe will benefit from: 1) improved bargaining
power with emerging and existing branded partners; 2) secular trends in health, wellness, and
expanding athletics across all ages and demographics; and 3) new experiential formats
including House of Sport and Golf Galaxy, enabling DKS to own the category. Following the
earnings release BMO on 11/25/25, DKS shares closed +0.2% (SPX +0.9%).
Tariff Exposure Update. During the quarter, DKS noted that it effectively absorbed tariff costs
within its merch margin,  delivering +5bps of merchandise margin expansion despite tariffs and
a slightly unfavorable product mix. This performance is consistent with prior management
commentary that tariff impacts in 2H25 would be modest. DKS’s updated 3Q25 guidance fully
reflects all tariffs currently in effect, and management reiterated confidence in its ability to
navigate the evolving macroeconomic environment.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: DKS in "HUNT" Mode
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.

Tariff Exposure Update. During the quarter, DKS noted that it effectively absorbed tariff costs
within its merch margin, still managing to deliver +5bps of merchandise margin expansion
despite tariffs and a slightly unfavorable product mix. This performance is consistent with prior
management commentary that tariff impacts in 2H25 would be modest. DKS’s updated 3Q25
guidance fully reflects all tariffs currently in effect, and management reiterated confidence in its
ability to navigate the evolving macroeconomic environment.
Prior tariff commentary. DKS noted minimal tariff impacts in 2Q25 with some impacts
expected in 2H25 incorporated into guidance. The company has already observed price
increases being pushed through by brand partners, and has not seen a corresponding demand
slowdown on these items. Recall, DKS's vertical brands (aka "direct imports") were 13% of
revenue in FY24. DKS does not disclose the precise percentages for manufacturing mix by
country for this 13%, but indicated that exposure is negligible from Mexico and Canada, and
that the company has already significantly diversified away from China in recent years. The
remaining ~87% of revenue is exposed to tariffs indirectly through pass-through costs.
Barclays Tariff "QuikCalc" Model assumptions. We have updated our Tariff "QuikCalc" model
analysis, which now assumes global tariff assumptions effective 8/7/25. Our prior tariff analysis
had assumed 30% China and 10% Rest of World (excluding Mexico and Canada). Note our
estimate of Tariff on Goods Imported to the US is a gross unmitigated amount for a full year, and
we calculate the price increase necessary to mitigate full tariff cost (absent other mitigation
strategies). Our updated tariff calculator under 8/7/25 effective rates, suggests that for DKS,
total average unit cost will increase by 15.8% with pass-through to the U.S. market only
increasing prices by 6.9% (see Figure 8).
