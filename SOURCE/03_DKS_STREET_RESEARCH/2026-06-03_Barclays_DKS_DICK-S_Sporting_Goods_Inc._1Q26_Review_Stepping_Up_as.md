Quarter in review. Consolidated DKS adj. EPS of $2.90 beat consensus of $2.83, driven by
higher sales. Total revenue of $5.16bn came in above consensus of $5.05bn. Comp sales
grew +6.0% at the DICK'S business, driven by increases in average ticket (+5.5%) and
transactions (+0.5%). Meanwhile, the Foot Locker business accelerated, posting FL's first
positive comp since 4Q24 with a +0.6% comp sales growth and notably US FL +6.4%. Overall,
proforma comp sales increased +4.1%. GM of 33.4% matched consensus, down -328bps y/y
driven by mix impact from the Foot Locker business. OpEx rate of 26.1% of sales also matched
consensus. FY26 guidance was updated for the consolidated entity, with management seeing
adj. EPS in the range of $13.50 to $14.50 (unchanged and vs. cons. of $14.48); net sales in the
range of $22.1bn to $22.4bn (unchanged and vs. cons. of $22.3bn); comp sales at the core
DICK'S business in the range of +2.5% to +4.0% (from prior +2.0% to +4.0%), and comp sales at
the Foot Locker business of +1.5% to +3.0% (from prior +1.0% to +3.0%); and EBIT of $1.71bn to
$1.83bn (from prior $1.68bn to $1.81bn and vs. cons. of $1.77bn).
Inventory. (Until the anniversary of the FL acquisition, our inventory metrics only include core
DICK'S Business and back-out Foot Locker Business). Based on our proprietary inventory analysis,
DKS has posted three consecutive quarters of positive sales-to-inventory growth. In FY1Q26 the
Inventory Management Spread ("IM Spread") was 377 basis points and improved from 228 basis
points in the prior quarter. DKS's Gross Margin Return on Inventory ("GMROI") has improved for
three consecutive quarters, and its Operating Margin Return on Inventory ("OMROI") has
worsened for one quarter.
Raising estimates and PT to $280 (from $264). Our FY26, FY27, and FY28 adj. EPS estimates
are $14.48, $16.48, $18.03 (unchanged from prior), respectively, as we lower our GM
assumptions offset by better SG&A leverage. Our new PT of $280 (from $264) is based on 17x
(from 16x) our FY27E adj. EPS of $16.48 (unchanged). We raise our multiple 1 turn, above the
Street NTM multiple of 14.6x, due to increased confidence in the Foot Locker turnaround and
ongoing strength at core Dick's banner.

Proprietary PRISM Curve: DKS in the "HUNT" Phase
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
The HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.
