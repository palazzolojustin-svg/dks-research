Style Exposure
PM’s take: short the inventory build, own the clean up; we’ve entered stage 1. 
The imbalances that get created against discretionary/highly seasonal retailers when 
large brands lack innovation and rush to promote to get/keep channel inventories 
clean can be substantial, especially when the retailer is highly dependent on one brand 
(a la FL). Indeed, DKS comments rhymed with 2Q17 when a more extreme dynamic 
was set in motion (perhaps by the same player) with DKS merch margin down ~200 
bps in 3Q17 and ~100 bps in 4Q17, on our math. DKS assortment expansion and brand 
diversification since keeps it relatively resilient this cycle (est. 90 bps of promo 
pressure in 3Q), but this significantly delays the FL margin story with our 2027 FL op 
profit cut by more than half to $103MMe. Bigger picture, DKS remains bullish on its 
core business and the broader footwear category with legacy silhouettes the headwind 
(e.g., Nike classics and Jordan where newness is lacking, along with aspects of Adidas 
and New Balance). 
Tactically, being short an inventory overhang is typically fruitful, even if you are 
a bit late, up until the point that the channel clears and the headwind becomes 
the easy lap next year (especially if sales trends improve). Mgmt is planning for the 
financial impact of promotions to be higher in 3Q, but isn’t expecting the backdrop to 
fully resolve before we turn to 2027 (with DKS’ prudently assuming a more 
promotional backdrop in 4Q). The near-term caveat to the bear case is sales trends 
(and the data) should improve as mgmt acknowledged that the back-to-school shift is 
cosmetically hurting sales, but is expected to now turn, with the self-described upside 
case to its guide the strong core DKS two year stack in 2Q. DKS also noted a better 
footwear release calendar in 2H. 
Said succintly, the stock is oversold, especially as the market fearlessly pushes the 
stock into the trough valuation range of 5-6x EV/EBITDA on our 2026 forecasts 
(on the back of the inventory overhang and burden of proof until we see 
resolution). That said, we remain optimistic that the stock experiences some recovery 
as we move through the BTS shift, with the next significant pivot point 3Q earnings 
when we get a full picture of how quickly the channel cleans up.  Mark your calendar 
for stage 2.  DKS could ultimately set up for a TGT-like move in 2027. 
Key takeaways from the call and our follow-up with management: 
•
(1) Guidance summary; FL turnaround pushed back a year. For core 
Dick’s, mgmt reiterated +2.5-4.0% SSS outlook on continued confidence in 
HoS, GameChanger, and newness/innovation but lowered its op margin guide 
to 10.6-10.9% due to increased promotional pressures and higher fuel/
healthcare costs. For Foot Locker, mgmt now expects -2% to flat SSS on 
continued pressures in the broader footwear market and its EMEA business,

so much other costs). On Foot Locker, our updated FY27 op profit estimate is 
now in-line with the lower end of its prior FY26 guide. See Figure 2 for full 
guidance details, prior guide, and Street compares.

Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation
Investment Thesis
With DKS being the only national sporting goods retailer, 
alongside its emphasis on better/best products, we believe the 
brands need DKS, which drives better product allocations, and 
the FL acquisition only improves this balance of power. DKS 
merchandising and marketing expertise are expected to 
ultimately yield strong sales and margin improvements at FL 
(weighted to the latter), while the stock should see some 
recovery through the back to school season and ultimately 
release once inventory cleans up, which we expect by early 
2027. Lastly, DKS alternative profit pools support all-important 
gross margin expansion for the core business.
Valuation
Our updated $188 Dec 26 price target is based on ~13x 2027E 
P/E (equivalent to ~7x EV/EBITDA) with both below historical 
averages as the stock nears trough valuations.

2Q comp of +4.9% (Street and JPMe largely in-line) vs. our preview indicating market 
expectations have come down over the past month to ~+4.5% (with the lower end of 
expectations at +4.0%) outpaced the industry by ~200 bps, with growth driven by +3.6% 
ticket growth and +1.3% traffic growth. Specifically, mgmt noted (i) continued strong 
responses to newness/innovation, (ii) all consumer cohorts healthy with no signs of trade 
down, (iii) broad-based strength across apparel, hardlines, and footwear, and (iv) without 
directly speaking to cadence, World Cup results were “strong.” License, team sports, and 
running/cleats/outdoor footwear all remained healthy as well. Looking ahead, DKS still 
expects 1H-weighted comps due to the timing of the World Cup with the FY guide 
maintained on continued confidence in the core business (2H implied ~+1.3% at the 
midpoint). We are now modeling +3.0% 3Q and +4.0% FY26 comp for core DKS. See 
Figure 11. 
•
(3) Foot Locker topline miss on challenging footwear market. Indeed, FL posted -
3.6% comps (after inflecting positive in 1Q), below the Street’s +0.3% and our lowered 
-2.0%e, though largely in-line with our work suggesting negative L-MSD SSS 
expectations. Mgmt attributed the decline in both North America and International (-
3.3%) to (i) consumers shifting away from legacy footwear and apparel franchises, (ii) 
the number of launches were meaningfully lower than 1Q, and (iii) they did not resonate; 
all leading to a promotional marketplace that started with the brands and spread to the 
specialty retail market (and FL more exposed given its more narrow assortment/higher 
exposure to legacy brands). See Figures 6-9 for our updated promo trackers. We are now 
modeling -2.0% 3Q comp and -1.2% FY26 comp for Foot Locker. See Figure 12.
•
(4) Gross margin: the toughest pill. 2Q core DKS gross margin of 37.8% was up ~80 
bps YOY and beat the Street’s 37.1%, driven by 50 bps of IEEPA refunds ($19MM), the 
Dick’s Media Network (est. 25 bps and likely aided by the World Cup/Adidas 
partnership), and GameChanger (est. ~35 bps), partly offset by promotions picking up at 
the end of the qtr (especially athletic footwear and apparel) and (“to a lesser extent”) 
higher fuel and SC costs. FL’s GM of 25.7% faced an even larger promotional headwind.  
Looking ahead, mgmt now expects core Dick’s FY26 GM down “slightly” with 3Q most 
pressured as promotional headwinds continue throughout the back half, albeit less so in 
4Q on full price selling benefits from launches including Caitlin Clark’s “Nike Caitlin 1” 
(10/1 launch), Air Jordan 11 “Space Jam” collab (12/12 launch), and further Nike Mind 
newness (these should also aid FL). The lower guide now includes fuel headwinds given 
current volatility as well as expectations for higher SC costs. For core Dick’s, we are now 
modeling 3Q gross margin of 35.5% (down ~60 bps YOY) and FY26 gross margin of 
36.3% (down ~10 bps YOY). For Foot Locker, we are now modeling 3Q/FY26 gross 
margins of 23.4% and 26.4%, respectively. 
•
(5) SG&A puts/takes. Core DKS SG&A as a % of sales peaked and deleveraged ~100 
bps YOY (with dollars up 9.8% YOY) on World Cup marketing, continued investments 
in stores and digital, and higher health care costs. Foot Locker SG&A reached 27.5% of 
sales given the aforementioned promotional headwinds and continued investments 
supporting the brand turnaround including marketing initiatives and “Stripers” payroll. 
Looking ahead, at core DKS, mgmt still expects SG&A deleverage for the year with 3Q 
deleveraging 50 bps (vs. prior 3Q leverage guidance) with leverage still expected in 4Q 
as it will have annualized investments. For FL, SG&A dollars are expected to step up in 
3Q on advertising related to BTS and the brand relaunch (e.g., “It Will Always Be Foot 
Locker” released on 10/7).  DKS expects pre-opening to peak in 3Q ($36MMe) on the way 
to ~$90MM for the year. We note DKS now plans 14 and 20 HoS and Field House 
openings for FY26 vs. 14 and 22 prior guide. We expect core Dick’s ad expense peaked 
in 2Q on World Cup with FL ad expense peaking in 3Q on the aforementioned re-launch. 
Thus, we are now modeling 3Q SG&A as a % of sales 27.2%, 26.3%, and 29.0% for 
consolidated company, core Dick’s and Foot Locker, with 25.8%, 25.0%, and 27.3% 
expected for FY26, respectively. See Figure 10.

Dick's Sporting Goods (Overweight; Price Target: $188.00)
Investment Thesis
With DKS being the only national sporting goods retailer, alongside its emphasis on better/
best products, we believe the brands need DKS, which drives better product allocations, and 
the FL acquisition only improves this balance of power. DKS merchandising and marketing 
expertise are expected to ultimately yield strong sales and margin improvements at FL 
(weighted to the latter), while the stock should see some recovery through the back to school 
season and ultimately release once inventory cleans up, which we expect by early 2027. 
Lastly, DKS alternative profit pools support all-important gross margin expansion for the 
core business.  
Valuation
Our updated $188 Dec 26 price target is based on ~13x 2027E P/E (equivalent to ~7x EV/
EBITDA) with both below historical averages as the stock nears trough valuations.
