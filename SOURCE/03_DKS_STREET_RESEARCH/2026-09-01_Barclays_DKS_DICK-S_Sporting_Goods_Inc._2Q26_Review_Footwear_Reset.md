Footwear Reset. We believe today's reaction to DKS earnings reflects a significant reset of
investor expectations for the athletic footwear ecosystem. While Dick's delivered a robust +4.9%
comp and maintained its full-year sales outlook, there is an industry-wide reset driven by
slowing legacy lifestyle footwear, weak launch product, elevated inventory levels, and an
increasingly promotional marketplace. Importantly, performance categories including running
remain healthy, while pressure is concentrated in classic lifestyle silhouettes, suggesting the
issue extends beyond Nike and now encompasses much of the lifestyle footwear complex. The
largest surprise was the magnitude of the Foot Locker earnings reset, with expectations moving
from a projected $110mn - $150mn profit to a $40mn - $80mn loss, reinforcing investor concerns
that both the footwear correction and Foot Locker turnaround will take longer than anticipated.
In our view, the key debate has shifted from market share gains, which Dick's continues to
demonstrate, to the duration of the lifestyle footwear correction and the timing of a return to
meaningful product innovation that can reignite category growth and restore margin
expansion. Despite lack of visibility, we believe 2H26 expectations are reset. Near term, we
acknowledge lack of evidence of the Foot Locker turnaround but believe the sell-off in shares
reflects much of the negative investor sentiment. We would expect shares to trade largely range
bound until there is greater clarity on innovation, which is slated to come from many of its
branded partners during 2027. On 8/25/26, DKS shares closed -30.8% versus the S&P 500 of
+0.3%.

Tariff Exposure Update. During 2Q26, the company received $59.0mn in tariff refunds and
$2.1mn in related interest income. Of the $59mn in tariff refunds, $57mn was related to the core
Dick's business and $2mn was related to the Foot Locker business. Of the total refund received,
approximately $21mn was recognized in adjusted 2Q26 results, consisting of a $19mn benefit to
Dick's merchandise margin and a $2mn benefit to Foot Locker merchandise margin, while the
remaining $38mn was excluded from adjusted results as it related to tariff expense recognized
in FY25. The related $2.1mn of interest income was also excluded from adjusted
results. Collectively, the $38mn refund and $2.1mn in interest income benefited reported GAAP
pre-tax income by approximately $40mn above adjusted results. Tariff refunds helped to offset
the broader promotional pressure across parts of the athletic footwear and apparel
marketplace, with DKS largely re-investing the refund to remain competitively priced amid an
increasingly promotional athletic footwear and apparel environment and to help offset higher
fuel, supply chain, and other inflationary cost pressures. The company also noted that it has
now received substantially all tariff refunds and does not have any material additional refund
claims outstanding.
Quarter in Review. Consolidated DKS adj. EPS of $3.53 missed consensus of $3.76, driven by
lower sales and higher OpEx. Total revenue of $5.59bn came in below consensus of $5.65bn.
Comp sales grew +4.9% at the Dick's business, driven by increases in average ticket (+3.6%) and
transactions (+1.3%), while the Foot Locker business inflected negative to -3.6%. Overall,
pro forma comp sales increased +2.1% (vs. cons. of +3.6%). GM of 34.1% beat consensus of
33.9%, driven by a $21mn tariff refund benefit, with improvement in the core Dick's business GM
driven by growth in Dick's media network and game changer, which helped offset increased
promotions and higher fuel and supply chain costs, as well as the negative mix impact from the
Foot Locker acquisition. OpEx rate of 25.9% of sales missed consensus of 25.5%, driven by lower
sales and the impact of the Foot Locker acquisition, as well as investments in World Cup
marketing, investments in digital and in-store experiences, and higher teammate healthcare
costs. FY26 guidance was updated for the consolidated entity, with management seeing adj.
EPS in the range of $11.00 to $12.00 (from prior $13.50 to $14.50 and vs. cons. of $14.34); net
sales in the range of $21.9bn to $22.2bn (from prior $22.1bn to $22.4bn and vs. cons. of
$22.4bn); comp sales at the core Dick's business in the range of +2.5% to +4.0% (unchanged
from prior), and comp sales at the Foot Locker business of -2.0% to 0.0% (from prior +1.5% to
+3.0%); and adj. EBIT of $1.46bn to $1.56bn (from prior $1.71bn to $1.83bn and vs. cons. of
$1.80bn). Note consensus numbers as of 8:00 AM ET on 08/25/2026.
Inventory. Based on our proprietary inventory analysis, DKS has posted four consecutive
quarters of positive sales-to-inventory growth. In FY2Q26 the Inventory Management Spread
("IM Spread") was 88 basis points and worsened from 377 basis points in the prior quarter.
DKS's Gross Margin Return on Inventory ("GMROI") has improved for four consecutive quarters,
and its Operating Margin Return on Inventory ("OMROI") has worsened for two consecutive
quarters. (Until the anniversary of the FL acquisition, our inventory metrics only include core Dick's
Business and back out Foot Locker Business.)

Proprietary PRISM Curve
From a fundamental perspective, we use our proprietary PRISM cycle as a leading indicator of
potential sales inflection (i.e., either from positive to negative or negative to positive). Our
PRISM Curve attempts to look for fundamental changes in the business model of a company,
driven by proactive management decisions to stabilize margins (e.g., purge inventory, cut costs,
share best practices, invest in product and brand). Figure 2 and Figure 3 below depict our model
graphically and identify where we would theoretically expect to see sales and margin changes
along the business model curve.
Dick's Sporting Goods in the HUNT Phase (Strategically Offensive)
During the HUNT Phase, management’s strategy is on the offense. They are in “HUNT” mode
and are in control of taking market share. Management is focused on driving company-specific
strategies forward, building on their own brand equity and positioning, rather than focusing
primarily on competitors or the environment around them. At this point, retailers are usually
beating sales and margin expectations and may begin to grow earnings faster than total sales.
Investors would want to hold and accumulate shares of a company in Region C in Figure 3, the
area of maximum potential stock appreciation. This phase may take place over multiple years
as the company enters its sweet spot.
Foot Locker in the TRAP Phase (Strategically Defensive) 
While there are four distinct phases of our PRISM Curve as per the graphics below, we believe
Foot Locker is in the “TRAP” phase, where fundamentals are deteriorating as competition in all
categories remains stiff and pricing power is fleeting for its core franchises. As market share
erodes, the lack of sales is causing operating deleverage. During the TRAP Phase,
management’s strategy is on the defensive – meaning actions are being taken to change
business trends, but the issues have not yet stabilized.
