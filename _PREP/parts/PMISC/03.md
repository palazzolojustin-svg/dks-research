### Summary
- Tough Q2 season: limited gdnc upgrades (a few downgrades); results-day moves adidas -12%, On -20%, Puma -5%, JD Sports -14%. Growth slowing as brands prioritise full-price DTC in highly promotional market; DTC-led model costly (stepped-up marketing/activations to drive online + store traffic). Investors concerned about structural slowdown in sector profit growth. JPM: market conditions not to improve materially next two quarters, but limited evidence of structural slowdown in footwear demand. JD update: inventory controlled; brands + retailers disciplined on supply. Scope for wholesale order growth to re-accelerate into Q4-26 and 2027 as newness builds (notably adidas, On); CMDs to show solid newness pipelines; Q3 expectations reset. OW adidas + On.
### JD Sports Q2 trading update (day before note) — DKS-READ
- Surprise: significantly softer sell-through in North America (previously described as more resilient than UK/Europe). JD attributed to (1) soft consumer environment, (2) ongoing footwear product cycle evolution, (3) back-to-school timing. Inventory "well controlled"; FCF gdnc maintained despite lower profit gdnc and unchanged capex gdnc → extra working-capital cash release. Mgmt: "encouraging momentum in performance-based running and newer footwear styles."
- DKS-READ: weak NA athletic-footwear sell-through at JD (US c.40% of JD sales) in Q2 CY26 + back-to-school timing; same product-cycle transition affects DKS/Foot Locker.

### Lifestyle footwear: cyclical not structural (JPM)
- Recurring investor debate: structural decline in sneaker demand. Q2 points to cyclical slowdown tied to product-cycle transition + seasonal effects (Zalando: sandals and pumps outperforming sneakers). Apparel analog: over-inventoried market → muted apparel growth much of 2022-2023, re-accelerated 2024. Expect footwear newness esp. toward 2027 to drive re-acceleration (adidas Stan Smith, On Cloudsurfer 3).
### Opex: DTC growth comes with a cost
- Q2 EBIT misses (esp. On, adidas) mainly from higher DTC mix and lack of wholesale-driven opex leverage, plus one-offs incl World Cup investment. Q2-26 DTC mix at/near all-time highs (last five Q2s) for adidas, Puma, On (Fig 12; range ~20-50%). DTC broadly similar EBIT margin vs wholesale long-run, but Q2 DTC margin hurt by brand investment (marketing, store activations) to drive traffic. Into Q3: normalises for adidas (DTC vs wholesale growth gap narrows); remains opex growth driver for Puma + On (DTC-led). Figs 13-16 DTC growth vs opex growth Q1 21-Q4 26E (adidas opex ex marketing; adidas variable opex ex marketing + HQ; On SG&A ex SBC; Puma opex incl marketing).
