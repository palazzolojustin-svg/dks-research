### Sector view 2026
- Group 2025 perf down -15% avg vs SPX +16%. Hope for 2026: resilient consumer, hopefully no more tariffs, potential refund of already-paid tariffs, margin tailwinds (pricing, FX).
- Consumer positives: resilient during key events (BTS, Holiday); "Big Beautiful Bill" expected bigger tax refunds → could help Q1; low unemployment, real wage growth, rates may keep falling, stock mkt ATHs (wealth effect for higher-income). Negatives: household debt at ATH, savings rate below pre-COVID norms, inflation threat, geopolitical noise.
- Margins = big 2026 story: tariff mitigation tailwind; optionality if Supreme Court rules tariffs refunded (key to SHOO U/G); weaker USD = lesser-discussed GM tailwind — hedging pushes FX GM impact out 12-24mo, so FX top-line tailwind in 2025 becomes GM benefit 2026 as hedges roll off; all covered cos have 20%-60% intl rev.

### VFC (add to Conviction List, PT $25)
- PT basis: low-20s P/E on CY2027 EPS ~$1.17 (likely still "under-earning").
- Holiday checks: strong demand TNF (largest brand) & Timberland (3rd largest); strong F/W'25 sell-through → strong F/W'26 wholesale order books. Vans "green shoots". Dickies sale = "addition by subtraction": barely impacts P&L, cuts leverage ~0.5 turn.
- Q4 skew: TNF & Timberland each ~35% of annual rev and ~55% of annual EBIT in CY Q4 (fiscal Q3); company-wide CY Q4 ~30% of consol rev and last yr almost 85% of annual EPS — "make or break" qtr.
- Weather: NYC proxy — Oct avg temp ~4°F lower y/y, Nov ~5°F lower, Dec ~4°F colder; West far less favorable but Northeast/Midwest cold → net positive (boots, outerwear, fleece).
- NYC store checks: brisk traffic, long lines at Timberland & TNF stores. Google searches T3M: TNF +18% y/y, Timberland +11%.
- Best-seller lists (Nov/Dec combined): DKS — TNF ~15 of Top 100 Men's apparel (vs ~9 LY), 16-17 of Top 100 Women's (vs <16 LY). REI — TNF ~7 Men's (vs <5 LY), almost 10 Women's (vs <8 LY). DKS-READ: TNF gaining share of DKS best-seller lists.
- Promo: TNF & Timberland less promotional y/y DTC and wholesale; TNF limited Cyber Monday promo to single day (LY "50% off select items" all week); Timberland headline promos similar y/y but sale/clearance SKUs on Timberland.com down 67% y/y (obs 1/12/26).
- Wholesale markdown penetration Nov+Dec: TNF down y/y at every tracked retailer (Dick's, Academy, JD Sports, REI, Macy's, Nordstrom). Timberland down y/y at 6 of 10 tracked; exceptions modest increases at Dick's, REI, DSW, flat at Academy. DKS-READ: Timberland discounting up modestly y/y at Dick's.
- Vans: Journeys Holiday 2025 ~20 of Top 100 Men's shoes (from 15 LY), ~11 Women's (from <10). Google "Vans shoes" T3M searches inflected from often -DD to +HSD-LDD; credited to brand president Sun Choe (ex-LULU, joined ~18mo ago) product and wide-leg denim/skater fashion shift. Bloomberg ALTD US DTC card data T13W: fiscal Q1 (Jun'25) & Q2 (Sep'25) observed sales down 40%-50% y/y (actual Vans Americas -14% Q1, -9% Q2 incl wholesale); as of wk ending 12/28/25 improved to MSD decline.
- Vans underlying trend -HSD each of past 4 qtrs; distribution cleanup worsened reported (4Q25 -20%, 1Q26 -15%, 2Q26 -11%); Q3 few pts headwind; Q4 apples-to-apples → declines "less bad" next 2 qtrs.
- Margins: pre-COVID EBIT margin low-to-mid teens; trough 5.6% FY24 (4.8% ex-Supreme, divested next yr); FY25 almost 6% despite losing Supreme; FY26 likely approaching 7% despite tariffs (cost cuts, less discounting, Vans cleanup); Needham ~8% FY28. Mgmt 2024 Analyst Day: 10% margin by FY28 w/o rev growth — tariffs may delay; if achieved ~$1.60 FY28 EPS vs Needham $1.21.
- Balance sheet: net debt peaked nearly $6.2B ~2 yrs ago, net debt/EBITDA peak ~6x; asset sales (Supreme, Dickies, corporate aircraft); expected net debt <$3B, leverage <3.5x (lowest in 14 qtrs). FCF-only deleveraging ~0.5 turn/yr next 2 yrs → below mgmt 2.5x target in FY28; ~2x in 24mo (fig 13).

### NKE (D/G to Hold, PT withdrawn)
- CEO Elliott Hill doing right things LT (sport, innovation, wholesale re-engagement) but turnaround slower, low visibility. Concern: aggressive NA wholesale sell-in (+24% last qtr, +11% qtr prior) — brand heat doesn't support; tough compares later CY. China & Converse very intense issues. Cons too aggressive on recovery pace → NTM risk. Shares up almost 15% since post-EPS trough (vs SPX +1%), driven by insider buys (Hill $1M on 12/29; board member Tim Cook almost $3M a week earlier) → near prior $68 PT, <10% upside.
- NA +9% organic in Nov qtr; channel divergence: 1Q25 (Aug'24) wholesale & DTC both down LDD; since, DTC down 10%-15% in 4 of past 5 qtrs, wholesale +24% in F2Q26 (Nov'25); 34 ppt spread. $3.55B NA wholesale in single qtr = biggest NA wholesale rev qtr in history. Mgmt: significant portion of wholesale growth clearance/liquidation-driven. Risks: reduced future orders, discounting, tough FY27 compares. Partly by design (prior regime over-extended DTC; oversaturation of Classics — Jordan retros, Dunks; wholesale neglected previously).
- Needham Holiday survey (>600 US consumers 18-59): planning to shop Nike ~42% in 2024 → <37% Nov 2025 (LDD decline in absolute count; consistent men & women).
- Google searches for Nike negative y/y ~2 yrs (US & global, rolling 3mo); Nov 2025 blip (US +MSD, global flat), Dec reversed (-LDD US, -MSD global).
- China: LTM rev ~$6.1B, ~10% below pre-COVID, nearly 30% below reopening peak; EBIT margin from high-30s to low-20s (LTM 2Q26). Mgmt Q2 call quote (abridged in source): "In Greater China... we were facing a longer road to a healthier business... What we've done is a start, but it's not happening at the level or the pace we need to drive wider change... declining store traffic, softer in-season sell-through rates and higher levels of aged inventory... Our brands have consistently been off price for consumers, especially in digital... higher mix of off price sales with higher markdowns, higher sales related returns, higher wholesale discounts, and higher obsolescence charges".
- APLA -4% in Q2, 7 straight DTC declines; EMEA higher promo pressure than anticipated; Converse -31% last qtr, 10th consecutive negative qtr.
- Estimates: Street has sales & margins up y/y in F4Q; Needham sees EBIT margin unlikely up in Q4 (tariffs, rev trends, demand creation ahead of World Cup early-1Q27). FY27 rev Needham +LSD vs Street +MSD; Street ~250bps EBIT margin recovery aggressive. EPS FY26/FY27 Needham $1.40/$1.72 vs Street $1.54/$2.37.
- Running: shunned pre-COVID for Sportswear (opened door to Hoka); renewed focus Vomero, Pegasus; Running turned positive 6 qtrs ago, +20%+ each of past 2 qtrs.
