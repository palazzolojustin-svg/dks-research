### Risk factors — FY2022-specific substance (vs FY2023 10-K)
- Macro list incl. Ukraine conflict, banking-sector instability, long-term COVID impacts; COVID may heighten other risks.
- Trade tensions with China and Russia could disrupt vendor/vertical-brand supply; foreign currency effects on supplier prices.
- Omni-channel expectations: localized assortments, regionally relevant products, fast delivery/pick-up, convenient returns.
- Real estate risk (expanded FY2022 text): optimize portfolio (open/relocate/consolidate, opportunistically buy real estate in barrier-to-entry markets, renew/restructure leases, remodel, close underperformers, repurpose holdings for specialty banners); store closures → ongoing rent, termination charges; post-assignment/sublease liability; shopping center traffic/landlord/anchor tenant distress may force markdowns/promotions.
- Distribution: may need new eCommerce fulfillment center sites to satisfy omni demand.
- Data: no knowledge of material breaches to date; state consumer privacy laws.
- Pandemic: possible renewed store closures/government mandates incl. workforce reduction.
- Product safety concerns (national & vertical brands) → enforcement/litigation/reputational damage.
- Litigation incl. disputes with landlords and vendors due to COVID disruptions.
- **Firearms: "We sell firearms and ammunition in some of our stores"** — ATF/state compliance, background-check lawsuits incl. potential class actions, improper-use lawsuits incl. municipalities seeking cost recovery. (FY2023 10-K: firearms "no longer actively sold in our stores".)
- Anti-takeover: Class B; blank-check preferred; no cumulative voting; no partial written consent (unanimous written consent only if Class B no longer outstanding); advance notice; DGCL 15% business-combination restriction.
- Debt: risks named $575M 3.25% Convertible Senior Notes due 2025, the Notes, Revolving Credit Facility; consequences incl. reduced flexibility and dilution from conversion. Revolver limits certain subsidiaries' unsecured debt; revolver + Notes indenture limit secured debt and sale of substantially all assets.
- Converts indenture: fundamental change → holders may require cash repurchase; make-whole fundamental change → conversion rate increase. Senior Notes: change of control triggering event → repurchase at 101% of principal.
- Convertible note hedge/warrant risks: counterparty hedging activity may affect stock price; unsecured counterparty credit risk (insolvency → unsecured creditor; adverse tax/dilution); dilution from conversion or net-share-settled warrants; may encourage short selling.
