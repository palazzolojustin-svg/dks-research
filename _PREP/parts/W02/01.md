### Risk factors — company-specific substance (FY2023 wording)
- Macro: inflation, elevated rates, recession, reinstatement of student loan payments, banking-sector instability, Ukraine & Middle East conflicts; may force pricing strategies hurting GM.
- Competition incl. AI/machine learning innovation pace; real-time price comparison; social media marketing.
- Product cost/availability: raw materials, fuel, transport (Ukraine/Middle East, ocean trade route challenges), labor shortages in transportation, port congestion.
- Foreign sourcing: duties/quotas, destination-based tax, loss of MFN status, freight costs, trade route inaccessibility, climate/natural disasters.
- Demand shifts: COVID-driven category demand (health/fitness, outdoor, athleisure) — uncertain persistence; misjudging market → markdowns, write-downs.
- Vertical brands ~13% of sales: product liability/recall without third-party indemnification, sourcing reputational risk, IP/counterfeit, talent/advertising risk. New store concepts/experiential (climbing walls, fields, ice rinks): bodily injury/property liability, build/maintenance costs, skilled staffing, license compliance, competitor copying.
- Reputation: ESG goal progress/transparency, firearms policies, public stances on social/political issues, sponsorships/influencers, real estate choices, foreign manufacturer labor practices.
- Strategy execution: tech/AI, eCommerce, ScoreCard, vertical brands, specialty concepts, real estate (remodels, experiential, relocations). Growth depends on HoS, next-gen 50k sq ft DICK'S and GGPC; real estate/lease terms, construction costs, sale-leaseback financing access.
- Distribution/fulfillment network & CSC dependence; third-party carriers for substantially all shipments.
- Cyber: no knowledge of material breaches to date; AI-enabled threats; state privacy laws & federal cyber disclosure rules; third-party payment processor; hybrid remote work reliance on IT.
- Labor: wages, equity comp, on-site/remote policies, immigration, minimum wage. Key person: CEO Lauren Hobart; Exec Chairman Ed Stack (operating since 1984; oversees merchandising and key strategic growth initiatives); no key-person insurance generally.
- Seasonality: highest sales/OI in Q2 (golf, team sports) and Q4 (holiday, cold weather); weather/climate risk.
- Dividends/buybacks discretionary.
- Inventory shrink: "We have recently experienced, and may continue to experience elevated levels of inventory shrink relative to historical levels" (incl. organized retail crime); sustained high shrink at certain stores could cause asset impairments.
- Class B (Ed Stack, relatives, family trusts) controls majority of combined voting power.
- Vendors: ~1,500; Nike ~24%; vendors increasingly sell DTC/alt channels (department stores, family footwear stores, eCommerce); vendor incentives (return privileges, volume allowances, co-op advertising) not guaranteed.
- Regulatory incl. eCommerce/AI, privacy, knives/food; still subject to firearms/ammunition regulation due to historical sales though "no longer actively sold in our stores".
- Tax incl. Inflation Reduction Act. Pro sports team merchandise exposure (team performance, lockouts/strikes, athlete scandals).
- Debt service: Senior Notes and Revolving Credit Facility.

### MD&A — overview & strategy
- Net sales +48.4% FY2023 vs FY2019; pre-tax income % of sales 4.7% (FY2019) → 10.2% (FY2023). Mgmt belief: consumers made lasting lifestyle changes prioritizing sport/active lifestyles.
- Field & Stream Exit (Q4 FY2022): converted remaining 17 F&S stores (mostly DICK'S/F&S combos) to HoS, expanded DSG, or other specialty concepts; closed twelve for conversion in Q4 FY2022; pre-tax charges $30.1M ($28.5M non-cash store asset impairment, $0.8M severance, $0.7M inventory write-down); sold F&S trademark in FY2023 for proceeds near carrying value.
- Macro: inflation, end of student loan payment deferments, elevated rates may affect spending and promotional landscape; FY2022 apparel overages from late-arriving inventory and elevated container/transport costs (moderating 2H FY2022); actions in Q3/Q4 FY2022 to address overages.
- Trends affecting 2023 (and 2024 outlook):
  - Shrink: ~50 bps higher than 2022 as % of net sales (full year). "We expect inventory shrink in the first quarter of 2024 to remain higher than the comparable 2023 period, until the trend is reflected in the results of the current and prior year periods beginning in the second quarter of 2024."
  - Supply chain costs lower in 2023; "We do not expect meaningful year-over-year declines to continue in 2024."
  - SG&A: after Business Optimization, Q4 FY2023 SG&A moderated ~78 bps as % of net sales; "In 2024, we expect selling, general and administrative expenses to leverage as a percentage of net sales, driven by the future benefits from our Business Optimization actions and our ongoing focus on improving productivity and reducing discretionary costs."
- KPIs: comp incl. online; store enters comp in 14th full month; relocations comp from original open date; closed stores excluded. Store productivity metrics: new store productivity, sales/sq ft, store operating contribution margin, store cash flow. Lower OCF typically in Q3 (holiday inventory build), higher in Q4.
