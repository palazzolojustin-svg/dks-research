## SRC 02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/events/DKS_Foot-Locker-Acquisition-Call_2025-05-15.md | 2025-05-15 9:00 AM ET | DKS acquisition of Foot Locker announcement call (FactSet corrected transcript; slide deck on investors.dicks.com) | Nate Gilch (Sr Dir IR), Ed Stack (Exec Chair & Chief Merchandising Officer), Lauren Hobart (Pres/CEO), Navdeep Gupta (EVP/CFO); analysts: Brian Nagel (Oppenheimer), Paul Lejuez (Citi), Adrienne Yih (Barclays), Simeon Gutman (MS), Robby Ohmes (BofA), Kate McShane (GS), Michael Lasser (UBS), Chris Horvers (JPM), Michael Baker (D.A. Davidson) | deal announcement + prelim Q1 FY2025

### Admin
- Definitive merger agreement to acquire Foot Locker (FL). Same morning: prelim Q1 2025 results released; Q1 report scheduled Wed 2025-05-28, call 8:00 PM ET (as stated). Form S-4 to be filed.

### Prelim Q1 FY2025 (Gupta)
- Q1 comp +4.5%; non-GAAP EPS $3.37, "ahead of last year".

### Deal terms (Gupta)
- $24/FL share; holder election: $24 cash or 0.1168 DKS shares; election not subject to min/max cash or stock. Implied equity value ~$2.4B, EV $2.5B.
- Cost synergies $100M-$125M "in the medium-term", primarily procurement and direct sourcing; "well-supported and achievable". EPS accretive in first full fiscal year post-close excl. one-time transaction costs and costs to achieve synergies.
- Financing: combination of cash, long-term debt and equity; DKS ended last yr (FY2024) with $1.7B cash; even if fully debt-financed, no unfavorable impact on current credit ratings; committed to maintain investment grade ratings. FL bonds: working w/ advisors on possible exchange of obligations; "if need be we'll be able to take those notes out". Mix of consideration unknown until close. (Yih asked re ~2x → ~3x leverage; not directly answered.)
- Unanimously approved by both boards; expected close 2H 2025; subject to customary conditions incl. regulatory and FL shareholder approval. Hobart: "not expecting any regulatory concerns"; process not expected "too cumbersome" (re FTC/divestitures).

### Strategic rationale (prepared)
- Stack: DKS "approaching from a position of strength"; convergence of sport & culture; health/wellness, casualwear, sneakers as self-expression. Creates "new global leader in the sports retail industry". Precedent: Galyan's, similarly sized relative to DKS at time. Mostly organic history.
- TAM (slide 4): US footwear+apparel+hardlines ~$140B; with FL, combined store count >3,200 in key regions; access to ~$300B global sports retail market; "will make DICK'S a global business".
- FL profile (slide 5): banners Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS, atmos; ~2,400 stores across 26 countries at end 2024 (North America, Europe, Asia, Australia, NZ) + licensed stores in Europe, Middle East, Asia; >400 store refreshes in 2024; digital 18% of revenue; 2024 revenue $8B.
- Hobart 5 benefits (slide 6): (1) global platform in growing sports retail; (2) broader consumers across differentiated concepts (FL: sneakerheads/lifestyle vs DKS performance-focused); (3) stronger brand partnerships via global reach (slide 9); (4) invest in omnichannel growth (DKS House of Sport + FL reimagined concept stores; FL digital modernization/store refresh); (5) operational efficiencies (slide 11) mainly procurement/direct sourcing. FL US real estate "highly complementary" — different markets/demographics. "growth oriented transaction grounded in operational discipline."

### Q&A
- Q [Nagel, Oppenheimer]: why now; brand engagement? A [Stack]: long considered; FL pressured by Nike's DTC shift away from wholesale; Nike now "leaning back into wholesale" ("Elliott and his team are doing a great job"); FL beneficiary → "timing of this is perfect". Talked to "a couple" of brands, "pretty enthusiastic", value DKS operational excellence, "willing to help".
- Q [Lejuez, Citi]: synergy timing; SG&A synergies; customer visibility/banners? A [Gupta]: $100M-$125M over medium term, more color closer to close; opportunity on GM line (scale) and SG&A (procurement). A [Hobart]: run the two businesses separately; DKS team stays on DKS growth; FL team exclusively on FL; small integration team led by Ed; synergies counted = procurement/purchasing. FL "Lace Up" plan elements: reimagined/refreshed stores, investing in tag [as transcribed], personalization, consumer experience, Stripers/culture. Separate banners; consumer "may or may not know"; no banner change plan.
- Q [Yih, Barclays]: pre-deal talks with Nike; market share assumptions in $140B/$300B; DKS 25% and FL 59% merch purchases (from vendor concentration) as % of combined sales? A [Stack]: talked with "several brands" pre-deal; they gave impression of FL; "part of our job is to be able to see around the corners"; World Cup 2026 "biggest sporting event this country has ever seen", Olympics 2028; footwear big part. A [Gupta]: footwear & apparel largest part of TAM; "footwear is the engine that drives the train"; combined footwear "close to about 50% of the total sales of the company".
- Q [Gutman, MS]: FL shrinking on comp, DKS could take share organically — why buy? Distribution risk 5 yrs out? A [Stack]: FL serves neighborhoods DKS "never going to get" (no 50,000-60,000 sq ft sites with return); FL "bore the brunt" of Nike DTC shift; Nike moving back to wholesale; "Foot Locker is a very important part of Nike's long term strategy"; "we're pretty conservative. We don't get over skis"; board precept: deal "cannot impact" DKS / HoS / Field House / GameChanger / tech investment / flywheel; "That glide path is still there." A [Hobart]: TAM $140B→$300B; additive. A [Stack, distribution]: makes DKS "much bigger, much more important partners"; brands not looking to broaden distribution of high-heat product; brands to "continue to lean into specialty" and premium positioning; "you never know".
- Q [Ohmes, BofA]: Nike % of FL sales up? HoS growth changes? FL store closures? A [Stack]: "there's that possibility"; FL to get more high-heat product; "Nike business will go up at a higher margin rate" via more full-price selling — "big precept". A [Hobart]: no change to DKS growth rate, HoS, Field House; portfolio repositioning continues; FL team assessed all stores, some potentially closing; DKS to weigh in over next several months; "not our expectation that we'll be closing a significant number of Foot Locker stores".
- Q [McShane, GS]: tariff risk/timing? A [Stack]: manage as before w/ brands & supply chain partners; "good news that came out of Geneva last week"; "all the pieces fell together right now".
- Q [Lasser, UBS]: why different from failed retail mergers (strong buying weak)? A [Stack]: footwear core competence; visibility to FL "performing meaningfully better" via Nike pipeline/new Nike team; FL apparel deteriorating & under-invested ("This kid dresses from toe to head"); aim to return FL "to its rightful position"; "not... big egos"; would not do it if it impacted DKS; "we're up for the jab". Follow-up Q: FL comp assumption in underwriting; cannibalization? A [Stack]: won't disclose now; at close will provide FL comp, EPS, accretion view.
- Q [Horvers, JPM]: DKS international expansion? A [Hobart]: run separately; DKS strategies don't include intl expansion; "business as usual". A [Stack]: "we have no plans to take DICK'S internationally"; big US opportunity (HoS, Field House). Q: DKS/FL customer crossover (20% vs 50%)? A [Hobart]: DKS share of $140B market "is still around 80%" [as transcribed; likely ~8%]; DKS footwear growth not from taking FL share — 90% of DKS stores have premium full-service footwear decks, better assortment; "not... cannibalistic". No crossover number.
- Q [Baker, D.A. Davidson]: Golf Galaxy analogy? A [Stack]: similar — strengthened golf brand partnerships helping Golf Galaxy and DKS; didn't recall share figures; "very good analogy". Q: FTC/divestitures? (see above). Q: procurement = merch only? A [Gupta]: both — product/direct-sourcing vendors and non-merch vendors.
