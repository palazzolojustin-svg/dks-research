## SRC 02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/conferences/DKS_JPMorgan-Retail-Forum_2026-04-08.md | 2026-04-08 10:00 AM ET | JP Morgan Retail Round Up Forum, fireside + audience Q&A (FactSet corrected transcript) | Ed Stack (Exec Chair), Lauren Hobart (Pres/CEO), Navdeep Gupta (EVP/CFO); host Christopher Horvers (JPM) | post-Q4 FY2025 (FYE 2026-01-31), FY2026 outlook

### Foot Locker (FL) rationale
- Horvers framing: FL = "playing long ball" balancing power vs vendors (vendors "irrational at times"), plus retail-101 margin gains; bear case = needed to expand TAM given high DKS share.
- Stack: "It's not to increase our TAM"; DKS "only got roughly 9% market share here in the US" → lots more share to take. FL = different consumer than DKS; value creation since FL "did kind of forget about retail 101"; big opportunity in assortment + retail execution. "if we had a mulligan, we'd buy this all over again, and we would do nothing different" (Masters week analogy). "not a defensive play"; wouldn't call brands irrational publicly. Rationale: value creation, unserved consumer, global footprint. "since we closed, we're even more excited about the opportunity than we were when we first bought the business."
- Vendor pushback: "no pushback from the vendors at all"; brands helped "cleaning out the garage", access, allocation; want "growing, vibrant Foot Locker"; "the brands are totally jazzed".
- Investor pushback: why do it when DKS doing so well; can you turn FL around → investors in "wait and see"; "we really can do it, and we'll prove it to you".

### Fast Break remodels
- 11 Fast Break test stores (had full qtr): plan set in diligence, started at close. Old wall "merely a run-on sentence". Took everything off wall; removed "roughly 30% of the SKUs"; rebuilt wall around hot shoes/story; "huge impact on the business, on those 11 stores". Cross-section: lower- and higher-volume, NYC street stores, mall stores. "more than pleasantly surprised"; apparel brought back (FL had removed much apparel), "results were great".
- Around NBA All-Star Game (Feb 2026, LA): 10 more in LA, same type of result → 21 now. Plan: ~250 Fast Break stores in US by back-to-school (BTS) 2026. Internal timeline for holiday 2026 not disclosed; not giving timeframe for further rollout.
- Brands seeing stores → giving "access and allocation to product that Foot Locker didn't have before"; brands that had taken allocation/access away "come back and said, we're back in"; brands could find product for 11-store test and 10 LA stores even when couldn't supply everything.
- Lease line (storefront "billboard") revived: Valentine's Day pack (red/white/pink shoes) in window "highly successful"; Air Max 95 launch few weeks ago "highly successful". Different wall, more apparel, different lease line, different marketing — "results so far have been really phenomenal."
- 21 vs FL base: "Not that 21 is a whole lot different than 11 in a store of like 650 or 700 stores" (US FL fleet ref). "we're relatively conservative and we wouldn't be out here talking about this if we weren't confident".
- Capital-light (Hobart): "not swinging hammers"; visual merch update, "low risk and high reward". Exception (Stack): where House of Hoops adjacent to FL → remove wall, eliminate House of Hoops, bigger FL store, new sightlines, apparel space.
- Europe: started Fast Break; "a couple of Fast Break stores in Europe", pleased; slower to get fixtures; mgmt still being built (Matthew Barnes ex-Aldi; brought back a person as chief merchant). "meaningful number" in Europe by BTS, "Won't be near the 250"; FL Europe "probably going to kind of run roughly six months behind what we do in the US."

### FL margins
- Horvers: FL merch margins down 500-600 bps over past 5 yrs → Stack "Correct": too much wrong product, not enough right product → markdowns; significantly reduced apparel. "I won't say that we're going to get that back in year one, but you're going to start to see margin rate improvement."
- Audience: can FL margin recover to historic level? Stack: "I don't think there's anything structural"; no timeframe; (re apparel prompt) "we think that there's meaningful margin expansion"; will give more info as progress; "this was a transformational acquisition".
- Bad merchandising vs lost vendor support (~600 bps): can't bifurcate — lost vendor support → bought next-tier product → didn't sell → markdowns. Fix: better access, allocation, merchandising standards, buying standards. FL lacked defined planning, allocation & replenishment group; too siloed.
- Horvers: buying synergies should be fast (BTS/2H26–1H27) and garage cleaned out → accelerating merch margin rebound? Stack: won't guide; "we expect that margin rate to get better"; won't "get out in over our skis"; "you will see margin rate expansion, which is part of what's taken us to grow this business again and to return it to profitability"; more visibility once proven.
- High-heat: FL "too dependent on that high-heat product" (e.g., Jordan Retro); building base business. FL "pretty much missed the retro run category"; FL Europe didn't buy a retro run shoe, P-6000, "Merrell 5" (as transcribed) — "big miss". More high-heat access than before via brand relationships.
- Kids (KFL / Kids Foot Locker): "really important"; after holiday video calls w/ "well over 100" store managers (10 stores per call): common theme "kids' basketball" — underinvested; will invest.
- FL store culture: Stripers love/know sneakers; internal culture "somewhat siloed", being broken down; Ann Freeman (ex-Nike) President FL; FL Head of HR from DKS (trained under Julie, DKS head of HR); culture "changed meaningfully" vs 6-8 months ago. Hobart: "wait till you see what these Stripers could do."

### Synergies
- Horvers: DKS+FL ~22% of Nike wholesale; 1 pt volume discount would make $100M-$125M look low. Gupta: synergy gdnc "$100 million to $125 million over the medium term"; two components: merchandising synergies + non-merchandise buying; broader opportunity: better access, allocation, partnership (e.g., current DKS ad with athletes sponsored by Nike, Nike gave access to its athletes). "we didn't buy this asset for one year." Did not confirm 22%.

### Category / secular drivers / consumer
- Hobart: consumer/country "obsessed with sport"; intersection sport & culture across portfolio; hardlines seeing bat-launch trends like footwear launches; secular trends "not helping everybody"; DKS increasingly where athletes choose; HoS "redefining retail"; brands give newest product; head-to-toe brand stories; footwear innovation coming from core brands.
- HOKA/On US wholesale "incredible" but slowing (second derivative) — end of footwear cycle? Stack: "we plan to Dick's to 2% to 4% comp growth" (FY2026 DKS comp plan); "We will have growth in footwear"; On & HOKA still door-count upside; lean into what's hot (Nike run construct growing).
- Macro: tax stimulus; gas >$4 national (not $5 like 2022); Liberation Day a year ago. Hobart: consumer "very, very healthy"; no trade down best→better→good; growth across all income demographics (DKS). FL consumer responds well to newness/right product.
- Q4 FY25: traffic declined (comparison). Gupta: Q4 comp 3.1% on top of 6.6% prior yr; 2-yr stack ~10% ("almost about 10%"), consistent w/ all 2025 trend; strength in apparel, footwear, team sports, golf, licensed, collectibles; quality of sales good by income demo. No traffic/ticket split in outlook; FIFA World Cup 2026 contemplated in gdnc.

### House of Sport (HoS)
- Horvers: guiding 67 HoS by end 2027 vs initial 75-100, despite capex $1.5B this yr (FY2026). Stack: "got nothing to do with" capital/mgmt stretch; being patient for better real estate unavailable before (two-level DKS stores in malls): Tysons Corner (DC), Cerritos (LA), Palm Beach Gardens (FL), Barton Creek (Austin), couple of others; DKS will be "main new anchor". Volumes expected "meaningfully higher than what we had originally anticipated"; 75 vs 67/68 "pretty meaningless difference".
- Rent: "don't assume that the rent is going to be higher, or meaningfully higher"; vacant dept store (e.g., Sears) boxes; HoS lets landlords re-lease wing to better tenants at higher rents; DKS can benefit.
- Capex FY2026 gdnc includes "just over 30" HoS openings "including 18 that we'll be opening in 2027" (i.e., spend for 2027 openings); biggest capex driver = portfolio repositioning; no long-term capex outlook; DC opening this yr.

### DKS gross margin drivers / GameChanger (Gupta)
- "we don't call core non-core"; DKS and FL both core. DKS GM secular drivers intact: product access (first unlock, "even further enhanced"); HoS, Field House, DKS Media Network → deeper emerging-brand partnerships.
- Vertical brands margin premium: previously 600-800 bps higher; recently 700-900 bps; "Actually, the margins are even becoming better" than current expectations.
- GameChanger: "almost about $150 million in revenue size, very fast growing, very profitable", "true hidden gem"; Hobart: "about a 40% CAGR three years"; Gupta: 40% CAGR "for the last several years"; no long-term margin rubric given.
- GC/DKS integration (Hobart): GC branded "a Dick's Sporting Goods company"; increasing overt linkage; GC Bat Lab (top HS players test new bats) → in-store "Bat Lab recommendation by GameChanger"; long-term ideas for loyalty program access to GC; GC + DKS (ScoreCard) data combined for co-marketing and Media Network (sells as one: ScoreCard data, GC data, teammate product knowledge). "early innings in terms of overt brand recognition". Gupta: purposeful about combining brands to not distract GC's 40% CAGR.

### SG&A / ad spend
- Horvers: DKS advertising ~4% of sales vs consumables retailers 1.5%-2%. Gupta: drive top and bottom line growth; interplay SG&A vs margin — investments (DKS Media Network, GC, personalization, e-com) in SG&A, benefits in margin; balance near-term results vs long-term capabilities; share opportunity — "We are at 9%, even just looking at Dick's."

### Culture / Going Going Gone (GGG)
- Stack: annual engagement survey benchmarks "almost 2,000 basis points higher than the average retail"; credits Hobart and HR head Julie.
- GGG (value/off-price concept): clears unsold product from DKS stores, enables new full-margin product; "part of our margin rate improvement that we've had over the last several years has come from the execution from our Going Going Gone". Will help FL clear footwear (FL lacked channel); "footwear capacity in the Going Going Gone stores has never been at 100%" → room to take FL excess inventory; won't let "run-on sentence" return.

### Energy / capital allocation
- Energy: direct exposure via supply chain costs (domestic trucking; Nike bought onshore so ocean freight later); "volatile"; team has navigated before.
- Capital allocation (Gupta): #1 invest in business (store repositioning, DC being built this yr, GC, tech). Sales "close to 60%" above 2019 with "same exact number of distribution center[s]". Dividend grown 12 straight yrs. Buyback: consistent but opportunistic; gdnc contemplates offsetting normal dilution only; "plenty of capacity on the balance sheet."
