Sydney Goodman (Retail) | BI athleisure survey May 6-15 2026
### Summary (sec 1)
- Nike can widen lead as budgets tighten/sneaker demand weakens; economic stress hurting discretionary purchases more than brand preference. Lower-income most pressured: 19% of <$50,000 earners don't expect to buy footwear next 6 mo; 38% waiting for discounts/sales vs 31% a year ago. Nike top planned purchase across income, demographics, occasions → recent challenges = execution more than brand affinity. World Cup chance to build loyalty. Adidas No. 2; New Balance and Hoka skew mid/higher income → divide premium demand vs value-seeking.
### Nike Lead Persists (secs 2-8)
- Tariff + economic pressure curbing sneaker demand, but Nike leads BI's latest athleisure survey; struggles reflect execution (inventory, innovation) more than demand; nearly half plan to buy Nike next 6 mo, ahead of Adidas.
- Sec 2: Nike undisputed global footwear leader despite softer post-pandemic performance and share loss in recent years; most cited go-to brand for both athletic performance and everyday lifestyle in US; ranked 1st future purchase intent: almost half (48%) plan Nike next 6 mo vs 53% last year; 58% bought Nike past year vs 59% last year → modest pressure from tariffs, higher gas prices, softer sneaker spending. [chart: Which Shoes Bought/Plan to Purchase]
- Sec 3 World Cup: rising viewership of 2026 men's World Cup could broaden reach for Nike/Adidas; Olympics still larger global audience (eMarketer); ~1.5 billion watched 2022 final vs ~1.1 billion 2018 vs 222 million 2023 women's final; games return to traditional summer slot, no longer overlap NFL season in US. [chart: TV Viewership of Select Global Sporting Events]
- Sec 4 Adidas: regained momentum/trust/relevance since CEO Bjørn Gulden (refined strategy, refreshed product); strong No. 2: 42% bought Adidas sneakers past 12 mo, 27% intend next 6 mo (Nike 58% / 48%). Adidas most used for lifestyle/hanging out with friends (Samba, Gazelle, Taekwondo). Hoka and On still small but rising share (comfort, cushioning, technical design; focused positioning, brand loyalty). [chart: Footwear Worn (Select Brands)]
- Sec 5 Gas prices: higher gas + inflation pressure $50,000-$150,000 households most (buy fewer items, curb discretionary as budgets shift to needs); <$50,000 more likely to delay purchases, fewer store trips; aligns with Walmart commentary (higher-income still spending, fuel/affordability weigh more on lower-income); ≥$150,000 most insulated (largest share no change). <$50,000: 19% forgo shoe purchases next 6 mo vs 13% overall; 38% wait for sales/discounts vs 30% overall. [chart: How Have Higher Gas Prices Impacted Spending]
- Sec 6 Tariffs not significantly denting sneaker buying but rising caution: 32% say tariffs haven't affected purchases; 30% waiting for more discounts; 14% buying less frequently; 10% reduced spending due to broader budget pressure; 8% shopping in advance to avoid future price increases. Shoppers more selective: heavier discount use, fewer purchases, some trading down to cheaper brands or retail channels; "measured caution" not broad pullback. (DKS-READ: trade-down to cheaper retail channels / discount-driven behavior.)
- Sec 7 Generations (past 12 mo brands purchased): millennials led volume: Nike 66%, Adidas 51%, then New Balance, Jordan, Skechers. Gen Z No. 2: Nike 69%, Adidas 49%, Jordan 31%, New Balance 28%, Converse 20%. Gen X/boomers buy fewer; seek comfort/value → Skechers, New Balance. Hoka, On larger millennial base. Purchase reasons: comfort top for all; price/value 2nd except millennials (style/aesthetics 2nd, shifting from Gen Z); brand perception, exclusivity rank low.
- Sec 8 Methodology: BI + Attest, May 6-15; n=1,191; 681 bought athletic sneakers past 12 mo, 469 lifestyle shoes; controlled geography/gender/age to US demographics; ages 18-24 ~13%, 25-34 23%, 35-44 22%, 45+ 40%; women 51%, men 49%.
### Sneaker Spending by Income (secs 9-16)
- Income divide widens: affluent drive footwear demand; lower-income more selective (inflation, tariffs, fuel). Premium/performance brands best positioned for higher-income; value, promotions and wholesale channels gaining importance among budget-conscious. (DKS-READ: wholesale/value channel gaining with lower-income.)
- Sec 9: Nike dominant across income cohorts; >half of <$50,000 and >$150,000 earners plan Nike next 6 mo; Adidas, New Balance, Hoka greater penetration mid-to-higher income (premium/performance); Skechers skews lower/middle income (value). [chart: Top Brands by Income]
- Sec 10: spending intentions relatively stable across income; affluent greatest willingness to trade up → premium/performance/fashion brands; demand increasingly polarized by income; strong premium-positioned brands may outperform.
- Sec 11: replacement primary driver across incomes; >21% of >$100,000 earners cite social-media influence or travel/vacation/event purchases; nearly 1/3 of <$50,000 say most recent purchase motivated by discounts/sales; footwear both functional and fashion purchase depending on income.
- Sec 12: nearly 20% of <$50,000 don't expect to buy footwear next 6 mo; casual/lifestyle + athletic most cited planned purchases across most incomes; lower-income more focused on seasonal/necessity (sandals, clogs, slippers) → could benefit Nike/Adidas expanding into adjacent categories (broader TAM, opening price points); premium/performance resilient among higher income.
- Sec 13 Channel: higher-income favor buying direct from brands; DTC penetration among <$50,000 fell 9 ppts to 32%, while department + discount store shopping rose to 39%; budget shoppers prioritize value, promotions, in-store comparison. Need balanced distribution: DTC for affluent; broad wholesale + value-oriented retail partners critical for lower-income share. (DKS-READ: multibrand/wholesale channel importance rising.) [chart: Where do you typically buy sneakers]
- Sec 14: sneaker purchase volume drops across incomes; higher-income still buy at meaningfully higher rates; lower-income extending replacement cycles, prioritizing essentials.
- Sec 15: 38% of <$50,000 increasingly waiting for sales/discounts vs 31% a year ago and vs 23% of >$150,000; $100,000-$150,000 most likely to report buying fewer items/spending less on discretionary (gas, costs); lower-income already cautious, middle-income increasingly adjusting. [charts: Tariff Impact on Sneaker Buying; sec 16 "Low-Income Consumers Pull Back on Discretionary Spending" = chart only: Higher Gas Prices Impact on Consumer Spending]

### Nike Remains Favorite Brand (secs 24-29)
- Nike leads recent purchases + future intent across generations and income in May survey = 4th poll in two years it dominated; BI view: positioned to regain share via stronger innovation/engagement; early turnaround signs and improving North America trends support sustainable growth, firmer share momentum despite softer spending/more competition.
- Sec 24: ~56% rank Nike among top-3 most-worn brands past 12 mo; ~58% purchased Nike past 12 mo; ~48% plan to buy next 6 mo; modestly improved vs November survey but below last year (softer discretionary + brand-specific pressures); clear lead over Adidas and New Balance. [chart: Which Have You Bought, Worn or Plan to Purchase]
- Sec 25 DTC: nearly 58% of past-12-mo Nike buyers bought directly from Nike (vs 66% last year; softer discretionary + Nike-specific execution) — still well ahead of other channels; DTC central to turnaround (brand control, margin protection, data capture). BI: DTC sales may fall double digits in FY2026 as Nike clears excess/outdated inventory; stronger pipeline should return channel to growth in 1H27. [chart: Where Do You Typically Buy Your Sneakers]
- Sec 26: Nike leads every use category (running, basketball, casual, commuting); largest share in running, basketball, soccer, gym/fitness; also leads lifestyle/errands; Adidas consistent 2nd most categories; New Balance strong running/fitness; Jordan, Vans strong in specific segments but trail. Nike owns Jordan and Converse. [chart: What Footwear Brand Do You Wear For...]
- Sec 27 price: $100-$250 sweet spot; ~29% (casual/lifestyle) and 30% (athletic) of past-12-mo Nike buyers chose this tier = most dominant bracket across generations; most new launches may stay in tier even with planned tariff-related price hikes; Ja 3 "Let Me Be Ja" debuted at $125. [chart: How Much Do Nike Shoppers Plan to Spend]
- Sec 28 innovation pipeline (May launches): Vomero Premium "Black and Rush Violet" $230; Air Jordan 12 Retro $215; Ja 3 x Kool Aid $135; LeBron XXIII "Old Glory" $210; A'Two "WNBA 30th" $155; Caitlin Clark signature shoe slated 2026 (women's basketball driver); Women's Shox Z Calistra "Pale Ivory and Oatmeal" $130; Air Jordan 3 "World's Best" $155.
- Sec 29 (07/23/26) "Downward Revisions Appear to Be Finished": years of cons downgrades may be ending; latest cons projects slight decline in FY2027 rev; estimates could inch up as US turnaround helps, China weakness may persist; sales cons achievable at minimum. "We expect fiscal 2027 sales could near $46 billion, topping estimates." Greater focus on wholesale partnerships, esp Foot Locker, likely to improve assortment and spur growth; Amazon.com partnership and NikeSKIMS can fuel sales. (DKS-READ: Nike wholesale re-engagement, Foot Locker named as key wholesale partner.) [chart: Sales Expectation FY2027, Stock Price]
