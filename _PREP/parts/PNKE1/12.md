### Thesis
- "We don't see a reason to buy the stock": pivotal Q "Is all the 'bad news' now priced in?"; not a good entry point; "still not cheap at ~25x our FY27 EPS estimate", solid rebound priced in; balanced skew. Upside risk: return to positive sales growth with expanding GM faster than expected; downside: rebound takes much longer. Jordan actions reinforce view.
### Jordan at Famous Footwear (channel checks / website analysis) (DKS-READ: Jordan distribution broadening into lower-end/family footwear channel; Foot Locker named among premium-Jordan-dependent retailers with weak trends)
- Famous Footwear website Jordan footwear SKUs ~143 for BTS 2026 vs ~88 BTS 2025 (+~55 SKUs, ~63% y/y); ~39% of current assortment (~55 SKUs) new vs last year.
- Drivers: (1) more silhouettes—Court Connect, Flight Court, Skyline, Trunner Flow; (2) more colorways on carryover franchises; (3) broader age/gender (men's, women's, kids', age-band extensions); substantial portion kids' (BTS relevance). [Fig 1: FamousFootwear.com Jordan assortment growth]
- ~25% of Jordan products on site sold on promotion; avg markdown ~23% (less "on sale" than other brands at Famous Footwear, but newsworthy); historically Jordan sold out at full price, recently some end-of-season clearance; everyday discounting at BTS peak surprising; store checks: many Jordan products already on clearance racks. [Fig 2]
- Premium growth brands On and Hoka have essentially no presence at Famous Footwear. UBS believes Under Armour's mid-2010s expansion into Kohl's contributed to deterioration of its momentum/premium positioning.
- Signs Jordan struggling at high end: retailers dependent on premium Jordan (Foot Locker, JD Sports) recently reported weak sales; UBS Evidence Lab Jordan avg resale price -2.8% y/y July 2026 ($185 vs $190), vs +2.0% in June; several core retro franchises' avg prices pressured recent months. "We are increasingly concerned we may be seeing the beginning of a longer-term decline in Jordan's brand relevance." [Fig 3: Jordan avg last sale price]
- Why Nike doing it: prioritizing near-term sales under "Win Now"; struggling in many regions; Nike historically good at segmenting (serve all channels while keeping premium image). Critical difference: Jordan lacks high-end innovative products to offset low-price-point impression—"almost no new, innovative, high-priced products over the last few years".
- 4 concerns: (1) damages scarcity perception (kids lining up 6:00am Saturday at local Foot Locker for launches); (2) cannibalizes full-price Jordans—Famous Footwear knock-offs of Air Jordan 3 and Air Jordan 11 looks at ~$100 vs Nike.com Air Jordan 3 ~$230; kids sizes ~$68-$70; (3) releasing many styles/colors at once leaves fewer ways to grow Famous Footwear next year and suggests Nike needs it to hit Famous Footwear growth objectives; (4) muddles brand image—if everyday price-point brand, what does it represent; basketball-only limits appeal; fashion-only risky as success driven by retros, not new must-have franchises.
- Risks to view: Nike executes segmentation successfully; Famous Footwear used to attract younger consumers into Jordan ecosystem who trade up later; Jordan brand name more durable than expected; premium sneaker weakness may be cyclical not structural.
