### Hoka (08/26-27/25)
- Crown jewel; 1.1% share of $169 billion global sports-footwear market; DTC reach + wholesale share gains + diversification → mid-to-high teens annual growth through FY2027 (BI scenario). On track to be >$2.5 billion brand in FY2026. Gains more balanced wholesale/DTC this year as brand leans into selective wholesale door additions; franchise refreshes skew near-term growth to wholesale.
- International early stage; China + Europe largest opportunities (wholesale + new stores). Euromonitor: market $169B may reach $222 billion by 2029. Europe majority of Hoka intl sales; China greatest opportunity (recent focus).
- US: top-10 sports-footwear brand in 2023 (topping Asics, Brooks; tied Under Armour for 10th); 7th largest in 2024 (Euromonitor); only 3.4% share.
- DTC leads LT, wholesale skews near term: new launches draw in-store traffic; disciplined marketplace mgmt; wholesale could outpace DTC this year (sell-in, incremental store additions, reorders as sell-through outpaces shipments). DTC could reach 50% of brand sales; e-com majority of DTC; select retail stores in key markets outsized role in awareness.
- Category expansion: hiking, lifestyle, kids biggest near-term opportunities; diversification needed as competitors adopt oversized soles making Hoka "less differentiated than it was several years ago." Kids launched 2023, five styles incl Clifton + Mach.
- Running core: Nike, Adidas, Puma racing for fastest marathon shoe but struggle with everyday runners; Hoka a top-selling brand in US running specialty stores, ~20% share in some stores.

### Ugg (08/26-28/25)
- May grow at least HSD in FY26; potential to top cons 6.6% revenue gain; men's traction catalyst.
- DTC + intl + diversification could boost FY26 sales at least 10%, then sustainable MSD-HSD annual (vs 13% growth in FY2025). Sandals reduce seasonality; men's whitespace. DTC now 50% of Ugg sales; wholesale growth from share gains within existing doors after segmentation + allocation reset over past several years.
- Spangenberg (since mid-2022, ex-Nike chief merchant): product diversification, consumer adoption, franchise evolution; tightly managed marketplace creating scarcity → high full-price sell-through; brand heat at all-time high this year.
- Quote: "[Anne's] got incredible experience over her years in Nike... She spent three years on the ground in China... she is an exceptional merchant, first and foremost..." —Dave Powers, CEO, 1Q23 call July 28, 2022.
- Year-round: weather boots, sandals, sneakers; derivatives of classics (Tazz, platform) → owns five top-selling products vs two; Goldenstar clog/sandal boosted fiscal 1Q sales by mid-to-high teens in past 2 yrs; Tasman slipper adopted as outdoor shoe (sneaker competition). Style count reduced 30%.
- Men ~1/3 of Ugg customer base; men's outpaced women's growth in 1Q (Lo Lowmel sneaker, Tasman weather hybrids, expanded sizing); pro athlete adoption; Post Malone campaign. Apparel slower (fit, channel positioning; loungewear, ready-to-wear).

### Margins (10/03/25)
- GM + OM may decline modestly FY26 (tariffs, elevated freight, wholesale shift); full-price sell-through, phased price increases, opex discipline → EBIT margin over 21%. Rising DTC penetration (esp Hoka) LT margin lever.
- GM expanded 800+ bps since FY2023 (Ugg gains, Hoka mix, channel/inventory mgmt). Ugg GM +11 pts, surpassing Hoka's in 2025 (higher ASP, mix, full-price).
- EBIT margin record 23.6% FY2025 likely declines modestly; opex +11% in 1Q (brand campaigns, retail expansion, EMEA logistics upgrades). Ugg EBIT margin up nearly 10 pts since FY2023 to 39.6%.
- Channel: 1Q Hoka wholesale +30% vs DTC +3%; UGG wholesale +30%, DTC -1%; drivers elevated sell-in, door expansion, consumers trying on new styles; margin headwind FY26; expect DTC recovery toward mgmt LT 50/50 channel mix.
