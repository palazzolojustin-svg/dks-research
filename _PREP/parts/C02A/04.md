## SRC 02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/earnings_calls/DKS_Q3-FY2025_Earnings-Call_2025-11-25.md | 2025-11-25 | Earnings call (FactSet corrected transcript) / Ed Stack (Exec Chairman), Lauren Hobart (Pres/CEO), Navdeep Gupta (EVP/CFO), Nate Gilch (now VP IR) | Q3 FY2025 (13 wks ended ~2025-11-01); first call as combined co. with Foot Locker (FL closed 2025-09-08; 8 wks of FL in Q3)
- Analysts: Robby Ohmes (BofA), Simeon Gutman (MS), Kate McShane (GS), Adrienne Yih (Barclays), Michael Lasser (UBS), Michael Baker (D.A. Davidson), Jolie Wasserman for Christopher Horvers (JPM), Paul Lejuez (Citi), Cristina Fernández (Telsey), Steven Forbes (Guggenheim)

### Reporting definitions (Gilch)
- FL enters comp base beginning Q4 FY2026 (start of its 14th full month post acquisition); all reported comps this quarter and in FY2026 = DICK'S Business only.
- "DICK'S Business" = DICK'S Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, Going Going Gone!, Public Lands banners + GameChanger; DICK'S Business EPS excludes dilutive effect of 9.6M shares issued for FL acquisition.
- "Foot Locker Business" = Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS, atmos banners.
- Q4 FY2025 results tentatively 2026-03-10.

### Stack — prepared
- "doing all that our shareholders would expect us to do to make the Foot Locker Business accretive in 2026"; Stack = largest shareholder.
- DICK'S Business Q3 comp +5.7%; flywheel = differentiated on-trend assortment + omnichannel athlete experience.
- FL closing = "bold and transformative moment"; global platform at intersection of sport and culture; broader consumer base, deeper brand partnerships, larger TAM. "When we announced this acquisition, we knew that business was going to need work."
- Candid diagnosis: "Foot Locker strayed from Retail 101 and did not execute the fundamentals. Post-COVID, Foot Locker did not react quickly enough when its largest brand pivoted toward a direct-to-consumer model, leaving Foot Locker with the wrong inventory, too much of what didn't sell and not enough of what did sell."
- FL pro forma Q3 (full quarter) comp -4.7%, incl -10.2% international.
- Conviction in turnaround "only growing"; bring operational excellence, supplier relationships, merchandise expertise; return FL "to its rightful place as a top player in the specialty athletic channel"; committed to investing to return to profitable growth.
- Leadership: Ann Freeman (longtime former Nike exec) = Foot Locker North America President, supported by mix of FL execs (respected by Stripers, Blue Shirts, brands), DKS leaders, outside talent. Matthew Barnes (former CEO of Aldi; ~3 decades global retail; transformation track record) joins next month as President, Foot Locker International — stabilize then accelerate with targeted turnaround strategies.
- Priority #1: "clean out the garage of underperforming assets": clear unproductive inventory, close underperforming stores, rightsize non-aligned assets; began shortly after Sept 8 close; identified initial underperforming assets globally incl inventory to mark down/liquidate and preliminary number of stores to impair or close.
- Pricing actions initiated late Q3; more aggressive in Q4; intent: vast majority of inventory charges behind by YE → fresh start 2026; position FL for inflection at back-to-school 2026.
- FL Q4: margin rates down 1,000–1,500bps; pro forma Q4 comp down mid- to high-single digits.
- Met all key vendor partners: "fully-aligned," committed to "investing alongside us to reignite" FL. Kicked off 11-store pilot testing product and in-store presentation changes; early, encouraging.
- Expect FL accretive to EPS in fiscal 2026, excluding one-time costs.
- FL people: Stripers, Blue Shirts "love sneakers," "hungry for leadership," "want to get back to playing offense." >100,000 teammates across all banners.

### Hobart — prepared (DICK'S Business)
- Raising FY outlook: comp +3.5% to +4%; EPS $14.25–$14.55 (DICK'S Business).
- Q3 comp +5.7%, growth in ticket and transactions; on top of +4.3% LY and +1.9% in 2023 [note: Q3 FY24 call reported Q3 2024 comp as 4.2%]; continued share gains. GM +27bps, in line with expectations. Non-GAAP EPS $2.78 vs $2.75 PY.
- HoS: opened 13 in Q3 (most ever in a quarter), YTD 16 → 35 HoS nationwide. Field House: 6 in Q3 + 1 last week → completes 15 planned for year → 42 total. Formats delivering "powerful financial results," engagement with athletes, brand partners, landlords.
- Categories: unparalleled access to national and emerging brands; vertical brands resonating; Q3 growth from more athletes, more frequent purchases, more spend per trip; inventory well-positioned for holiday.
- Trading cards/collectibles: with Fanatics launched Collectors Club House in 20 HoS; plan to include in every new location; trading cards, autographed memorabilia; response exceeded expectations; "unique and fast-growing category."
- E-com (multibillion-dollar, highly profitable) again grew faster than DICK'S Business overall. Three examples: (1) app experience incl app-exclusive reservations → leader in launch culture; (2) personalization of content, recommendations, search — e.g., NFL fans targeted with team-specific creative and product recs; (3) new holiday capability to build and share wish lists.
- GameChanger: new features/partnerships/content; new Game Insights feature (fast actionable takeaways for coaches after each game); leadership in "multibillion-dollar youth sports tech ecosystem."
- DMN: great momentum; expanding across new ad platforms; owned + full spectrum of offsite channels; ramping in-store capabilities (interactive digital experiences, programmable spaces) driving brand activations in HoS.
- Holiday brand campaign launched a few weeks ago.

### Gupta — Q3 consolidated and segment results
- Context: FL closed Sept 8; Q3 excludes FL's peak August BTS; includes only 8 weeks (Sept–Oct) of FL = historically unprofitable period for FL.
- Consolidated net sales +36.3% to $4.17B: ~$931M FL partial-quarter contribution + DICK'S Business comp +5.7%.
- DICK'S Business comp: 2-yr stack 10%, 3-yr stack 11.9%; avg ticket +4.4%, transactions +1.3%; broad strength across footwear, apparel, hardlines.
- FL pro forma Q3 (entire quarter) comp -4.7%: North America -2.6%, Foot Locker International -10.2% (mainly Europe softness).
- Consolidated gross profit $1.38B, 33.13% of sales, -264bps y/y — "driven entirely by the mix impact" of lower-GM FL. DICK'S Business GM +27bps, in line.
- Consolidated non-GAAP SG&A +40.8% (+$320.9M) to $1.11B, deleveraged 84bps vs LY non-GAAP; $259.9M of increase from FL. DICK'S Business SG&A $ +7.7%, deleveraged 45bps, in line (strategic investments digital, in-store, marketing).
- Consolidated pre-opening $30.6M, +$13.8M y/y (13 HoS + 6 Field House opened in Q3).
- Consolidated non-GAAP OI $242.2M, 5.81% vs $289.5M, 9.47% LY. DICK'S Business non-GAAP OI $288.6M, 8.92% of sales. FL Business operating loss $46.3M in quarter, mainly GM decline from late-Q3 pricing actions. FL pro forma non-GAAP OI for entire Q3 ~$6.8M (incl profitable Aug/Labor Day BTS not in DKS results).
- Non-GAAP other income (mainly interest) $12.7M, -$7.8M y/y (lower cash, lower rates).
- Consolidated non-GAAP EBT $239.9M, 5.76% vs $297.1M, 9.7% Q3 LY.
- Non-GAAP tax $59.4M, 24.7% rate; DICK'S Business income taxed at low-20% rate; combined higher due to FL EMEA where full valuation allowance remains.
- Consolidated non-GAAP EPS $2.07. DICK'S Business non-GAAP EPS $2.78 on 81.2M shares (excl FL-issued shares) vs $2.75 LY. Bridge: FL operations -$0.52 (GM decline + higher tax rate); increased share count -$0.19 (shares up 5.9M, prorated for 8 weeks of ownership). [d] $2.78 - $0.52 - $0.19 = $2.07.
- GAAP EPS $0.86: incl non-cash gains on non-operating investment in FL stock, and $141.9M pre-tax FL acquisition-related costs.
- Cash & equivalents ~$821M; no borrowings on $2B unsecured credit facility.
- Inventory +51% y/y consolidated; DICK'S Business +2% y/y; FL Business pro forma ~+5% y/y; clearing unproductive FL inventory underway.
- Q3 net capex $218M ($201M DICK'S Business, $17M FL Business); dividends $109M.

### Q&A
- Q [Ohmes, BofA]: DICK'S drivers and holiday confidence. A [Hobart]: 2-yr stack 10%; 7 quarters in a row >4% comp; differentiated assortment (strategic partners' newness, emerging brands, vertical brands); engaging athlete experience in stores and digital. Q3 growth across all key categories; BTS/back-to-sport — footwear, apparel, team sports "knocked it out of the park"; also golf, licensed, trading cards doing well; great gifts across portfolio.
- Q [Ohmes]: FL inventory cleanup effect on DKS; store closures count/timing? A [Stack]: still addressing; some stores will close; also evaluating upside — how many need closing vs can be made more profitable; guidance on Q4 call. A [Gupta]: FL Q4 GM -1,000 to -1,500bps mainly from quickly addressing unproductive inventory to make room for "excitement assortment" for 2026.
- Q [Gutman, MS]: FL softer than Street in Q3, Q4 OI slightly negative — building blocks to 2026 accretion? A [Stack]: clean out garage (old inventory, impair underperforming assets); management team (Ann Freeman NA — respected by DKS and brands; Matthew Barnes international — "he's a Brit, and we think that EMEA truly needs to be run by a European"; real changes to international approach); Stripers/Blue Shirts = "secret weapon"; every brand has renewed interest in supporting a "stable and growing Foot Locker"; "complete alignment with the brands." Confident FL accretive to 2026 earnings.
- Q [Gutman]: is accretion standalone FL incl synergies, not buybacks? FL GM parity with DKS feasible? A [Stack]: won't guide until Q4; FL GM "can be meaningfully different than it is right now"; FL lacked access/allocation to key product; stores out of stock; anecdote: NY store manager said "we're a great running store" and just got Nike's running construct last week — sold at full price; confident of "meaningful increase in their gross margin." Accretion = "core Foot Locker"; separately "we'll be opportunistic based on what happens with the stock. We may buy back some stock."
- Q [McShane, GS]: can FL return to full price after heavy Q4 markdowns? Will Q4 market be discount-heavy, hurting DKS footwear? A [Stack]: marked-down goods are older unsold product; new fresh innovative product (which FL mostly lacks now) will sell full price, arriving into 2026. Right now don't expect discounting "meaningfully different than it was last year"; DKS has different, innovative, more premium, less fully distributed product; promo activity not expected to impact DKS much.
- Q [Yih, Barclays]: FL = underperforming ops vs structural; banners that don't fit; 1,000–1,500bps — write-off/reserve, third-party channels? A [Stack]: largely historically underperforming operations, away from Retail 101 (right product, right store); some capital to invest in stores, but 11-store test was "pretty capital-light": removed most inventory, re-laid out wall; DKS visual merchandising expertise; FL walls "merely a run-on sentence of shoes" → segmented to show what's important; "pretty enthusiastic about the results." Markdowns on older underperforming SKUs; at YE a program to sell some to a jobber to clear remaining inventory for fresh 2026 start. A [Hobart]: core challenges = underperforming operations, inventory management, Retail 101; transferring DKS merchandising (art + science), visual presentation, marketing/brand belief, brand relationships, operational focus via coaching/mentorship. [Banner question not directly answered.]
- Q [Yih]: are markdowns on lifestyle with no crossover to DKS premium performance? A [Stack]: marked-down product "is not key product at DICK'S Sporting Goods"; older non-selling inventory; "clean out the garage" visual caught on globally at FL.
- Q [Lasser, UBS]: is FL accretion base $14.25–$14.55 FY2025? Dependence on BTS sales inflection? A [Gupta]: yes, base = $14.25–$14.55 FY2025. Building blocks: clean out garage, position inventory, bring newness/excitement assortment → GM and merch margin expansion first priority; store portfolio review of unprofitable stores — not only closure but whether right product/innovation can turn them profitable; some unproductive assets won't be part of go-forward business; starts with sales and margin; profitable growth esp. from BTS 2026.
- Q [Lasser]: ring-fencing core DKS; Q4 implies flat to slightly positive DKS comp — why? A [Hobart]: ring-fencing DICK'S team was an "absolute prerequisite"; 8–10 weeks in, more confident; FL team set up; Ed spending time there; DICK'S team fully focused; teams share learnings without distraction. Q4: coming off 5.7% comp, lapping 6.4% Q4 LY; raised comp — "the high end of our previous guidance now is the low end"; "really bullish on the holiday"; "We don't ever guide to the best possible outcome."
- Q [Baker, D.A. Davidson]: 11-store test results/details. A [Stack]: won't disclose results; early, very encouraged; not just wall re-layout: removed older product, added newer fresher product; bringing apparel back to FL (they "had really kind of walked away from the apparel business"); apparel "selling really quite well"; more color on test at Q4 call with 2026 guidance; basic Retail 101 → meaningful impact.
- Q [Baker]: why BTS not spring as inflection? A [Stack]: spring product bought by previous management; will talk to brands to plug holes; Q3/BTS 2026 = first time with complete control over assortment.
- Q [Wasserman for Horvers, JPM]: can DKS affect FL orders before Q3 2026; % of spring ordered; Kick It Club? A [Stack]: some impact on Q1 and Q2, "hopefully, a little bit more on Q2 than Q1"; brands helping get needed product; big difference in Q3 when team has fully bought and merchandised product.
- Q [Wasserman]: core DKS Q3 GM — promo, tariffs, GC. A [Gupta]: GM +27bps on top of +70bps LY. Market promo "dynamic"; participated in select BTS promotions. Tariff impact within merch margin in quarter; merch margin still +5bps on top of ~60bps positive impact LY; slight unfavorable mix: licensed business performed really well but slightly lower margin. Expect GM expansion for FY, 2H and Q4; GC and DMN support GM expansion this year and future.
- Q [Lejuez, Citi]: $500M–$750M charges — cash vs non-cash; # stores reviewed; split US/intl/banner? A [Gupta]: 10 weeks in; store detail on Q4 call. Three buckets: (1) unproductive inventory — "quite a decent chunk," vast majority addressed in Q4; includes some store portfolio valuation; (2)/(3) deeper review of assets incl technology assets and legacy contracts, evaluated in Q4. Mix of cash and non-cash: inventory = cash; balance-sheet asset cleanups = non-cash.
- Q [Lejuez]: synergy capture in FY2026; bigger than $100M–$125M LT? A [Gupta]: lot of work done; ongoing conversations with brands and non-merchandise vendors; better line of sight ~12 weeks (Q4 call); will pursue additional opportunity.
- Q [Fernández, Telsey]: FL merchandising mix — basketball/sneaker culture/kids; apparel via private label or national brands? A [Stack]: basketball remains very important; enthusiastic about basketball constructs coming across a couple of brands; FL stepped away from national brand apparel toward private brands; private brands have a place, but national brands to see "meaningful increase" in FL apparel → drive AURs, "very profitable."
- Q [Fernández]: FL reimagined store remodel program — continue/pause? A [Stack]: "interesting test"; parts good, parts need rethinking; e.g., remove "Kick It Club" and "drop zone" in center of store (unproductive, more of a social space) → better sightlines, repurpose space for apparel presentation.
- Q [Forbes, Guggenheim]: demographic differences (FL more lower-income exposure?). A [Stack]: merchandise FL for FL: more basketball-inspired, more trend-inspired, "definitely more urban"; DKS more sport-led plus lifestyle, "more suburban concept"; all income levels want new, innovative, differentiated product, which FL lacked; more in 2026, fully invested by Q3 2026.
- Q [Forbes]: confirm FL Q4 slightly negative adj EBIT vs $118M LY; normalized Q4 / LTM adj EBITDA vs ~$395M [ph] in presentation? A [Gupta]: yes, comparison is to FL's normalized non-GAAP Q4 LY; link between 1,000–1,500bps GM decline and slightly negative OI = inventory cleanup. [LTM EBITDA not directly answered.]
