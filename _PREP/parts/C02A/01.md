## SRC 02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/earnings_calls/DKS_Q3-FY2024_Earnings-Call_2024-11-26.md | 2024-11-26 | Earnings call (FactSet corrected transcript) / Lauren Hobart (Pres/CEO), Navdeep Gupta (EVP/CFO), Nate Gilch (Sr Dir IR) | Q3 FY2024 (13 wks ended ~2024-11-02) + YTD 39 wks
- Analysts: Adrienne Yih (Barclays), Simeon Gutman (MS), Brian Nagel (Oppenheimer), Kate McShane (GS), Robby Ohmes (BofA), John Kernan (TD Cowen), Jolie Wasserman for Christopher Horvers (JPM), Michael Lasser (UBS), Kelly Crago for Paul Lejuez (Citi), Justin Kleber (Baird)
- IR: Q4 FY2024 results tentatively 2025-03-11.

### Prepared remarks — Hobart
- Q3 comp +4.2% (calendar-shift adjusted), driven by 4 pillars: omnichannel athlete experience, differentiated product assortment, DICK'S brand engagement, teammates. Excellent back-to-school (BTS); continued share gains. Q3 GM +67bps vs LY non-GAAP on higher merch margin; EPS $2.75. Raising FY outlook again: comp +3.6% to +4.2%; EPS $13.65–$13.95.
- Investing across digital & stores, focus on elevating training, service, product knowledge. Sport influences culture and vice versa; House of Sport (HoS) serves performance + lifestyle.
- HoS: opened 3 in Q3 + 2 more early Nov → 19 open ahead of holiday. 2025: ~15 HoS openings; on track 75–100 HoS by 2027.
- Field House (next-gen 50,000 sq ft DICK'S format, inspired by HoS): "continue to do very well in both sales and profitability"; opened 5 in Q3 + 4 early Nov → 26 open. 2025: ~20 Field House openings.
- Texas = growth opportunity: investing in new HoS, marketing, infrastructure; broke ground on Fort Worth TX DC, expected open early 2026.
- Digital: improving dicks.com and DICK'S app; expanded product launch reservations in app beyond footwear and golf to product drops in other key categories; new teammate tech tools incl expanded RFID.
- GameChanger (GC): Q3 >5.5M unique active users, +21% y/y; ~2M avg daily active users in quarter; "multibillion dollar youth sports tech ecosystem."
- HoS/Field House opened doors to new brand partnerships, strengthened existing. Priority categories performing very well led by national brands + flagship vertical brands; "deliberately taken steps to be fully stocked across key products and categories for holiday." Optimistic on product pipeline into next year. Holiday campaign w/ NFL legend J.J. Watt and family.

### Prepared remarks — Gupta: YTD (39 wks)
- Net sales +4.8% to $9.55B. Comp (calendar-adjusted) +4.7%: avg ticket +3.7%, transactions +1%.
- EBT $1.12B, 11.75% of sales vs non-GAAP EBT $975.3M, 10.71% LY. EPS $10.43 vs non-GAAP $9.08 LY, +15%.

### Q3 results
- Comp +4.2% ("clearest view" = calendar-adjusted): avg ticket +4.8%, transactions -0.6%. BTS strong across footwear, athletic apparel, team sports.
- Net sales +0.5% to $3.06B, incl unfavorable calendar shift from LY 53rd week moving a key BTS week out of Q3 into Q2: ~-$105M sales, -$0.35 EPS.
- Gross profit $1.09B, 35.77% of sales, +67bps vs LY non-GAAP; merch margin +84bps (favorable sales mix, assortment quality) [Q&A later says +83bps]; partly offset by occupancy deleverage from calendar shift on total sales.
- Non-GAAP SG&A +7.2% to $787.1M, deleveraged 162bps vs LY non-GAAP; ~65bps of that from calendar shift; rest = strategic investments (marketing, technology, talent) + higher incentive comp.
- Pre-opening $16.8M, -$3.6M y/y, favorable vs expectations due to timing of new store openings.
- EBT $297.1M, 9.7% of sales vs LY non-GAAP EBT $321.1M, 10.6%; incl ~95bps unfavorable calendar-shift impact.
- EPS $2.75 vs LY non-GAAP $2.85; calendar shift -$0.35.

### Balance sheet / capital allocation
- Cash & equivalents ~$1.5B; no borrowings on $1.6B unsecured credit facility.
- Inventory +13% y/y at Q3 end: conscious lean into key items/categories expected to drive growth in Q4 and early 2025; "inventory is clean and well-positioned"; clearance inventory "down meaningfully" y/y.
- Q3 net capex ~$185M; dividends $90M; repurchased ~35,000 shares for $6.7M at avg $194.22. YTD buybacks $170.3M; FY buybacks still ~$300M.

### Q&A
- Q [Yih, Barclays]: why DKS wins with full-price selling/AUR-led comp while value players win; multi-brand power. A [Hobart]: 3 straight qtrs >4% comp; access to differentiated product; merchants; national + vertical brands; athletes prioritizing healthy/active lifestyle, team sports, outdoor, golf. 1.5M more athletes entered ecosystem in quarter; growth across all income demographics. Tailwinds: women's basketball "on fire," expanded NCAA football playoff, 2026 World Cup mostly in US, 2028 LA Olympics; DKS rooted in performance but increasingly lifestyle of sport.
- Q [Yih]: tariffs (25% Canada/Mexico, 10% China announced prior evening). A [Gupta]: timing/categories still unknown; navigated 2018–2019 effectively, same playbook. Vertical brands "very, very negligible amount of exposure" — diversified supply chain from China, little Mexico/Canada exposure; will navigate with national brand vendors.
- Q [Gutman, MS]: inventory tracking to YE; slow cold start? A [Hobart]: deliberate investment in differentiated assortment: key items, key brands, fleece, footwear, licensed (NCAA playoff expansion); "we don't peanut butter spread the inventory"; surgical. Two Q4 opportunities: (1) avoid being broken on size/color pre-Christmas in key items like Nike fleece; (2) in warm-weather climates transition from holiday to spring merchandise earlier (accelerated). Many products have life into 2025; results expected back half of Q4 and 2025. Implied Q4 comp gdnc raised. A [Gupta]: inventory in line with expectation; weather only very early Q4, vast majority of holiday ahead; clearance "down significantly."
- Q [Gutman]: SG&A leverage vs GM going forward. A [Gupta]: 2025 outlook next quarter. YTD comp 4.7%; midpoint FY comp ~4% on top of 2.6% LY comp; EBT margin at midpoint +40bps y/y. "puts and takes" between GM and SG&A; using GM expansion to fund strategic SG&A investments (e.g., RFID in stores to find product quickly in apparel/footwear) for 2025+. "look to us to continue to drive strong top-line and strong profitability improvement with little bit of an interplay between the margin and SG&A."
- Q [Nagel, Oppenheimer]: HoS rollout and comp impact. A [Hobart]: opened 4 in past couple weeks; athletes drive longer distances, spend more time; vendors bring whole brand to life esp. co-lab spaces; landlords/mall operators see incredible traffic increases to mall → access to better long-term real estate. A [Gupta]: ended FY2023 with 12 HoS; expect ~20 at end FY2024; +15 in 2025; 75–100 by 2027. Vast majority of HoS openings are relocations/remodels of existing stores and stay in comp → little difference comp vs total sales growth. Selective new locations (e.g., Prudential Center HoS 2024 was brand new) "far and few between."
- Q [Nagel]: is Q4 gdnc raised vs internal; short holiday (5 fewer days). A [Hobart]: beat Q3 estimates; super excited for holiday; J.J. Watt marketing kicked off few weeks ago; "appropriately cautious" given uncertain macro and fewer holiday days; Black Friday/holiday has expanded into early Nov for years. A [Gupta]: raised Q4 top-line expectations on inventory position and store/web readiness, balanced vs macro and short calendar; FY EPS raised with margin raised slightly vs prior, offset by SG&A investments for 2025.
- Q [McShane, GS]: product overlap with other retailers. A [Hobart]: highly differentiated footwear; exclusives across hardlines incl team sports; enables surgical promo participation; "less vulnerable to the entire market going up and down"; DKS doesn't disclose overlap amount. A [Gupta]: vertical brands (flagship CALIA, DSG) resonating; basket-building; merchandising curation drives top-line and margin gains.
- Q [McShane]: ticket drivers — same-SKU inflation vs mix. A [Gupta]: consistent with prior; differentiated product not exposed to promo pressure → better margin recovery and higher ticket; "mix, the differentiated product, and the pricing opportunity."
- Q [Ohmes, BofA]: GameChanger flow-through/LT opportunity; new HoS brand partners. A [Hobart]: forecasting FY2024 GC revenue $100M; "highly profitable" subscription software model; Q3 5.5M unique active users +21%; ~2M daily active users; expanding video streaming enabling basketball and other sports; streamed video games +50%. Co-presented "Bat Lab" in Florida (athletes demo/rate bats). A [Gupta]: GC ~$100M sales 2024, growing 30%–40% CAGR each year for last several years; SaaS extremely profitable; youth sports tech landscape multi-billion dollars. A [Hobart]: HoS expanded/built brand partnerships; national brands love Co Lab spaces; new/emerging brands e.g., GOAT (lacrosse brand) w/ holiday presentation; vertical brands demoed in Co Lab; Free People Movement came in via Co Lab; brands from HoS translating to big partners chain-wide.
- Q [Kernan, TD Cowen]: deck slide 11: HoS $35M omnichannel sales and Field House $14M omni sales in year 1 with 20% four-wall omni EBITDA margin — trajectory yr 2–3? A [Gupta]: stores in yr 2 (some in yr 3) comp positively in second year as excitement builds; teammates/community learn to engage with in-store field, climbing wall, community center; will give more color.
- Q [Kernan]: softlines/footwear outlook, footwear decks, ON/HOKA door expansion. A [Gupta]: 4 core categories: athletic apparel, athletic footwear, team sports, golf — growth continues from them, gaining strong share. "footwear is the engine that drives the train." Premium full-service footwear deck + House of Cleats differentiate assortment/service; brands ON, HOKA, Nike, New Balance; 90% of stores have premium full-service footwear deck; opportunity to expand ON, HOKA deeper into chain.
- Q [Wasserman for Horvers, JPM]: Q4 merch margin view; shrink vs 2019. A [Gupta]: Q3 GP +67bps, merch margin +83bps (mix, product quality, assortment). Expect Q4 merch margin to increase again (watch promo landscape). Q3 shrink slightly better to in line w/ expectations; Q4 shrink expected ~flat y/y.
- Q [Wasserman]: cadence — election, hurricanes, warm weather. A [Hobart]: strong BTS early quarter (footwear, apparel, team sports); warmer than liked late quarter, no material comp impact; election: nothing meaningful/material; "good for the country that there's some certainty"; consumer "just fine" after.
- Q [Lasser, UBS]: 2025 merch margin expansion drivers beyond private label/inventory. A [Gupta]: 2025 gdnc next quarter. Three levers: (1) differentiated assortment/full-price selling; (2) pricing team models → surgical one-on-one promotions/pricing; (3) vertical brands carry 600–800bps higher margin than national brands; growing penetration (CALIA, VRST, DSG flagship apparel). Biggest unknown = tariffs.
- Q [Lasser]: inventory up just under ~$0.5B y/y; Going Going Gone! clearance engine; FY EBT gdnc +40bps vs ~70bps/qtr avg so far — math for Q4? A [Hobart]: inventory in key items/categories not expected to "become toxic"; not a heavy markdown risk; guided Q4 margin increase. A [Gupta]: +40bps = EBT expansion at midpoint. Q4 pressures: ~$200M unfavorable sales impact (53rd wk + calendar shift); Q4 pre-opening slightly higher than LY (Q3 pre-opening under expectation due to timing; 3 new HoS opened right at start of Q4); caution for macro and shorter holiday.
- Q [Crago for Lejuez, Citi]: SG&A philosophy, incentive comp impact, comp needed to leverage. A [Gupta]: drive top/bottom line and position for long term; investments sometimes on margin line (e.g., upcoming DC) or SG&A (technology); 2025 gdnc next quarter; won't give specific leverage-point comp — depends on investment levels. Did not quantify incentive comp.
- Q [Crago]: 2025 store openings, relocations vs remodels. A [Gupta]: 15 HoS openings are HoS only; plus ~20 Field House; some Golf Galaxy openings; full 2025 count later. Vast majority HoS/Field House are relocations/remodels → little new store count growth; 2024 square footage +2% y/y from new stores.
- Q [Kleber, Baird]: did softer late-quarter exit (warm October) drive transaction decline? A [Hobart]: No — "every month was a great quarter"; weather not material overall, maybe a little in back half; no dip at tail end. A [Gupta]: weather puts/takes within category portfolio; cold-weather softness offset by moderate-temp categories; golf exceeded expectations in back half; portfolio helps navigate month-to-month transitions better than past (doesn't fully insulate).
- Q [Kleber]: which HoS elements most impactful in Field House? A [Hobart]: HoS is cornerstone to reposition entire portfolio; improved service, better assortment/product access, experiences translate to Field House — visually "a smaller version of a House of Sport" in 50,000 sq ft box; elevated service/product spreading chain-wide; HoS impact broader than HoS stores alone.
