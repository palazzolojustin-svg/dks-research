### Item 1 Business – overview
- Banners: DICK'S Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, Public Lands, Going Going Gone!, experiential DICK'S House of Sport (HoS), Golf Galaxy Performance Center (GGPC) (all US). Owner/operator of Foot Locker (FL): Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS, atmos — N. America, Europe, Asia, Australia + licensed stores in Europe, Middle East, Asia. Owns GameChanger (youth sports app: streaming, scheduling, comms, scorekeeping).
- Founded 1948 Binghamton NY (Dick's Clothing and Sporting Goods) by Richard "Dick" Stack; Edward W. Stack joined full-time 1977, President/CEO 1984 of then 2-store chain; renamed DICK'S Sporting Goods Apr 1999.
- FY2025: acquired Foot Locker → 2 reportable segments: DICK'S and Foot Locker. "DICK'S Business" = DSG, Golf Galaxy, GGG, Public Lands, GameChanger (employees "teammates", customers "athletes"). "Foot Locker Business" = FL, KFL, Champs, WSS, atmos (customers "sneakerheads"; store staff "stripers", Champs "blue shirts").
- Fiscal yr ends Saturday closest to end of Jan.
- Strategic pillars: athlete experience, differentiated product, brand engagement, teammates. Mission: inclusive environment; create/build leading brands; community impact through sport; shareholder value via growth & relentless improvement; aim "best sports company in the world."

### Item 1A Risk factors (company-specific substance)
- Macro: inflation, rates, confidence, debt burdens, gov policy shifts → lower traffic, comps, avg transaction value. Geopolitical: Middle East "(particularly the recent U.S. military operations in Iran)", Ukraine, Venezuela → supply chain, fuel/transport costs, cyber; uncertainty in EU esp. affecting FL Business given intl footprint. Reduced demand may require promotions/pricing → GM pressure.
- Competition: multiple formats incl vendors DTC; real-time price comparison; marketing incl digital/social; tech disruption incl AI/ML.
- Product costs: raw materials; tariffs/trade tensions in China, Mexico, Canada "(including recently enacted tariffs and potential additional shifts in tariff policies in the future)", FX; may need alternative suppliers/pricing actions. Transport: fuel, weather, war, port congestion, gov shutdowns, labor; "the military conflict recently launched in Iran has had, and may continue to have, adverse impacts on global trade with respect to goods passing through the Strait of Hormuz and material supply chain disruptions resulting in significant increases in oil and fuel prices globally"; higher transport costs for company & vendors.
- Intl sourcing/ops: most vertical brand merch made abroad; duties, quotas, tariffs, trade remedy, taxes on imports, excise/VAT, digital services taxes, customs valuation; FX on vendor pricing.
- Consumer trends: omni-channel expectations; FL Business global multicultural customer base; advance purchase commitments → markdowns/write-downs; premium athletic footwear/apparel fashion-driven, reliance on suppliers' innovation, long lead times.
- Vertical brands ~13% of DICK'S Business sales; risks: impairments/write-downs if abandoned, product liability/recalls w/o indemnification, sourcing reputation, IP/counterfeits, third-party IP claims, marketing. New concepts: HoS, Field House, GGPC, FL Fast Break pilot; experiential (climbing walls, batting cages, fields, ice rinks, group fitness) → bodily injury/property liability, build/maintenance costs, skilled staff, competitor copying, license compliance.
- Reputation: social media/misinformation.
- Strategic plans may hurt near-term results; incl FL integration; investments in tech/AI/ML, eCommerce, GameChanger, DICK'S Media Network, supply chain, ScoreCard, vertical brands, real estate (openings, remodels, relocations), Fast Break, talent/org re-alignment.
- Real estate: DICK'S = larger-format concepts & repositioning; FL = productive urban corridors, high streets, enclosed regional & neighborhood malls. Factors: landlord relationships/consolidation, construction costs, sale-leasebacks, local regs. Meaningful portion of stores esp FL in malls → mall traffic risk (anchor closures, online shift); DICK'S affected by regional/off-mall nodes; need to operate multiple concepts in proximity. Unprofitable stores → impairments, lease obligations, termination charges, post-assignment/sublease liability; "These risks may be more prevalent within our Foot Locker segment as we continue our review of the global Foot Locker business store fleet and evaluate closure of certain underperforming stores."
- Distribution network: multiple DCs worldwide, eComm FCs, stores as forward distribution points; rely on third-party carriers for substantially all shipments; switching risk.
- Data security: payment card data, PII; third-party providers; AI-accelerated threats; "experienced non‑material data security issues from time-to-time"; not aware of any material breach to date; could discourage loyalty participation; costs may exceed insurance.
- Privacy regs: GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA, other state/intl laws, PCI; "we have received inquiries from government authorities from time-to-time regarding our data practices"; regs may limit data use for personalization/marketing.
- IT systems: third-party payment processor; hybrid remote work; gen-AI/ML risks (biased datasets, latency, cost).
- Labor: wage rates, equity comp, immigration compliance, intl labor standards; unionization/organizing efforts domestic or intl could raise costs.
- Key people: at-will, no key-person life insurance generally; recent FL Business exec hires; Hobart (CEO) and Stack (Exec Chair, "continues to oversee our merchandising group and key strategic growth initiatives, including the Foot Locker Business"); succession planning.
- Seasonality/weather: within DICK'S Business Q2 (golf, team sports, back-to-school) and Q4 (holiday, cold-weather goods) highest; many stores in cold-weather regions; warm winters / wet-cold spring-summer risk; climate/natural disasters.

### Critical accounting estimates
- Inventory: DICK'S Business = weighted average cost (direct merch cost incl freight, net of shrink, obsolescence, vendor allowances), LCNRV. FL Business = primarily retail inventory method (incl freight, DC & sourcing costs; net of shrink & reserves where prices not yet marked down to market), lower of cost or market; WSS and atmos = weighted average cost. 10% change in obsolete inventory reserves at 2026-01-31 → ~$10.6M pre-tax income impact FY2025. RIM requires estimates of markups, markdowns, shrink; judgment to differentiate promotional vs other markdowns.
- Shrink accrued as % of merchandise sales on historical trends; physical inventories through year; reserve = cumulative loss since last count. 10% change in shrink reserves at 2026-01-31 → ~$4.4M pre-tax FY2025.
- Goodwill: reporting-unit FV via income + market approaches; indefinite-lived intangibles (trademarks/trade names) via relief-from-royalty. **FL acquisition recognized goodwill $618.8M and indefinite-lived intangibles $710.0M; carrying value approximates FV at 2026-01-31 → "any significant or adverse change in estimates or assumptions could result in an impairment in the future."** Other goodwill/intangibles FV substantially exceed carrying; 10% change no impairment indicated. No significant goodwill/intangible impairments FY2025/24/23.
- Long-lived assets: store-level, income approach over remaining lease term (sales growth, GM, store payroll). 2023 "Business Optimization" (talent, org design, spending, cost structure) → non-cash impairment charges (Note 12). No other significant LLA impairments FY2025 or FY2024.
- Business combinations: outside appraisal firms; preliminary PPA adjustable up to 1 yr post-close.
- Deferred tax valuation allowance: significant judgment esp regions w/ cumulative losses, limited near-term profitability visibility, recent acquisition-related changes (relevant to FL foreign jurisdictions).
