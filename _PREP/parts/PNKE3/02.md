### Thesis (09/23/26)
- NKE curve may tighten toward equal/better-rated retailers; 10-yr currently wide of peer regression curve while 2030 and long-dated bonds trade inside direct comps. Support: A2/A+ (Moody's/S&P), $9b liquidity, GM expansion beginning fiscal 1Q27.
### Liquidity/debt
- Cash+ST investments $9b > $8b funded debt at May 31 (funded-debt net cash before $3.1b operating-lease liabilities); title cites "$11 Billion of Debt" [incl leases]. Two committed facilities totaling $3b + $3b CP program, undrawn.
- Maturities: $1b Nov-2026, $1b Mar-2027, then $1.5b 2030; readily fundable; NKE hasn't disclosed repay vs refinance.
- Debt well above pre-pandemic but metrics solid vs single-A retail peers. Peer leverage: Adidas adj net leverage 1.6x at 2Q (target <2x); Ralph Lauren net leverage usually <1x; Target net leverage 1.4x end fiscal 2Q26.
### FY26 results/cash flow
- FY26 rev flat reported, -2% c-n. OCF fell 22% to $2.9b ($2.87b vs $3.70b) as working capital used $1.68b incl $684m tariff receivable (substantially collected after YE); AR consumed $1.21b. FCF ~$2.18b after $684m capex. Dividends $2.41b; buybacks $123m, none in 4Q; dividend > FCF, but tariff collections + $9b liquidity limit near-term funding pressure. Return to large buybacks before 2026-27 maturities addressed would weaken BS flexibility. Collection should improve FY27 cash conversion.
- Inventory: EMEA up low double digits; Greater China down double digits (reduced sell-in, promotions).
- NKE beats Adidas on inventory turnover and cash-conversion cycle.
- Knight family ownership ~20% (largely Class A) per BI calc; significant reduction could shift financial policy.
### Brand/mix
- Global sportswear share 2025: NKE 14% vs Adidas 8.7%. Sports footwear NKE 23% vs Adidas 12%; sports apparel NKE 7.9% vs Adidas 6.3%.
- FY26 Nike Brand rev: ~45% NA, 28% EMEA, 14% APLA, 13% Greater China; ~61% wholesale / 39% Nike Direct. Wholesale +4% c-n; Direct -8% (Digital -12%, stores -4%). Performance categories gaining momentum; mgmt expects Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear negative in FY27 before improving in 2H. "Brand scale preserves recovery capacity, and full-price sell through determines when it converts to earnings."
- Progress clearing wholesale-channel inventory and bringing new product; faces lingering tariff costs, consumer uncertainty.
### Bond valuation (09/17/26)
- $6b of NKE bonds in Bloomberg US Corp index; regression curve trades wide of equal/better-rated retail peers (single A and AA), 10-yr point 6 bps wider than peer regression. Select maturities at premium to Target, Home Depot, Amazon.
- 2030 bond: 20-bp discount to Target (A2/A) in Aug-2024 → now 2-bp premium. 2046/2050 bonds traded wide of peer curve earlier this year, now slight premium; 2050 bond 4 bps inside Home Depot (A2/A) similar maturity vs 6-bp discount in May-2026.
- Spread gap can narrow as GM expansion begins FY1Q27, supply-chain actions lift FY27 profitability, Nov-2026 maturity addressed.
### Embedded post-1Q earnings outlook (10/01/26; Goyal/Goodman) — =same as BI "Nike Revenue Reset Extends Into Fiscal 2028" (see SRC 12) summary
- Sales pressured into FY28 as mgmt cuts supply in sportswear/Jordan and cleans up Greater China digital marketplace; performance keeps growing. FY27 rev gdnc high-single-digit decline; 2Q ~400 bps added pressure from EMEA Cyber Week and NA wholesale comparisons. GM pressured by discounts, mix, fixed-cost deleverage, higher input costs, partly offset by supply-chain savings, better full-price realization. China digital cleanup multiple seasons. New cost program "Pace" to lower cost base ~$2.5b, most savings FY29-30; reinvestment could limit near-term upside.
- 1Q: FX-neutral sales -5%, reported -4%; FXN NA +2%, EMEA -5%, Greater China -26%, APLA flat; Nike Brand Digital -13%, Nike-owned stores -5%, wholesale -1%; GM +60 bps (lower warehousing/logistics); SG&A -3% (lower wage, admin); EPS 48c beat cons.
