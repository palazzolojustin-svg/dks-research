### Methodology
- Fielded May 6-15 (2026) w/ Attest; n=1,191; 681 bought athletic sneakers past 12m, 469 bought lifestyle shoes; controlled for geography, gender, age. Age: 18-24 13%, 25-34 23%, 35-44 22%, 45-54 22%, 55-64 12%, 65+ 6% (45+ = 40%). Women 51%/men 49%. HH income: <$25K 9%, $25-50K 16%, $50-75K 15%, $75-100K 15%, $100-150K 16%, $150-200K 15%, $200K+ 14%. Region: Northeast 26%, Midwest 26%, South 27%, West 22%.
### Key takeaways (exec summary)
- 58% purchased Nike sneakers in past 12m (Adidas 42%). 45% typically buy sneakers directly from brand. 77% use sneakers for lifestyle/style/hanging out.
- "Nike can widen its lead as household budgets tighten and sneaker demand weakens, with BI survey data showing economic stress is hurting discretionary purchases more than brand preference."
- Lower-income most pressured: 19% of <$50K earners don't expect to buy footwear next 6 months; 38% waiting for discounts/sales, up from 31% a year ago.
- Nike top planned purchase across income levels, demographics, occasions → recent challenges reflect execution more than brand affinity; World Cup chance to build loyalty. Adidas No. 2; New Balance and Hoka skew to middle/higher-income shoppers (premium vs value-seeking divide).
- 67% bought 1-3 pairs in last 12m (n=800).
