## SRC 02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/conferences/DKS_Goldman-Conference_2025-09-04.md | 2025-09-04 | FactSet corrected transcript, Goldman Sachs Global Retailing Conference fireside; DKS: Ed Stack (Exec Chairman), Hobart (CEO), Gupta (CFO); host Kate McShane (GS, covering DKS ~20 yrs) | ~1 wk after Q2 FY2025 print; FY2025 = FYE 2026-01-31
- Stack's first appearance at this conference in ~7-8 yrs.

### Q2 FY25 / newness
- Q2 comp +5% on top of +0.5% LY (tough compare per Stack). "anything that's new from a design standpoint, technology standpoint is doing extremely well. Things that have been around for a while are a little bit slower" —Stack; newness drove the 5% comp.
- Hobart: 6th consecutive quarter of >4% comps; growth across entire portfolio (hardlines + softlines; diamond sports now having launches like footwear); growth across every income demographic; "We're not seeing trade down." HoS and Field House "doing incredibly well"; "in a position to continue to gain significant market share."
- Innovation examples (Stack): Nike new running construct (Peg/Pegasus, Structure, Vomero "great"); diamond sports hardline brands, new bats ("Betts" in transcript) at $500 apiece "flying off the shelves".
- Sport/culture intersection "going to last for a while"; World Cup 2026 (matches across North America: first match LA, final NY, plus Dallas, Atlanta, Boston etc.) → local markets "jazzed"; good for business and long-term US soccer; Olympics 2028.

### Foot Locker
- "we closed on that on Monday" (i.e., 2025-09-01 week; closed Monday before 2025-09-04). "huge opportunity" globally and in US. FL "struggled a little bit over the last couple of years"; fix is "[ph] retail 101": right product, right place, right time. Store "striper culture" (sneaker-loving young associates) "one of the main reasons why we bought them." "the next couple of quarters… we're going to kind of clean things up, get real assessment of what's going on, and we can give you a lot more information on our fourth quarter call as we go into 2026." —Stack
- Hobart: DKS #1 sporting goods retailer in US, "soon to be in the globe", only 9% market share; FY25 focus on driving footwear business (FL), e-com, portfolio repositioning.

### Inventory / tariffs
- Q2 inventory +7% vs sales +5%; "really, really clean"; clearance levels "at historic lows"; inventory growth expected to continue to moderate in 2H.
- Tariffs: Q2 impact "negligible"; will grow in back half (as other retailers said); manage via surgical pricing capabilities + national brand partnerships, balancing assortment/innovation.
- Clean inventory approach: curated, measured buy depth; constant dialogue with national brands; mistakes cleared via Going, Going, Gone! (50 stores). Q2 GM +30 bps "on top of over 200 basis points of gross margin" (verbatim; compare basis unstated).
- Inventory growth: decelerating; prior months' significant investment due to out-of-stock issues, to be more size/color appropriate and regionally relevant; tapered last quarter, growth to keep narrowing (Hobart).
- Pricing: "Very, very small changes" on like-for-like, very selective/surgical; watching demand elasticity; "more to come".

### Promotions
- DICK'S Deals Day/"Green Day" (July, around Prime Day) run for several years; "we don't have enormous sitewide promos anymore"; surgical/targeted; great success.

### Vertical brands
- Outpace company growth; margin rates "700 basis points to 900 basis points higher" (Stack; vs 600-800 bps cited in 2024 SRCs 01-03). Brands: VRST, CALIA, DSG (family brand), Walter Hagen; some priced at premium/national-brand levels; "real following". Penetration "will grow a bit as a percentage, but I don't think it'll ever get to be super high" because footwear is key and DKS won't do vertical footwear; tech bats kids want what's used in College World Series/pro wood bat. Helps margins.
- Maxfli golf ball: played on tour by Lexi Thompson, "[ph] Fred Fung", Ben Griffin (won twice on tour this year; playing in Ryder Cup) → business "gone right through the roof"; Maxfli history 13 majors, >100 tournaments.

### Golf
- Golf COVID beneficiary; new golfers stuck; entry-level-club buyers now upgrading; tech advances (drivers, hybrids, high-lofted fairway woods); innovation cycle "very good"; expected to continue; golf = 3rd largest department; "huge opportunity" in golf apparel merchandising.
- Golf business = roll-up of original DKS golf stores, Golf Galaxy (started Minneapolis), Golfsmith stores (after its bankruptcy); were "good, but… not great" → Golf Galaxy Performance Center: performance stats, better apparel, broader services, lessons; performing well; will continue opening.

### Gross margin drivers (Gupta; 2019 vs 2024)
- (1) Differentiated product → full-price selling, non-promoted product, insulation from promo intensity. (2) Vertical brands: Field & Stream was largest vertical brand in 2019; exited; sales replaced by DSG, CALIA, VRST at much higher margins. (3) Mix: exited Hunt (significantly lower margin than company); plus clearance mgmt/Going Going Gone!/pricing & promo capabilities. These continue as future drivers.
- New drivers: (4) GameChanger: "$100 million of sales last year, very profitable. We expect that business to grow to $150 million." SaaS recurring revenue with non-retail margins → GM expansion as it grows. Hobart: GC growing "40-plus percent CAGR every year". (5) DICK'S Media Network (RMN): others ahead in journey but DKS has unique athlete database; avg in-store customer has >1 brand in basket; invested in infrastructure, tech, partner reporting; sales force in place; no guidance on size/timing; "I would expect it's going to become a more meaningful part of our business going forward." —Hobart
- DMN channels: DKS website, GameChanger, in-store (HoS: product info displayed as you interact with product; to expand), and offsite (buying media for partners using anonymized/tokenized DKS data).

### Back-to-school / holiday
- BTS spans Q2 and Q3; Q2 5% comp, broad growth in footwear, apparel, team sports (back-to-school/back-to-sport). Holiday: "I feel great"; new assortment, brand innovation; new trading card business with Fanatics ("very small right now, but incredibly giftable"); weighted vests a holiday item. "we're bullish. That's why we took our guidance up." —Hobart

### Units / formats
- Total unit count ~flat in core DKS (exception: Golf Galaxy growth); square footage rising. "tired old chain" avoidance; retail theater.
- HoS: 16 openings FY25; 13 planned in Q3 incl New Jersey (NY market) opening next week; end FY25 at 35 HoS; FY26 ~15-16 openings; aspiration 75-100 by 2027.
- Field House "is the workhorse"; vast majority of portfolio is 50,000-60,000 sq ft boxes; all new 50K openings, relocations and conversions will be Field House format. Field House footwear area ~50% bigger than traditional DKS store; House of Cleats; some interactivity (climbing-wall-like).

### Rapid-fire
- 2H vs 1H consumer: "same" ("no worse" —Stack).
- 2026 freight/wages/materials: TBD, "a lot that will be changing in the next six months"; DKS "employer of choice", retention rates "phenomenal", store engagement high; freight capabilities good.
- 2026 market share consolidation: "speed up"; "tariffs could have an impact on it." —Stack

### History / change agent (Stack)
- Transformation predated pandemic. After Parkland shooting exited gun business; whole outdoor business ~"$1 billion"; margin rates ~1,700 bps below company avg; needed to recapture ~60% of sales to break even on GP$; 10-store test recaptured 105%; then 25 more stores; then changed "virtually everything": dropped newspaper circulars for brand/digital marketing; expanded team sports; invested in footwear: moved from shared-service footwear (all product on floor) to premium footwear → access to product Nike and others previously wouldn't sell DKS; changed apparel; vertical brands: got behind CALIA, launched VRST, created DSG (previously licensed product from Reebok).
- Pre-pandemic several quarters of ramping sales; post-pandemic surge; market expected reversion of sales/margins but durable changes (footwear access, vertical penetration) sustained margins.
- Store of the future: project began few years pre-pandemic; built parts in lab store at HQ; "not different enough" → scrapped; ~6-7 yrs ago restarted with precept "build the concept that will kill DICK'S Sporting Goods" → HoS; key elements cascaded to Field House. "we do virtually nothing the same as we did back then"; will keep innovating.
