## SRC 02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/conferences/DKS_Barclays-Conference_2024-12-03.md | 2024-12-03 | FactSet corrected transcript, Barclays Eat, Sleep, Play, Shop Conference fireside; DKS: Hobart (CEO), Gupta (CFO); host Adrienne Yih (Barclays US Specialty Retail) | post-Q3 FY2024
- Host framing: Barclays calls DKS "the next great compound growth retail story". Hobart = 3rd CEO in history (2021), after Exec Chairman Ed Stack and founder Dick Stack; joined Feb 2011 as CMO; President 2017. Gupta joined 2017, CFO 2021; leads finance of HoS/next-gen, spearheads GameChanger growth strategy. Same-day earlier webcast referenced. Safe harbor on IR site (IR contact Nate).

### Consumer
- Consumer "has held up very well"; multi-year trend of prioritizing healthy/active lifestyle (fitness, golf, team sports, outdoors); increasingly coming to DKS for all athletic + athletic lifestyle needs.
- Q3 FY24 comp +4.2%; >4% every quarter this year; beat-and-raise every quarter → consumer "a little better than we even have predicted". Growth across all income demographics; new athletes joining.
- Sport ↔ culture convergence a tailwind; premium full-service footwear decks + HoS give access to performance and lifestyle-of-sport product.

### Tariffs / 2025 planning
- Tariffs: "so much unknown" (negotiation tactic vs implementation); "going back to the playbook that we had in 2018 and 2019" for vertical brands and partnering with national brands.
- 2025 budget relies on controllables: brand portfolio; 15 additional HoS openings next year (FY25); cascade into Field House (50K format): "We intend to open an additional 20 of those next year." Ground-up revenue/comp build irrespective of industry/consumer; consumer tailwind would help; can pivot if geopolitical/tariff issues grow.
- Excited for 2025: "moment in sports" in US: post-Olympics, women's basketball craze, World Cup in US 2026, LA Olympics 2028; vision "be the best sports company in the world"; opportunities in footwear, licensed, team sports. Gupta: sport/culture intersection.

### Promotions / holiday / Q4
- Holiday: some players have "a little too much inventory"; "a little bit promotional" (similar to LY). DKS differentiated product → no knee-jerk promos; decides how many weeks/how deep to participate in a MAP break based on products needing turn. "We factored in all of that promotionality into our guidance. We're still expecting a merch margin increase in the fourth quarter." —Hobart. More "surgical".
- Analyst: DKS comp driven by basket/transaction size (AUR, mix) vs peers sacrificing AUR/margin to drive transactions.
- Gupta: also resilient e-com platform, in-store service; basis for raising Q4 comp expectations vs 3 months ago.

### Brand relationships
- Not transactional/"battleground negotiation"; strategic partners: share trends, product pipelines, feedback; "results in a profit sharing that makes sense." Key brand relationships "one of our biggest assets".
- Emerging brands: key strategy; HoS (19 HoS now) as on-ramp; collab space where brand controls presentation; brands wary of wholesale can go at own pace; example FP Movement (Free People Movement) started at Public Lands then HoS.
- Analyst framing pre-pandemic op margin ~5% → peak 16%/16.5% → ~11%.

### Footwear cycle
- Lifestyle (adidas Terrace collection, flat-bottom; soccer shoes as lifestyle) and performance (HOKA, On) "100% they can live together". "footwear is the engine that pulls the train"; outfit starts with foot; kids need on-court/on-field, slide, cleat, lifestyle, running, training shoes. HoS premium full-service footwear decks "bigger than some of our competitors in the mall"; belief category continues to grow.

### Moat / competition
- Moat: strategic brand relationships (trust takes time); athlete experience across stores + online; service model/training; "secret sauce" = ~50,000 teammates & culture (empowered store teammates).
- No single national #2 competitor; compete with best specialty player in each category.

### Margin bridge 2019→now (Gupta)
- Analyst: since 2019 sales +~50%, GM$ +~80%, EBIT$ +>200%; op margin ~5% → peak 16.5% → ~11% (pre-tax).
- Drivers: (1) sales +>50% → fixed-cost leverage (supply chain, occupancy). (2) 2019 base depressed: 2018/2019 EBT ~"$300 million or $400 million" during hunt-category exit (changed sq ft, investments); refocus on "core four" categories: apparel, footwear, team sports, golf = where share gains came last 5 yrs. (3) Merch margin: national-brand access + vertical brands (DSG brand didn't exist then, Second Skin brand era, CALIA in infancy); less exposed to promo intensity; vertical brands 600-800 bps higher margin. (4) E-com (always profitable) leverage from in-house platform; BOPIS launched 2019. (5) Supply chain/value chain; better clearance margin recovery. (6) RFID invested 2+ yrs, starting to bear fruit: labor productivity, recovering in-four-walls sales lost to unlocatable product.
- Forward framework: goal drive sales + profitability long term; FY24 beat top-line expectations, grew merch margin and GP significantly above expectations; reinvesting portion of beat → SG&A deleverage; still growing top & bottom line.
- Future margin levers: differentiated product; "just launched our retail media network capability"; vertical brands ended FY23 at $1.6B, goal $2B.

### House of Sport
- >100,000 sq ft (~2x typical DKS store); climbing walls, track & field, treadmills in fitting rooms; "first step" of repositioning portfolio. Ed Stack's challenge: design a store that, if opened across the street, "would kill DICK's Sporting Goods". Landlords love it, drives mall traffic → better real estate; seasonal fashion showcase (incl team sports). Field House = 50,000 sq ft version with same principles.
- Economics: "the returns that we see in a year one are really strong" (not 2nd/3rd-yr return story). "we don't want to have a tired, old chain"; pricing/availability ubiquitous, differentiation = expertise, service, full-basket solution selling, community engagement.
- Ross Park (Pittsburgh) HoS opened <1 mile from a "very high-performing" 50K box; purposeful self-disruption; returns "really attractive".
- ~100 stores/yr up for lease renewal → flexible portfolio; looking at 2026-2027 lease events to reimagine markets.
- Texas: ~50 stores, do well; underpenetrated (analyst: vs double-digit exposure of some retailers); distribution gap in middle of US (DCs on East and West Coast) → new DC opportunity in Texas; Katy and Baybrook HoS conversions "really, really exciting results"; 2025 opening plans to be shared at next earnings call. Hobart: strong competition in TX; entered Houston "with a splash" ~5-6 yrs ago; relatively new; holiday campaign with Chris Paul, Simone Biles, Dak Prescott, Quinn Ewers competing as "Chief Holiday Decorating Officer" in 4 major TX cities; "putting roots down" past 10 yrs.

### Capital allocation (audience Q)
- Q [audience]: leverage balance sheet for buybacks? A [Gupta]: "optimally levered" not under-levered; conservative; pre-COVID (not IG) intent was no debt; IG status "really important"; COVID treasury mgmt a learning experience → caution. Last 3 yrs returned 100% of FCF to shareholders; doubled dividend in 2023; dividends paid >10 yrs. Priorities: invest (capex up to $800M this year/FY24), maintain liquidity/capital structure, then return excess FCF: grow dividend, opportunistic buybacks.

### Nike
- Nike = largest vendor, 24% of sales in 2023 (analyst: from just under 20%). "80% of our shoppers come in and want a multi-branded experience." Premium full-service footwear decks driven by previously lacking cool Nike shoes to complete outfit. "there's growth ahead of us for sure with Nike because we still have allocation and access gains to have"; seen Nike pipeline, excited; balanced portfolio, vertical brands growing; "we don't feel like we have an overexposure." —Hobart. Analyst: 2022 Nike partnering elevated other brands' engagement.

GameChanger better understood now: "$100 million size that is growing at 30% to 40% each year, extremely profitable"; youth sports tech TAM $30B-$40B; opportunity to combine GC subscriber/youth-athlete data with DKS data.
