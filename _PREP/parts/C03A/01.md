## SRC 02_DKS_TRANSCRIPTS/conferences/DKS_Goldman-Conference_2024-09-05.md | 2024-09-05 | FactSet corrected transcript, Goldman Sachs Global Retailing Conference fireside; DKS: Lauren Hobart (Pres/CEO), Navdeep Gupta (EVP/CFO); host Kate McShane (GS) | day after Q2 FY2024 print
- Intros: Hobart joined DKS 2011 as SVP/CMO; President 2017; CEO 2021. Gupta joined 2017 as SVP Finance/CAO; CFO 2021. (Safe harbor read, not transcribed.)

### Consumer / Q2 FY24
- Q2 comp +4.5% on top of +2% LY. "feeling really good about both the momentum in our business and the state of our consumer." —Hobart
- Q2: increased ticket, increased transactions; 1.6M new athletes; no trade-down; growth across every income demographic (Hobart: "maybe a little bit of an outlier"). Attributes to value of healthy/active/outdoor lifestyle, team sports, plus long-term strategies.
- Strategy pillars: (1) differentiated product (5-7+ yr strategy; best product from new-to-category to elite/high performance; premium full-service footwear experience enabling performance + lifestyle "cool shoes"); (2) athlete experience (service in store/online, getting into right product; reinvention of entire portfolio: House of Sport (HoS) learnings translating into Field House concept and across portfolio).

### Industry / TAM / share
- TAM $140B; DKS share "[ph] just under" 8.5% today. Opportunity = industry growth + share gains.
- Industry pre-pandemic grew "[ph] low" single digits; over last ~4-5/6 yrs ~6% CAGR; "maturation" since post-pandemic surge; DKS can gain share "even if the industry continues to remain relatively neutral to flat." Analyst framing: DKS said 2024 industry ~flat, growth mostly share gains.
- 3/4 of US population participating in active lifestyle; 27M kids under age 17 active in youth sports = core customer.

### Brand access / wholesale
- Hobart: "from 2016 on, we say nothing about our business is the same, absolutely nothing." Now access to cool footwear/"cool kids"; sport + culture converged; HoS at center of sport/culture/lifestyle/performance; can outfit entire athlete (previously had to go elsewhere for cool shoes). Vendor relationships "at an all-time high"; access across board incl. depth of soccer cleats/performance footwear at "much higher elevation".
- Gupta: transformation beyond product: website capability, in-store experience, balance sheet/capital structure "at a very different level" vs pre-pandemic.
- On brand returning to wholesale (vs DTC): "many of our preferred partners, I think are realizing the benefits of wholesale and that's a positive for us." —Hobart. DKS has square footage to showcase brand apparel+footwear, performance+lifestyle; HoS collab spaces tell brand stories. Partnerships strong through "back and forth of the last few years, but even stronger now."

### ScoreCard / GameChanger
- >70% of sales through ScoreCard loyalty; in place ≥20 yrs; "best data set in youth sports"; first-party data used to help vendors target athletes; "only upside" in making it more exciting/access to product.
- GameChanger (GC): 6M athletes on platform (parents, coaches, families); GC users who are also ScoreCard members: revenue 2x avg ScoreCard user; higher profitability/engagement.
- GC = largest scoring & statistics app in youth sports; started in diamond sports (baseball/softball); now video, kid-specific highlights, stats, scatter charts, recruiting tool; users spend 45 min/day ("TikTok levels of engagement"). "$100 million in revenue this year"; "highly profitable"; freemium subscription SaaS, relatively inexpensive; post-game article, radio/listen to game.
- Gupta: GC growth ~35%-40% CAGR over recent 5 yrs; expanded to basketball, volleyball; AI auto-pans camera, auto-generates highlights emailed to families.

### Vertical brands
- Renamed private label "vertical brands": building brands with personality, also filling whitespace. DSG = opening price point brand (high fashion/function, attractive price; previously underserved), now advertised. CALIA = women's apparel (Inspire collection; gym-to-life). VRST = men's apparel (new Limitless Pant). Contrast with old label swapping (Umbro exclusive, Nike ACG mentioned by analyst).

### Gross margin
- GM well above pre-pandemic. Three drivers of merch margin (Gupta): (1) differentiated product → more control of pricing/promotion; (2) vertical brands: outperformed company average in Q2; carry 600-800 bps higher margin rate → mix; (3) pricing & promotion mgmt capabilities (selective/targeted).
- Q2 marketplace "much more dynamic" (more promotional) vs Q1 per other retailers; DKS still delivered +90 bps merch margin expansion Q2; same drivers expected going forward.

### Store formats / unit growth
- HoS: >100,000 sq ft; experiential (track, rock climbing wall, batting, golf swing), higher service, collab spaces, elevated product & fitting rooms; "best expression of the DICK'S brand". Translating into ~50,000 sq ft Field House. Mall developers/landlords see increased mall traffic & sales/sq ft → access to "whole different real estate base"; "really great returns" shared prior qtr; "moving forward aggressively with 75 to 100" (by 2027).
- HoS count: ended FY23 with 12; goal open 8 in FY24; 2 opened YTD, 6 more coming in next ~quarter-plus; ~20 by end FY24; ~15 opening in 2025; march to 75-100 by 2027. Constraint: need 100,000-120,000 sq ft site in right node; learning on markets/demographics. First = Victor (NY); then small markets like Knoxville did "fantastic"; opened Prudential Center (Boston, urban) earlier this year; Ross Park Mall (Pittsburgh). Works small/mid-market and urban.
- Field House = internal name for next-gen 50K sq ft format; ~11 to open FY24, reaching ~26. Combination of new openings and remodels/relocations. DKS owns none of its stores (vast majority leased, few HoS exceptions); ~100 stores/yr come up for lease renewal → opportunity to reposition/remodel with landlord. "we don't want to have a tired old chain."
- Golf Galaxy Performance Center: focus on fitting experience, TrackMan tech, PGA Pro lessons; stated goal ~45-50 GG Performance locations; most new GG openings are this format. Field Houses also have golf with TrackMan (enthusiast goes to GG Performance Center).
- Concepts: True Runner, Chelsea Collective (past); Public Lands = "newest baby", iterating on right store size for outdoor; "I'll never say never" on more offshoots but focus on reinventing portfolio.
- Going, Going, Gone!: ~50 stores; moves clearance out of regular stores; serves value-conscious athlete; brings new athletes to database; full size/color runs; good online activation/engagement. "Relentless improvement."

### Rapid-fire
- 2H24 consumer vs 1H: strong momentum expected to continue; "we actually raised our guidance" (Gupta).
- 2025 costs: tariffs "still unknown"; cost pressure will moderate vs recent past but some pressure "especially on the labor side."
- Value-seeking cyclical vs secular: both; consumers choose where to spend, willing to spend on passions/performance; growth across every income demographic.
- Points of distribution: more; return to positive square footage growth this year, sq ft +~2% (FY24); new distribution center opening 2026; stores critical e-com fulfillment nodes.
- Holiday promo: industry "extremely promotional last holiday"; hope slightly less; "I don't expect it to be materially different." —Hobart
