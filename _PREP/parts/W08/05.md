## SRC 01_DKS_SEC_FILINGS/Foot-Locker-merger-docs/425-deal-communications/DKS_425_2025-05-15_120906.md | 2025-05-15 | Form 425; acc 0001193125-25-120906 | Transcript of DKS "Acquisition of Foot Locker" investor call; Nate Gilch (Sr Dir IR), Edward W. Stack (Exec Chairman & Chief Merchandising Officer), Lauren R. Hobart (President, CEO & Director), Navdeep Gupta (EVP & CFO) | FL deal + prelim Q1 FY2025
- Analysts: Brian Nagel (Oppenheimer), Kate McShane (Goldman Sachs), Paul Lejuez (Citigroup), Michael Lasser (UBS), Adrienne Yih (Barclays), Christopher Horvers (JPMorgan), Simeon Gutman (Morgan Stanley), Michael Baker (D.A. Davidson), Robert Ohmes (BofA).
- Gilch: presentation on investors.dicks.com; prelim Q1 results released same morning; Q1 2025 report scheduled Wed May 28, call 8:00 PM ET [sic]; comments focused on FL deal; Form S-4 to be filed.
### Prepared remarks — Stack
- DKS approaching deal "from a position of strength" (Q1 prelim). Convergence of sport and culture "never been stronger"; consumers focused on health/wellness, casualwear, sneakers as self-expression.
- Combination = "new global leader in the sports retail industry"; admire FL community of "strikers" [transcribed; =Stripers]; "By applying our operational expertise, we are confident we can enhance Foot Locker's position in the industry and unlock their next phase of growth."
- DKS growth mostly organic, but acquired/integrated Galyan's — similarly sized acquisition relative to DKS scale at the time.
- Slide 4: US TAM across footwear, apparel, hardlines ~$140B; w/ FL total store count >3,200 in key regions; opens ~$300B global sports retail market; FL international footprint "will make DICK'S a global business." Confidence based on diligence, brand-partner feedback, ops expertise.
- Slide 5 FL profile: global footwear & athletic apparel retailer; banners Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS, atmos; ~2,400 stores across 26 countries at end-2024 (North America, Europe, Asia, Australia, NZ) + licensed stores in Europe, Middle East, Asia; >400 store refreshes last year (2024); digital sales grown to 18% of total rev; 2024 FL rev $8B. "major accelerator of our future growth"; deal "is about the consumer"; FL consumer to get "a more complete assortment and a reinvigorated brand."
### Prepared remarks — Hobart (slides 6–11)
- Five benefits: (1) global platform in growing sports retail industry; (2) serve broader set of consumers across differentiated concepts; (3) strengthen brand-partner relationships via global reach; (4) invest in future growth via industry-leading omni-channel experience; (5) unlock operational efficiencies to create shareholder value. "This is a growth oriented transaction grounded in operational discipline."
- Slide 7: FL US real estate highly complementary—stores in different markets serving different needs/demographics; expands DKS internationally for first time; established ops in Europe, Middle East, APAC; $300B global TAM; tailwinds: sport/culture convergence, health/wellness, casualwear shift.
- Slide 8: FL reaches sneakerheads & lifestyle shoppers vs DKS performance-focused core; shared omni-channel commitment.
- Slide 9: more robust platform for brand partners (established & emerging) — new demographics, formats, brand positioning, international footprint.
- Slide 10: FL modernized digital, refreshed stores; continue investing in experiential formats (DKS House of Sport; FL reimagined concept store) and digital.
- Slide 11: operational efficiencies "primarily through procurement and direct sourcing."
### Prepared remarks — Gupta (deal terms)
- Prelim Q1 2025: comps +4.5%; non-GAAP EPS $3.37, ahead of last year; Q1 report May 28.
- Terms: $24/sh; FL holders elect $24 cash or 0.1168 DKS shares; election not subject to min/max cash or stock; implied equity value ~$2.4B, EV $2.5B.
- Synergies: "$100 million to $125 million in cost synergies in the medium-term, primarily from procurement and direct sourcing efficiencies"; "well-supported and achievable." EPS: "accretive to EPS in the first full fiscal year post-close excluding one-time transaction costs and costs to achieve these synergies."
- Committed to strong B/S and investment grade ratings; unanimously approved by both boards; expected close 2H 2025, subject to regulatory + FL shareholder approval.
- Combination creates "one of the most dynamic, diversified platform in the global sports retail industry."
### Q&A
- Q [Nagel, Oppenheimer]: why now; brand discussions? A [Stack]: looked at it a long time; FL pressure largely from Nike relationship — Nike moved to DTC/away from wholesale, "hurt Foot Locker pretty significantly"; Nike now "really leaning back into wholesale"; "Elliott [Hill] and his team are doing a great job"; FL "going to be a beneficiary"; "timing of this is perfect"; talked to a couple of brands — enthusiastic, cite DKS operational excellence; brands "willing to help."
- Q [Lejuez, Citi]: timing of $100–125M synergies (medium term?); mostly GM line—SG&A synergies?; will customer see banner changes? A [Gupta]: more color closer to close; opportunity on GM (scale) and SG&A (procurement). A [Hobart]: plan to run the two businesses separately; DKS team focused on DKS growth ("flywheel is turning"); FL team focused exclusively on FL; small team led by Ed to help integration/share expertise; synergies counted on = procurement/purchasing. Customer: FL "Lace Up" plan has terrific elements — reimagined stores, refreshed stores, investing in tech ["tag" as transcribed], personalization, consumer experience, stripers, culture; consumer gets "a reinvigorated Foot Locker"; run as separate entities; "The consumer may or may not know that DICK'S and Foot Locker are one."
- Q [Yih, Barclays]: pre-signing Nike/brand conversations?; market share assumptions in $140B / $300B?; DKS 25% and FL 59% of merchandise purchases [from presentation] as % of combined sales? A [Stack]: yes, talked with several brands; they gave view on FL; "some people might not ... immediately see everything that we see"; "see around the corners"; World Cup 2026 ("biggest sporting event this country has ever seen", matches in cities nationwide, footwear big part), Olympics 2028. A [Gupta]: footwear & apparel largest part of TAM; "footwear is the engine that drives the train"; footwear as combined co "close to about 50% of the total sales of the company."
- Q [Yih]: take out FL bonds? leverage was 2x, now ~3x? A [Gupta]: consideration mix unknown until close; finished last year w/ $1.7B cash; even if fully debt-financed "it won't impact unfavorably our credit ratings today"; intend to maintain strong IG rating; financing = combination of cash, long-term debt, equity; working w/ advisors on possibly exchanging FL obligations, "if need be we'll be able to take those notes out."
- Q [Gutman, MS]: FL shrinking on same-store basis while DKS grows; DKS could take FL share anyway—why buy? A [Stack]: FL serves consumers in neighborhoods DKS "in many cases are never going to get" — can't find 50,000–60,000 sq ft sites w/ return; footwear "the engine that pulls this train"; FL "bore the brunt" of Nike DTC shift; Nike moved back to wholesale; "Foot Locker is a very important part of Nike's long term strategy"; "we're pretty conservative. We don't get over skis"; board "very adamant" deal cannot impact DKS (House of Sport, Field House, GameChanger, tech investment, flywheel); "That glide path is still there." A [Hobart]: TAM grows $140B→$300B via global reach; additive to DKS story.
- Q [Gutman]: distribution protection over 5 yrs (Nike, HOKA, On, Adidas)? A [Stack]: makes DKS "much bigger, much more important partners"; no indication brands will broaden high-heat distribution; brands want specialty environment, premium positioning; "you never know."
- Q [Ohmes, BofA]: Nike % of FL sales to rise? A [Stack]: "there's that possibility"; FL will get more high-heat product; "Nike business will probably go up... at a higher margin rate because they will be able to sell more product at full price"; "a big precept of this."
- Q [Ohmes]: DKS/HoS growth rate changes? FL store rightsizing? A [Hobart]: nothing changing for DKS growth, HoS, Field House; FL team assessed all stores, some potentially closing; DKS to get involved in next several months; "It's not our expectation that we'll be closing a significant number of Foot Locker stores."
- Q [McShane, GS]: why now given tariff risk? A [Stack]: tariffs change; "good news that came out of Geneva last week"; managed through tariffs before; working w/ brands & supply chain; "all the pieces fell together right now."
- Q [Lasser, UBS]: why different from failed retail horizontal mergers (strong + underperformer)? A [Stack]: footwear is core competence; visibility to FL "performing meaningfully better" (Nike pipeline improving, new Nike team "doing a great job"); FL apparel opportunity — FL said apparel deteriorating, underinvested; kid dresses "toe to head"; "clear line of sight"; get FL "back to its rightful position"; "if we didn't see this clear line of sight... we wouldn't be doing it"; "we knew there would be some skepticism."
- Q [Lasser]: FL comp assumption in underwriting? A [Stack]: won't disclose; at close will provide comp ["camp" as transcribed], EPS, accretion view.
- Q [Horvers, JPM]: does FL international knowledge enable DKS abroad? A [Hobart]: run separately; DKS current strategy excludes international expansion; "who knows about the future." A [Stack]: huge US opportunity (HoS, Field House transition, traditional stores); "we have no plans to take DICK'S internationally."
- Q [Horvers]: customer crossover 20% vs 50%? A [Hobart]: "our market share in this $140 billion market that we've been competing in thus far is still around 80%" [as transcribed]; DKS footwear growth not from taking FL share — 90% of DKS stores have premium full-service footwear decks, improved assortment; "I don't see these two things as being cannibalistic."
- Q [Baker, DA Davidson]: analogy to Golf Galaxy acquisition? A [Stack]: similar—strengthened golf brand partnerships, helped Golf Galaxy and DKS; can't recall share figures; expects same in footwear.
- Q [Baker]: FTC issues/divestitures? A [Hobart]: "we are not expecting any regulatory concerns... not expecting that to be too cumbersome."
- Q [Baker]: procurement synergies = merch or non-merch? A [Gupta]: both — procurement/direct sourcing vendors and non-merch vendors.
- Close [Stack]: next talk at earnings call in a couple of weeks.
