### Item 1 Business
- Inc. 1967 Oregon. Largest seller of athletic FW & apparel in world. Channels: NIKE Direct (owned stores + digital = "NIKE Brand Digital") + wholesale (independent distributors, licensees, sales reps) in nearly all countries; nearly all product made by independent contractors outside US.
- Brands: NIKE, Jordan, Converse. Strategy: "sustainable, profitable long-term growth by leading with sport, creating innovative, 'must-have' products, building deep personal consumer connections with our brands and delivering compelling consumer experiences through digital platforms and at retail."
- Equipment line: bags, socks, sport balls, eyewear, timepieces, digital devices, bats, gloves, protective equip. Jordan (Jumpman; sport performance + streetwear) reported within NIKE Brand geographic segments. Converse (Boston; Converse, Chuck Taylor, All Star, One Star, Star Chevron, Jack Purcell) reported stand-alone. Licensees make some apparel, digital devices/apps, equipment. Interactive services: events/activations, fitness apps, content, in-store digital services.
- Seasonality: moderate fluctuations in aggregate sales volume; mix varies by season/geography.
- Reportable segments: NIKE Brand North America; EMEA; Greater China; APLA (incl NIKE + Jordan) + Converse (Converse DTC incl digital reported in Converse).
- US: ~44% of total rev FY26 (43% FY25, 42% FY24). US wholesale accts: footwear stores, sporting goods stores, athletic specialty, dept stores, skate/tennis/golf shops. 3 largest US customers ≈29% of US sales FY26. 8 significant US DCs.
- US retail stores: NIKE Brand factory 212; NIKE in-line (incl employee-only) 75; Converse (incl factory) 60; total 347.
- Non-US: ~56% of rev FY26 (57% FY25, 58% FY24); 65 DCs outside US; 3 largest non-US customers ≈16% of non-US sales FY26. Owned digital commerce in >40 countries.
- Non-US retail stores: NIKE factory 537; NIKE in-line 50; Converse 54; total 641.
- No customer ≥10% consolidated rev FY26.
- R&D: biomechanics, chemistry, exercise physiology, engineering, digital tech, industrial design, sustainability specialists + athlete/expert advisory boards; technologies Nike Air, Zoom, Free, Dri-FIT, Flyknit, FlyEase, ZoomX, Air Max, React.
- Manufacturing (as of 2026-05-31 / FY26): FW: 95 finished-goods factories in 11 countries; 15 contract mfrs; Vietnam ~52%, Indonesia ~27%, China ~16% of NIKE Brand FW; 4 FW mfrs each >10%, aggregate ~60%. Apparel: 321 factories in 34 countries; 64 contract mfrs; Vietnam ~34%, Cambodia ~15%, China ~12%; 2 apparel mfrs >10%; top 6 ≈54%. 205 strategic Tier 2 (materials) suppliers. Air-Sole suppliers: Air Manufacturing Innovation (wholly owned; facilities near Beaverton OR, Dong Nai City Vietnam, St. Charles MO) + contract mfrs in China & Vietnam. Sufficient raw materials sourced FY26. Sojitz America (sole owner of redeemable preferred) provides import-export financing since 1972.
- Trade: protectionist measures "have resulted in increased costs of our products, and additional measures, if implemented, will adversely affect sales and/or profitability for NIKE... possibly materially." US trade policy changes incl tariffs/retaliation "have negatively affected, and could in the future materially negatively affect" NIKE. Believes can develop alternative supply sources over time; disruptions likely short-term but alternates may be less cost-effective. FCPA compliance program.
- Competitors named: adidas, Anta, ASICS, Deckers, Li Ning, lululemon athletica, New Balance, On, Puma, Under Armour, V.F. Corporation. Competition aspects: product attributes (quality, innovation, performance, style, price/value); consumer connection (marketing, digital, social, athletes/influencers/teams/leagues); sourcing/distribution and merchandising in-store & digital.
- IP: NIKE & Swoosh registered in >190 jurisdictions; trade dress, copyrights, utility/design patents (expire at various times).
- Human capital: ~73,000 employees as of 2026-05-31 (incl retail/part-time). Mostly non-union except some EMEA/APLA; works councils; never material labor interruption. Goal to invest up to 2% of prior FY pre-tax income in communities (youth sport). ### Item 1A Risk factors (company-specific substance only)
- FLS risk list now explicitly includes "risks related to an increased focus on sport and rebalancing of our product and channel mix"; advance orders may not indicate future revenues (shipment timing, shorter-lead-time order mix, discounts, cancellations, returns); laws re tariffs, import/export, trade, taxes, wages, labor, immigration; debt rating declines.
- Macro: consumer discretionary declines → lower demand, higher inv, reduced retailer orders, cancellations/returns, higher discounts, lower GM; FX volatility; commodity/raw material (cotton, petroleum derivatives) volatility; supply chain issues from geopolitical conflicts, tariffs/trade policy, pandemics; retailer insolvency history; contract mfr financing risk.
- Competition: incl private label brands of major retailers; competes for contract-mfr capacity & raw materials; NIKE Direct competes with multi-brand retailers and digital platforms. NEW emphasis: AI/machine learning use by consumers, competitors, retailers, digital platforms — if NIKE can't leverage AI-enabled tech across DTC & wholesale, competitive position could suffer; lower barriers to new FW/apparel brands (some more nimble).
- FX/inflation: central bank rate hikes affect borrowing costs; government shutdowns/elections add volatility; weaker foreign currencies vs USD hurt reported sales/earnings; hedging lessens/delays but doesn't eliminate.
- Wholesale customer health: credit generally without collateral; futures ordering program lets customers order 5–6 months ahead; cancellable under certain conditions; past customer bankruptcies hurt; retailers cautious when economy weakens; sales depend on retailer merchandising investment.
- Climate/disasters: Philip H. Knight Campus in seismic zone.
- Sustainability/ESG: diverging expectations and regs (GHG disclosure vs limits on diversity programs).
- Pandemic: past supply chain disruption, factory cancellation costs, store closures, volatile ETR.
- Reputation/brand: supplier compliance, social causes/public personalities, social media amplification.
- Consumer preferences: long lead times; AI-enabled tools/digital platforms/data analytics may accelerate preference shifts and shorten design windows.
- Innovation/quality: failure → lower demand; quality problems "(including the introduction of bias or inaccuracies in our products)".
- Seasonality: timing of NBA Finals, Olympics, World Cup affects mix; customers cancel/change orders with minimal notice.
- Endorsers: rising sponsorship costs; competition intensified incl "growing influence of athletes' personal brands, the proliferation of athlete-led commercial ventures"; risk endorsers launch/align with competing brands or athlete-led ventures.
- Demand forecasting: buys outside futures program ahead of orders; excess inv → write-downs, discounted sales impair brand (has happened); shortages hurt relationships; risk from regional demand variability, retailer inventory/shelf-space decisions, promotional actions.
- NIKE Direct: high fixed cost; substantial operating lease commitments; high-profile stores need more investment; closures → lease termination, write-offs. NEW: may need AI-enabled search, discovery, personalization, recommendation; risk from consumer use of "AI-enabled search, comparison-shopping, agentic shopping or other third-party technologies"; digital risks incl diversion from own/retailer stores, pricing pressure; may not realize satisfactory return on NIKE Direct.
- IT/cyber: experienced cyberattacks incl phishing, none material to date; AI integration risks (misuse, biased outputs incl employment uses; threat actors using AI).
- Licensees: royalty and brand-misuse risk.
- Retailer consolidation: "a few large athletic footwear, apparel and equipment retailers" dominate some countries with growing share via acquisitions, new stores, digital, and attrition of struggling retailers → concentrated credit risk; if one substantially reduces purchases NIKE may be unable to find sufficient outlets. ### Factors impacting business / strategic actions
- External: geopolitical dynamics, tax regulation, FX, evolving tariff policies.
- Focus: "driving distinction within key sports, building a complete product portfolio, creating stories to inspire and emotionally connect with consumers, and elevating and growing the entire marketplace."
- Product Management: "Accelerating product innovation and reducing the supply of certain footwear products in the marketplace to rebalance the mix of our footwear portfolio."
- Marketplace Management: "Repositioning NIKE Brand Digital as a full-price platform and reinvesting in wholesale distribution. This includes liquidating inventory through increased markdowns across NIKE Direct, and higher sales returns and discounts with our wholesale partners to reduce inventory and create capacity for new product. We are also making investments to elevate the presentation of our brands in physical retail."
- Brand Management: increasing demand creation (brand + sports marketing) for key launches and sports moments.
- "we expect to complete these actions by the end of December 2026. ... North America has made the most progress against these actions, while Greater China and Converse will take more time."
- Greater China: "a trend of declining store traffic, elevated promotional activity and higher levels of inventory across the marketplace are negatively impacting revenues and overall profitability"; Converse "in the midst of a strategic reset of the brand and marketplace. We expect negative impacts from Greater China and Converse to continue throughout fiscal 2027."
- Mgmt believes actions "will reignite brand momentum and reposition our business to drive long-term shareholder value" despite negative rev/profit impact.
- Cost realignment across supply chain and technology "to serve an integrated marketplace"; FY26 employee severance charges $385M; may take additional actions/charges (Note 18).
