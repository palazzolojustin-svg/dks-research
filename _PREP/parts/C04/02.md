### Role / exposure
- Leads sales team doing planning, selling, merchandising, sell-through monitoring, pre-ordering, relationship mgmt w/ DKS. Supplies underwear & socks (commodity: hosiery/legwear, underwear). DKS = <10% of co rev.
### Helping / hindering (May–Jul 2026)
- Hindering: "ongoing turbulence around the tariffs in the U.S." + rising shipping cost (containers, fuel, vessel booking) due to global conflicts.
- Helping: local supply chain equipped to process DKS orders fast; reorder delivery 2–3 days max.
### Ordering behavior / inventory
- Consumer demand changes affect ordering "in a way"; commodity → less volume influx, but more frequent order pattern from DKS vs prior yrs.
- Historically forward-order business (order 6–9 months ahead, bulk delivery); last yr decided to rely less on front-loading, more in-season mgmt → smaller, more frequent in-season orders; SKU rationalization; ↑sell-through & ↑volume.
- DKS inventory mgmt: "appropriately" (reorders based on consumer demand).
- Ops issues (chargebacks/compliance/forecasting/logistics): yes but not significant to business; change in operating model required relieving supply-chain pressure for large bulk goods, delivering closer to consumer demand → better merch & financial planning.
- Ordering this qtr: "consistent" for them, "although from sources within the industry, I have heard that they have been buying quite aggressively."
- Main driver of DKS perf for them: product availability on supplier's end (inventory available to meet DKS consumer demand).
### Sell-in numbers
- May–Jul 2026 vs May–Jul 2025 (bulk forward + in-season): +2%, "behind on the posted growth... that DICK'S as a entire business is posting", but very good for commodity cats. Met expectations.
- Aug–Oct 2026 vs 2025: +2%–5% on commodity cats (given strong prior qtr & strong Q1). Will meet expectations.
- CY2026 vs CY2025: ~+5% ("roughly 5%"), heavily backloaded to last qtr (Black Friday promos, holiday). CY26 outlook exceeding expectations coming into yr; change over past 60–90 days driven by positive sell-through results.
- Visibility next 60–90 days "slightly clouded" vs LY (front-loading gave exact visibility; frequent reorder pattern less so) "but the forecasts do look very promising."
### Relative performance / share
- DKS outperforming some major other retail customers: several dept stores (rev vs expectation & actual rev) and value channels; attributed to commodity cats supplied. Expect DKS to outperform other key accts next qtr.
- Share vs Academy & mass merchants: maintain this yr. Share vs other retailers they supply: stable (no info from DKS on why; commodity seller).
- Inventory allocation: no change across retailers, only within DKS (less front-loading, SKU rationalization, in-season replenishment).
### Pricing / costs
- Pricing w/ DKS "more stable" – mainly due to supplier's global scale/negotiating competitive pricing globally; less hit by US cost influx than other mfrs/wholesalers selling to DKS.
- Expect tougher negotiations, esp on margins – ongoing w/ all retailers, not DKS-specific.
- Cost inputs: almost all – tariffs & instability; freight (containers, vessels, fuel/petrol incl own trucks to DKS); raw materials; labor (mostly Far East production).
### Private label

Believe brand recognition/quality strong enough to remain key player; monitoring closely; would need to venture into different cats if PL took share. Focus more on DKS PL than on competitors (no brand-level momentum info shared).
### Categories / assortment / innovation
- Strongest: hosiery/legwear, underwear (their only cats; no slowing cats).
- Assortment shifts (SKU rationalization, depth changes, category emphasis): all seen, evident already at sell-in of Q2 & Q3 LY → SKU rationalization; Q1 & Q2 closed showing more depth sold to DKS.
- New launches exceeded expectations (sell-through, desirability). No innovative product sold (commodity). Innovative products earn more space on fashion/sports apparel side of DKS. DKS receptiveness to innovation = good insight into LT growth potential.
### Foot Locker / Fast Break
