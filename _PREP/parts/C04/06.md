- Export meta: call 11 of 14. Title: "Senior Director at Major Apparel Manufacturer Sees Positive Growth and Collaboration with Sporting Goods Retailer".
### AlphaSense summary/takeaways (vendor-written)
- Background: Sr Dir, sales planning & eCom across accts incl DKS, Macy's, Penney's, Kohl's, specialty; jeans, shorts, tops, jackets. Sentiment: cautiously optimistic, low double-digit sell-in increases for 2026; partnership positive/supportive, collaborative on elevated costs; DKS to maintain share, niche but growing partner.
- Takeaways: DKS <5% of rev, consistent low-double-digit growth; tariffs, elevated freight, reduced discretionary income, but demand resilient for right products/trends; DKS aggressive inv mgmt, winning, strong comps, positive denim trends esp women's wide-leg & baggy; pricing stable despite costs, both sides absorbing margin pressure; no major ops issues; focus on differentiated, elevated products.
### Role / exposure
- Manages clubs, off-price, grocery, eCom on sales planning side; eCom for DKS, Macy's, Penney's, Kohl's, Boot Barn, specialty. Supplies 100% apparel – primarily jeans/long bottoms; also shorts, tops, jackets. DKS "very small"; "less than 5%, maybe"; not a largest acct like Macy's, Penney's, Walmart, Amazon. "niche business" at DKS (denim in a SG retailer).
### Helping / hindering (May–Jul 2026)
- Tariffs; gas cost → higher freight; elevated cost of doing business reduced demand – less discretionary income for apparel (non-necessity); consumers more conservative/particular "but they still are willing to pay if you give them the right product, right trend."
- Planned for slowdown: "Q1 was strong. Q2 pulled back a little bit. We're cautiously optimistic for half two, specifically fall"; consumer "fairly resilient" despite slight pullback; gas prices trying to stabilize; DKS orders adjusted but "have not fallen off too much."
### Inventory / ops
- DKS inv mgmt "fairly aggressive. They're winning in the marketplace. They've posted good comp results. Our denim trend is good. They've been fairly aggressive with trying to grow our business."
- Ops issue: fit trend shift (wider men's jeans, bigger leg openings for women) flipped from skinny/slim faster than supply could change a yr ago → hurt biz w/ DKS & all retailers; transitioning inventory; "probably won't be right-sized 100% until 2027", better position fall 2026 than spring 2026.
### Sell-in numbers / outlook
- May–Jul 2026: planning up +10% to +12% (brand trending, denim trending, DKS foot traffic/business strong); met expectations/plan. Ordering consistent ("ordering to the need... getting foot traffic... brand is resonating"); "about low double-digit increases".
- In line w/ other major retail customers – DKS not major denim seller, planned for conservative growth ("I'm sure sporting goods guys have them planned for crazy growth because they've been growing").
- Qtr drivers: seasonal products strong out of gate & into Q2 – seasonal shorts, capris, tees/short-sleeve tops; denim trend early/post-Easter + cooler start to summer.
- Clarification: Q1 better than Q2; "Q2, we lost a little bit of a leaky bucket"; Q3 uncertain whether as good as Q2 or "leak anymore". Sell-in "up about 10%-12% over last year".
- Aug–Oct 2026: ~+10% y/y; meeting expectations ("trending and selling out, so so far, so good"); in line vs other key accts.
- Visibility: order flow up; fall set mode – DKS "setting a little bit larger than they did a year ago", set orders up 10%–12%.
- CY2026 vs 2025: +10% sell-in shipments; right around meeting expectations; outlook change drivers: strong denim trend + strong DKS traffic/business.
- Share vs mass merchants: maintain.
### Pricing / costs
- Prices "a little bit choppy" – tariffs, gas, transportation elevated; supplier, retailer, end consumer all eating some cost.
- No greater margin pressure w/ DKS; "everybody wants to keep the prices flat"; DKS understands elevated costs; navigated together.
- Cost inputs all (freight, labor, materials, tariffs); "We need to pass those on so we can continue to make the same margin." Biggest: tariff & freight.
### Private label
- "Supportive" – partnership not combative; DKS wants brand to succeed.
### Categories / fits
- Strongest: seasonals in Q1; long bottoms in Q2 (cool weather until ~now).
- Slowing: skinnier/slimmer fits & (those) long bottoms; trucker jackets.
- Women's trends stronger than men's – women moved to wide-leg/baggy; men slower & less far; skinny/slim/narrow down in both; wider legs, baggier top, fit-block up.
- Assortment shifts: none; held back since DKS core = sporting goods, jeans not core competency, but DKS wants to offer category – "biggest hindrance".
### Channel share (supplier's other retailers)
- Growing: clubs (traffic), off-pricers, premium & elevated retailers. Biggest challenge: dept stores & middle tier. Driver: value-seeking – discretionary income "under siege" (tariffs on goods, gas & food prices, wage growth lagging).
- Allocation unchanged.
### Innovation / launches
- New launches trending as wanted; DKS = specialty niche retailer – growing w/ them to offset dept store challenges; similarly pursuing Boot Barn, Tractor Supply.
- Innovation: limited effect. DKS wants differentiated product vs Penney's/Kohl's/Macy's in same mall so not pure price comparison; supplier places elevated products at DKS. LT growth "fine... nice niche player with us."
- Fast Break: no change in orders yet.
### Partner quality
- "Really good partners... fans of the brand"; very positive; relationship consistent.
- Closing: "DICK'S Sporting Goods is a U.S. retailer who's really owned the full market space across all 50 states. There's no other really nationwide sporting goods retailer. They've been able to command and get elevated product. They're getting higher average unit transactions for their products. They're getting special makeup products." Value play: if not cheapest, go to DKS for more elevated; supplier must differentiate product vs dept stores to win; "currently, they're winning in the marketplace."
