Thesis 2 · Closures above consensus

Supported, with a regional split

Consensus models zero further European closures. We found 35+ named closures since the deal across 11 countries. At least 3 came after the 559 count on 1 August (Brixton, Avignon, Budapest Váci utca), and two more stores now show as closed on FL's own locator (Ulm, Częstochowa).

Outside Italy and France the pace is ~6–8% a year (Germany, Spain, UK, Benelux, Hungary, Portugal). Italy and France, 53% of stores, are running at 3–4%, in line with the old run-rate. Our estimate for all of Europe: ~4–6% a year, which puts the fleet at ~510–526 by FYE27 against ~550 in consensus.

Thesis 1 · Sale or exit

Groundwork visible, no live process

There is no scoop, no adviser, no merger filing, no licensing job and no owned-to-licensed conversion since the deal. What we did find is the legal and governance groundwork a carve-out would need:

  • FL's Spanish European holding company was folded into a single Dutch holding company.
  • A DICK'S executive (Reid Sigal) now sits on every European board.
  • The EMEA CFO, GM, merchandising head and HR head have all left.
  • New head-office hires in Utrecht are on temporary contracts.
  • FL Europe B.V.'s stated business purpose was rewritten a month after the deal.
  • Eight tail markets (~12% of stores) already sit outside FL's loyalty programme and app.

The same steps also fit DKS simply taking direct control.

Against both

Money is still going in

FL EMEA's real-estate VP targets ~100 European Fast Break refreshes by year-end (about 18% of the fleet). FL has opened or relocated about a dozen stores since the deal, including a new Liverpool flagship, and appointed a pan-European ad agency.

Poland, Czechia, Austria and Luxembourg have had no closures. Italy, France, Austria, Hungary and Poland are profitable at the local-entity level. A full sale in the next one or two quarters looks less likely than continued pruning.

1. Closures: the fleet is shrinking faster than consensus allows

Bloomberg's flat ~550 is likely reached this fiscal year, and the decline does not stop there.

Solid points are DKS-reported FL Europe owned stores (Q3 FY25, FYE25, Q1 FY26, Q2 FY26). The open point is our estimate for 7-Oct-26. The range comes from 556 locator store IDs plus ~9 Swiss stores with no locator, minus at least 10 entries that are dark or stale. Dashed projections run from 559 at 4% and 6% a year. FYE26 would be ~548 at 4% or ~542 at 6%. At ~$2.9M of sales per European store, a 25–40 store gap by FYE27 is roughly $70–120M of revenue (inference).

3. Foot Locker Europe B.V.: where the European losses sit

Its own accounts for 2021–25 have never been filed. That looks like a habit of filing late, not a sale step. The country subsidiaries are lending cash into it.

Update from the KvK documents (bought 7-Oct-26).
  • No 403 statement, and no withdrawal. The overview of filings lists no parent liability statement, so our earlier 403 inference (E252) was wrong, and the "403 withdrawal" test does not apply.
  • The accounts are simply unfiled. FL Europe B.V. is registered as a "big" company. For 2021–2025 it has filed only the parent group's consolidated accounts (art. 408), never its own. This is a long habit, not a DKS change:
    • FY2016 accounts were filed in 2019.
    • FY2017–2019 accounts were all filed on 16-Feb-2024.
    • FY2020 accounts were filed on 13-Sep-2024.
    So the missing years are the usual backlog and say nothing about a sale.
  • Its stated business purpose was changed a month after the deal. The previous description (importing, trading and distributing sports products, owning other companies, and buying and selling property) ended on 7-Oct-2025. Poland's company purpose was narrowed to three retail codes three days later. The new wording needs the current register extract (€2.95).
  • US directors and the European finance chief left.
    • Giovanna Cipriano, a long-time Foot Locker Inc. officer, left 23-Feb-2026.
    • Gözde Şen Lönell, the EMEA CFO, left 13-Jul-2026.
    • Tomas Petersson left 1-Aug-2025.
    • Erik Amperse's full power of attorney ended 23-Feb-2026, and Ben Boylan's 4-Jun-2026.
    The history lists only departures, so the current board (presumably including Sigal) needs the €2.95 current extract.
  • "Large company regime" notice, 24-Jun-2025. Filed during the deal process, it says FL Europe B.V. meets the conditions of the Dutch regime for large companies (structuurregime). That regime normally requires a supervisory board with strengthened works-council rights, which adds a layer of consultation to any restructuring or sale.
  • The parent FLE Holdings shows no losses. Its 2024 accounts are a micro-entity balance sheet only, adopted on 22-Apr-2026 (about four months late) and signed by Amperse and Şen Lönell:
    • Participations €867.7M (2023: €859.0M).
    • Equity €810.4M (2023: €803.8M).
    • Debt €57.3M, of which €42M is long-term.
    There is no profit and loss account, and the valuation basis isn't stated, so it does not reveal FL Europe B.V.'s result.
  • Earlier read (superseded above). KvK holds FL Europe B.V.'s own accounts for 2016–2020 only. For FY2021–25 there are just the parent group's consolidated accounts (art. 408). Even FL's dormant Dutch shells still file their own accounts E252.
  • Cash flows into Utrecht. FL Germany's receivables from group companies rose to €44.9M (from €36.4M), and it is funded only by shareholder loans and FL Europe B.V.'s cash pool. The UK group went from owing group companies £7.66M to being owed £4.21M. Company.info gives FL Europe B.V. a current ratio of 0.43 (year not shown) E255.
  • Two operating companies are overdue. Foot Locker Netherlands B.V. and Foot Locker Europe.com B.V. (the EU web business) have not filed FY2024 accounts, about 9 months past the deadline. Europe.com deposited something on 13-Aug-26 that was not accounts E253.
  • The holding merger has reached the Dutch register. Foot Locker Dutch Holdings B.V. amended its articles on 5-Dec-25, the first post-close deed in the Dutch chain. KvK flagged it "merged and/or demerged" on 6-Oct-26, about five weeks after the Spanish holding company was dissolved. No operating company moved: the CZ, AT and PL subsidiaries are still owned by the same Dutch B.V.s, so a share sale of FL Europe B.V. was already executable without further restructuring E254 E234.
  • FL Europe B.V. and FL Netherlands B.V. are the registered shareholders of the Czech company. The Czech 2026 shareholder acts were signed in Utrecht, and they appoint no FY2026 auditor, unlike the year before E226.

Still open (optional, €2.95 each at KvK): FL Europe B.V.'s current register extract, which would show the current board and the new business purpose; and the FY2025 art. 408 consolidated accounts filed 20-Apr-2026 (€3.90), which would show which parent, DKS or Foot Locker Inc., is named.

Appendix: evidence we already had before the scrape

This is the baseline from the research folder (filings, calls, broker notes, Bloomberg) and the two earlier pitch primers. Everything above was checked against it, and nothing above repeats it.

Store footprint

  • FL Europe owned stores: 581 (Q3 FY25) → 573 (FYE25, 31-Jan-26) → 567 (2-May-26) → 559 (1-Aug-26), about 22 closures in ~9 months (~4%/yr).
  • FL APAC owned stores: 124 → 123 → 122. At 1-Aug-26 that was 92 APAC stores plus 30 atmos (Japan). International owned stores: 710 → 681.
  • Licensed FL stores (Middle East, Asia, India via Metro Brands, SE Europe via Fourlis): 246 at close → 266.
  • FL owned stores in total: 2,347 at close → 2,212 at 1-Aug-26. FY26 to date: +9 opened, 104 closed, 67 under "unproductive-asset review". Management's base case is ~100 global FL closures in FY26.
  • Before the deal, FL exited or licensed out about nine countries: Singapore, Malaysia, Greece and Romania (Fourlis, ~$12M), India (Metro Brands), Korea, and the Nordics (Denmark, Norway, Sweden; outright closure decided Aug-2024).

Consensus

  • Bloomberg holds FL Europe at ~550–551 stores through FY32. Total FL owned stores run ~2,205 → 2,145 → 2,117 (~60 closures a year).
  • No broker models an EMEA exit, sale or licensing conversion. Bloomberg's tax rate drifts to 25% by FY29 without explaining how the European losses stop.

Financials

  • International sales: $1,030.5M in the FY25 stub (33% of FL); $534.2M in Q1 FY26 and $580.5M in Q2 FY26. The largest markets are Italy, France, Canada and Australia. ~11,100 international employees.
  • International comps: −10.2% (Q3 FY25), −3.2% (Q4), −1.7% (Q1 FY26), −3.3% (Q2 FY26). FY25 −8.1%. FY24 Europe +4.4%.
  • FL's international pre-tax result went from +$84M (FY22) to −$49M (FY24). Foreign losses carry a full valuation allowance (~$280–316M), which is why the tax guide went 25.5% → 27% → 29%. That costs ~4 points of tax, ~$0.65 a share.
  • A new Dutch distribution centre. FL leases run 5–10 years. European works councils are said to make exits costly.
  • The acquisition-charge budget is up to $750M, with $516M used through Q2 FY26 (~$234M left). There is $591M of goodwill and $710M of tradenames at impairment risk.
  • The Sep-26 $1B DKS notes exclude non-US subsidiaries from their covenants.

Management statements

  • Dec-25 (Stack): "we're keeping" EMEA, pre-COVID international was very profitable but "really got forgotten". Matthew Barnes (ex-CEO ALDI UK) hired as President of FL International from 3-Dec-25. EMEA runs ~6 months behind North America.
  • 25-Aug-26 Q2 call: EMEA "more challenging than expected" (promotions, cautious consumer, Middle East war). Gupta: EMEA promotions "remain very aggressive … the industry is carrying too much inventory". Stack: "simply yes" to rightsizing the fleet and monetising assets.
  • 14-Sep-26 (GS conference): "creating a menu … no preconceived ideas". FL turnaround "taking longer than we anticipated".
  • Barnes bought 3,665 DKS shares ($499,869 at $136.39) on 30-Sep-26.
  • Fast Break resets were described as tested in "some Europe" stores. Nike names JD, Sports Direct and Snipes, not FL, as its key European partners.

Analysis already in the primers

  • An exit would cut revenue ~10% and add ~$0.90–1.30 to FY27 EPS, mostly from removing losses and lowering the tax rate.
  • Probabilities: ~45% that a Europe action is announced within four quarters, of which ~60% licensing rather than closure. 70% that FY27 closures beat consensus by at least 100.
  • Licensing (the Fourlis model) is the most likely exit template. The "signs pointing to an exit" list: the change in management tone, the tax reserve, FL's own pre-deal plan to pull back from international, the charge budget left, Barnes's restructuring background, the covenant terms, and Nike's European partner list.
  • Arguments against an exit: international was part of the deal pitch, Europe was FL's best region in FY24, exits are costly, JD is cutting guidance, and Barnes bought stock.
