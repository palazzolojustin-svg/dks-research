The one-paragraph read. DKS is two businesses under one share count. The DICK'S Business (~$14.7B sales, 10.6–10.9% guided segment margin, 7 straight quarters of 4%+ comps) is a share-gaining, premium-positioned sporting goods box whose incremental margin has gone negative for a year despite mid-single-digit comps, because it is reinvesting (House of Sport pre-opening, Fort Worth DC, World Cup marketing, price-matching a promotional footwear market). Foot Locker (~$7.4B, guided to a $40–80M loss this year) is a mall-based, Nike-dependent, launch-and-retro sneaker chain bought at the trough of its own margin collapse; DKS has cleared its inventory, cut ~30% of SKUs, re-added apparel, closed 135 stores and hired new leadership, but back-to-school 2026, the promised inflection, failed against a Nike/Jordan legacy-silhouette glut that Nike itself now says runs "beyond fiscal 2027." Consensus ($13.36 FY27E, +16%) assumes FL goes from a ~$73M loss to a ~$20M profit on flat sales, DICK'S margin recovers 30bps, tax falls ~115bps, and carries neither the $1B of 6.2–6.9% notes issued on 22-Sep-26 nor any buyback, closure program or international exit. The biggest unmodeled items cut both ways and are laid out in Section 12.

1. The business model and every avenue of monetization (read this first)

DKS is a two-segment retailer: the DICK'S Business (~$14.1B FY25 sales, 892 stores, 11.1% segment margin) and the Foot Locker Business (~$7.4B run-rate, 2,212 owned + 266 licensed stores, loss-making), consolidated since 8-Sep-2025 D. Combined FY25 sales were $17.2B on 21 weeks of FL; pro forma full-year combined sales were $21.8B D. It is a wholesale-dependent model: DKS buys finished goods from ~1,500 vendors (Nike ~31% of consolidated FY25 purchases: ~25% at DICK'S, ~59% at FL) and sells them through boxes, apps and a loyalty database of ~30M active athletes D.

6.2 How competition flows into pricing

The set-up. DKS does not set the price of 87% of what it sells; brands do, through MAP and their own sites. DKS's pricing power is therefore (a) mix: own brands at +700–900bps and allocated full-price product (On, HOKA, UGG) where MAP holds and no markdown money is needed; and (b) discipline: not chasing brands down when they break MAP. In Q2 FY26 it chose to chase. Stack, 14-Sep-26: "What changed is a number of brands got very promotional on their sites… we're not leading this margin erosion. We are participating in it… I use the word investment very purposely." Via UBS: "maintaining value leadership during periods of industry stress often represents one of the highest-return investments available."

Who is discounting what (Aug–Sep 2026). Needham: >30% of Nike's assortment discounted across 14 wholesale partners (25% a year ago); FL and JD showed fewer items on sale but deeper cuts and "very promotional" BTS messaging ("BTS EXTENDED NOW 50% OFF"; New Balance week 40% off + 3x FLX). UBS Evidence Lab: US wholesale Nike footwear discount factor 8.4% (−50bps y/y) with ~27 weeks of inventory (~14 fewer than a year ago) versus wholesale apparel 13.2% (+420bps) with ~43 weeks (+12): the glut is Nike apparel and Nike's own DTC, not wholesale footwear broadly E. JPM's discount-breadth screen: New Balance has the highest discount breadth at both JD US and FL US; On and adidas screen least discounted on multi-brand sites; "Foot Locker, on the other hand, is visibly more promotional in France." Deutsche Bank's US retailer checks (Jul-26) said inventory was "relatively controlled… limiting the industry's ability to increase promotions materially" and that Nike running (Pegasus, Vomero, Structure) was the standout, while retailers were cutting On orders: the opposite of the Needham/UBS Nike-glut narrative, and a reminder that the promo problem is franchise-specific.

Promo trackers at DKS itself. JPM: 70% off clearance events (Mar–Apr 2026), NKE/UA/DSG promos raised from 40% to 70% off (31-Mar to 6-Apr), "promotions rational y/y" in May, a "modest uptick around key moments" in August. Barclays' promo score moved from "Flat/Better" (2Q25) to "Flat/Deeper" (1Q26) and its absolute score fell from 65 (1Q25) to 42 (1Q26) and 31 (4Q25). UBS's Oct-25 basket: DKS 2.1% above Amazon vs a 3-year average of 3.1% (price gap narrowing) E.

Tariffs and price. Barclays' QuikCalc (Aug-25 rates): a 15.8% average unit-cost increase needs 6.9% pass-through (~$952M) to hold margin; DKS absorbed tariffs in merch margin in 2H FY25 (+5bps merch in Q3 FY25) and then got $59M back after the Supreme Court ruling. Brands raised staples ~$10 and pinnacle up to ~$50 (Nike-side planner); "because the tariffs were returned… pricing stable." The relief is now a FY27 lap (Section 12).

Vendor funding is the hidden pricing variable. DKS's incentives are "not contractually assured"; MAP brands give no markdown support; Nike guides FY27 EBIT to fall faster than revenue on higher input costs and is lapping a $986M IEEPA refund of its own, so Nike has less room to fund retailer markdowns in 2027 (Needham: FY27 EBIT margin −150bps "largely from lapping tariff refunds") E. Experts in Jun–Jul reported no incremental markdown-money requests from DKS except for World Cup closeouts, and tighter margin negotiations "every season" from UGG and the Nike planner, who warned the FL purchase "starts to eliminate who you can use as leverage" X.

Where it lands. Pricing power accrues to DKS in performance running, own brands and exclusives; it leaks away in Nike/Jordan lifestyle, where the brand sets both the wholesale and the street price and is currently clearing. FL is ~60% Nike by revenue, so FL has almost no pricing power of its own: its gross margin is a function of Nike's launch calendar and Nike's discounting posture. That, more than anything DKS can execute, is why the FL margin recovery slipped.

7.4 What it should trade at

  • Sum of the parts says the market is carrying Foot Locker as a liability. DICK'S-only FY27 earnings power on consensus: segment OI $1,647M, less ~$137M of interest (post-notes) plus ~$25M other income, taxed at ~25% ex-FL = ~$1,150M, or ~$12.9 per share on 89M shares Der. At the pre-deal DICK'S multiple of 12–13x that is $155–168; at $137 the stock prices FL at roughly −$1.1B to −$1.5B (−$12 to −$17/share), consistent with the author primer's SOTP P and UBS's observation that the shares sat below the pre-deal price with a larger core.
  • What a core-only retailer with these characteristics deserves: ~9% share of a $140B market gaining ~50bps a year, 4–6% comps, an 11% segment margin that is structurally re-based from 5% pre-COVID, 20–40% cash-on-cash store formats, but capex at 2.3x D&A, negative incremental margin for four quarters, Nike at ~25% of buys and a deliberate price-matching posture. Specialty-retail peers trade at ~12x; DKS traded 12–15x through 2023–24 and 17–18x at the Jan-25/Jan-26 peaks. 12–13x FY27 core EPS looks like the right band; 15x needs the 2027 clean-up thesis to show up in the Q3 print.
  • Fair value framework (mine): base ~$155–165 (12–13x core $12.9, FL at zero); bull ~$195–210 (core at 14x on $13.5–14.0 as SG&A finally levers, plus FL back to a 2–3% margin by FY28 worth $1.5–2.5/share of EPS); bear ~$100–110 (core margin slips to ~10% on a 1–2% comp, FL loses $100M+ again in FY27, tax stays 29%: EPS ~$10–11 at 10x). Probability-weighted this is roughly $150–160 before the two unmodeled binaries (a buyback-financed by the new notes, an international exit) that Section 12 sizes at +$0.5–1.2 of FY27 EPS each.
  • Why the discount persists: the multiple de-rated from 15x to 10x in one print because the market lost confidence in two things at once: that DICK'S's margin was "re-baselined at 11–12%" (it cut to 10.6–10.9% on a 4.9% comp), and that FL could be fixed with "Retail 101" (it guided to a loss after a raise 90 days earlier). Both are credibility problems that only prints fix; the next one is 24-Nov-26.

10.2 Why Foot Locker is bad: five causes, quantified

(1) Nike dependence, and Nike's 2021–23 allocation cut. Nike was 56% of FL's purchases in FY07, 73% by FY14, 75% at the FY20 peak. In Feb-22 FL said Nike would fall from ~70% to ~60% of sales (≈$900M) from Q4 2022 and the FY21 10-K promised "we do not expect any one supplier to represent more than 55% of merchandise purchases" from Q4 2022. Nike stayed ~65% through FY23 (the sentence was dropped from the next 10-K) and fell to ~59% only in FY24. What actually left was the high-heat product: Wells' checks put FL's share of Nike high-heat allocation at ~40% (≈1M pairs) pre-cut and ~20% (≈400k) after; FL's Nike high-heat sales went from $1,613M (FY21) to $735M (FY23), about 980bps of sales, while Nike's markdown rate at FL rose from 14.3% to 16.8%. Wells attributes ~490bps of the ~880bps FY21–23 operating-margin decline to Nike (markdowns ~175bps, occupancy ~90bps, SG&A deleverage ~225bps) and the rest to freight and excess-inventory promotion. Regression: FL comp ≈ 0.72 × Nike growth + 0.7%; FL comped positive in 11 of 19 years since 2007 and only three times while Nike was declining E. Stack's diagnosis (25-Nov-25): "Foot Locker strayed from Retail 101… did not react quickly enough when its largest brand pivoted toward a direct-to-consumer model, leaving Foot Locker with the wrong inventory, too much of what didn't sell and not enough of what did sell." The proxy never names Nike; it is "an important Foot Locker brand partner" whose "delays in the repositioning" and "slower recovery" are cited in board minutes from Sep-24 through May-25.

(2) The real estate: 2,200 small boxes in malls. Average FL US store ~3,500 selling sq ft; Champs 3,800; Kids FL 2,000; Europe 2,200. Only 21% of NA locations were off-mall in Jan-22 (42% of NA gross sq ft by Feb-25, mostly because WSS boxes are 10K+ sq ft). Lace Up planned to close ">400 locations" (>200 C/D-mall doors and >200 weaker A/B doors) worth ~10% of 2022 sales at ~800bps lower profit; ~35% of FL's mall stores are in C/D malls (Wells). Store count fell 24% from FY19 to FY24 but gross square footage only 3%; sales per gross sq ft went $510 (FY19) → $548 (FY22) → $507 (FY24). Occupancy is 13.3% of sales vs 8.5% at DICK'S. >75% of Champs stores sit within a mile of a Foot Locker, often in the same mall D E.

(3) A launch-and-retro model in a market that stopped lining up. Footwear is 84% of FL sales; "a substantial portion of our highest margin sales are to young males (ages 12–25)… as a fashion statement" (FL 10-K). Stack, Apr-26: FL was "too dependent" on high-heat Jordan Retro, "pretty much missed the retro run category," and FL Europe "didn't buy a retro run shoe… P-6000… a big miss." Apparel was "deteriorating. They haven't invested": FL apparel comps fell double digits for five straight quarters in FY24 while Champs, the "lead apparel banner," shrank 40% from its FY21 peak. By summer 2026 the launch calendar itself failed: "not only were there fewer launches… but launches we did see performed below industry and our expectations" (Stack, Aug-26). Needham's tracker: none of July 2026's six retro Jordans traded at a resale premium; 7 of 10 August Jordans sat at discounts; retro Jordan resale premiums fell from 90–100% of launches pre-COVID to 26% in Nike's FY1Q27 E.

(4) Operations: "Retail 101." Inventory rose 37% in FY21 and 30% in FY22 as sales fell; gross margin fell 670bps peak-to-trough; FL had no planning, allocation or replenishment group (Stack); three CFOs in fifteen months (Page → Higginbotham → Baughn); no out-of-home marketing "since roughly 2013"; the dividend was paused in Aug-23 and "2024 will serve as a cash rebuilding year"; HQ was being moved from New York to St. Petersburg FL (DKS reversed this and moved people back to a NYC HQ in Mar-26 at a $40–55M cost). SG&A went from 20.6% of sales (FY19) to 25.4% (1H25) with technology spend already above the Lace Up ">3% of sales by 2026" target.

(5) Diversification that didn't work. WSS (bought 2021 for $809M; planned >300 stores and ~$1.3B by 2026): 151 stores and $660M by FY24, comps negative in every quarter since Jan-2023 (−8.1% in Q2 FY25), a $140M tradename impairment in Q1 FY25, and 52 closures under DKS (151 → 99). atmos (bought 2021 for $372M): $138M of FY24 sales, tradename impaired twice, US stores closed, earn-out written to zero. Champs: repositioned to the "active athlete," 525 → 364 stores, sales $1,939M (FY21) → $1,155M (FY24). International: Sidestep wound down, Hong Kong/Macau closed, Singapore/Malaysia sold to a licensee ($24M), South Korea, Denmark, Norway, Sweden exited, Greece/Romania sold to Fourlis under license ($12M); 29 countries → 20. EMEA goodwill was fully impaired and all European deferred tax assets reserved in Q1 FY25, before DKS owned it.
