[N3-4] **Academy's 10-K says plainly that swapping national-brand units for private-label units raises the gross-margin rate but lowers net sales. Any owned-brand mix model needs a revenue haircut as well as a GM-rate lift.**
- Verbatim (ASO FY25 10-K SOURCE L1463; FY26 Q2 10-Q L892): "A shift in our sales mix in which we sell more units of our private label brand products and fewer units of the national brand products would generally have a positive impact on our gross margin rate but an adverse impact on our total net sales."
- Wording history (from the script):
  - The sentence is in every ASO 10-K from FY22 to FY25.
  - It is not in the Q3 FY25 10-Q.
  - It appears in the Q1 and Q2 FY26 10-Qs. That reflects 10-Q formatting, not new information.
  - In the FY25 10-K, "gross margin" became "gross margin rate".
- INFERENCE: DKS comps are ticket-led (ticket +5.5% in 1Q26 and +3.6% in 2Q26; known, F6). That is hard to square with large unit substitution into cheaper owned brands. Any DKS owned-brand gain looks more like CALIA/VRST at better/best price points, or space gains, than an opening-price-point swap.

## Evidence against / caveats
- No owned-brand facts in these files. The four files contain no DSG, CALIA, VRST, Maxfli or other DKS owned-brand data. Barclays' "increased private label" pillar is DUP.
- No private-label surge at Academy. ASO apparel was flat in Q2 FY26 (N3-2). Its private-label dollars fell about 2% in FY25 and its reach shrank from 60% to 53% over six years (N3-3). The value peer gives no sign of an industry-wide surge.
- Price competition at entry points. ASO is putting tariff refunds into "strategic pricing" (N3-5). This adds price competition at opening price points.
- National brand gained holiday share. TNF took more of DKS's Top-100 apparel best-seller slots in Holiday 2025 (N3-7).
- Discounting cuts both ways. Mid-2026 footwear discounting is "broad-based" (JPM, DB), which narrows national-brand price points. But it is concentrated in footwear, where DKS has almost no owned product, so the effect on owned-brand apparel is limited.
- The footwear lever is inferred, not observed at DKS. N3-1 relies on peer and channel data. DKS's own 2Q26 footwear share is not in these files. Check W04 or the 3Q26 print.
- Rounding. ASO's percentages are rounded "approximately" figures, so its dollar series (N3-3) carries about ±$30M per year.
