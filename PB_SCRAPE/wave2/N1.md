- **Wording has been toned down since FY2021 (cuts against the thesis).** Since the FY2021 10-K, management's written plan for vertical brands has been demoted step by step: first from a business-section intention, then to a forward-looking-statement list item, and in the FY25 10-K to a risk-factor bullet that no longer mentions "increased marketing" or "growing".
- **The reported vertical figure includes licensed brands, and that roster has changed.** The $1.6-1.8B figure has always included brands DKS licenses from third parties, not just brands it owns. The licensed roster changed a lot: Reebok was in it until FY2018. Lotto was added in 2023 and expanded into pickleball in 2024. The Cobra license widened from youth golf sets to golf.
- **Implication for the model.** Part of the FY23→FY25 rise in "vertical" dollars could be newly licensed product rather than owned brands.

## Top findings

[N1-1] **The 10-K "plan" language for vertical brands peaked in FY2020-FY2021 and has been demoted every year since.**

Verbatim wording by filing:
- **FY2014-FY2016 10-Ks:** Forward-looking statements (FLS): "plans to grow our private brand business".
- **FY2016 10-K:** Item 1: "The Company anticipates the private brand business exceeding $1 billion in sales during fiscal 2017."
- **FY2017 and FY2018 10-Ks:** Item 1: "Looking forward, we intend to grow the CALIA line of women's athletic apparel and introduce new private brands during fiscal 2018 [2019]." FY2018 FLS: "plans to invest in the growth and marketing of our private brand business".
- **FY2020 10-K (filed Mar-2021; the first year the term "vertical" is used):**
  - Item 1: "Looking forward, we intend to provide our vertical brands with improved space in-store, increased marketing and expansion into additional product categories."
  - FLS: "plans to invest in our vertical brands with improved space in-store, increased marketing, and expansion into additional product categories".
- **FY2021 10-K (filed Mar-2022):** the same Item 1 sentence, with "... which will provide an opportunity for future growth" added.
- **FY2022, FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks:** the "Looking forward" sentence is gone from Item 1. The plan survives only in the FLS list: "plans to provide our vertical brands with improved space in-store, increased marketing and expansion into additional product categories". The FY24 version prefixes it with "our vertical brand strategy and".
- **FY2025 10-K (filed 2026-03-27):** the plan is reframed as a risk bullet: "Risks associated with our vertical brand offerings, including brand strategy and marketing, improved space in-store, expanding product categories, product safety and labeling, product liability and recalls, and specialty concept stores". "Increased marketing" and "plans to provide" are gone.
- **The word "growing" appears in FY24 and is dropped in FY25:**
  - FY24 IP risk factor: "critical to our success, including and increasingly with respect to our growing vertical brands". FY24 FLS: "including with respect to our growing vertical brands".
  - FY25 versions: "critical to our brand strength" and "including with respect to our vertical brands".

  - "Collectively ... second largest vendor" (FY2024)

[N1-6] **The FY25 risk-factor rewrite has small changes in meaning.**

## Evidence against / caveats
- **No 2026 data in these filings.** The 10-K/ARS content ends with the FY25 10-K (2026-03-27) and the 2026 ARS letter (2026-05-01). Neither gives 2026 vertical-brand data. The 2026 letter does not mention vertical brands (KNOWN). The FY2022 letter (2023-05-05) said "creating powerhouse vertical brands" without naming them, so letters mention vertical brands in FY22 and FY23 and not after. That is a minor nuance to KNOWN_BRIEF's "named only FY23", which is technically correct about naming.
- **Wording de-emphasis (N1-1, N1-4, N1-5).** This is consistent with the F3/F1 "management has gone quiet" picture. It could be pure drafting (the FY25 10-K rewrote all its FLS into a bullet list after the Foot Locker deal), so weight it lightly.
- **Licensed brands inside the metric (N1-2).** This is the most material caveat for M1's penetration math. The new FY23-FY24 licensed lines could account for part of the +$0.2B FY23→FY25.
- **Rounding.** The 10-K dollar figures are rounded to $0.1B, so year-over-year growth computed from them has error bands of about ±6pts.
- **Other indirect items in W01/W02 are not owned-brand specific.** Inventory valuation allowances (incl. vendor allowances) were $86.1M (FY21), $139.5M, $167.7M, $180.1M and $219.8M (FY25, incl. FL). Non-cancellable purchase commitments (marketing, naming rights, technology, trademark licenses) were ~$265M (FY22), ~$215M, ~$180M and ~$450M (FY25, incl. FL). Advertising net of co-op was $410.9M (FY21) → $641.1M (FY25). None can be isolated to vertical brands. All are BURIED (W01/W02).
