DICK'S Sporting Goods · owned brands · overnight scrape, Oct 7 2026

DKS Owned-Brand Evidence

What a night of web scraping and a full re-read of the research folder found on whether DICK'S own brands (DSG, CALIA, VRST, Maxfli and the rest) are gaining mix faster than the Street assumes, and what to model. Everything below is new relative to the notes and primers.

Bottom line. Yes, owned brands are gaining, and the gain sped up from about April 2026. The best evidence comes from the free data dicks.com itself publishes. Owned brands' share of apparel reviews rose from 40% to 46% in two years, mostly at Nike's expense. Their share of the products dicks.com lists jumped by almost a point in the last year after nearly two flat years, and their share of new styles nearly doubled since FY23.

The gain is steady rather than explosive. It supports modelling about +0.5 point a year of extra penetration, not +1 point. The money is small: the base case adds about +7.5bps to DICK'S segment gross margin and +$0.09 to FY27 EPS. Pitch owned brands as a margin cushion against the promotional footwear market, not as an EPS driver.

01What to model

Bottom line: model owned-brand penetration of non-footwear sales rising about half a point a year, with footwear share easing. That takes owned brands from about 12.8% of DICK'S sales to 13.7% in FY27 and 14.2% in FY28, against a consensus that implicitly holds them flat.

Why the headline has sat at "about 13%" for years

DICK'S does not make its own athletic footwear, and footwear grew from 20% to 30% of DICK'S sales over FY19–FY25. Measured against the sales owned brands can actually compete for (apparel plus hardlines), penetration rose from a FY23 trough of about 17.7% to about 19.1% in FY25. That rise is real: the bands from the rounded 10-K disclosures don't overlap. But FY25 is only back at the FY20 and FY22 highs.

What this shows: owned share of total DICK'S sales (dashed) stays flat because footwear (grey) keeps growing. Share of non-footwear merchandise (green) is recovering.

  • Scale check. Moving FY27 EPS by 5% from mix alone would need about 6.7 extra points of owned mix, which is not plausible. By contrast, a single quarter of promotional pressure (JPM had about 90bps for Q3) equals years of mix benefit. That is why the cushion framing fits better.
  • What the base case asks for. Owned-brand dollars growing about 6–9% a year, against about 4.6% implied by consensus. The review data below points to roughly +5–7% in Q2 to early Q3 FY26.
  • Licensed brands are not the explanation. The "vertical brands" figure includes licensed lines (adidas football, Cobra, Marucci, Lotto), but they are only about 3–6% of it and shrinking. Owned-only sales grew about 16% over FY23–FY25, versus 12.5% for the reported total.

02The evidence, strongest first

Bottom line: four independent readings of dicks.com all point the same way from spring 2026: reviews, listings, new styles and page placement. All come from data the site publishes itself and can be re-run for free.

dicks.com hosts its reviews on Bazaarvoice, whose public feed shows every review with its date, brand and category. Reviews prompted by the post-purchase email track units sold. Owned brands' share of apparel reviews rose every year, while Nike's fell.

One quirk to know: DICK'S stopped sending its weekly post-purchase review email after August 11, 2026, the first non-holiday pause longer than three weeks. The cause is unknown. So the cleanest Q3 read is August 1–17, where owned share is +1.9pt in apparel and +5.3pt in men's.

2. Owned brands' share of what dicks.com lists jumped in the last year Strong

Common Crawl, a free public web archive, captures dicks.com category pages every month or so. Each page carries the site's own "Vertical Brand" flag on every product. Across 735 category pages captured in both years, owned brands went from 5.82% to 6.76% of listed products (Aug/Sep 2025 to Aug/Sep 2026). The 95% range is +0.56 to +1.42pt, so the gain is real. Over the previous 20 months the share had slipped 0.26pt. Owned listings grew 25% while all listings grew 8%.

What this shows: growth in listed products by brand on the same 735 pages, Aug/Sep 2025 to Aug/Sep 2026.

  • Where it came from: women's apparel +1.4pt, men's apparel +1.4pt, fitness +2.4pt, accessories +1.6pt, outdoor +1.1pt. Kids, team sports and golf were flat to down.
  • Golf Galaxy: owned share of women's golf apparel went 13.1% → 17.6% → 21.0% (June 2026). CALIA is now the #1 brand there by product count.
  • Same-season check: in the 45 core categories, owned share was flat from February 2024 to August 2025 (7.1% to 7.1%), then rose 8.1% to 8.9% in the same season a year later.

3. DICK'S is launching owned styles faster than ever Strong

The Bazaarvoice catalog lists every style DICK'S has ever sold, with a season code. Owned brands' share of new styles has stepped up, and DICK'S is assigning its own product barcodes about 25–30% faster than in 2022–24. Those barcodes now go almost entirely to owned brands. That's about 47,000 a year since Q3 2025, versus 36,000–38,000 before.

What this shows: owned share of new styles each season, excluding footwear and fan gear (FY26 is season to date).

  • Men's: VRST plus DSG already have 102 new men's styles this season, against 94 in all of FY25. Nike is at 56% of its FY25 count and adidas at 64%.
  • Women's and golf moved the wrong way: women's went 14.2% to 12.1% (CALIA 76 new styles vs 169) and golf 7.2% to 6.3%.
  • Owned footwear is back, but tiny: 21 styles in FY26 versus 4 in FY25, including the first adult DSG performance shoe (pickleball). That is 0.4% of new footwear styles, so keep it at zero in the model.

03What cuts against

Bottom line: owned brands are growing partly by being discounted heavily, CALIA is weakening, and management has stopped talking about the brands. None of this breaks the base case, but it rules out the bull case for now.

  • Heavy discounting. In Jun–Sep 2026, 47–54% of owned products were marked down, against 25–28% for national brands. The owned figure rose about 12 points y/y (national about 1.5). CALIA went from 56% to 67% on sale, and DICK'S runs "Exclusive Brands" clearance days at up to 90% off.
  • CALIA is the weak spot. Women's review share −4.8pt, listings −5%, search interest −13% y/y in Jul–Sep, Instagram likes per post −33%. Women's is where owned share is highest, so CALIA matters.
  • Organic reviews disagree. Among reviews not prompted by the email (about 5% of the total), owned apparel share fell from 56.8% to 50.1% y/y.
  • Management went quiet. Owned brands got 33 mentions at the September 2025 Goldman conference and none at the June 2026 AGM, the August call or the September 2026 conference. The 10-K and 10-Q language about growing the brands has been dropped, and the August 2026 release added "competition, innovation" to the vertical-brand risk wording.
  • Golf clubs and imports. Owned golf-club review share fell from 18.1% to 13.2%. The Jul–Sep 2026 import surge turned out to be a catch-up after a tariff-hit 2025: owned brands' share of DICK'S direct imports is flat, and owned hardgoods imports are 43% below 2024.
  • History is slow. Owned mix has sat at 12–15% of sales since FY17. Past jumps of 1–2 points came only when DICK'S dropped vendors (as in 2017). Academy's private label slipped from 23% to 22% in FY25.

Paid routes worth asking about:

  • YipitData: has SKU-level DICK'S sales data, the only true monthly owned-brand mix series. Its gated reports include brand share at DICK'S.
  • Particl: estimates product-level online sales on dicks.com since May 2023. Use only the brand shares; its absolute dollar figures look inflated.
  • Numerator: receipt-panel data, through a broker with access such as JPM.
  • Panjiva: full shipping records, which would give monthly series by brand.
  • iSpot or Vivvix: advertising spend in dollars.

Google Trends is not usable for April–June 2026: every shopping search spiked in that window while unrelated searches stayed flat.
