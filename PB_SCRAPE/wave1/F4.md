## Evidence against / caveats
- **Consumer trading up, not down (the DKS shopper in 2026).** This is DUP context: it is already in the primers, but it matters for framing.
  - UGG Sr Dir (07-29): "They're not observing any trading down from their consumer regarding price point tiers"; DKS consumer "trading up and shopping up".
  - UA (07-10): "DSG consumer is trading up and buying more pinnacle, more premium product", while mass/Academy get the trade-down.
  - So the thesis should rest on owned brands taking the mid-tier / "basics" slot that national brands are leaving, plus 700-900bps margin. It should not rest on DKS shoppers trading down. VRST is pitched as the "largest premium men's apparel brand", which fits a trade-up shopper.
- **DSG fleece softness.** UA expert: premium fleece at $120-150 (Nike Tech Fleece, "DSG fleece", adidas Z.N.E.) is soft and "Fleece becomes more of a commodity". This is a DUP (already in PRIMER_DENSE) but it is a direct AGAINST datapoint on an owned brand. Verified at SOURCE line 227.
- **The UGG manager (07-28) says DKS is "very, very brand driven"**, with name brands selling through better than PL. Already known (E13).
- **New national and emerging brands are being added for freshness**, competing for the same floor space as owned brands:
  - premium men's apparel brand (07-08) brought in because "some brands getting stale";
  - Free People, Skims and "smaller brands taking share";
  - UA says DKS is "picking more one brand to stand for something".
  - Vendor consolidation can cut either way. The primer already reads it as a vertical-brand tailwind (DUP).
- **Rounded deck numbers show owned brands only barely beating the company.** INFERENCE from known numbers: $1.7B FY24 → ~$1.8B FY25 is about +6%, vs DKS Business sales +5.0% ($13.44B → $14.11B). Mix moved roughly 12.6% → 12.8%, inside rounding. "Outpace" is real but small. Treat this as a ceiling check on aggressive mix-path assumptions.
- **No new disclosure since Mar-2026.** The Aug-2026 deck repeats the FY25 vertical-brand slide word for word (F4-1).
- **The consensus GM path is already captured elsewhere** (DUP, so not reported as a finding): Bloomberg DICK'S segment GM is 35.42% in both 3Q26E and 4Q26E, vs 36.04% and 35.63% a year earlier, and ~36% through FY27-FY28E (C05 setA/setB; PRIMER_DENSE). This is the baseline any owned-brand mix upside has to beat.
