[F6-2] **Excluding footwear, the falling headline owned-brand % is actually flat at about 18%. Footwear mix explains all of the decline from the FY20 peak (INFERENCE).**
- Owned-brand % of sales (WF Exh 85): FY20 15%, FY21 14%, FY22 14%, FY23 13%, FY24 13%.
- Footwear % of sales (10-K): FY20 19%, FY21 21%, FY22 24%, FY23 26%, FY24 28%.
- Owned-brand share of non-footwear sales:

| FY | Owned-brand % of non-footwear sales |
|---|---|
| FY20 | 18.5% |
| FY21 | 17.7% |
| FY22 | 18.4% |
| FY23 | 17.6% |
| FY24 | 18.1% |

- The calculation assumes DKS has about 0% vertical footwear, which matches Stack's "won't do vertical footwear" (KNOWN).
- Over FY21-FY24, Nike rose from 17% to 25% of sales. Exh 83 merch mix: apparel flat at 34-35% and hardlines down from 45% to 37% over FY21-FY24.
- **Sources:** C06R5 › 2026-08-17_WellsFargo_..._Retail_Primer (Exh 83 SOURCE lines 5700-5772; Exh 85 lines 5873-5938; both verified); W01_DKS_10K_FY2024-FY2025 and W02 mix tables.
- **Method:** arithmetic on BURIED and KNOWN data.
- **Tag:** BURIED inputs, NEW calculation. **Confidence:** M. **Direction:** SUPPORTS a conditional thesis.
- **Model use:**
  - Model owned-brand mix as about 18% × (1 − footwear mix), plus any gain within apparel and hardlines.
  - If DICK'S-banner footwear mix stalls or falls in the 2H26-2027 "legacy footwear hangover" (UBS, WF and Barclays all say it could last into 2027), mix rises mechanically by about 0.18pt for each 1pt drop in footwear mix.
  - This is the cleanest argument that consensus's flat 13% is too low if footwear weakens. It also explains why mix fell without the brands being weak.
  - Caveat: the 13% figure is "% of merch" in WF's wording but "% of sales" in KNOWN, so the inputs differ slightly in definition.

[F6-3] **The best historical analog (INFERENCE) is 2017. Then, a Nike-led channel glut that JPM says 2026 "rhymes with" coincided with private-brand mix jumping from 10% to 14% in two years.**
- JPM (25-Aug-26): DKS's 2Q26 comments "rhymed with 2Q17", "perhaps by the same player". In 3Q17 DKS merch margin fell about 200bps, and about 100bps in 4Q17.
- WF: in 2017 DKS split vendors into strategic, transactional and "other". The "other" group was about 20% of the vendor base and "could be replaced with higher returns from larger brands and/or private label" (SOURCE line 1716 of the WF upgrade).
- Private brand %: FY16 10% → FY17 12% → FY18 14% (WF Exh 85).
- **Period:** 2017-2018 analog vs 2H26-2027.
- **Sources:** C06R5 › 2026-08-28_JPMorgan_..._Burden_of_Proof (PM's take); C06R4/R5 › WF upgrade and primer (vendor segmentation; Exh 85).
- **Method:** I combined BURIED sources. The 2017→2026 link is mine.
- **Tag:** BURIED inputs, NEW framing. **Confidence:** M-L. **Direction:** SUPPORTS.
- **Model use:** gives a precedent for +1-2pts of mix in each of the two years after a national-brand glut. Use it as an upside case, not the base case. In 2017 DKS also exited weak vendors, and nothing similar has been announced in 2026.

- Barclays OW reason #3 "increased private label" was repeated 25-Aug-26 (DUP).

[F6-5] **The 2Q26 core GM bridge leaves no room for an owned-brand mix contribution. This cuts AGAINST "rapid acceleration".**
- Core 2Q26 GM was 37.8%, up about 80bps y/y.
- JPM breaks it down as +50bps IEEPA tariff refunds ($19MM), DMN about +25bps and GameChanger about +35bps. Promotions (athletic footwear/apparel at quarter-end) and fuel/supply chain partly offset this.
- Barclays: "core Dick's GM improvement from DMN and GameChanger growth offset higher promotions, fuel and supply chain costs, and negative mix".
- UBS: DICK'S segment OM fell 42bps on "targeted pricing investments".
- 1Q26: core GM −36bps y/y, with trading-card mix (lower margin rate) cited as a drag.
- **Sources:** C06R5 › 2026-08-28_JPMorgan_..._Burden_of_Proof (takeaway 4); C06R6 › Barclays 2026-09-01 and UBS 2026-08-31; C06R4 › JPM 2026-06-02.
- **Tag:** BURIED (also in C01_SYNTHESIS_B line 1326). **Confidence:** M. **Direction:** AGAINST.
- **Model use:** if owned brands were accelerating sharply, about 700-900bps × Δmix should show up. For example, +1pt of mix would add roughly 8bps. That is too small to see against these items, so the bridge neither proves nor disproves a ~0.5-1pt mix gain. It does rule out a very large one.

[F6-6] **Management describes no trade-down, comps are ticket-led, and new national apparel brands are taking space. This cuts AGAINST the value/trade-down route to owned-brand gains.**
- JPM (1Q and 2Q call takeaways): "all consumer cohorts healthy… no trade-down". Ticket and traffic: 1Q26 +5.5% / +0.5%; 2Q26 +3.6% / +1.3%.
- UBS (26-Aug-26): demand moved to "newer performance-oriented products, innovation-driven launches, and emerging brands… UGG, Birkenstock, and several newer apparel concepts". The apparel concepts are unnamed. I checked SOURCE line 319 and it does not say whether they are owned.
- Barclays (27-May-26): new brand introductions in select stores (Vuori, Gymshark, Helly Hansen, Salomon), plus expansion of FP Movement and On Apparel.
- These overlap directly with CALIA (women's) and VRST (men's premium).
- **Sources:** C06R4 › JPM 2026-06-02, Barclays 2026-06-03; C06R5 › JPM 2026-08-28; C06R6 › UBS 2026-08-31.
- **Tag:** BURIED. **Confidence:** H. **Direction:** AGAINST.
- **Model use:** do not justify the mix lift with trade-down. If you argue it, use the footwear-mix mechanics (F6-2) and the price-gap channel (F6-7).

[F6-7] **National-brand price points are inflating and markdowns are disorderly. These are the conditions for a wider value gap (indirect, INFERENCE).**
- PCE sporting goods prices are +3.5% 2026 YTD after 2010-2025 deflation. Monthly: Jan +2.0%, Feb +3.9%, Mar +3.7%, Apr +4.1%, May +4.5%, Jun +3.0%. Units are +6.0%. WF attributes this to tariff pricing.
- WF and Barclays (25-Aug-26): "MAP breaks, some retailers cutting well below MAP" in lifestyle footwear.
- JPM estimates about 90bps of promo pressure at DKS in 3Q26.
- Barclays absolute promo score (100 = least promotional): 4Q24 65, 1Q25 65, 2Q25 62, 3Q25 50, 4Q25 31, 1Q26 42.
- Owned brands have negligible China/tariff exposure (KNOWN), so tariff-led price increases at national brands should widen the gap.
- **Sources:** C06R5 › WF primer Exh 94-95; C06R6 › WF 2026-09-01, Barclays 2026-09-01; C06R4 › Barclays 2026-06-03.
- **Tag:** BURIED. The Barclays promo series is also in the digest. **Confidence:** M. **Direction:** SUPPORTS (indirect).
- **Model use:** supports holding owned-brand prices while national brands carry tariff price increases. The markdown chaos is mostly in footwear, where DKS has no owned product.

[F6-8] **Benchmark: ASO, the value-positioned peer, moved owned-brand penetration only +3pts in eight years and fell 1pt in FY25.**
- ASO private brands (WF Exh 86): 20% each year FY17-FY23, 23% FY24, 22% FY25 (SOURCE lines 5972-5981, verified).
- ASO GM drivers include a "softlines/private label mix".
- ASO's FY25 decline happened while it was adding Jordan and HOKA allocation, which is the same crowd-out dynamic DKS saw with Nike.
- **Source:** C06R5 › WF primer.
- **Tag:** BURIED. The KNOWN brief has only "~22%". **Confidence:** H. **Direction:** AGAINST rapid acceleration.
- **Model use:** caps a realistic path at about +0.5-1.0pt a year, unless footwear mix falls (F6-2).

## Evidence against / caveats
- Owned-brand discounting increased in 2026: six tracked owned-brand events at 50-75% off vs one in the 2025 windows (F6-1). There is also a 12/18/25 "75% off vertical brands" event.
- The 2Q26 core GM bridge is fully explained by tariff refunds, DMN and GameChanger. There is no identifiable owned-brand contribution (F6-5). Core GM was −36bps in 1Q26.
- "No trade-down; all cohorts healthy" (management via JPM, 1Q and 2Q26). Comps are ticket-driven.
- New third-party apparel brands (Vuori, Gymshark, FP Movement, On Apparel, Helly Hansen, Salomon) are competing for CALIA/VRST space.
- The headline owned-brand % fell from 15% (FY20) to 13% (FY24) while Nike rose from 17% to 25%. Brand access, not owned brands, is the core DKS narrative in every 2026 broker note: "brands need DKS" (JPM) and "gatekeeper for athletic brands" (WF).
- The pro forma consolidated figure is about 11% (FL dilutes it). Any FL apparel shift toward national brands (KNOWN, Stack) pushes consolidated mix down further.
- ASO, the value peer, did not accelerate in FY25 (F6-8).
- WF Exh 83 merch mix differs slightly from the 10-K. WF has FY24 footwear at 29% vs 28% in the 10-K and apparel at 34% vs 33%, because WF excludes "other". I used the 10-K for F6-2.
- None of the R4-R6 broker notes quantifies vertical-brand sales or growth for 2026.
