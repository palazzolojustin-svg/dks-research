# X13: Benchmarks and historical analogs for modelling the DKS owned-brand mix path
Agent: X13, wave 1. Run date 2026-10-07. All web/EDGAR items accessed 2026-10-07.
Method summary: (1) SEC EDGAR full-history pull of every 10-K / S-1 / 424B4 for DKS, ASO, SPWH, BGFV, HIBB, KSS, TGT, TSCO, COST, FL, with regex extraction of every private/owned/vertical-brand sentence containing a number (scripts/X13_edgar_privatebrand_history.py builds the cache; scripts/X13_extract_fast.py re-extracts; raw/X13_privatebrand_sentences_fast.csv); (2) Canadian Tire Investor Factbook PDF (pypdf); (3) earnings-call transcripts / trade press via WebFetch; (4) grep of CORE_NOTES / WORKING_NOTES / primers / PB_SCRAPE for novelty.
Arithmetic marked [d]; anything not reported by a source is labelled INFERENCE.

[X13-2] **The $2B vertical-brand goal is 8.5 years old, and its stated timing has slipped.** Q4 FY16 call (Fortune, 2017-03-07): in-house brands at "$1 billion a year". 10-K FY16 (filed 2017-03): "The Company anticipates the private brand business exceeding $1 billion in sales during fiscal 2017." Q4 FY17 call (2018-03-13, fool.com transcript): "we continue to believe our private brand business can reach $2 billion over a relatively short period of time". Q2 FY18 call (2018-08-29): "grow our private brand business to $2 billion over time". Q3 FY18 call (2018-11-28): CFO Belitsky declined to give penetration "for competitive reasons". Q4 FY18 call (2019-03-12): "$2 billion sales goal". FY23-FY25: "goal $2B" (DUP). Status: NEW for the 2017-2018 quotes and the "relatively short period" wording. Confidence: H. AGAINST. INFERENCE: going from $1.0B to $1.8B took 8 years (~7.6% CAGR [d]), roughly the same pace as total DKS sales. Management targets on owned brands have historically been met late. Model use: treat any mgmt mix ambition as a ceiling, not a base case.

[X13-3] **DKS's past bursts of mix gain came from deliberately exiting or replacing vendors, not from organic consumer pull.**
- Mar-2017: DKS cut ~20% of vendors, about 320 of 1,600 (Fortune, 2017-03-07). At the time Nike was "20% of its business" and Under Armour "12%". Chain Store Age (2017-03-30) described the new three-tier model: strategic partners, a "growing collection of private labels", and "transactional relationships".
- FY17→FY18 +2pts (12%→14%): UA was losing distribution (Q2 FY18 call: UA about 300bps of comp drag), and in 2019 DSG was launched to "replace Reebok" with "meaningful floor space" (Q4 FY18 call). Q4 FY17 call: Reebok was "90% of the apparel product on our floor" in that line.
- SGB Online (2020-03-10): DSG was expected to "surpass Field & Stream to become Dick's largest private brand by end of year".
- Analog: Target's All in Motion launched Jan-2020 replacing licensed C9 by Champion and passed $1B in its first year (StarTribune / Store Brands, Jan-2021).
Status: NEW for the vendor counts, the 2016 Nike/UA shares, the Reebok replacement and the DSG>F&S line (the folder has only WF's "~20% of vendor base"). Confidence: H. Model use: a step-up of +1-2pts in one year has precedent only when DKS hands owned brands a national or licensed vendor's floor space. That makes the trigger to watch any national-brand pullback or exit in apparel or accessories. In 2026 the visible flow runs the other way (KNOWN_BRIEF: national brands to get a "meaningful increase" in FL apparel).

[X13-6] **Kohl's is the upper-bound analog for pace, and the cautionary one for reversal.** Kohl's 10-Ks, private + exclusive brands as % of sales: FY05 30.0% → FY06 ~35% → FY07 ~39% (+300bps) → FY08 ~42% (+260bps) → FY09 44.3% (+220bps) → FY10 48% (+290bps) → FY11 50% (+240bps) → FY12 52% (+160bps), so about +3pts a year for 7 years. GM effect: FY10 GM +41bps "driven by strong inventory management and increased penetration of private and exclusive brands". FY13 10-K: "In the last 6 months of 2013, national brands had improved sales results and represented a growing percentage of our sales". After that the mix fell to about 34% by FY24 (secondary source: RetailWire; images in the 10-Ks, not text). In the FY24 10-K, inventory was +2% "as we invested into private brand inventory". Source: EDGAR CIK 885639. Status: NEW. Confidence: H for FY05-FY12, M for the ~34% figure. Model use: +3pts a year is the empirical ceiling, reached only by a dept store converting floor space wholesale. It reversed, by about 18pts, once national brands regained heat.

## Recommended modelling range (INFERENCE)
Starting point: FY25 vertical brands are about 12.8% of DICK'S Business sales ($1.8B / $14.11B). Consensus implicitly holds about 13% flat.
- **Bear: flat at ~12.8-13%.** Vertical-brand $ grow in line with DKS Business (~+3-4%). This matches FY21-FY25, Target, TSCO, ASO FY25 and SportChek post-2019. It is the more likely outcome if the 2026 national-brand pipeline (Nike recovery, new brands) keeps the space.
- **Base: +30-50bps a year.** Vertical $ grow about 3-4pts faster than DKS Business (~+7-8% vs ~+4%). That gives FY26E ~13.2%, FY27E ~13.6%, FY28E ~14.0%, which is a return to the FY21-22 level, not a new high. Support: DKS's own FY23-25 trend (+25bps a year), ASO (+0.6), CTC (+0.45), Costco (+0.5), and the 2026 double-digit growth in peer "better" owned brands (ASO Freely/R.O.W.). Implied $: FY26E ≈ 13.2% × $14.6B (guide midpoint $14.5-14.7B) ≈ $1.93B [d], so the $2B goal lands around FY26-27.
- **Bull: +100bps a year.** That gives FY28E ~15.8%, above the FY20 peak of 15%. Precedent exists only with deliberate vendor or space conversion: DKS FY16-20 (+1.25 a year) and FY01-06 (+2.3 a year), Kohl's FY05-12 (+3.1 a year). It needs evidence of a national-brand exit, or of vertical brands taking incremental House of Sport / Field House space. Do not use it without a scraped catalyst.
- **GM translation:** the mix effect is about Δmix × 700-900bps premium, so +40bps of mix a year ≈ +3bps DSG GM a year, and the bull case ≈ +8bps a year. Against Bloomberg's flat DSG GM of 36.2-36.3%, even the bull case adds only about 20-25bps by FY28E [d]. Owned-brand mix is a small GM lever by itself. Its larger value is differentiation and full-price selling, which this avenue cannot quantify.
- **Disclosure granularity matters:** DKS reports "approximately 13%" and $ to $0.1B. Under the base case the 10-K would still say "~13%" through FY26, and might first print "~14%" in the FY27 10-K (Mar-2028). Consensus cannot be disproven from company disclosure for about 18 months, so alternative data (X01 reviews, X02 census, X04 imports) is the only way to front-run it.

## Evidence against / caveats
- The base rate is poor. Across 10 filers, sustained owned-brand mix gains of more than 1pt a year happened only when a retailer actively converted space (Kohl's 2005-12, TSCO 2010-13, DKS 2001-06 and 2016-20). Every one was followed by a plateau or a reversal: Kohl's -18pts, DKS -2.7pts FY20-23, Target -3pts by FY25, ASO -1pt FY25.
- DKS vertical-brand dollars were flat at $1.6-1.7B from FY21 to FY24 (10-Ks). FY25's $1.8B is the first $ growth in 4 years, and the reported "~13%" has not moved since FY23.
- The $2B target dates from 2017-18 and was described then as "a relatively short period of time". It slipped.
- The athletic-retail analog (SportChek) has also plateaued at ~13%. This fits Stack's "never super high" (footwear is national-brand-only).
- ASO's 2026 private-label commentary is qualitative only. Its deck still shows 22% (as of Dec-2025), and customer reach for owned brands fell 60%→53% over FY19-FY25.
- Definitional breaks: DKS FY06 vs WF exhibit, TSCO FY13 and FY25, Target "sales" vs "merchandise sales". Treat the old % series as directional.
- Kohl's ~34% FY24, Costco +50bps, and JD Sports' "+12% private label 2025" come from secondary or search snippets and are unverified.
