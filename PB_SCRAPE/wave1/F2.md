## Evidence against / caveats
- **Category tailwind is national/licensed, not owned.** 1H FY26 hardlines growth is attributed to the World Cup (adidas partnership), trading cards (Fanatics; dilutive, lower-margin) and team sports. Golf, outdoor equipment and accessories declined in Q2 FY26 (W04 L1157). Those are owned-brand-heavy pockets (Maxfli/Top-Flite/Walter Hagen/Tommy Armour; Alpine Design).
- **Derived DICK'S apparel is flat-to-down** (F2-3). If owned brands are mostly apparel, dollar growth is capped unless share-of-category gains are large.
- **GM has never visibly reflected PL mix** (F2-5). Q2 FY26 core merch margin ex-IEEPA refunds/DMN/GameChanger was ≈ −30bps (C01B L347, L1334). That is consistent with promo offsetting any mix benefit, or with mix not rising fast.
- **Mgmt's own FY26 GM lever list omits vertical brands.** It cites GC, Media and buying synergies vs HoS costs and the new DC (JPM CFO takes 2026-03-12, C01B L1459). Q4 FY25 merch margin was "not down" with "mix/GC/Media modest contributors". UBS (2026-03-13, C01B L1472) is the broker that names "private label" as an offset.
- **Promo-discipline expectation.** Barclays (2025-11-26, C01B L1256/L1430) expected FL to be "more disciplined under the DKS umbrella", improving industry promos. That would narrow, not widen, the owned vs national price gap. The Aug-2026 evidence (MAP breaks, JPM ~90bps 3Q promo pressure) points the other way.
- **Data consistency.** Wells' % series (13% FY23) vs mgmt $1.6B on $12.98B (12.3%) disagree by ~0.7pt. "~$1.8B" FY25 is rounded. The FL category mix (80-84% footwear in FL 10-Ks) is the key assumption behind F2-1 (FY25) and F2-3. F2-1 for FY21-FY24 needs no FL assumption.
- **Mechanical, not organic.** Part of the F2-1 upside is a denominator effect from footwear weakness. A Spring-2027 footwear recovery (Nike innovation; brokers expect clarity "during 2027") reverses it (scenario: 12.2% FY27).
