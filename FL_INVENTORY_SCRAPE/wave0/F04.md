## Known evidence FOR the thesis (FL inventory/SKU/assortment shift → higher uptake / less discounting)

### Diagnosis (the old inventory was the problem)
- Stack, 2025-11-25 (Q3 FY25 call): "Foot Locker strayed from Retail 101 ... did not react quickly enough when its largest brand pivoted toward a direct-to-consumer model, leaving Foot Locker with the wrong inventory, too much of what didn't sell and not enough of what did sell." (C02A › earnings_calls/DKS_Q3-FY2025_Earnings-Call_2025-11-25.md)
- Stack, 2025-12-03 (MS conf): biggest FL profit deterioration was margin rate, "500 to 600 basis points of margin erosion"; "we can reverse that trend"; "the solution is simple, the work is hard"; turnaround = "back to retail 101". (C03B › conferences/DKS_MorganStanley-Conference_2025-12-03.md)
- Stack, 2026-04-08 (JPM forum): agreed FL merch margins down 500-600bps over 5 yrs ("Correct"): too much wrong product, not enough right product → markdowns, plus apparel cut; "I don't think there's anything structural"; lost vendor support → bought next-tier product → didn't sell → markdowns; FL lacked a planning/allocation/replenishment group. (C03B › conferences/DKS_JPMorgan-Retail-Forum_2026-04-08.md)
- Stack, 2025-11-25: FL "lacked access/allocation to key product; stores out of stock"; anecdote: NY store manager ("we're a great running store") had just got Nike's running construct the week before, and it sold at full price; confident of a "meaningful increase in their gross margin". (C02A › Q3-FY2025 call)
- Stack, 2025-05-15 (deal call): "Nike business will go up at a higher margin rate" via more full-price selling = "big precept" of the deal; FL apparel "deteriorating & under-invested". (C03B › events/DKS_Foot-Locker-Acquisition-Call_2025-05-15.md)
- Gupta, 2025-12-03: international brands say there is "so much low-hanging fruit in just buying the product better". (C03B › MS 2025-12-03)

### 11-store pilot → Fast Break (SKU cut, wall reset, apparel)
- 2025-11-25: 11-store pilot "capital-light": removed most inventory, re-laid out the wall; FL walls were "merely a run-on sentence of shoes", now segmented; Stack "pretty enthusiastic about the results"; apparel brought back and "selling really quite well". (C02A › Q3-FY2025 call)
- 2025-12-03: the test started about 10 days after close; all merchandise removed; franchises highlighted (Nike AF1, New Balance 9060, adidas Campus); "roughly 30% less SKUs"; apparel "much better"; "real green shoots". (C03B › MS 2025-12-03)
- 2026-03-12 (Q4 FY25 call), Stack: Fast Break stores drove "very strong positive comps, actually meaningfully exceeding the DICK'S Business, while also delivering strong gross margin improvement"; ~30% of shoe-wall styles removed; +10 LA stores before NBA All-Star, "strong early perf"; ~250 stores by BTS ("Herculean effort"); closure list "much smaller than we initially estimated" because Fast Break makes losing or marginal stores "very profitable". (C02B › earnings_calls/DKS_Q4-FY2025_Earnings-Call_2026-03-12.md)
- 2026-03-12 Q&A (Horvers): the 11-store test spanned urban, suburban, high- and low-volume stores; walls re-laid with key product (e.g. "Air Force 1 in color, New Balance launch"); comps "extremely strong"; "If we weren't highly confident ... we wouldn't be rolling out 250 of them by back-to-school." (C02B › Q4-FY2025 call)
- 2026-04-08 (JPM): 11 stores showed a "huge impact"; LA +10 → 21 stores; "more than pleasantly surprised"; results "really phenomenal"; lease-line windows: Valentine's Day pack and Air Max 95 launch both "highly successful"; House of Hoops walls removed where adjacent; ~250 US stores by BTS out of a US FL fleet of "like 650 or 700". (C03B › JPM 2026-04-08)
- 2026-05-27 (Q1 FY26 call), Stack: ~90 stores added in Q1 → ~100 Fast Break stores; "double-digit comps in Q1 + meaningful merch margin improvement"; each remodel typically done in a few days at limited capital; plan ~250 by BTS across FL, Kids FL and Champs globally. (C02B › earnings_calls/DKS_Q1-FY2026_Earnings-Call_2026-05-27.md)
- 2026-08-25 (Q2 FY26 call): Fast Break passed the ~250 BTS goal; Fast Break "definitely outperformed the traditional legacy stores"; year-end target "somewhere in the north of 300, 350 doors ... globally". (C02B › earnings_calls/DKS_Q2-FY2026_Earnings-Call_2026-08-25.md)
- 2026-09-14 (GS conf), Stack: initial plan was a 30% SKU cut, but "there's more editing to be done": the 20%-of-sales tail still holds too many SKUs. Plan is to reinvest in working colors/franchises, deeper sizes and in-stock, and to add fixtures and apparel, which "will help drive the margin rate". McShane: 250 stores now, ~350 by year-end. (C03B › conferences/DKS_Goldman-Conference_2026-09-14.md)
- Expert (Sr Dir, global footwear brand / UGG category, 2026-07-29): DKS has "done an incredible job of editing to amplify ... really decluttered the space for Foot Locker, which has strengthened our ability to grow the business". DKS cleaned up FL breadth "aggressively". "SKU reduction of about 20%-30%, which isn't dropping revenue expectations, it's just offering a cleaner and tighter assortment." The cleanup "removed clutter (closeouts, markdowns)" and produces a "much stronger margin". Fast Break "cleans up shoe wall, highlights key volume drivers"; ordering is "positive and more strategic". (C04 › expert_calls_alphasense/DKS_Expert-Call_2026-07-29_Senior-Director_Global-Footwear-Brand_womens-footwear-apparel.md)
- Expert (Jordan/basketball merchandising director, 2026-07-13): DKS buying FL produced a "huge revenue shift" and a "four- to five-year plan" with projections and agreements across both businesses. FL is raising door penetration with apparel; "Fast Break gravitating to apparel"; the FL deal "increased apparel strategy (FL historically footwear-only)". (C04 › DKS_Expert-Call_2026-07-13_Merchandising-Director_Global-Athletic-Apparel-Brand.md)
- Expert (sock maker / Delta Galil, 2026-07-02): demand beat expectations on the World Cup and on FL integration; "The biggest proxy to shoes, it is actually socks"; with Fast Break, "they might order more stock", because Fast Break sells proxy categories. ### Garage clean-out (old inventory cleared → fresh base)
- 2026-03-12: clean-out "essentially complete". The goods were cleared through store markdowns, FL and Champs stores, and DKS's Going Going Gone channel, which recovered more cash than a jobber would have. "This inventory at Foot Locker is probably cleaner than it has ever been." Stack: "should have margin expansion here. We're confident of that." The $218M GAAP gross-profit hit from FL inventory optimization in Q4 FY25 was in line with plan. (C02B › Q4-FY2025 call)
- 2026-03-12 (Civello Q): GGG removes old inventory and clears the store so it can feature newer styles sold at "basically full price". (C02B › Q4-FY2025 call)
- 2026-04-08: GGG "footwear capacity ... has never been at 100%", so it has room for FL excess; Stack won't let the "run-on sentence" return. (C03B › JPM 2026-04-08)
- 2026-05-27: inventory "in terrific shape". (C02B › Q1-FY2026 call)
- 2026-09-14, Hobart: FL sees trends ahead of DKS, so DKS can taper buys on slowing franchises; GGG handles clearance. Stack described the ecosystem as atmos (Tier 0 seeding) → FL street/fashion doors + HoS → scale at DKS/FL → end of life in GGG: "nobody else has got that kind of visibility". (C03B › GS 2026-09-14)

### New product sells at full price; markdowns were on old goods
- 2025-11-25 (McShane Q): the marked-down goods are older unsold product; "new fresh innovative product (which FL mostly lacks now) will sell full price, arriving into 2026". Marked-down product "is not key product at DICK'S". (C02A › Q3-FY2025 call)
- 2026-08-25 (Hutchinson Q): brands supplied extra higher-heat product that can sell at full price, offsetting some margin; bringing apparel into FL stores and new-product allocations also help. (C02B › Q2-FY2026 call)
- 2026-09-14: running legacy discounts next to new full-price product is "not that difficult", because consumers know what is new (Nike Mind, Nike running, On Cloudtilt, new adidas running). AF1 in patent leather and AF1 in black: "can't keep those in stock". (C03B › GS 2026-09-14)

## Known evidence AGAINST
- FL Q2 FY26 PF comp −3.6%, down in both NA and international; FL op loss $31.9M; FY26 FL guide cut to comp −2% to 0% and OI −$80M to −$40M (from +1.5-3% and $110-150M). (C02B › Q2-FY2026 call)
- FL "hit harder": more exposed to legacy footwear silhouettes and more dependent on launch/retro product. There were fewer Q2 launches, and they performed "below industry and our expectations". Promotions, especially on legacy silhouettes, are expected "at least through the fourth quarter". (Stack, 2026-08-25, C02B)
- "a number of brands got very promotional on their sites, and those promotions spilled into the broader marketplace". A brand exec told Stack: "I've never seen the specialty channel of distribution so promotional in my career." (2026-08-25, C02B)
- "we're not leading this margin erosion. We are participating in it" (Stack, 2026-08-25). On 2026-09-14 he said DKS chose to "stay with the market from a price standpoint"; balance of year "similar to the second quarter"; discounting "going to continue through the fourth quarter". (C02B; C03B)
- "Q3 is going to be a bit more difficult than Q4" for FL (Stack, 2026-08-25). (C02B)
- The 2026-08-25 framing of the Fast Break stores themselves: they "still carry some legacy silhouettes, not perfect yet". On 2026-05-27 the improvement was attributed to presentation, not product ("Right now, yes"). (C02B)
- 2026-09-14, Stack: the transition to other brands/allocation is "taking longer than we anticipated"; he expected "more legacy brands" to support FL quicker, and "that hasn't happened"; "believe me, this is not easy with Foot Locker"; the legacy-silhouette pivot is "taking longer than we thought"; "menu" of options (possible restructuring). (C03B)
- 2026-09-14: "New Balance is a bit more difficult at Foot Locker. It's rocking in DICK'S." The FL consumer is "more fashion-conscious, faster" and "may be a little bit more under pressure". (C03B)
- 2026-09-14: FL "was not able to or didn't" pivot to On/Hoka the way DKS did; FL "pretty much missed the retro run category" (JPM 2026-04-08; Europe didn't buy P-6000 etc.). (C03B)
- Q1 FY26 FL US comp ~6% was helped by launch/Jordan Retro product (legacy launch product, not the new assortment); Q2 was "a disappointment in that product". Traditional AF1 (triple white/black, varsity colors) "slowed significantly". (Stack, 2026-09-14, C03B)
- EMEA: promotions "very aggressive", industry overinventoried, consumer cautious (Middle East war, fuel). Europe runs "roughly six months behind" the US (2026-04-08) and was expected to stay behind through YE (2026-05-27). (C02B; C03B)
- The profit cut is partly self-inflicted: "$200M revenue cut driving $200M profit cut" (Boruchow Q). Drivers per Stack: lower sales, margin, a "sizable" marketing investment, and higher Stripers payroll. (2026-08-25, C02B)
- 2026-05-27: "still some clearance in GM" ("always going to be clearance"); no buy-in synergies in FL GM yet. (C02B)
- Expert (Nike-side planner, 2026-07-06): hypothesis that "the two combined will purchase less than historically they have" (store rationalization and assortment overlap); impact "over the next two quarters". (C04 › DKS_Expert-Call_2026-07-06_Executive_Footwear-Brand-Planner_forecasting-logistics.md)
- Expert (Jordan merch dir, 2026-07-13): at FL, "Retro driving biz, bright spot", meaning FL's growth still leaned on legacy Retro; lifestyle is the "harder" space. (C04)
- Expert (UA merch dir, 2026-07-10): NB and Asics are doing well at DKS "despite softness at other accts esp mall retailers (Foot Locker, JD)"; Nike lifestyle/footwear "relatively slow"; "DICK'S and the marketplace at large are definitely getting more promotional."
