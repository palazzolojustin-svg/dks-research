## Known evidence FOR the thesis (FL inventory/SKU/assortment shift → higher uptake / less discounting)
(These are pre-DKS baseline facts that support the thesis indirectly: they show how much of FL's GM swing historically came from promo/markdown and Nike allocation, i.e. the lever the new assortment is supposed to pull.)
- GM rate history (on sales): 2019 31.8% | 2020 28.9% | 2021 34.4% | 2022 31.9% | 2023 27.7% | 2024 28.9%. Merch-margin component of GM change: 2020 −340 bps, 2021 +450, 2022 −240, 2023 −340, 2024 +100 (cost of merch % sales 56.3% → 59.7% → 58.7% for 2022/23/24, i.e. merch margin 43.7% / 40.3% / 41.3%). Promo/markdown is the dominant GM swing factor; 2021 peak to 2023 trough = −670 bps total GM, −590 bps of it merch margin [d]. (P_FL_10K_A › FL_10-K_FY2024 GM table; P_FL_10K_B › FL_10-K_FY2023/FY2022/FY2021 GM sections)
- Company's own attribution of GM by year (verbatim, SOURCE-checked): 2021 "increased to 34.4% as a result of decreased promotions, in part due to increased demand and relatively short supply of certain merchandise"; 2022 "a more promotional marketplace… We took pricing actions in line with the market in order to ensure that our inventory position remained current"; merch margin hit by "higher markdowns versus historically-low levels last year"; 2023 "higher promotional activity in the current marketplace and markdowns recorded to reduce overall inventory levels and improve aging… higher cost of merchandise and increased shrink"; 2024 "we were less promotional in 2024 despite the highly promotional marketplace and the liquidation of inventories in the geographies that we are closing… vendor allowances… improved our gross margin rate by 20 basis points… loyalty program… pressured the gross margin rate by 10 basis points". → Scarcity/demand (2021) produced the +550 bps year; the thesis mechanism (desirable product, less discounting) has a precedent in FL's own P&L. (P_FL_10K_A/B › FL 10-Ks FY2021-FY2024)
- 2024 was already a turn: comp +1.4% (stores +0.4%, DTC +6.2%), GM +120 bps on "decreased promotions", inventory turns 2.8x (+5% vs 2.7x), FL NA banner comp +3.5%, KFL +3.4%, FL EMEA +4.4%, Champs +2.8% Q3'24 and +1.8% Q4'24 after "Sport For Life" repositioning; drivers "positive response to product, refreshed assortments, strategic promotions". (P_FL_10K_A › FL_10-K_FY2024 Sales/GM)
- Pre-DKS assortment intent already pointed at sell-through: FY2024 10-K risk factor (verbatim): "developing additional capabilities to analyze customer behavior and demand, which we believe will allow us to better localize assortment and improve store-level allocations to further tailor our assortments to customer needs and increase sell-through… leveraging technology and data science to optimize our product-to-market processes… enhance our in-season responsiveness." Lace Up was primarily a STORE program (format/brand-standard), not a SKU-cut program. (P_FL_10K_A › Risk factors; SOURCE FL_10-K_FY2024 line 300)
- Nike dependence falling before DKS: Nike share of FL purchases ~75% (2020) → 68% (2021) → 65% (2022) → 65% (2023) → ~59% (2024); banners individually 50%-75% Nike (2021-22). Top-5 suppliers 91% / 87% / 86% / 84% / 85%; suppliers #2-5 combined [d] 16% / 19% / 21% / 19% / 26% (2020-2024), i.e. non-Nike top brands gained +7 pts in 2024 alone. Original target (FY2021 10-K): "Beginning with the fourth quarter of 2022, we do not expect any one supplier to represent more than 55% of merchandise purchases" (missed: 65% in 2022-23, 59% in 2024). ## Known evidence AGAINST
- FL's history is that GM improvements came from SCARCITY/stimulus (2021) rather than assortment skill; when supply normalized (2022-23) merch margin fell −240 then −340 bps. Any new-assortment uplift must overcome a market the company itself called "highly promotional" even in 2024 (EMEA "continued highly promotional marketplace"). (P_FL_10K_A › FY2024 Sales/GM)
- Pre-DKS "Lace Up" already promised localized assortment, store-level allocation, data science for sell-through and supplier diversification; 2024 result was only comp +1.4%, GM 28.9% (still −290 bps vs 2019's 31.8%, −550 vs 2021). Prior assortment resets did not restore margin. (P_FL_10K_A › Risk factors/MD&A)
- Vendor-mix changes hurt comps when Nike pulled allocation: 1Q23 comps −9.1% "due to changing vendor mix, coupled with macroeconomic headwinds"; Champs 1Q23 comp −24.6%; 2023 comp drivers incl. "changing vendor mix"; FY2022 footwear declines concentrated in men's & kids' basketball (1H22) and men's & kids' running (Q3'22); atmos hurt by "lack of certain key Adidas styles" and "declines in availability of key styles". → Launch/allocation dependence is high; diversification away from Nike lifestyle can cost comps in transition. (P_FL_10Q_A › FL_10-Q_FY2023-Q1 MD&A; FL_10-Q_FY2022-Q1/Q2/Q3 product commentary; P_FL_10K_B › FY2023)
- Supplier concentration still extreme: top-5 = 85% of 2024 purchases; "Each of our banners is highly dependent on Nike"; suppliers "allocate high-demand product on own criteria; may divert to own DTC"; markdown allowances / volume discounts / return rights "may not continue" (risk that the new-brand assortment carries less vendor markdown support). (P_FL_10K_A › Risk factors)
- Lead time: "we order most of our footwear four to six months prior to delivery" → markdown risk on any mis-buy; supports the timing that the first fully DKS-bought assortment lands only ~BTS 2026, but also means a mis-calibrated first buy shows up as Q4/holiday markdowns. (P_FL_10K_A › Risk factors; SOURCE FL_10-K_FY2024 line 298)
- 2024 GM benefit partly non-repeatable/one-off: occupancy leverage from rent renegotiations and store closures (139 closed in 2024), vendor allowances +20 bps. (P_FL_10K_A › GM)
- Highest-margin customer = "young males (ages 12–25)" buying as fashion statement — the exact Nike-lifestyle/sneakerhead cohort a de-emphasis of retro/lifestyle risks alienating. (P_FL_10K_A › Risk factors)
