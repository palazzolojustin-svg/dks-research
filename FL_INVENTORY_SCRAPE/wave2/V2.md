## Method
- Same source and definitions as PB_SCRAPE/wave1/X11.md: Common Crawl (CC) captures of www.dickssportinggoods.com/f/ category pages, embedded JSON `dcsg-ngx-plp-server-state` (first 48 "Featured" products per page). **On sale = displayed SKU offer < list price (by >=1c); depth when on sale = mean(1 - offer/list) over on-sale products; avg discount = mean over ALL products (0 for full price); owned = attribute 6025 "Vertical Brand", national = everything else; products de-duplicated by parent part number within a crawl.** Sampling: same extractor (X11_cc_extract.py copied to scripts/V2_cc_extract.py), up to 800 pages per crawl, 60% priority slugs + 40% random, seed 11. PB_SCRAPE/raw is absent locally, so every crawl was re-extracted from scratch (not copied from X11).
- Infrastructure fix: index.commoncrawl.org resets connections through the proxy, so scripts/V2_cc_index2.py binary-searches cluster.idx on data.commoncrawl.org and reads the cdx shard blocks directly. Capture counts match X11 exactly (Oct-25 815, Nov-25 450, Dec-25 656, Sep-25 2,748, Sep-26 2,250, Jul-26 2,633).
- Crawls used: CC-MAIN-2025-33 (Aug-25), 2025-38 (Sep), 2025-43 (Oct), 2025-47 (Nov), 2025-51 (Dec), 2026-30 (Jul-26), 2026-34 (Aug-26; X11's "Aug-26" crawl), 2026-39 (Sep-26). **No crawl later than CC-MAIN-2026-39 (4-17 Sep 2026) exists yet** (2026-40..43 cluster.idx 404 on 2026-10-08), so there is NO October 2026 or Q4-to-date CC data; the next crawl should appear mid/late Oct.
- Wayback (second source): CDX at web.archive.org works for 2025 (returned 4,999 alphabetically-capped /f/ 200-captures Aug-Dec 2025: Aug 3,459, Sep 908, Oct 272, Nov 136, Dec 225) and the archived HTML carries the same SSR JSON. Extracted all Oct/Nov/Dec-2025 captures with the same parser (scripts/V2_wb_extract.py). **Wayback CDX for Aug-Oct 2026 returned 504 on every attempt (3+3 tries), so no 2026 Wayback data.**
- Live pull 2026-10-08: curl_cffi impersonate=chrome, 4 requests to 2 category pages: all HTTP 403 (1.4KB Akamai page). Playwright Chromium (/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194, headless, no-sandbox), 2 pages: 403. Stopped as instructed; no live data.
- Scripts: scripts/V2_cc_index2.py, V2_cc_extract.py, V2_wb_extract.py, V2_analyze.py, V2_analyze2.py, V2_wayback_live.py, V2_live_pw.py. Data: data/V2_dks_sale_share.csv (month x source; all/national/owned n, on-sale %, depth when on sale, avg discount, 10 brands). Raw in raw/V2 (git-ignored).

## Findings (A hard and directly relevant, B quantified but indirect, C soft, X against the user's thesis; all NEW as a monthly Oct-Dec 2025 series and as a pooled all-SKU series, otherwise KNOWN from X11)
- [A] **Oct/Nov/Dec 2025 comparators now exist**: share of all listings on sale 21.0 / 21.5 / 33.9%; national 20.1 / 20.0 / 32.9%; national avg discount 6.6 / 6.6 / 10.8%; depth when on sale ~33% flat (32.7-33.0). Wayback independently gives 23.3 / 22.3 / 36.6% (all) and 22.5 / 21.0 / 35.7% (national): same shape, 1-3pp higher. So the FQ4 FY25 base is mild in Nov and seasonal in Dec; depth when on sale does not rise into holiday, the promo lift is entirely breadth.
- [A] **Q3 FY26 to date vs Q3 FY25, all SKUs on sale: 23.0% -> 27.0% (Aug+Sep pooled, +4.0pp, 90% CI +2.3 to +5.8)**; national 21.5% -> 24.9% (+3.4pp, CI +1.7 to +5.0); depth when on sale 33.9 -> 35.0%; avg discount (all listings) 8.1 -> 9.9% of list. Category-mix-adjusted (2026 re-weighted to 2025 primary-category mix, 143 categories, 88% of 2025 products): national on sale 21.7 -> 25.6%, avg disc 7.4 -> 9.0%. So the rise is not mainly mix of categories.
- [B] Brand rise is broad and the depth is stable-to-lower: Nike 18.2 -> 24.9, adidas 24.6 -> 34.1, Jordan 20.3 -> 32.6, NB 24.7 -> 39.5, Columbia 17.8 -> 27.8 (more items marked down, shallower: Nike depth 38.2 -> 36.8, adidas 41.0 -> 36.3). Sep-26 national 24.6% vs Sep-25 18.9% (+5.7pp) and Aug-26 25.1% vs Aug-25 24.1% (+1.0pp): the whole y/y gain is concentrated in September because Sep-25 was unusually clean (14.2% Nike).
- [B] Jul-26 (FQ2, not Q3) was the peak: national 29.0% on sale, depth 39.7%, avg disc 11.5%. Aug-Sep 2026 has stepped down from that (25.1 / 24.6%, depth 34.6 / 35.4%): promo intensity is easing sequentially into Q3, still above last year.
- [X] **Composition caveat, material**: on 220 URLs captured in BOTH years (page-averaged), national on-sale is flat, 25.1% -> 24.9%; all products 26.8% -> 28.0%. Whole-assortment "Sale" facet on 245 matched pages: pooled 13.6% -> 14.3% (+0.7pp), equal-weight 24.7% -> 21.5% (down). The pooled +3-4pp comes from which pages were captured. A fair statement: listing-level markdown breadth is **+0 to +4pp y/y** depending on method, central ~+3pp.
- [X] Same-product-in-both-periods (5,550 national SKUs) on sale 14.7% -> 30.3% is survivorship (a year-old SKU is a carry-over, naturally marked down) and should NOT be read as like-for-like promo growth.
- [X] Mean displayed list price of sampled national listings is NOT rising: $115.08 -> $111.62 (-3.0%), mean offer $105.53 -> $102.06 (-3.3%), median offer $60.00 -> $59.99 (flat). That is listing mix (not units sold), but it does not show price inflation in the assortment shown.
- [X] The North Face (33.5 -> 17.9%) and golf brands fell; owned brands are up 7pp (43.6 -> 50.6). Owned markdown is flagged in X11-4.
- [C] Wayback and CC agree within 1-3pp for Oct-Dec 2025, which supports the measurement; 2026 Wayback/live are unavailable.

## Implication for Q4 ticket (INFERENCE, labelled)
- Displayed-price arithmetic: avg discount off list across listings rose ~1.4pp (national 7.3 -> 8.7%) to ~1.8pp (all 8.1 -> 9.9%) of list y/y in Aug-Sep 2026. If sales mix mirrored the listing mix and list prices were unchanged, that is a **price/promo drag on average unit retail (AUR) of roughly -1.4% to -1.8%** (about -1% to -2%); on the matched-URL basis it is nearer 0 to -1%. Mean displayed list price itself is -3%, but that is catalogue mix.
- What this says about Q4: online markdown intensity is HIGHER y/y, not lower, so the data do not support an argument that consensus ticket of +1.0% (or any ticket growth) is conservative because "discounting is falling". The data are consistent with a ticket that is roughly flat to modestly negative from price/promo, to be offset by mix (premium footwear, Nike/Jordan/New Balance share), higher-ticket categories, and unit-per-transaction. A +1.0% ticket therefore already requires mix/UPT to offset a ~1-2pt markdown drag; it is not an obviously low bar. The thesis that consensus "implies ticket weakness that is not there" is NOT supported by listing-promo data; if anything the data lean toward ticket pressure from promotions. (Note: consensus comp 1.59% vs ticket +1.0% plus transactions +1.57% sum to about 2.6%; the components appear to come from different contributor sets, so check before quoting.)
- The one favourable nuance: depth when on sale is only +1pp and for Nike/adidas/NB/Jordan depth is lower y/y (shallower, broader promos), i.e. the extra promo is breadth of items on modest markdowns, and Nov-25's base is mild, so early-Q4 y/y promo comparison will look worse than Q3's unless Nov-26 reverts to ~20%.
- Limits: listing share is not sales mix (best sellers and clearance weight differently); only first 48 featured products per page; online only, no stores, ScoreCard/coupon/loyalty promos invisible; displayed swatch can be a clearance colour; CC page sets differ by month (matched-URL test shows composition sensitivity of 3-4pp); ticket = AUR x units per transaction and category mix (cleats vs hardlines vs apparel), and the DKS comp includes stores; no Oct-26 or Q4 data yet.
