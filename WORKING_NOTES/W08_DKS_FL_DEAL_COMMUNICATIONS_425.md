### Prepared remarks — Hobart (slides 6–11)
- Five benefits: (1) global platform in growing sports retail industry; (2) serve broader set of consumers across differentiated concepts; (3) strengthen brand-partner relationships via global reach; (4) invest in future growth via industry-leading omni-channel experience; (5) unlock operational efficiencies to create shareholder value. "This is a growth oriented transaction grounded in operational discipline."
- Slide 7: FL US real estate highly complementary—stores in different markets serving different needs/demographics; expands DKS internationally for first time; established ops in Europe, Middle East, APAC; $300B global TAM; tailwinds: sport/culture convergence, health/wellness, casualwear shift.
- Slide 8: FL reaches sneakerheads & lifestyle shoppers vs DKS performance-focused core; shared omni-channel commitment.
- Slide 9: more robust platform for brand partners (established & emerging) — new demographics, formats, brand positioning, international footprint.
- Slide 10: FL modernized digital, refreshed stores; continue investing in experiential formats (DKS House of Sport; FL reimagined concept store) and digital.
- Slide 11: operational efficiencies "primarily through procurement and direct sourcing."
### Prepared remarks — Gupta (deal terms)
- Prelim Q1 2025: comps +4.5%; non-GAAP EPS $3.37, ahead of last year; Q1 report May 28.
- Terms: $24/sh; FL holders elect $24 cash or 0.1168 DKS shares; election not subject to min/max cash or stock; implied equity value ~$2.4B, EV $2.5B.
- Synergies: "$100 million to $125 million in cost synergies in the medium-term, primarily from procurement and direct sourcing efficiencies"; "well-supported and achievable." - Combination creates "one of the most dynamic, diversified platform in the global sports retail industry."
### Q&A
- Q [Nagel, Oppenheimer]: why now; brand discussions? A [Stack]: looked at it a long time; FL pressure largely from Nike relationship — Nike moved to DTC/away from wholesale, "hurt Foot Locker pretty significantly"; Nike now "really leaning back into wholesale"; "Elliott [Hill] and his team are doing a great job"; FL "going to be a beneficiary"; "timing of this is perfect"; talked to a couple of brands — enthusiastic, cite DKS operational excellence; brands "willing to help."

A [Hobart]: plan to run the two businesses separately; DKS team focused on DKS growth ("flywheel is turning"); FL team focused exclusively on FL; small team led by Ed to help integration/share expertise; synergies counted on = procurement/purchasing. Customer: FL "Lace Up" plan has terrific elements — reimagined stores, refreshed stores, investing in tech ["tag" as transcribed], personalization, consumer experience, stripers, culture; consumer gets "a reinvigorated Foot Locker"; run as separate entities; "The consumer may or may not know that DICK'S and Foot Locker are one."

A [Gupta]: footwear & apparel largest part of TAM; "footwear is the engine that drives the train"; footwear as combined co "close to about 50% of the total sales of the company."
- Q [Yih]: take out FL bonds? leverage was 2x, now ~3x? A [Gupta]: consideration mix unknown until close; finished last year w/ $1.7B cash; even if fully debt-financed "it won't impact unfavorably our credit ratings today"; intend to maintain strong IG rating; financing = combination of cash, long-term debt, equity; working w/ advisors on possibly exchanging FL obligations, "if need be we'll be able to take those notes out."
- Q [Gutman, MS]: FL shrinking on same-store basis while DKS grows; DKS could take FL share anyway—why buy? A [Stack]: FL serves consumers in neighborhoods DKS "in many cases are never going to get" — can't find 50,000–60,000 sq ft sites w/ return; footwear "the engine that pulls this train"; FL "bore the brunt" of Nike DTC shift; Nike moved back to wholesale; "Foot Locker is a very important part of Nike's long term strategy"; "we're pretty conservative. We don't get over skis"; board "very adamant" deal cannot impact DKS (House of Sport, Field House, GameChanger, tech investment, flywheel); "That glide path is still there." A [Hobart]: TAM grows $140B→$300B via global reach; additive to DKS story.
- Q [Gutman]: distribution protection over 5 yrs (Nike, HOKA, On, Adidas)? A [Stack]: makes DKS "much bigger, much more important partners"; no indication brands will broaden high-heat distribution; brands want specialty environment, premium positioning; "you never know."
- Q [Ohmes, BofA]: Nike % of FL sales to rise? A [Stack]: "there's that possibility"; FL will get more high-heat product; "Nike business will probably go up... at a higher margin rate because they will be able to sell more product at full price"; "a big precept of this."

- Q [Lasser, UBS]: why different from failed retail horizontal mergers (strong + underperformer)? A [Stack]: huge US opportunity (HoS, Field House transition, traditional stores); "we have no plans to take DICK'S internationally."
- Q [Horvers]: customer crossover 20% vs 50%? A [Hobart]: "our market share in this $140 billion market that we've been competing in thus far is still around 80%" [as transcribed]; DKS footwear growth not from taking FL share — 90% of DKS stores have premium full-service footwear decks, improved assortment; "I don't see these two things as being cannibalistic."
- Q [Baker, DA Davidson]: analogy to Golf Galaxy acquisition? A [Stack]: similar—strengthened golf brand partnerships, helped Golf Galaxy and DKS; can't recall share figures; expects same in footwear.
- Q [Baker]: FTC issues/divestitures? A [Hobart]: "we are not expecting any regulatory concerns... not expecting that to be too cumbersome."
- Q [Baker]: procurement synergies = merch or non-merch? A [Gupta]: both — procurement/direct sourcing vendors and non-merch vendors.
- Close [Stack]: next talk at earnings call in a couple of weeks.
