## SRC 04_PEERS/NKE/2026-06-08_BloombergIntel_NKE_Gen_Z_and_Millennials_Affinity_for_Nike_DTC_Purchases_Endures.md | 2026-06-08 | Bloomberg Intelligence industry note / Abigail Gilmartin (BI Industry Analyst, Retail) | BI US sneaker survey May 2026 (>1,000 US consumers)
- Linked report: "BI Survey: Nike Brand Has Pull Across Income Levels" (=SRC 02 parent). Summary: millennials ~quarter of US pop, propelling sneaker momentum; >1/3 plan to boost spending, focus Nike, Adidas, New Balance; lifestyle + athletic sneakers dominate Gen Z/millennial planned purchases, driven by men (buy more frequently); DTC still important but multibrand retailers gaining popularity, driven by convenience.
### 1. Spending intent
- 60% of all respondents expect no change in sneaker spending next 6 mo; >1/3 of millennials and Gen Z expect to spend more than last year on athletic + lifestyle sneakers; male Gen Z lead (greater intent to boost spend vs female).
- Planned spend centers on $100-$250 shoes = preferred budget across all generations/genders; younger men more inclined to spend >$250 (premium/performance/collectible). [chart: Future Spending Intentions]
### 2. Channel (DKS-READ: multibrand/wholesale share gain vs DTC)
- Retailers must balance DTC with broad wholesale; shoppers increasingly favor omnichannel. Buying direct from brands (online or in-store) still preferred by younger consumers but DTC preference slowed from peak: May 2026 48% of millennials and 59% of Gen Z typically buy direct from brands vs 61% and 67% a year ago.
- Gen Z more selective in where they shop (authenticity, product discovery, access to limited-release); millennials broader channel mix → convenience-driven. [chart: Sneaker-Buying Location Preference by Generation]
### 3. Brand intent
- 51% of millennials, 56% of Gen Z plan to buy Nike next 6 mo; Adidas clear No. 2 among younger; No. 3 varies by generation.
- Performance brands gaining: New Balance broad cross-generational appeal; Hoka benefits from comfort/performance; Asics improving momentum vs prior surveys esp millennials; Skechers skews older, esp strong boomer intent. [chart: Planned Upcoming Sneaker Purchases by Generation]
### 4. Categories
- Casual/lifestyle sneakers lead planned purchases across generations; ~half of Gen Z and millennials plan to buy a pair next 6 mo; sustained demand for lifestyle + performance; interest in fashion/dress/work shoes elevated among millennials/Gen Z → opportunity for slimmer profiles, mixed-use sneakers, lifestyle designs. [chart: Planned Upcoming Purchases]
### 5. Purchase reasons
- Replacement = primary driver: nearly half say last purchase was to replace worn-out shoes. >37% of Gen Z + millennials bought for a style/color they didn't own. Millennials lead impulse purchases (promotions, shopping activity); social media/peer influence disproportionately important for younger. Boomers practical, replacement-concentrated. [chart: Top Reasons for Buying by Generation]
### 6. Volume
- May 2026 poll: a little over 30% of Gen Z and 45% of millennials bought 4+ pairs past 12 mo; majority of Gen X/boomers bought 1-3 pairs. Millennials strongest: 11% bought 7+ pairs. Gen Z share buying >7 pairs fell → more selective vs 2025. Buying moderated vs earlier surveys. Women buy fewer than men in all cohorts; millennial women: 32% bought 4-6 pairs. [chart: Pairs Purchased Past 12 Mo]
### 7. Spending power
- Millennials largest US adult cohort (overtook boomers), >$7 trillion spending power; key digital-commerce demographic (buy more online than other ages). Gen Z = 20% of US; per Kantar est $980 billion buying strength; likely to grow exponentially as more join workforce. [chart: % of US Population, Spending Power]
