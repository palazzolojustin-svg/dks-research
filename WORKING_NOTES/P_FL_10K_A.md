#### Lace Up strategic plan / store program
- Aim: elevate, optimize, standardize store experience; bring fleet to brand standard. 8 "Reimagined" concept stores at 2025-02-01 (N.Am, Europe, Asia, Australia; incl 1 licensed in India). Store refresh program (lower capex per store): 407 refreshes in 2024; YE 44% of FL/KFL/Champs gross sq ft at current brand standard.
- Off-mall share of N.Am gross sq ft 42% (2025-02-01) vs 39% (2024-02-03); off-mall favorably affected occupancy costs. Strategic imperative: raise N.Am off-mall penetration to 50% (42% of N.Am locations off-mall).
- 2025 capital plan: continue store refresh; 80 additional Reimagined stores planned.

#### Banners
- Foot Locker: 50th yr in 2024; "Heart of Sneakers", "Stripers"; 1,469 stores in 25 countries: 677 US+territories, 84 Canada, 601 Europe, 96 Australia+NZ, 11 Asia. Avg selling sq ft: domestic 3,500; intl 2,200.
- Kids Foot Locker: 369 N.Am (356 US+territories, 13 Canada) + 7 Europe; avg 2,000 selling sq ft.
- Champs Sports: primarily mall-based, N.Am; launched new brand platform "Sport For Life" in Q2 2024 (sports-style enthusiast); lead banner for apparel; 383 stores (354 US+territories, 29 Canada); avg 3,800 selling sq ft. Repositioned; delivered positive comp in 2H24.
- WSS: acquired 2021; athletic-inspired, Hispanic consumer focus; 151 off-mall stores in CA, TX, AZ, FL, NV; avg 10,400 selling sq ft.
- atmos: acquired 2021; digitally-led, premium sneakers/apparel, in-house label, vendor collabs; 30 stores in Japan avg 900 selling sq ft; brand licensed in Asia.
- Competition: athletic footwear specialty, sporting goods stores, dept stores, shoe stores, mass merchants, online retailers (some are suppliers).

### Risk factors (company-specific substance)
- Strategic plan execution (Lace Up): capital/mgmt attention; customer acceptance of renovated stores/locations, loyalty program, digital, supplier diversification, cost-cutting, integrating acquisitions; missing expectations on sales, supplier diversification, cost cuts, op margins, EPS -> stock volatility.
- Fashion/innovation: dependent on suppliers' innovation; premium-end exposure; long lead times; "substantial portion of our highest margin sales are to young males (ages 12–25)" who buy as fashion statement and are frequent purchasers.
- Competition incl vendors' DTC: "all of our significant suppliers operate retail stores and distribute products directly through the internet and others may follow"; faster shift to online risk. Competitive factors: selection, experience, reputation, location, advertising, price.
- Supplier concentration: 85% of 2024 merchandise purchases from top 5 suppliers; ~59% from Nike; "Each of our banners is highly dependent on Nike." Suppliers allocate high-demand product on own criteria; may divert to own DTC. Negotiated volume discounts, co-op advertising, markdown allowances, order cancellation/return rights—may not continue. Nike reputation/innovation/financial risk flows through.
- Inventory: most footwear ordered 4–6 months before delivery; markdown risk. Initiatives: localize assortment, store-level allocation, data science for product-to-market and supply chain/in-season responsiveness; significant inventory system changes.
- Malls: many N.Am stores in enclosed malls; anchor closures; landlord concentration/consolidation affects lease terms.
- International expansion incl licensing; reliance on foreign partners.
- Comp volatility factors: fashion, innovation, promotions, economy, tax refund timing, mix, holiday calendar shifts, traffic, supply chain, weather; discretionary.
- Disasters/terror/war; Ukraine & Middle East conflicts; public health; social unrest/riots/vandalism; shrink incl organized retail crime.
- Acquisition integration risk (WSS, atmos).
- Tech/cyber: FL or third parties "have experienced and will continue to experience cyber incidents of varying degrees"; cyber insurance may be insufficient. Digital ops risks (bot attacks, DoS, card fraud).
- ERP: multi-year program implementing new ERP (global accounting, ops, finance; warehouse mgmt, order mgmt), phases over next few years; significant investment; risk of delays/cost overruns/ICFR impact. Omni-channel tech enablement complex (fulfill from any store/DC).
- Privacy: EU GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA (CPPA; private right of action), other states; has received inquiries from governmental authorities on practices.
- DCs: mid-2025 planned move of European DC to new facility w/ upgraded WMS; port strikes, carriers.
- Social compliance of independent manufacturers; key mgmt reliance/succession; store/field labor turnover, possible unionization, wage rates.
- Impairment of long-lived assets/ROU/goodwill (estimates from long-range plan).
- Minority investments: $115M non-controlling minority investments at 2025-02-01; lack of control; dilution/down-round risk; investee financing risk.
- Shareholder activism risk.
- Intl exposure: significant portion of 2024 sales & OI outside US; EUR and GBP translation; import/excise duties, VAT.
- Inflation -> lower demand, higher markdowns, promotional environment.
- Stock volatility list (supplier relationship, results, analyst research, guidance changes, competitor announcements, data breaches, litigation, share sales, mgmt changes, volume, market growth, macro); possible securities class action.
- Macro: Russia/Ukraine, Middle East, Brexit uncertainty; discretionary spending sensitivity.
- Tariffs: significant portion of products (incl from US-based suppliers) and most private brand merchandise manufactured abroad; US-China tariffs; recently proposed/enacted tariffs on Mexico, Canada, China; possible retaliation; may need alternative private-brand suppliers or raise prices.
- Financial markets: revolver in place until 2029-06-20. Cash & equivalents $401M at 2025-02-01, mostly short-term deposits in highly-rated banks, all investment grade.
- US pension trust assets $342M at 2025-02-01.
- Tax: multi-jurisdiction; digital service taxes; OECD Pillar II.
- Employment law (min wage, PRO Act, European works councils, NLRB); climate change regs; ESG reporting (California Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act & Climate-Related Financial Risk Act; European Sustainability Reporting Standards); anti-ESG sentiment; litigation (wage & hour, data privacy, class actions); FCPA/UK Bribery Act; SOX 404; intl IP.
- Debt: $400M 4% Senior Notes due 2029. $600M ABL revolver expiring June 2029; borrowing base tied to inventory; financial covenants; rates may be affected by credit ratings.
