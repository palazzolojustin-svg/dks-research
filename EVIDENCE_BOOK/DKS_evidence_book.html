Two long points on DKS, and how much the evidence supports each

One place for everything gathered on the Q4 core comp and owned-brand mix: the research folder, the two overnight web scrapes, and new analysis run for this book. It ends with a short note on why a Foot Locker Europe exit is still an option rather than a thesis.

Point 1 · Q4 core comp is too low

Supported, once reframed

Consensus only works if DICK'S older stores (everything except House of Sport and Field House) stop growing: about 0% comp, against +3.4% to +4.4% over the last eight quarters. That holds at any per-store House of Sport gain you assume.

Two parts of the original framing need adjusting. The scraped data does not show the Street under-modelling the House of Sport gain per store; it points slightly lower. And "ticket collapse" only holds if transactions are flat or better.

Q4 base +$0.09 to +0.13 · 2H +$0.11 to +0.17 · Q4 up to +$0.19

Point 2 · Owned-brand mix is margin accretive

Direction proven, size small

Owned brands are gaining share at DICK'S while private label at Academy Sports, the closest peer, is flat to down, so the gain is specific to DICK'S. It turned up around April 2026 in reviews, listings, new styles and full-price placement.

The evidence supports +0.25 to +0.65 points a year of penetration. That is +2 to +10bps of DICK'S gross margin in FY27. It works as a margin cushion against footwear promotions, not as an EPS driver.

FY27 +$0.04 central (+$0.13 high) · FY28 +$0.07 (+$0.19)

Foot Locker Europe exit

An option, not a thesis

You were right to doubt it. There is real groundwork: one Dutch holding company, a DICK'S executive on every board, local leaders gone, and closures running faster than consensus. But there is no sale process, adviser, licensing hire or held-for-sale language.

DKS is also still spending in Europe: a ~100-store refresh target, new doors and a new European ad agency.

1. Consensus has the older DICK'S stores stalling in Q4

Bottom line. Bloomberg's 1.59% Q4 comp gives a two-year stack of 4.7%, the lowest since the post-stimulus quarters of early FY23. Strip out what the new formats add and the rest of the fleet has to comp at about zero. Those stores have grown in every one of the last ten quarters. Our base case is a 3.0–3.5% Q4 comp, about +$0.09 to +$0.13 a share. The case relies on DICK'S usual pattern of beating its own guide, because consensus already sits slightly above the guide midpoint.

Put the stack in context, not in a headline

Q4 is the lowest two-year stack of the post-COVID era. It is not the lowest ever, and a buy-side skeptic will check.

  • Post-COVID: 4.7% is below all 12 quarters since Q3 FY23 (6.0% to 10.5%), at the 14th percentile.
  • Full history: it sits at the median of non-COVID Q4s. Q4 two-year stacks were 8.8 (FY13), 10.7, 0.9, 2.5, 3.0, −4.2, 3.1 (FY19), then 8.1, 9.4, 9.7 (FY23–FY25). The FY15–FY19 years were the hunting-exit and promotional era.
  • Three-year view: 11.3% matches Q2 FY25 (11.3%) and is 1.2 points below Q4 FY25. Most of the two-year dip is the arithmetic of lapping a soft +3.1% Q4 FY25, and the CFO later called weather in that quarter "net immaterial".
  • Brokers who publish a Q4 number: JPM 2.5% (two-year 5.6%) and Barclays 1.5% (4.6%). Before the August cut, JPM, Barclays and Wells Fargo all had Q4 at 2.0–3.0%.

Ticket: "collapse" is true only if transactions hold up

If transactions are flat or positive, consensus needs ticket to fall 2 to 3 points, which has almost never happened. If transactions repeat last year's −1.3%, ticket only has to ease to +2.9%.

The ticket record. Ticket has been at least +2.6% for 11 straight quarters and at least +3.6% for the last 7, averaging +4.3% over the last 9, the longest run since FY12. Year-on-year drops of 3 points or more happened in 2 of 42 quarters: Q4 FY17 (the hunting exit) and Q2 FY23 (the stimulus lap).

Why it can hold. Management says ticket comes from mix and more units per basket, not price ("It's really not due to inflation", Hobart, March 2026). Category prices are still running about +3.6% to +4.3% (footwear and apparel +3.6% in August). Academy, the closest peer, grew ticket +4.5% and +4.9% in its last two quarters even while its transactions fell. Footwear inventory is lean: UBS's discount factor is down 50bp year on year and weeks of supply are down about 14. Q4 launches carry $210–230 price points.

  • The A-mall tail is above plan. Tampa and Freehold run at about +$24–25M. Macerich calls Freehold its "strongest opening out of 35". Stack (April 2026) said A-mall volumes are "meaningfully higher than what we had originally anticipated".

Claim instead that consensus implies the older fleet stalls, and that the format lift is durable and invisible to the traffic data bears use.

Third-quarter read so far

  • Alt data points to a Q3 core comp of about 3–3.5% (range 2–5%) against 1.69% consensus. A rebuild from Placer's chart data gives +2.7% to +3.3%, and consensus would need older-store visits to fall about 6% in September and October, worse than August.
  • Against that, card data for the week to 27 September showed ticket at only +1.2% and customer counts down about 4.5–5.9%. Management also said Q3 is "going to be a bit more difficult than Q4".

What cuts against point 1

  • Promotions run "at least through the fourth quarter". DICK'S is price-matching and put tariff refunds back into price. Nike says its marketplace clean-up runs to the end of December 2026, and JPM has JD North America like-for-likes at −5% for holiday.
  • Consensus Q4 is above the guide midpoint, so a print at the midpoint is a miss.
  • Q4 FY25 included Foot Locker inventory cleared through Going Going Gone!. That flatters the compare by an undisclosed amount (25–50bp would cost ~$0.02–0.03).
  • Foot Locker enters the comp base in Q4 FY26. If any contributor put a consolidated comp in the DICK'S row, the average is understated; the terminal's contributor count and high/low would settle this.
  • Management guided about 50bp of Q3 cost deleverage, so a sales beat flows through at less than 20%. At segment margin the 2H gain falls to about +$0.06–0.08.
  • El Niño risk: the 2015 analog had a −2.5% Q4 comp. Golf rounds were down ~5% in August.

2. Owned brands are gaining faster than modelled, and it adds margin

Bottom line. Consensus has no owned-brand line and effectively holds the mix flat at about 12.8% of DICK'S sales. From spring 2026 four independent readings of dicks.com turned up: reviews, listings, new styles and full-price placement. The peer control shows the gain is not an industry trend. The margin effect is positive but small: our central case is +3.6bps of FY27 gross margin (+$0.04), with +10 to +14bps (+$0.13 to +0.17) in the bull case. A second, less visible margin lever is that owned prices are holding while national brands discount.

What consensus assumes

  • Headline mix is flat because footwear grew. Owned and licensed "vertical" brands were $1.6B (FY23), $1.7B (FY24) and $1.8B (FY25): 12.3%, 12.6% and 12.8% of sales. DICK'S has no owned athletic footwear, and footwear rose from about 21% to 30% of DICK'S sales over FY21–FY25.
  • Against the sales it can compete for, it is rising. As a share of apparel plus hardlines, penetration went from a FY23 trough of about 17.6–18.3% to 18.7–19.6% in FY25, about +0.3–0.5 points a year. Licensed lines are only ~5% of "vertical" and shrinking. Owned-only sales grew ~16% over FY23–FY25, against 12.5% for the reported total.
  • Bloomberg's DICK'S gross margin is 36.23% (FY26E), 36.24% and 36.31% (FY28E), essentially flat. No broker quantifies owned-brand penetration or its margin effect.

The evidence it is gaining faster, strongest first

Owned-brand share of apparel reviews on each retailer's site (Bazaarvoice public feeds). Academy's 2024–25 drop is distorted by its switch to text-message review requests, which skew to national brands.

1. Reviews, with a peer control. Reviews triggered by the post-purchase email track units sold. At DICK'S the owned share of apparel reviews rose while Nike's fell (25.6% → 24.5% → 21.2%). At Academy, private label fell over the same window. The 2025→26 gap is +3.5 points (z = 5.5).

The defensible claim is smaller: DICK'S own gain of about +2 points is specific to DICK'S, because there is no rising industry trend to subtract. Men's is the cleanest case: DICK'S +2.4 points against Academy flat, with VRST passing Under Armour and owned brands passing Nike. In kids, DSG passed Nike in Q2 FY26.

The first 17 days of Q3 (same email batches both years) show owned share at 46.0% vs 44.2%, men's 37.3% vs 32.0%; Academy was flat.

Is it margin accretive? Yes, and the premium may be widening

The headline markdown data looks bad for owned brands. Matching the same products in both years tells a different story.

  • Inflated compare-at prices explain the gap. Owned list prices on the same products rose 7.3% against 2.3% for national brands, which inflates the "% off" without cutting what customers pay. DSG's full-price share rose (47% to 56%) as its list price went up; VRST's median price rose from $46.50 to $58.00. National markdown listings rose +143% against +15% for owned.
  • Tariffs. Owned brands are most of DICK'S direct imports. Lower duty on them is worth roughly $20M a year (about 13–14bps of DICK'S gross margin) in FY26 versus FY25. The Street books this as generic tariff relief.
  • Management on the premium: 600–800bps (2024), then 700–900bps from May 2025. Gupta (April 2026): "Actually, the margins are even becoming better." Hobart (May 2026): owned brands are "continuing to expand gross margins".

The evidence supports about +0.45 points a year of penetration. At a markdown-adjusted 600bp premium that covers about a quarter of consensus's +7bps of FY27 gross-margin expansion.

How to say it in the pitch. Owned-brand mix is growing faster than the flat line consensus implies, and the gain is DICK'S-specific. Each point of mix is worth 7–9bps, and the premium is holding while national brands discount. That makes it a margin cushion against footwear promotions. The central FY28 effect offsets about 18bps of national footwear margin, roughly a fifth of the ~90bps promo hit JPM sees in Q3. Don't size it as an EPS driver.

What cuts against point 2

  • Discounting. On the day of the census 47–54% of owned products were marked down, against 25–28% for national brands. CALIA's on-sale share rose from 56% to 67%. Owned share of the deepest clearance rose from 8% to 23% between July and September. "Exclusive Brands" sale days run 70–91% off.
  • Women's and CALIA are weak. Women's review share was −0.7pt, with CALIA −4.8pt. CALIA's new styles fell from 245 to 128 and its listings were −5%. Women's is where owned share is highest.
  • Management went quiet. Owned brands got nine mentions on the Q1 FY26 call and none on the Q2 call or at the September Goldman conference. The 10-Q line about expanding the brands was dropped in 2025, and the August 2026 release added "competition, innovation" to the risk wording. Q2 gross margin was credited to tariff refunds, the media network and GameChanger; excluding those, core merchandise margin was about −30bp.
  • History is slow. Mix has stayed between 12% and 15% since FY18 and the $2B goal is still unmet. Academy's private label slipped from 23% to 22%. Owned direct imports are flat as a share of DICK'S imports.
  • The review email paused. DICK'S stopped its weekly post-purchase review email after 11 August 2026 and it has not restarted, so there is no clean Q3 read after 17 August. On the live October site, Nike re-expanded and the listing gain disappears.
  • Small tension with point 1. Owned products are cheaper (women's apparel list price $58.77 vs $97.60), so faster owned mix slightly dilutes ticket.

3. What supplier calls and card and traffic data say

Bottom line. The supplier calls back both points with color on trading up, House of Sport sell-in and private-label momentum. The card and traffic panels show DICK'S beating a falling category by a wide margin. They do not back ticket holding up: card-panel ticket has slowed every month since February. Where those panels look most stretched against consensus is transactions, not ticket. All the hard numbers for owned brands come from the scraped dicks.com data in Section 2.

The 14 supplier calls were AI-moderated AlphaSense interviews in June–July 2026, before the 25 August print and the turn to heavier promotions. Most are small vendors (DICK'S is under 5% to about 18% of their revenue). The card and traffic panels come from Bloomberg's alt-data screens, pulled 4 October 2026.

Scraped alt data: the strongest support

  • Traffic panels miss House of Sport. Placer tracks House of Sport as a separate chain, so the "DICK'S traffic is down" series that bears quote excludes those stores. With them added back, DICK'S-family visits have been positive every quarter since Q3 FY25 (+1.2% to +2.9%).
  • Q3 read. A rebuild from Placer's chart data implies a Q3 comp of about +2.7% to +3.3%. The broader Q3 alt-data read is 3–3.5% (range 2–5%), against 1.69% consensus. To get 1.69%, older-store visits would have to fall about 6% in September and October, worse than August.
  • House of Sport durability. DICK'S own leasing deck (Advan data) shows malls +14% in visits in year one after an opening, against −6% at comparable malls. Advan puts House of Sport at about 1.33x a regular store's visits. Google review velocity roughly doubles at converted stores.
  • Owned brands. Owned share of apparel reviews rose from 40.3% to 46.0% while Academy's private-label share fell. Owned share of listed products rose from 5.82% to 6.76%, and of new styles from 4.7% to 8.1% (details in Section 2).

How to use this in the pitch. Use the supplier calls as color on House of Sport and private-label momentum. Don't lean on them or the card panels for ticket. The cleanest alt-data line: DICK'S is beating a falling category by 5–34 points, while consensus has its older stores flat.

4. Foot Locker Europe: keep it as an option

Bottom line. Nothing in the filings, calls, broker notes or the 30-agent Europe scrape shows a sale or licensing process. The groundwork for a carve-out is visible, and closures are running faster than consensus. That is enough for upside optionality and a tax angle, not for a thesis.

Pointing toward an exit

  • Stack, August 2026, on monetizing or right-sizing Foot Locker assets: "the answer to your question is simply yes". September: "creating a menu… no preconceived ideas".
  • The Spanish European holding company was merged into one Dutch holding company (dissolved 28 Aug 2026). A DICK'S executive now sits on every European board. The EMEA CFO, general manager, merchandising head and HR head have left.
  • 35+ named European closures since the deal. Bloomberg holds Europe at ~550 stores; we estimate ~510–526 by FYE27.
  • European losses carry a full valuation allowance, pushing the tax rate to ~29%. An exit would lower it (the primer puts the benefit at ~$0.65 a share).
  • A new 10-Q line: "including through licensed or franchise arrangements".

Against an exit

  • Stack, December 2025: "EMEA we're keeping that… we're not getting rid of that."
  • A new president of Foot Locker International (ex-Aldi, ex-Tesco) bought $0.5M of stock on 30 Sep 2026.
  • About 100 European Fast Break refreshes targeted by year-end, a dozen openings and relocations, a new pan-European ad agency, and a new Dutch distribution centre.
  • No adviser, press scoop, merger filing, licensing hire, owned-to-licensed conversion or held-for-sale language anywhere.
  • Italy and France (53% of stores) are closing stores at the old run-rate and are profitable at the local-entity level.

What would turn it into a thesis: held-for-sale or "strategic alternatives" language in the Q3 10-Q; a European store count at or below ~550 at Q3; Foot Locker UK accounts (due by 31 Dec 2026) listing closures or strategy changes; a 403-statement withdrawal or a management change in the Dutch registry for Foot Locker Europe B.V.; any owned-to-licensed conversion. Full detail is in the separate FL Europe Exit Evidence page.
