### A6. HoS / big-box conversion analog: ASO new-store productivity
- New stores averaged "approximately $13 million in net sales per store, including e-commerce". The number has been stable as the cohort grew:
  - 34 stores ≥12 months at Q3 FY25.
  - 39 stores at FY25.
  - 44 stores at Q1 FY26.
  - 47 stores at Q2 FY26 (verbatim, 10-Q line 957).
  - (PK, PQ)
- ASO caveat (verbatim): "performance of new stores in year one is partially affected by the season in which the new store opens and the brand awareness in the region".
- New-store sales contribution:
  - 24 FY25 openings: $142.8M in FY25.
  - 21 stores opened since Q2 FY25: $61.5M in Q2 FY26 and $108.9M YTD.
  - (PK, PQ)
- Fleet average ~$19.5M per store (FY25 sales $6,053M ÷ average 310 stores) [d]. New stores therefore run at ~67% of the fleet average [d]. ~21.9M sq ft for 322 stores (avg ~70K gross sq ft, ~85% selling) works out to ~$276 per gross sq ft for the fleet vs ~$186 for new stores [d, assuming average size].
- Cost of growth:
  - SG&A 24.8% → 26.3% of sales in FY25; $84.8M of the increase was new stores. Pre-opening $14.6M for 24 stores. (PK)
  - Opening plan: 20-25 stores in FY2026. FY26 capex $200-240M, 60% to new stores. (PK, PQ)
- 10-K risk factor: new stores "may have lower volumes than prior cohorts"; second-generation space is "very low and highly-sought after by our competitors". (PK)
- INFERENCE: ASO is a new-unit analog (new markets, low brand awareness), not a conversion analog. A DKS HoS conversion of an existing store keeps its customer base and awareness. ASO's ~67% new-store productivity is therefore a floor/bear analog for conversions, not a base case. **None of these three files contains any DKS House of Sport data.**

## B. THESIS 2: DKS owned brands grow faster than modeled and are margin accretive

### B1. ASO private label: penetration, trend, GM effect
- 10-K FY2025, verbatim (source line 294): "Approximately 53% of our customers purchased a private label brand from us in 2025 and approximately 22% of our 2025 merchandise sales was comprised of private brand products." (PK)
- "Approximately 78% of our 2025 merchandise sales was comprised of national brand products". 19 private label brands (Magellan Outdoors, Academy Sports + Outdoors, BCG, O'rageous, Game Winner, Outdoor Gourmet, Freely, Redfield, H2OX, R.O.W.). No single brand above ~12% of sales. (PK, source line 280)
- Positioning (source 294): PL "focuses on in-filling categories and price points that our national brand products may not satisfy", with "limited price-point overlap with national brands"; "our private label brands generate strong brand equity and drive significant customer loyalty". PL is "priced below the national brand price spectrum. As such, we receive favorable product allocations from leading suppliers" (source 292).
- **Explicit statement that PL lowers sales but raises GM rate** (10-K source line 1463; repeated verbatim in the Q2 FY26 10-Q, line 892): "A shift in our sales mix in which we sell more units of our private label brand products and fewer units of the national brand products would generally have a positive impact on our gross margin rate but an adverse impact on our total net sales." Same paragraph: "We have also continued to add private label brands to our assortment of products, which we generally price lower than the national brand products of comparable quality". (PK, PQ)
- GM driver list (source 1477): "product mix including private label brand merchandise sales" alongside promotions, tariffs, freight and shrink. (PK)
- Softgoods vs hardgoods: apparel and footwear carry higher margins than outdoors and sports & rec, so a mix shift to softgoods is GM-positive. (PK, PQ)
  - DKS-relevant INFERENCE: DKS vertical brands (largely apparel) sit in the higher-margin softgoods bucket.
- **Trend:** these filings disclose only the FY25 22% and 53% figures. No prior-year PL % appears in the files read, so I cannot show a penetration trend for ASO from them.
- ASO overall GM: 34.8% FY25 with 22% PL. Scale reference for comparing with the DKS margin structure.
- PL sourcing risk: "much of private label" is made in China, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Cambodia and Brazil. PL is more directly tariff-exposed because the retailer is the importer of record. Q1 FY26 GM took -110bps from tariffs. (PK, PQ)
- PL risks in the 10-K (source 861-881): licence sales minimums, recalls, IP, raw-material price increases. Almost all PL manufacturing is outside the US, so collecting vendor indemnities "may be impossible". (PK)

## E. Items that cut AGAINST the theses
1. **Ticket depends on price, not volume.** ASO transactions are negative everywhere and worsening (-5.3% in Q2 FY26). If pricing laps or is reinvested (ASO already put 70bps of refund money into "strategic pricing"), ticket support fades. Evidence of price resistance:
   - DB: "footwear price inflation beginning to affect purchasing".
   - Williams: the $150 price-point ceiling.
2. **Q4 backdrop softening:**
   - JD NA "significantly softer sell through", with JPM at NA LFL -6% Q3 and -5% Q4.
   - DB US #2: "holiday discounting could rise if 3Q sales don't improve".
   - JPM: a "highly promotional market".
   - BI: Fed hikes and "sticky inflation"; elevated delinquencies.
   - Lower-income gas-price pressure "echoed" by DKS and ASO themselves.
3. **ASO footwear/apparel weakness** (the categories most like DKS): footwear comp about flat in Q1 FY26, then lower footwear and apparel comps in Q2 FY26 (footwear sales -1.0%, apparel flat).
4. **Private label lowers sales.** ASO says PL mix is "adverse" to net sales. A faster owned-brand mix could pull down DKS comp/ticket, which is in tension with Thesis 1. PL is also more directly tariff-exposed (ASO -110bps tariffs in Q1 FY26). ASO, with 22% PL, still runs only a 34.8% GM, so PL is not sufficient on its own for a high GM rate.
5. **No PL trend data.** ASO discloses only FY25 22% penetration; there is no multi-year series in these filings.
6. **New-store productivity analog is weak.** ASO new stores at ~$13M vs ~$19.5M fleet (~67%) [d], with SG&A deleverage of +150bps in FY25. If HoS conversions are modeled like new units, the empirical gain could be smaller than claimed. These files hold no HoS data to rebut this.
7. **Credit:** DKS bonds are flagged as a "pressure point" and a potential "rating risk", which limits balance-sheet flexibility for an FL Europe restructuring.
8. **FL Europe:** no exit evidence at all in these three files.
