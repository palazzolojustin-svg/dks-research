# S3: HoS / Field House relocation uplift: the evidence book (scraped data)
Built 2026-10-07 from THESIS_SCRAPE wave 2/3 files B1, B2, B3, B4, B5, B6, B8, B9, B10, B11 (read in full), plus STATE.md, LEDGER.md, wave-1 H01/H03/H04/H07 (top findings) and B7 (model) for context. Raw files were spot-checked in THESIS_SCRAPE\raw\ (see section 6). Nothing under THESIS_SCRAPE was edited. Scratch scripts: scratchpad\q4sens.py and q4bt.py (they import and rerun scripts\B7_thesis1_model.py with different increments; the original is untouched).

Conventions: DKS FY ends late Jan (Q4 FY26 = Nov-2026 to Jan-2027). "Increment" = incremental annual sales from replacing a legacy DSG with a House of Sport (HoS) or Field House (FH). "Gross" = store-level (HoS sales minus replaced store, before cannibalisation of other DKS stores). "Net market" = change in the whole county/city category, which nets out cannibalisation and share taken from local specialty competitors, so it is a lower bound for DKS. Labels: FACT (document/data), MGMT (company claim), INFERENCE (agent or my arithmetic, marked [d] when mine).

## 1. Bottom line

1. **The scraped data does NOT show HoS relocations beating the Street's +$20M per relocation.** The reconciled per-relocation increment is **~$12-16M gross at the store level (central ~$15-16M), ~$6-12M net of market for a typical relocation, ~$2-7M for 2-into-1 conversions, and $20-28M only for flagship or A-mall sites (Victor, Tampa).** The only method that lands at or above $20M across a broad sample is the QCEW jobs event study (+$21-24M), and that rests on a jobs-to-sales calibration that one test store contradicts by 3x. So the argument "the Street under-models the empirical HoS gain" is **not supported** by the scrape; if anything the tax data says the gain is smaller.
2. **The thesis survives anyway, because the Q4 argument does not depend on the increment.** Rerunning B7's engine at increments from $8.5M (tax-panel average) to $22M (QCEW): Q4 FY26 net format contribution is **0.86-1.84pt**; the legacy-fleet comp implied inside consensus 1.59% is **+0.43% to −0.55%**; the same engine's trailing 8-quarter legacy average is +2.9% to +3.8%. **Consensus needs a ~3.4pt legacy deceleration in Q4 in every case (−3.35 to −3.44pt).** A lower HoS increment raises both the implied legacy comp and the history it is compared with, so the gap stays the same. That is the line to pitch, along with the Bloomberg 4Q26E 2-year stack of 4.74% (verified) vs 9.70-10.77% in the last six quarters.
3. FH is not where the variance is: measured +$0-5M per FH relocation (median ~+$2-3M), ~10-20bp of comp a year.

## 3. Method notes, sources and caveats by evidence family

### 3a. State sales-tax tests (H07 NY/TX; B4 NY FH; B11 VA/MN/IA)
- **Method.** Category taxable (or gross) sales by county/city around the opening date vs a control (state, median peer county, regional sum). Excess = pre-level × (local growth − control growth).
- **Datasets.** NY data.ny.gov ny73-2j3u (NAICS 4511/4591, quarterly); TX data.texas.gov 7z4d-yf2c (city retail trade) plus permits jrea-zgmq/3kx8-uryv; VA data.virginia.gov taxable sales by locality × NAICS 3-digit (2018Q1-2026Q2); MN DOR city × industry xlsx (annual); IA DOR (too coarse).
- **Result.** Pooled relocations/conversions (B11-4): midpoint average **~$8.5M, median ~$6M; only 1 of 6 (Victor) ≥ $20M.**
- **Caveats / AGAINST the tax read (i.e. reasons it understates).** It measures the net market: share from in-county specialty stores nets to zero. It is in-store only (no eCom, ~16-20% of DKS sales). Some NY counties exempt clothing/footwear under $110 (Monroe is unreadable); MN exempts all clothing and footwear (taxable series misses most DKS apparel). The category includes hobby/music/book stores. Small n. Charlottesville is 3 quarters old. NY 2023-26 data is preliminary. Ontario Sep-Nov 2025 = $240.9M misreport, excluded.
- **Caveats FOR the tax read (it is a fair measure).** Chesapeake's closure quarter (2023Q2 $8.25M vs $12.89M) shows DKS sales sit in VA NAICS 451 by locality (verified in raw\B11_VA_451_series.csv). No case shows negative net category sales after opening.
- **My extra flag (FACT, raw\B11_MN_459.csv):** Minnetonka's category **taxable** sales actually fell in absolute terms, $94.8M (2021) → $94.4M → $91.9M → $92.4M (2024). Gross rose $131.9M → $146.9M. B11's positive "excess" relies on control cities falling more. It weakens the Minnetonka read.

### 3b. BLS QCEW county event study (B5)
- **Method.** County sporting-goods jobs (NAICS 451110/459110, private, month-3) at event quarter k minus base-year same quarter × growth of 373 DKS control counties without an HoS. 36 events listed, 34 used, 31 "clean" (drops Tulsa [Scheels opened same quarter], Harris, Dallas, Glendale/Maricopa).
- **Results.** k=0 +140, k=1 +112, k=4 +130, k=8 +120 jobs (all 34). Clean: k=0 +121, k=4 +117, k=8 +90. **Placebo** (same dates on size-matched control counties, 0.5-2x base): −10 to −24 at every k; 71%/64% of events beat the placebo 95th percentile at k=0/1; pooled z 2.35. Own-county retail control gives the same answer (k=0 +124, k=4 +119).
- **$ conversion.** $210-220K of county taxable category sales per excess job, from Victor ($28.1M / 128.5 jobs) and Bass Pro Clifton Park ($31.1M / ~148 jobs) → typical HoS **+$21-24M**.
- **Caveats / AGAINST.** The calibration is the weak link: Johnson City gives ~$70K/job (124 jobs vs +$8.6M taxable), and Chesapeake's 42 jobs (≈$9M at $215K) vs +$3-7M tax also argues for a lower ratio. Large counties are noisy. Headcount includes part-time equally. 2025Q4 cohort has only 2 quarters. HoS staffing runs 2-5x a standard store (Novi 166 vs 34-55), so jobs overstate sales lift relative to a legacy-store ratio. That alone could explain why QCEW gives a higher $ figure than tax data.
- **Side result.** Saratoga NY "+$30M mystery" = Bass Pro Clifton Park (opened 2025-07-30), not DKS. Albany (Latham HoS + Crossgates) category sales stayed up y/y in all four quarters after Bass Pro opened.

### 3c. CMBS store-sales panel (H04, B8, B9, B10)
- **Method.** EDGAR full-text search of CMBS FWP/424H/424B2 annexes for tenant-reported DICK'S sales; 517 + 89 docs read; values checked in context.
- **HoS reads.** Ridgedale $31,197,485 TTM 12/2024 (H, verified); Tampa $12.3M first 3 months (H, verified).
- **Legacy reads.** 22 boxes; 11 clean FY22-24 y/y pairs; 0 FY25 pairs (Crossgates $21.1M TTM 5/2025 and Westroads $10.0M CY2025 are single points).
- **Caveats / AGAINST.** Store-only sales vs omni comp. Tenant self-reported, unverified by borrower. Survivorship and A/B-mall bias. Exact-fiscal-year FY24 pairs give +2.3% vs +5.2% reported (a 2.9pt gap, WF-like). FY23 weighted +0.5% vs +2.6% (2.1pt gap). The ~1pt wedge depends on TTM-shifted pairs.
- **Ridgedale specifics (AGAINST).** $31.2M is 11% below $35M on its face, in year 3. It is only above model after the store-vs-omni adjustment ($35M × 0.80-0.84 ≈ $28-29M), and that adjustment is unknowable if the reported sales include BOPIS. Same filing shows **no mall halo**: in-line ex-Apple $545 (2021) → $547/sf (2024), +0.4%, vs the deck's +17.9%.

### 3d. Traffic panels (B1, B2, H01)
- **Datasets.** Placer.ai Anchor charts (exact values via e.infogram.com/_/<id> JSON); Bloomberg ALTD Placer; Advan public posts.
- **Caveats / AGAINST.** August 2026 legacy banner −6.7% (est.); banner minus open-air centers ~−13pp. Comp-minus-visits gap includes ticket, and ticket is decelerating (BSM +3.5% Aug → +1.2% late Sep). The post-deal Bloomberg add-on (~8.46pp) is still unexplained (FL vs HoS). Visit multiples disagree 1.33x (Advan) / 2X (DKS deck) / 3.3x (Placer data) / 3.2x (Corpus Christi) / 5-6x (Placer text).

### 3e. Municipal documents (H03, B1, B3)
- **What they give.** Primary DKS numbers: Corpus Christi projection, Novi floor plan and staffing, Joliet capex, Braintree deck.
- **Caveats.** Projections, not actuals. Corpus Christi is a full-format 2028 store. Novi's "SALES FORECAST: $" field is blank. Norman OK city staff estimated only ~$26M taxable for a proposed HoS, and council rejected the rebate.

### 3f. Field House (B4, B5-6)
- FH relocations are like-for-like footprints (50-53K replacing ~50K; Niles 52K → 72K). Measured +$0-5M, median ~+$2-3M; durable in 1 of 2 mature cases.
- FH unit model unchanged ($14M year-1, ~20% EBITDA; CFO Mar-2024 and Nov-2025 deck).
- B4's bridge (INFERENCE): ~13 HoS relocations × ~$20M (1.9%) + ~13 FH × $2-3M (0.25%) ≈ 2.2%, which matches WF's FY25 relocation contribution.
- No FH sales anywhere in CMBS. No FH location list exists (no tag on locator, Maps or Workday).

## 8. Against-points summary (for the PM Q&A)
- The empirical HoS increment is, if anything, **below** the Street's $20M:
  - tax median ~$6M; only Victor ≥ $20M;
  - 2-into-1 conversions are common and small;
  - Ridgedale year 3 is $31.2M raw vs a $35M year-1 target;
  - Advan shows only 1.33x visits.
- So "the Street under-models HoS" is not defensible from this data. The thesis should be framed as **legacy-fleet deceleration implied by consensus (~3.4pt, robust to the increment)**, plus the 2-yr-stack optics.
- **Legacy traffic was genuinely weak in Aug-2026:** −5.6% same-store est.; −13pp vs open-air centers. Ticket is decelerating. A promotional Q3/Q4 (Nike discounting) could legitimately compress the comp-minus-traffic gap.
- **3-year stack** for Q4 (11.1%) is less extreme than the 2-year (4.74%), because Q4 FY25 was a soft base.
- **No mall halo** at Ridgedale. HoS capex has risen ~75%. Labour per $ of sales at HoS looks higher (Novi 166 heads vs 34-55 standard).
- **FH adds little:** +$2-3M per relocation, and not durable in Watertown.
- **CMBS limits:** the panel cannot test FY25/FY26 legacy comp; exact-year FY24 pairs (+2.3% vs +5.2%) look WF-like.
