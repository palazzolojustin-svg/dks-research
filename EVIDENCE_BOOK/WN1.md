## 1. Comp definition: how relocations, HoS/Field House conversions and Warehouse stores enter comp

- **Verbatim, FY2025 10-K (SOURCE line 1287):** "A store is included in the comparable sales calculation during the fiscal period that it commences its 14th full month of operations. Relocated stores are included in the comparable sales calculation from the open date of the original location. Stores that were permanently closed during the applicable period have been excluded from comparable sales results. Our digital revenue includes all eCommerce sales, including omni-channel transactions which are fulfilled by our stores, GameChanger subscriptions as well as revenue from our DICK'S Media Network. The Foot Locker Business will be included in our comparable sales calculation beginning in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, which is when these stores will commence their 14th full month of operations following the date of acquisition." (W01 › 10-K FY2025)
- **Why this matters for thesis 1 (inference):** a HoS or Field House that replaces an existing DICK'S store by relocation is in comp from day one, measured against the old store's base. The sales uplift from relocating a ~50K sq ft box into a ~100K+ sq ft HoS therefore lands inside DKS comp straight away. A remodel/conversion in place is not a new store, so it stays in comp too (the 10-K does not separately define "remodel"; inference). Brand-new HoS (not relocations) are excluded until their 14th full month.
- **History of method changes (each restated the prior year):**
  - FY2022: "Comp method revised beginning FY2022 to include relocated stores; FY2021 restated (8-K Exh. 99.2, 2022-03-08)." (W02 › 10-K FY2022). Note that the FY2022 and FY2023 10-K KPI text already carried the relocation sentence (SOURCE FY2022 line 957; FY2023 line 990).
  - FY2024: comp includes GameChanger revenue; prior year restated (8-K Ex 99.2, 2024-03-14). FY2023 comp went from 2.4% to 2.6% (W01 › 10-K FY2024; W02 › 10-K FY2023 shows 2.4%).
  - FY2024 10-K: digital revenue definition adds DICK'S Media Network revenue (SOURCE FY2024 line 1066). Renamed from "comparable store sales ... includes online sales" (FY2022/FY2023) to "comparable sales, which includes digital revenue".
  - FY2025: "beginning in fiscal 2025, we revised our method for calculating comparable sales to include Warehouse Sale locations beginning in the stores' 14th full month of operations ... Prior year information has been revised" (8-K Ex 99.2, 2025-03-11) (W01 › 10-K FY2025, SOURCE line 1444). Going Going Gone! beginning count was restated to include 29 Warehouse Sale locations and 1.3M sq ft.
  - FL enters **quarterly** comp in Q4 FY2026 (W01 › 10-K FY2025 fn 6). The 10-K does not say whether "DKS consolidated comp" in Q4 FY2026 blends FL. Segment comps are reported separately (DICK'S Business comp vs FL pro forma comp).
- **Cuts against:** the repeated definition changes (relocations, GameChanger, Media Network, Warehouse Sale) all push "comp" up relative to a pure like-for-like store measure. The Street can argue part of the ticket and comp strength is definitional. GameChanger is ~$150M of revenue growing about 40% a year (see §2), and it sits inside comp.

- ARS framing of the same numbers: FY2023 comp "driven by growth in transactions" (W09 › ARS 2024-05-02); FY2024 comp "fueled by growth in both average ticket and number of transactions" (W09 › ARS 2025-05-02). - **Guidance vs actual (conservatism record):** the FY2025 DICK'S comp guide given in March 2025 was +1% to +3%; the actual was +4.5% (W01 › 10-K FY2024 vs 10-K FY2025). The FY2026 DICK'S Business comp guide is **+2% to +4%**, with "slightly higher comps in 1H26 primarily due to FIFA World Cup". Segment profit is $1.58-$1.66B (11.0-11.2%), down in 1H and expanding in 2H (W01 › 10-K FY2025). [d] Higher 1H comps mean the guide implies a 2H/Q4 FY2026 comp at or below the midpoint (~3%) on top of FY2025's ~4.5% base. This is the "implied 2-yr stack" the thesis attacks.
- The 10-Ks give no quarterly Q4 comp or Q4 ticket/transaction split. Those need to come from C02/C05 (earnings calls, Bloomberg).
- **Cuts against thesis 1:** (a) transactions are close to zero (+0.3% FY2025), so the comp leans heavily on ticket. Ticket could fade if tariff-driven price increases or the footwear premiumization mix normalizes. (b) Management explicitly front-loads FY2026 comp to 1H on the World Cup. (c) Macro language in the FY2025 10-K is more cautious (Iran / Strait of Hormuz fuel costs; tariffs "recently enacted"), which could hit "traffic, comps, avg transaction value" (W01 › 10-K FY2025 risk factors).

- **Most HoS/Field House openings are relocations or conversions**, so they sit in comp from the old store's open date (§1):

  - FY2025: DICK'S −26; Field House +13; HoS +13, plus 8 other relocations/remodels (5 Golf Galaxy, 3 GGG) (W01 › 10-K FY2025, fn 9). FL relocated 35 stores (15 FL + 15 KFL North America, 5 international).

- **Total store counts:** DICK'S Business 853 (FY2022), 855 (FY2023), 856 (FY2024; restated 885 incl. 29 Warehouse Sale), 888 (FY2025). DICK'S banners total 723 → 721 in FY2025 (5 new, 7 closed) (W01/W02).
- **HoS unit economics (management claim):** "For a new House of Sport, in year one, we expect approximately $35 million in omnichannel sales and very strong profitability, with a target of approximately 20% EBITDA margins." (W09 › ARS 2024-05-02, Hobart letter). HoS shoppers "travel farther, spend more time in store, visit more frequently" than at a typical DKS store. HoS origin: a store that "if put across the street from a current DICK'S store could put that DICK'S store out of business". [d/inference] DICK'S Business sales per store (incl. eCom) ≈ $14.1B/888 ≈ $15.9M, so $35M per HoS is roughly 2.2x the average. If a relocation replaces a ~$15-20M box with a ~$35M HoS, the comp uplift on that store is +75% to +130%. The 10-Ks do not disclose actual HoS comp lift.
- FY2022 10-K: "Over the long-term, our DICK'S House of Sport stores will be the primary driver of this growth" (sq ft) (W02 › 10-K FY2022).
- FY2024 ARS: HoS "commanding unprecedented landlord interest, positioning us to be an anchor in some of the most successful retail centers in the country". FY2025 ARS: "unprecedented interest from landlords" (W09).

## 6. Vertical / owned brands: exact 10-K wording, $, brand lists, year-over-year changes

### Business-section paragraph (verbatim, verified in SOURCE)
- **FY2022 10-K (SOURCE line 254):** "...brands that we own ... such as Alpine Design, CALIA, DSG, ETHOS, Fitness Gear, MAXFLI, Nishiki, Quest, Top-Flite, VRST, and Walter Hagen, as well as brands that we license from third parties including adidas (baseball and football) and Prince (tennis) ... at higher gross margins as compared to sales of similar products from national brands. Our vertical brands are our **second largest brand category, representing $1.7 billion, or approximately 14%, of consolidated net sales in fiscal 2022**. We consider our vertical brand strategy to be a key area of opportunity to increase productivity in our stores and online..."
- **FY2023 10-K (line 257):** same owned list; licensed list adds **Cobra (golf), Marucci (baseball), Lotto (soccer equipment, footwear, socks)**, and adidas narrows to football only. "Collectively, our vertical brands are our **second largest brand, representing $1.6 billion, or approximately 13%, of consolidated net sales in fiscal 2023**."
- **FY2024 10-K (line 255):** owned list adds **Tommy Armour**; Lotto becomes "(soccer and pickleball)". "Collectively, our vertical brands are our **second largest vendor, representing $1.7 billion, or approximately 13%, of consolidated net sales in fiscal 2024**."
- **FY2025 10-K (line 343):** owned list unchanged (Alpine Design, CALIA, DSG, ETHOS, Fitness Gear, MAXFLI, Nishiki, Quest, Tommy Armour, Top-Flite, VRST, Walter Hagen); licensed list adidas (football), Cobra (golf), Marucci (baseball), Lotto (soccer and pickleball). **Prince dropped.** "**Within the DICK'S Business, our vertical brands are the second largest vendor, representing $1.8 billion, or approximately 13%, of net sales in fiscal 2025.**" The 10-K says "vertical brand" includes both owned and licensed names. It never splits owned from licensed $.
- Wording drift: "second largest brand category" (FY22) → "second largest brand" (FY23) → "second largest vendor" (FY24-25). The denominator changes from "consolidated net sales" (FY22-24) to "Within the DICK'S Business ... of net sales" (FY25, post-FL).

## 10. Items that CUT AGAINST the theses (consolidated)
1. **Vertical penetration is not rising on disclosed figures:** ~14% (FY22) → ~13% (FY23-25); $1.7B → $1.6B → $1.7B → $1.8B. CAGR ~2% vs DICK'S sales ~4.5%. The vertical mention disappeared from the FY2024 and FY2025 CEO letters. FY2025 merch margin gains are credited to "quality of assortment", not vertical. The licensed portion (adidas football, Cobra, Marucci, Lotto) is not separable, and Prince was dropped.
2. Footwear (national-brand heavy; Nike share of purchases ~23% → ~25% pre-FL) is the growth engine, which mechanically dilutes vertical mix. Most vertical-brand product is manufactured abroad, so tariff exposure is direct.
3. Comp quality: transactions only +0.3% in FY2025. Comp gains include definitional add-ons (relocations, GameChanger, Media Network, Warehouse Sale). The FY2026 guide front-loads comp into 1H (World Cup).
4. HoS: the 75-100 by end-2027 target was dropped in the FY2025 10-K; the implied ~67 is below it. FY2025 sq ft growth was +1.6% vs ~3% planned; Field House 15 vs ~18 and GGPC 9 vs ~14 against plan.
5. FL Europe: active investment (Haps DC, new International President, "global platform", "expand market share in international markets") and no exit/sale language anywhere in the 10-K/ARS. Licensing/franchise reliance is the only directional hook.
6. Leases not yet commenced ($756M) are consolidated and include FL, so the DICK'S-only pipeline cannot be isolated from these files.
